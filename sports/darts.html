Darts

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Darts is a precision throwing sport in which players ("darters") throw small pointed missiles at a circular numbered board, scoring to exactly 501 with a double to finish (Professional Darts Corporation [PDC], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The professional game is governed by two main bodies: the Professional Darts Corporation (PDC), founded in January 1992 when sixteen players split from the British Darts Organisation, and the World Darts Federation (WDF) (PDC, n.d.-a; World Darts Federation, n.d.). The flagship event is the PDC World Darts Championship at Alexandra Palace, London: the 2026 edition (Paddy Power World Darts Championship, 11 December 2025 to 3 January 2026, 128 players) ended with Luke Littler defending his title by beating Gian van Veen 7–1 in the best-of-13-sets final — Littler became the youngest back-to-back world champion and only the fourth man to defend the PDC title, with the winner's share raised to £1,000,000 (PDC, n.d.-a). Littler, world No. 1 with 14 PDC major titles, was the youngest world champion ever when he won in 2025 at 17 years and 347 days (PDC, n.d.-a). In the women's game, Beau Greaves of England is the 2025 Women's World Champion, pictured below with Littler (PDC, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in women's darts — world champion Beau Greaves and — in men's darts — world champion and world No. 1 Luke Littler, pictured below (PDC, n.d.-a; PDC, n.d.-b) (Professional Darts Corporation, n.d.-a) (Professional Darts Corporation, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the PDC and WDF rules, a match of "501" starts from 501 points and must be reduced to exactly zero, beginning and ending on a double (the outer ring) or the bullseye (PDC, n.d.-a). Players throw three darts per turn ("visit") from behind the oche, a raised throw line 2.37 m (7 ft 9¼ in) from the face of the board, which is mounted with the bullseye 1.73 m (5 ft 8 in) above the floor (PDC, n.d.-a). The board's twenty sectors are numbered 1–20 with double and treble rings and the 25/50 bull at the center (PDC, n.d.-a). A match is played as best-of-sets legs — at the world championship the final is best of 13 sets of best-of-5 legs (PDC, n.d.-a). Missed doubles at match point leave "checkout" opportunities, and the PDC Order of Merit ranks players by prize money to determine seedings and world championship qualification (PDC, n.d.-b). Women's and men's events use identical rules and boards; youth players compete on the PDC Development Tour and at the PDC World Youth Championship (PDC, n.d.-a).

History

Darts grew out of medieval archery practice and soldiers throwing spears at wine cask bottoms, and the modern "clock" board with its numbered sectors dates to the late nineteenth century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The British Darts Organisation (BDO) was founded in 1973 and ran the first world championship at the Lakeside Country Club in 1978, won by John Lowe (PDC, n.d.-a). In January 1992 sixteen leading players — including Phil Taylor — broke away to form the PDC, which held its first world championship in 1994, won by Dennis Priestley (PDC, n.d.-a). Phil Taylor, pictured below, is the most successful player ever with 16 world titles — two BDO (1990, 1992) and 14 PDC (1998–2013) — and 186 professional titles overall (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Other icons include Eric Bristow, Raymond van Barneveld and John Lowe, who hit the first televised nine-darter in 1984; on the women's side, Trina Gulliver won ten BDO world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
