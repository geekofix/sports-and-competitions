Darts

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Darts is a precision throwing game played with small pointed darts on a circular board, in two main forms: steel-tip (traditional, played in pubs and by the professional bodies) and soft-tip (electronic boards). The professional game is governed by the Darts Regulation Authority (DRA), which sets rules and discipline, and the two big professional circuits are the Professional Darts Corporation (PDC) and the World Darts Federation (WDF). The premier event is the PDC World Darts Championship at Alexandra Palace in London every December-January.

Rules

In the standard game 501, each player starts with 501 points and subtracts the score of each visit (three darts), aiming to reach exactly zero and finishing on a double (the outer narrow ring) or the bullseye. The board has 20 sectors with single, double and treble (triple) rings; the treble 20 is the highest score (180 per visit). Matches are played in legs (first to a set number of legs, e.g., best of five) and sets; the PDC World Championship uses a sets format. Missing the double finish or busting (going below zero or to one) returns the score to the start of the turn. Cricket is an alternative game scoring marks in numbers 15-20 and the bull.

History

Darts developed from medieval archery practice and became a pub game in Britain in the 19th century, with the standard board layout fixed in 1896 by Brian Gamlin. The News of the World championship began in 1927, and the British Darts Organisation (BDO) was founded in 1973, running the Lakeside World Championship until 2020. The PDC split off in 1992 and grew into the biggest circuit, with televised events and nine-dart perfect legs. The WDF (founded 1974, official site dartswdf.com) governs amateur darts in over 70 countries. Legends include Phil Taylor (16 world titles), Eric Bristow, John Lowe (first televised nine-darter, 1984) and Luke Littler, the youngest world champion in 2025.
