Crown Green Bowls

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Crown green bowls is the traditional outdoor form of lawn bowls played on a crowned (slightly domed) green, using heavily biased bowls that curve as they slow down. It is governed internationally by World Bowls, which runs the World Bowls Championships (outdoor singles, pairs, triples and fours) and the World Indoor Bowls Championships, and in England by Bowls England. The game is especially popular in the UK, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa.

Rules

Under World Bowls rules, two players (singles) or teams (pairs, triples, fours) alternate delivering biased bowls from a mat toward the jack, a small white target ball. The jack must be at least 23 m from the mat at the start of an end, and bowls that come to rest within the rink boundaries and closer to the jack than the opponent's count for one point each; the side with the closest bowl scores. An end is played with a set number of bowls per player (two in singles), after which the lead plays the jack to a new position. Matches are played over a set number of ends or to a points target, with handicaps in casual play.

History

Bowls with a bias has been played on English greens since at least the 16th century, and the modern biased bowl with its asymmetric weight was developed in the 19th century. The first Bowls Association (England) was founded in 1903, World Bowls (then the International Bowls Federation) in 1905, and the first World Outdoor Bowls Championship was held in Sydney, Australia, in 1966. Crown green bowls remains a club and tournament staple across the Commonwealth.
