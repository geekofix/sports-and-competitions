Chess

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chess is a two-player strategy board game played on an 8 by 8 board with 16 pieces per side: king, queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. The objective is to checkmate the opponent's king. It is governed worldwide by FIDE (Fédération Internationale des Échecs), founded in Paris in 1924, which publishes the Laws of Chess in the FIDE Handbook and runs the World Championship, the Chess Olympiad, the World Cup and rapid/blitz world championships.

Rules

The FIDE Laws of Chess govern all official play: pieces move as defined (the knight in L-shapes, the bishop diagonally, the queen both), castling, en passant and pawn promotion are special moves, and a player may not make a move that leaves their own king in check. A game is won by checkmate; it is drawn by stalemate, threefold repetition, the 50-move rule or insufficient mating material. In over-the-board play each player has a clock; time controls range from classical (hours per game) to rapid (15-25 minutes) and blitz (3-5 minutes), with increment options. Touch-move and scoresheet rules apply in tournaments.

History

Chess's ancestor, chaturanga, originated in India around the 6th century, spread to Persia, and reached Europe via the Islamic world by the 10th century, with the modern queen and bishop moves emerging around 1475 in Spain. The first modern chess club was founded in London in the 18th century, and the first official world championship match was played in 1886 between Wilhelm Steinitz and Johannes Zukertort. FIDE was founded in 1924 during the Paris Olympics and has organized the world championship since 1948. Landmark champions include Emanuel Lasker, José Raúl Capablanca, Alexander Alekhine, Mikhail Botvinnik, Bobby Fischer, Garry Kasparov, Magnus Carlsen and, since 2023, Ding Liren.
