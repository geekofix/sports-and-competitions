Buhurt

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Buhurt (also spelled behourd; from the French "behourd", a jousting/tournament combat) is full-contact armored combat: teams of fighters wearing historically inspired steel armor fight with blunt, foam- or rubber-tipped weapons in formats from small duels to mass battles of more than a hundred fighters per side. The main governing bodies are Buhurt International (BI) — the primary federation after the dissolution of the Historical Medieval Battle International Association (HMB) in 2022 — and the International Medieval Combat Federation (IMCF), founded in 2013, alongside regional bodies such as the Magna Carta and the Alianza Latinoamericana de Combate Medieval (Buhurt International [BI], n.d.) (International Medieval Combat Federation [IMCF], n.d.). The IMCF World Championship 2026 was held at Spøttrup Castle, Denmark, from July 23–26, 2026, with around 600 fighters from roughly 30 countries (IMCF, n.d.).

On the women's side, women compete in buhurt in their own divisions and mixed teams at IMCF and BI events — women's 1v1 and 5v5 categories are a regular part of modern world championships — continuing a tradition in which armored-combat reenactment has been open to women since the modern sport's beginnings (IMCF, n.d.) (BI, n.d.).

Rules

Under IMCF and BI rules, fighters wear a closed steel helmet (historically inspired, e.g. a bascinet with aventail), a gambeson (padded jacket), and rigid protection for hands, elbows, knees and shins; weapons are blunt steel replicas — swords, axes, maces, polearms — with rounded edges and no thrusting points, and thrusts are forbidden: only striking blows count (IMCF, n.d.) (BI, n.d.). A fighter is down when any part of the body except hands and feet touches the ground, or when they are pinned, held or submit; in duels (1v1, sometimes 2 rounds of 1 minute), the first fighter down loses the round. Mass battles (pro fights) range from 5v5 to 150v150 and end when all members of one team are down or surrender; the last team standing wins (IMCF, n.d.).

Scoring and victory: in tournament formats, teams accumulate points by winning bouts — typically each fighter fights one duel per bout; fouls include thrusting, striking the back of the head or spine, groin or knees, grabbing the opponent's helmet or weapon, and throwing an opponent; penalties range from warnings to disqualification, enforced by referees and marshals (IMCF, n.d.) (BI, n.d.). Equipment must pass weapons and armor checks before the fight; historically accurate steel is required, not modern sport fencing gear. Formats and classifications: 1v1 duels (men's and women's), 5v5, 12v12, 16v16, 21v21 and mass battles, plus "outnumbered" formats; age and experience classes exist at some events, and men's and women's divisions are separate but use identical rules (IMCF, n.d.).

History

Modern armored combat grew out of historical reenactment: the Society for Creative Anachronism (SCA) has organized rattan (wooden) combat tournaments since 1966 in Berkeley, California, and in the 2000s steel fighting with blunt weapons spread in Russia and Eastern Europe (Society for Creative Anachronism [SCA], n.d.). The Battle of the Nations, founded in 2009 at Khotyn, Ukraine, became the first large international mass-battle league and was dissolved in 2022; the sport then consolidated under Buhurt International and the IMCF, which today run the world championships, including the 2026 edition at Spøttrup Castle, Denmark, with about 600 fighters from 30 countries (IMCF, n.d.) (BI, n.d.). National teams from Russia, Ukraine, the United States, France, Poland and many other countries now compete regularly, and women's buhurt has grown into a full competitive division (BI, n.d.).
