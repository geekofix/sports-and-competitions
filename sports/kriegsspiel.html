Kriegsspiel

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kriegsspiel ('war game') is hidden-information chess: each player sees only their own pieces and the squares their pieces attack; an umpire (or software) adjudicates illegal moves and reveals captures. It was developed as a military training game by Georg von Reisswitz in 1824. Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are Antichess, Chess, Go; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Kirschenbaum, M, n.d.).

Figure: Kurzon. (2019-09-04). A match of Kriegsspiel in progress [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kriegsspiel-match.jpg

Rules

Players set up screens so the opponent's pieces are hidden; moves are announced to the umpire, who says only whether they are legal. Captures are announced; checks must be declared ('check', 'checkmate'). Modern play uses online adjudication (Kirschenbaum, M, n.d.).

History

Georg Leopold von Reisswitz published Kriegsspiel in 1824 in Leipzig; his son Georg Heinrich Rudolf Johann von Reisswitz popularized it in the Prussian army (the 1824 manual's loss tables are preserved). It influenced H. G. Wells' 'Floor Games' and modern hidden-movement wargames; the SoCal Kriegsspiel Society still plays it (Kirschenbaum, M, n.d.).
