Rabbit Hopping

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rabbit hopping (Swedish: kaninhoppning; also called rabbit agility) is an animal sport in which domestic rabbits, led on a harness and leash by their owners, hop over a course of small hurdles modeled on horse show jumping. It is one of the few animal sports built around rabbits, and it began in Sweden in the 1970s; today clubs and federations exist across Scandinavia, the rest of Europe, the United States and Australia. The competitive disciplines and record jumps are covered on the related page for rabbit show jumping.

Rules

A rabbit is led through a course of low hurdles — with rails that fall when knocked — wearing a harness on a leash; the rabbit must clear each obstacle in sequence without knocking the rails down, and a set time (usually 60 minutes per course in formal rules) applies, with disqualification for running out the clock. Faults are counted much as in equestrian jumping: each knocked rail or refusal adds faults, and rabbits are placed by the number of faults, with time used only to break ties. Courses come in straight, crooked and agility-style variants, and ladder classes from easy to elite govern hurdle heights and course lengths. All breeds are allowed, though very small dwarf rabbits (under 2 kg) and giants (over 5 kg) can find the jumps and distances hard; trainable, willing temperament is the decisive factor, and animal-welfare norms — never forcing an unwilling rabbit — are central to every organization's rules.

History

The sport originated in Sweden in the early 1970s, when a rabbit club there began arranging jumping competitions, with the rules initially borrowed from horse show jumping and later reformed for rabbits. The first national championship — a straight-line easy course — was held in Stockholm in 1987, and the Swedish national federation (Sveriges Kaninhoppares Riksförbund) was established on 3 September 1994. Norway joined in the early 1990s, Denmark began its own Kanin Hop clubs in 1993, England started in 1995, Germany around 2000 and Finland in 2004, with the Scandinavian countries harmonizing rules at the end of the 1990s. The Rabbit Hopping Organization of America was founded in 2001 with the help of the Danish judge Aase Bjerner, whose family also assisted the Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia (founded 2013); the American Hopping Association for Rabbits and Cavies was chartered with the American Rabbit Breeders Association in 2013, holding the first official US national competition at the 2011 ARBA convention in Indianapolis. The sport's growth has not been uncontroversial: some animal-rights advocates criticize leash use, a criticism the clubs answer with strict training and welfare rules.
