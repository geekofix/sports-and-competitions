Powered Paragliding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Powered paragliding, also known as paramotoring or PPG, is a form of ultralight aviation in which the pilot wears a backpack-mounted motor — a paramotor — that provides enough thrust to take off with a paraglider wing from level ground, alone and in still air. Flown low and slow, at speeds between roughly 15 and 50 mph, paramotoring has two distinct competitive identities under the FAI — "classic" all-round endurance and navigation flying, and slalom racing around inflatable pylons — governed worldwide through the FAI Microlight and Paramotor Commission (CIMA). The related unpowered sport has its own page on this site as paragliding.

Rules

In FAI competition, pilots race in solo classes (PF1 and PL1), a women's class (PF1F), a tandem class (PL2) and an electric class (PF1E), with team events completing the program. A classic championship tests all-round flying through tasks: precise navigation over courses of tens of kilometers, fuel-economy, precision landing and take-off tasks. Slalom championships instead send pilots around a tight inflatable course in heats, rewarding pure piloting speed — touching the ground or a pylon, or missing a turn, adds penalties, and each run is timed to the second. World records, such as those for distance and altitude in paramotor classes, are ratified by the FAI under its sporting code. In many countries, recreational PPG is minimally regulated, and pilot training is administered by national federations; safety practice is to avoid strong wind, turbulence and thermal activity.

History

The paramotor was born when British pilot Mike Byrne, inspired by the emerging sport of paragliding, flew a motorized paraglider for the first time in June 1980 in Essex; the concept spread through France, Poland and the US during the 1980s. Long cross-country flights and altitude records followed, and manufacturers in France, Poland and elsewhere built reliable two-stroke units, enabling the sport's classic competitions: FAI-sanctioned paramotor championships reward careful fuel management and navigation across long distances. The slalom discipline emerged in the 1990s and 2000s from pylon-race demonstrations, with the first FAI-approved World Slalom Paramotor Championships held at Aspres-sur-Buëch, France, in 2013 with 80 pilots from 18 countries; later editions in Legnica (2015), Faiyum (2018), Strachotín (2022), Bornos (2023) and Lusail, Qatar (2024) established France, Poland and later Qatar as the leading nations, with Alex Mateos of France and Saeed Al-Marri of Qatar prominent champions alongside the French women's champions Marie and Coralie Mateos.
