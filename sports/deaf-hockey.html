Deaf Hockey

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Deaf hockey is ice hockey for deaf and hard-of-hearing athletes, played under IIHF rules with visual adaptations — referees use flags and light signals instead of the whistle, and face-offs are started visually (ICSD, 2025). At the world level the sport is run by the International Committee of Sports for the Deaf (ICSD): the World Deaf Ice Hockey Championships are staged jointly with the IIHF under the ICSD Deaf World Championship regulations, and the fourth edition was held in Vancouver, Canada, from 8 to 17 May 2025 (ICSD, n.d.; ICSD, 2025). Ice hockey is also on the Winter Deaflympics program, making deaf hockey one of the team sports of the Deaflympic winter program (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Figure: Liza Chekhovska. (2019-11-26). Ukrainian national team at the 2019 Winter Deaflympics [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2019_Winter_Deaflympics_Ukrainian_Team_01.jpg

Teams wear national uniforms registered under IIHF rules and play with standard IIHF equipment; eligibility follows ICSD hearing-loss criteria for deaf athletes (ICSD, 2025). The 2019 Winter Deaflympics featured national deaf hockey teams, and the Deaflympics movement uses the same visual-signal adaptations in deaf ice hockey (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men’s world championships are established, and women’s deaf hockey is growing alongside club programs in Canada, the USA and Europe (ICSD, n.d.).

Rules

Competitions are conducted in accordance with the IIHF Rules and Regulations (version May 2024), with the ICSD Deaf World Championships Regulations governing deaf-specific matters: standard ice hockey play — six players per side including the goaltender, three periods, offside and icing — with all auditory signals replaced by visual ones, such as referee flags and raised arms for face-offs and goals (ICSD, 2025). Eligibility requires meeting the ICSD audiometric hearing-loss threshold for deaf athletes (ICSD, n.d.). Men’s and women’s events are run separately at world championships (ICSD, 2025).

History

Deaf ice hockey developed alongside the Deaflympics, where ice hockey has been contested at the Winter Deaflympics with national teams such as Ukraine’s 2019 squad (Wikipedia, n.d.). The ICSD formalized a standalone World Deaf Ice Hockey Championship in cooperation with the IIHF; by 2025 the event had reached its fourth edition, hosted by Canada in Vancouver with five nations across two regions, meeting the criteria for an internationally recognized competition (ICSD, n.d.; ICSD, 2025). The World Deaf Ice Hockey Federation (WDIHF) promotes the championship series (ICSD, n.d.).
