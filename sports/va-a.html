Va'a

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Va'a is the Polynesian outrigger canoe sprint: crews of one (V1), six (V6) or twelve (V12) paddlers race single-hulled outrigger canoes over 500 m and 1,000 m in flat or open water, with seat changes at the halfway mark in V6 and V12 (International Va'a Federation [IVF], n.d.). The International Va'a Federation, founded in 1986, governs the sport and sanctions the Va'a World Sprint Championships and World Distance Championships; va'a has been a Pacific Games medal sport since 1995 and is an ICF ocean-racing discipline at the world level (IVF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 IVF Va'a World Sprint Championships took place at Marina Bay, Singapore, from 17 to 31 August 2026 with 2,222 athletes from 29 countries and territories, including elite, junior, master and para categories (IVF, 2026; TotalPaddler, 2026). Women's crews compete in the same V1, V6 and V12 events — at Singapore 2026 Aotearoa New Zealand won the elite women's V12 500 m in 2:10.15 and the open women's V12 500 m in 2:06.69 with Horouta Waka Hoe (NZ Herald, 2026). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are Outrigger Canoeing, Canoe Sprint, Canoe Marathon, Waka Ama, Para Va'a, Surf Ski; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Races are held over 500 m (sprint championships) and longer distances up to 30 km (distance championships) in V1, V6 and V12 canoes; V6 and V12 crews change seats once per race at a marked zone. Canoes are measured and classed by the IVF technical rules, paddles are single-bladed, and races are started from aligned pontoons or beach starts depending on the venue. Para va'a events are run under ICF paracanoe rules in the VL classes over 200 m (IVF, n.d.; ICF, n.d.). Men's and women's events are held in identical boat classes; age categories run from junior U14/U16/U19 through open to master 40/50/60+ (IVF, 2026).

History

Polynesian outrigger canoeing has centuries of history in Tahiti, Samoa, Tonga, the Cook Islands and Hawai'i, where va'a were the principal watercraft for travel, fishing and warfare. Modern competitive va'a sprint developed in French Polynesia in the 1980s, and the IVF was founded in 1986 to standardize the V1/V6/V12 classes; the first IVF World Sprint Championships were held in 1988, and va'a became a Pacific Games medal sport in 1995. Iconic crews include the Tahitian national teams that have dominated elite V6 sprinting for decades (Wikipedia, n.d.; IVF, n.d.).
