Inline Speed Skating

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Inline speed skating is speed skating on inline skates (typically four large wheels, e.g. 4x110 mm or 3x125 mm) instead of ice blades, raced on 200 m banked tracks or on open road circuits. Distances range from a 100 m sprint to the 42.195 km marathon, in sprint, points, elimination and relay formats. The sport is governed internationally by World Skate, the IOC-recognized federation for skateboarding and roller sports, which stages the Inline Speed Skating World Championships on track and road.

Rules

Track events are held on a 200 m banked oval and include the 200 m dual time trial, sprints over 100 m, 500 m, 1000 m and one lap, points races (e.g. 5,000 m and 10,000 m, where intermediate sprints award points), elimination races (e.g. 10,000 m and 15,000 m, where the last skater is eliminated at each sprint) and the 3,000 m relay. Road events include the 10,000 m points race, 15,000 m elimination and the 42.195 km marathon on open or closed circuits. All skaters must wear a hard helmet, and leather suits are worn at elite level; false starts, cutting the course and dangerous riding are penalized.

History

Competitive roller speed skating dates back to the 1930s, with world championships held unofficially from 1937 and officially since 1966 under the international roller sports federation founded in 1952, now World Skate. For decades the sport was raced on traditional quad skates, but the introduction of inline skates in 1992 transformed the sport technologically. Italy and other European nations dominated the early decades, the United States dominated from the 1980s to the 2000s, and Colombia has been the leading nation since the 2010s. Stars include Belgian Bart Swings, a multiple world champion and Olympic speed skating medalist on ice. The inline marathon at the Berlin Marathon is among the biggest mass-participation skate races in the world.
