Adventure Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Adventure Racing is a multisport endurance race in which teams of two to four athletes (often mixed gender) cover long distances on foot and by mountain bike, kayak or other means, navigating with map and compass through checkpoints. Races range from a few hours to a full week. The Adventure Racing World Series (ARWS) crowns a world champion.

Rules

Teams must visit a series of checkpoints in any order and navigate between them using only a map, compass and GPS where allowed. The fastest team to visit all checkpoints wins. Rules cover mandatory equipment (first aid, whistle, lights), team composition (all members must stay together within a set distance), penalties for missed checkpoints and safety requirements. Sleep is optional and teams choose their own strategy — a hallmark of the sport.

History

Adventure racing emerged in the 1980s and 1990s from expedition races and the French Raid Gauloises, which began in 1989 and combined trekking, mountain biking, climbing and paddling. The ARWS was founded in the early 2000s and runs the Adventure Racing World Championship.
