Alpine Skiing Slalom

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Slalom is the most technical alpine skiing discipline, governed by the FIS: skiers race the shortest course with the tightest, quickest turns around a series of gates, making two runs. It is a core Olympic event decided by combined time.

Rules

Under FIS rules each athlete makes two runs; in the second run the starting order is the reverse of the first-run results (top 30 start in inverted order). The two times are added and the lowest combined time wins. Gates are set in pairs (quick turns) and skiers must pass with both ski tips through each gate; missing a gate means disqualification. Ski poles are used to block gates. Variants: parallel slalom (two skiers race head-to-head on identical courses) is also contested at World Cup level.

History

Slalom has been on the Olympic program since 1936 and the FIS World Championships since 1931. Its quick-turn character has produced icons like Ingemar Stenmark (SWE), Marcel Hirscher (AUT) and Mikaela Shiffrin (USA).
