Australian Rules Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Australian Rules Football (Aussie rules) is a contact ball sport played on a large oval field with 18 players per side. Teams score by kicking the oval ball through the opponent's goal posts: 6 points for a goal (between the two big posts), 1 point for a behind (between a big and a small post). The AFL (Australian Football League) is the top professional competition; the sport is Australia's most popular winter sport.

Rules

A match has four quarters of about 20 minutes (plus time on). Players can run with the ball but must bounce it every 15 m; they are tackled and can be awarded free kicks. Key skills are kicking (drop punt, torpedo, banana), handballing, marking (catching a kicked ball cleanly) and rucking. After a goal, play restarts from the goal square; after a behind, the defending team kicks the ball back into play from behind its own goal. Variants: AFLW (the women's league), AFL 9s and state leagues use modified rules.

History

Australian rules football codified in Melbourne in the 1850s (first rules 1859, attributed to Tom Wills and others), growing out of informal football games played in the goldfields. The VFL (founded 1897, renamed AFL in 1990) became the national competition; the AFL Women's (AFLW) launched in 2017.
