Racquetball

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Racquetball is an indoor racquet sport played on a fully enclosed court 40 feet long, 20 feet wide and 20 feet high, in which two (singles) or four (doubles) players use a short strung racquet to hit a hollow rubber ball off the walls and ceiling to win rallies (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed internationally by the IRF — International Racquetball Federation, founded in 1979 with 13 national federations and a member of ARISF/GAISF and a WADA signatory — which organizes the Racquetball World Championships (first held in 1981 as part of the World Games, biennial since 1984), and in the United States by USA Racquetball; the sport has been on the World Games programme since 1981 and the Pan American Games since 1995 (IRF, n.d.). Men's and women's divisions exist at every level, and at the 23rd IRF World Championships at the Centro Deportivo G2 in Temuco, Chile, 16–24 October 2026, the women's singles title went to Argentina's María José Vargas, who beat Guatemala's Gabriela Martínez 8–11, 11–1, 11–3, 11–3 for her first world crown — she was also the 2025 World Games champion in Chengdu and world No. 1 — while Bolivia's Conrrado Moscoso beat the USA's Jake Bredenbeck 11–8, 11–7, 8–11, 12–10 for his second men's title (after 2022) and the first ever by a Bolivian, and Mexico swept all three doubles titles, with Canada winning the men's team event (first since 2002) and Argentina the women's team event (first ever) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Pro Racquetball Stats, n.d.). Racquetball grew out of Squash and American handball, and belongs to the walled-court branch of the racket family — Rackets, Hardball Squash, Squash Tennis — with the full list under Important Links.

Rules

Under the official rules, the server stands in the service zone between the short line and the service line, bounces the ball once and strikes it to the front wall so that it rebounds beyond the short line without touching the floor again; drive serves and the lob Z-serve are the standard options, and two service attempts are allowed per point (USA Racquetball, n.d.). Since the 2021 World Championships, rally scoring applies — the winner of every rally scores a point — and at World Championships since 2022 matches are best of five games to 11 points, win by 2 (previously games to 15 with an 11-point tiebreak) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ball may be played off any wall and the ceiling, but must reach the front wall before its second floor bounce; hitting an opponent with the ball wins the rally for the hitter, while a "hinder" — blocking the opponent's shot — is a replay, and repeated obstruction is a hinder out (USA Racquetball, n.d.). Safety goggles are required; the racquet may be at most 22 inches (about 56 cm) long, and the ball is a hollow rubber sphere roughly 2.25 inches (5.7 cm) across (USA Racquetball, n.d.). Singles, men's doubles, women's doubles and mixed doubles are played, with junior and senior world championships alongside the open events (IRF, n.d.).

History

Racquetball was invented in 1949–50 by Joseph Sobek, a tennis professional and handball player in Greenwich, Connecticut, who combined the rules of squash and paddleball and originally called the game "paddle rackets" before working with a sporting-goods manufacturer on a short racquet and a lively hollow rubber ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport boomed in American fitness clubs in the 1970s — by the early 1980s more than ten million Americans were estimated to play — and the International Amateur Racquetball Federation was founded in 1968 in Memphis, becoming today's IRF (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). World championships have run since 1981, and the modern era has been defined by icons such as Rocky Carson of the USA, holder of a record five men's world singles titles, Mexico's Paola Longoria, dominant women's champion for nearly two decades, the Australian all-rounder Heather McKay, and professionals Sudsy Monchik and Michelle Gould (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
