Racquetball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Racquetball is an indoor racquet sport played on a fully enclosed court, 40 feet long, 20 feet wide and 20 feet high, in which two or four players use a short stringed racquet to hit a hollow rubber ball off the walls and ceiling in order to win rallies. Points are scored only by the server, and the standard game goes to 15 points. The sport's governing bodies are USA Racquetball in the United States and the International Racquetball Federation (IRF), which stages world championships and has kept the sport in the World Games program since 1981.

Rules

Under the official rules, the server stands in the service zone between the short line and the service line and must bounce the ball once and hit it to the front wall so that it rebounds over the short line without touching the floor again; drives and the lob-Z serve are the standard options, and two service attempts are allowed per point. The server scores a point when winning a rally; if the receiver wins, the serve passes over (side-out). The ball may be played off any wall and the ceiling, but must return to the front wall on each hit before touching the floor twice. Hitting the opponent with the ball scores the rally for the hitter, but "hinder" faults — standing so close that the opponent cannot take their shot — result in a replay of the rally. Games go to 15 points (11 in the deciding tiebreak game of a match), with match formats typically best of three. Safety requires goggles; the racquet is a maximum of 22 inches long, with a strung area no more than 11 inches long.

History

Racquetball was invented in 1949-50 by Joseph Sobek, a tennis professional and handball player in Greenwich, Connecticut, who combined the rules of squash and paddleball and worked with a sporting goods manufacturer to create a short racquet strung for a lively, hollow rubber ball, originally calling the game paddle rackets. The sport boomed in American fitness clubs in the 1970s — by the early 1980s it was estimated to have well over ten million players in the United States — and the International Amateur Racquetball Federation was founded in 1968 in Memphis, becoming the IRF, with world championships for men and women held since 1981. Mexico, the United States and Canada have produced most of the world champions, with players such as Michelle Gould (née Gilman) and Sudsy Monchik defining the modern professional era; the sport has been part of the World Games since the first edition in 1981, keeps a professional tour in the United States, and has enjoyed strong followings in Latin America and Asia.
