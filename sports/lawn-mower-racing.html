Lawn Mower Racing

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lawn mower racing is an amateur motorsport in which modified ride-on lawn mowers race wheel-to-wheel on short oval dirt, grass or asphalt tracks (British Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is most established in the United Kingdom, where the British Lawn Mower Racing Association (BLRA) was founded in 1973, and in the United States, where the US Lawn Mower Racing Association (USLMRA) was founded in 1999 (US Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Machines start as ordinary production ride-on mowers whose cutting decks are removed for safety; classes range from near-standard "stock" machines to heavily modified machines whose engines and chassis are extensively reworked, reaching speeds of 50 mph (80 km/h) or more in the fastest classes on longer ovals (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Meetings typically run heats, semi-finals and a final on quarter-mile ovals, and both men and women compete in the same classes at club and national level (British Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.).

Rules

Each national body publishes its own rulebook; the BLRA and USLMRA rules are broadly aligned (British Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.; US Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.). A racing mower must be based on a production ride-on lawn mower originally built to cut grass, and the cutting deck and blades must be removed before racing; steering and brakes must remain functional (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Engine, exhaust, gearing and chassis modifications are permitted within the limits of each class, which separate stock, prepared-stock and modified machines so that costs stay low (US Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.). Mandatory safety equipment includes a crash helmet, gloves, long-sleeved top, long trousers, sturdy boots and a driver-operated kill switch on an armband tether; machines are scrutineered before each meeting and drivers must be licensed by the association (British Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.). Races are short sprints of a few laps, run as heats plus a final, with a points championship decided over the season; junior classes introduce young drivers to the sport (US Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.). Men's and women's competitors race under identical rules in the same classes, and there are no separate women's machines or weight classes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Lawn mower racing grew out of informal garden races in the United Kingdom in the late 1960s and early 1970s, when enthusiasts discovered that standard ride-on mowers could be made to race safely once the blades were removed (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The BLRA was founded in 1973 and organized the first national championships, giving the sport formal classes, insurance and scrutineering; no single inventor is credited, and the BLRA's founding year is treated as the start of organized competition (British Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.). The sport crossed the Atlantic in the 1990s, and the USLMRA was founded in 1999 to govern American racing, which quickly developed faster modified classes (US Lawn Mower Racing Association, n.d.). Australia has a strong club scene too — the Swifts Creek Lawnmower Races in Victoria have been a fixture since the late 1970s — and women have taken part in British and American club racing from the earliest decades of the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
