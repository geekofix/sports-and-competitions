Khapsagay

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Khapsagay (also spelled Hapsagai) is the traditional wrestling style of the Sakha (Yakut) people of Yakutia, the Sakha Republic in Russia's Far East. It is a standing wrestling style in which the goal is to throw the opponent so that he touches the ground with a third point of support — for example a hand or a knee besides his two feet. Khapsagay has been practiced for centuries at the summer ysyakh festivals and is now a recognized competitive sport, included in the program of the World Nomad Games since 2014.

Rules

Under the official rules used at the World Nomad Games, two wrestlers face each other on a flat area and may grab each other's arms, legs and clothing, and use trips and throws; the bout is won when the opponent is forced to touch the ground with any third point of support. Grabbing the fingers, grabbing the head with both hands, and brutal or dangerous wrestling are prohibited and penalized. Matches are held in weight and age categories, with a referee and judges overseeing the bout; contests at major tournaments use a direct-elimination or round-robin format.

History

Wrestling has been central to Sakha culture for centuries: khapsagay was practiced at the ysyakh summer festivals, where whole districts gathered and the strongest wrestlers of each village competed for honor and prizes. Alongside khapsagay, the Sakha preserve other national sports such as mas-wrestling (stick pulling) and horse racing. In the 20th century the style was codified into a competitive sport with weight classes and formal rules, and it was added to the program of the World Nomad Games, first held in Cholpon-Ata, Kyrgyzstan, in 2014. Today khapsagay has its own federation in Yakutia and national championships, and it is taught in schools across the Sakha Republic.
