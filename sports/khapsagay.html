Khapsagay

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Khapsagay is the traditional standing wrestling style of the Sakha (Yakut) people of the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) in Russia, contested at the summer ysyakh festivals and on the programme of the World Nomad Games since 2014 (World Nomad Games, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A bout is won by forcing the opponent to touch the ground with any third point of support besides the two feet — a hand, knee, back or elbow — using grips on the arms, legs, torso or clothing, with trips and throws allowed (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a). Grabbing the fingers, grabbing the head with both hands and brutal techniques are prohibited (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a). The Khapsagai Wrestling Federation of the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) was founded in 2010, and an International Khapsagai Federation coordinates the sport abroad (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the VI World Nomad Games, held 31 August–6 September 2026 in Bishkek and Cholpon-Ata (Issyk-Kul), Kyrgyzstan — 43 sports, around 3,000 athletes from more than 100 countries — khapsagai was one of the wrestling disciplines contested at the Gazprom Sports Complex (World Nomad Games, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women and youth categories are contested alongside the men's events at the Games (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a).

Rules

The official rules of khapsagai wrestling are published by the World Nomad Games (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a). Wrestlers start standing, having taken a grip on the opponent's arms, legs, torso or clothing; the bout ends as soon as one wrestler touches the ground with a third point of support, and the opponent wins (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a). Permitted techniques include trips, sweeps, throws and lifts; prohibited acts are grabbing the fingers, seizing the head with both hands, striking and any brutal or dangerous wrestling, which the referees penalize (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Bouts are officiated by a central referee with side judges, are held on a mat or grass circle, and are organized in weight and age categories at the World Nomad Games, with men's and women's divisions (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a). Historically the style was given structured rules in 1927 and became a recognized competitive sport in Yakutia, with the Khapsagai Wrestling Federation of RS(Ya) founded in 2010 as its governing body in the republic (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Rule source: World Nomad Games, Khapsagai Wrestling Rules, https://worldnomadgames.org/ (World Nomad Games, n.d.-a).

History

Khapsagay grew out of the wrestling traditions of the Sakha people, practiced for centuries at the summer ysyakh festivals, where strong wrestlers demonstrated their skill before the community (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first structured rules were drawn up in 1927, and the sport was organized federatively in the 20th century, culminating in the founding of the Khapsagai Wrestling Federation of the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) in 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Internationally, khapsagay entered the programme of the World Nomad Games in 2014 and has been contested at every edition since (World Nomad Games, n.d.-b). The Games themselves were created by the philanthropist and sports official Askhat Akibaev and launched at the highest state level by the President of Kyrgyzstan Almazbek Atambayev in 2014; editions were held in Cholpon-Ata in 2014, 2016 and 2018, in Iznik, Türkiye, in 2022, in Astana, Kazakhstan, in 2024, and in Bishkek and Cholpon-Ata, Kyrgyzstan, from 31 August to 6 September 2026, with the next edition scheduled for Kazan in 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 2021 the World Nomad Games were inscribed by UNESCO on the Register of Best Safeguarding Practices of the 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of Intangible Cultural Heritage (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
