Belt Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Belt wrestling is a form of wrestling in which two belted contestants grapple by holding each other's belts instead of clothing or bare limbs, aiming to throw the opponent to the mat. Hundreds of national styles exist; the internationally competed ones are Alysh (Kyrgyz) and Kurash (Uzbek/Turkic). United World Wrestling (UWW) recognizes belt wrestling as an associated style and holds UWW World Belt Wrestling Championships.

Rules

Under UWW associated-style rules, belt wrestling bouts take place on a mat with weight classes, and the goal is to throw the opponent so that the back or a large part of the body lands on the mat (a fall), or to accumulate technical points. In Alysh, throws score 1, 2 or 6 points based on how the opponent lands, and the first to 6 points wins. In Kurash, the first to throw the opponent onto the back wins immediately. Belt wrestling is contested at the World Nomad Games, the Asian Games and the World Combat Games in addition to UWW events.

History

Belt wrestling is one of the oldest recorded sports: the Epic of Gilgamesh (c. 2000 BC) describes Gilgamesh and Enkidu belt wrestling, and bronze statuettes from c. 2600 BC show wrestlers. Turkic peoples of Central Asia have practiced belt wrestling styles for centuries, and the modern international disciplines Alysh and Kurash were codified in the early 2000s. UWW (then FILA) recognized Alysh in 2008, and UWW World Belt Wrestling Championships have been held since 2014.
