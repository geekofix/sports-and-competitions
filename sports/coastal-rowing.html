Coastal Rowing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Coastal rowing is the sea-rowing discipline of World Rowing (FISA), founded in 1892, in which crews start on the beach, run through the surf, row a marked course at sea and sprint back onto the sand (World Rowing, n.d.). The flagship event is the World Rowing Coastal Championships, first held as a World Challenge event in 2006 and given full world-championship status in 2007; the 2026 edition took place from 11 to 13 October 2026 in Qingdao, China, alongside the senior World Rowing Championships (World Rowing, 2026). In coastal rowing the heats and B-finals are raced over 4 km and the A-finals over 6 km, with a beach start and finish through the surf zone, turning buoys and a slalom section between them (World Rowing, n.d.). The events are men's and women's solo, men's and women's double sculls, mixed double sculls and coxed quadruple sculls; at the 2026 championships the USA's Kory Rogers and Chris Bak won the men's double sculls, Finland's Joel Naukkarinen and Eeva Karppinen the mixed double, Britain's Bass Andre took silver in the women's solo and women's coxed quad, and the Peruvian twins Alessia and Valeria Palacios took silver in the women's double and bronze in the quad (World Rowing, 2026). Rules

Coastal rowing is governed by the World Rowing (FISA) competition regulations and the specific coastal rowing rules published by World Rowing, the world federation founded in 1892 (World Rowing, n.d.). Crews launch from the beach, cross a start line in the water, row a course marked by turning buoys that includes a slalom section, and finish with a beach sprint to a finish line on the sand; heats and B-finals run 4 km, A-finals 6 km (World Rowing, n.d.). The boat classes are the solo (one rower, two oars), double sculls (two rowers, two sculls each), coxed quadruple sculls (four rowers plus coxswain) — all in dedicated self-bailing coastal boats — and the events are split by gender and by mixed crews: men's and women's solo, men's and women's double sculls, mixed double sculls and men's and women's coxed quads (World Rowing, n.d.). Qualification runs through heats, repechage where needed and semifinals into A/B finals; the fastest total time wins, and races can be decided photo-finish style at the beach (World Rowing, n.d.). Men's and women's events use the same boats and courses; mixed events require one woman and one man in the double sculls; junior and U23 coastal championships are held separately (World Rowing, n.d.). The rule source is World Rowing (worldrowing.com), the world governing body (World Rowing, n.d.).

History

Organized coastal rowing began in the 1990s in France and Italy, where rowers raced along the coast in traditional fixed-seat boats, and the first international coastal regatta under the name World Rowing Challenge was held in 2006; in 2007 the event became the official World Rowing Coastal Championships (World Rowing, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The discipline grew rapidly in Mediterranean countries — Italy, France, Spain, Greece, Turkey — and in 2015 the World Rowing Congress voted to add coastal rowing to the senior World Rowing Championships program, which happened in 2026 when the coastal championships were staged in Qingdao, China, alongside the flat-water worlds for the first time (World Rowing, 2026). Beach sprint rowing, a shorter 500 m-ish beach-to-beach sprint format, was developed in parallel and debuted as a medal event at the 2024 Paris Olympics, run under the same World Rowing umbrella (World Rowing, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Milestones include the first medal ceremony at the 2024 Olympics in Paris and the record entries of crews such as the Italian and French quads that dominated the 2010s; no single iconic individual dominates the young sport, but the 2026 Qingdao double titles of the USA men's pair and the Finnish mixed pair mark the current elite (World Rowing, 2026).
