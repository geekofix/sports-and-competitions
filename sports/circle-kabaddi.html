Circle Kabaddi

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Circle kabaddi (Punjabi kabaddi) is the traditional outdoor form of kabaddi from the Punjab region, played on a circular pitch (kaudi da bharha) about 22 m in diameter with an inner circle and a midline (“pala” gate) through the centre. Two teams of eight players compete; raids are one-on-one, no player leaves the field when touched, and the raider does not need to chant “kabaddi.” The circle-style Kabaddi World Cup has been organized by the government of Punjab since 2010 (women's tournament since 2012; the 2020 edition was won by Pakistan), and the Amateur Circle Kabaddi Federation of India (ACKFI) governs the sport nationally; men's and women's national teams compete internationally.

Within the tag and chase games on this site, its closest relatives are kabaddi, kho-kho and langdi; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The pitch is a circle of 22 m diameter (16 m for some women's/junior categories) divided into two halves by a midline with a central pala gate. A match lasts 40 minutes (two 20-minute halves, change of sides). One raider at a time enters the opponents' half; a touched defender has 30 seconds to stop the raider from returning to his own half — if stopped, the defenders score, if the raider returns, the raiders score. A raider or defender stepping out of bounds gives a point to the other side; two defenders grabbing one raider is a foul. The team with more points wins. World Kabaddi's published rules (approved for international competition) apply to the standardized game.

History

Circle-style kabaddi developed from the traditional rural kabaddi of the Punjab, where many local styles exist (lambi kauddi, saunchi kauddi, goongi kabaddi and more). The Punjab government began the circle-style Kabaddi World Cup in 2010 — India won every edition until Pakistan triumphed in 2020 — and the Asia Kabaddi Cup (circle style) was held in 2011, 2012 and 2016. The UK Kabaddi Cup (2013) and the short-lived World Kabaddi League (2014) spread the game in the diaspora.
