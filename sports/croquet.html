Croquet

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Croquet is a lawn game in which players hit wooden balls with mallets through a course of nine metal hoops (wickets) set in a lawn and score points for each hoop and for hitting the central peg. The main competitive forms are Association Croquet (the international standard, with complex rules and handicaps) and Golf Croquet (a simpler doubles format popular in the US and UK). The sport is governed worldwide by the World Croquet Federation (WCF); in the US the Croquet Association sanctions tournaments.

Rules

In Association Croquet, singles or doubles play on a lawn 32 x 26 m (35 x 28 yards). Players take turns striking their ball through the hoops in a set order, scoring one point per hoop per ball; after running a hoop, a player earns extra strokes by hitting another ball (a croquet shot), which allows them to place both balls for a continuation shot. The game is played to 26 points (each hoop run by each ball in both directions, plus the peg). Golf Croquet is played by two pairs on the same lawn but with a simpler scoring system: hoops are worth one point and the peg two, played in any order, first to 14 points wins. Time limits apply in tournament play.

History

Croquet was invented in Ireland in the 1850s, adapted from the French jeu de mail, and became a Victorian lawn craze in England, with the first rules published in 1856. The All England Croquet Club (now the Croquet Association) was founded in 1882, and the World Croquet Federation was founded in 2020 from the merger of international croquet bodies, taking over governance of Association Croquet and other forms. The World Croquet Federation now runs world championships, and golf croquet spread widely in the US from the 1990s.
