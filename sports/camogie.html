Camogie

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Camogie (Irish: camógaíocht) is the women's version of the Irish stick-and-ball game hurling, played with an ash stick (camán) and a small leather ball (sliotar) on a large grass pitch. Two teams of 15 players try to score by hitting the sliotar over the opponents' crossbar (one point) or into the net under the crossbar (a goal, three points). The sport is governed worldwide by the Camogie Association (camogie.ie), which operates within the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), and is played in Ireland and by Irish communities around the world. The premier competition is the All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship.

Rules

The official rules are published by the Camogie Association as the Rules of Camogie and closely follow the rules of hurling. A senior inter-county match lasts 60 minutes (two halves of 30 minutes) on a pitch 130-145 m long and 80-90 m wide, with goals at each end; a goal is worth three points and a point over the crossbar one point. A player may catch the sliotar in one hand, carry it for up to four steps, and pass by striking it with the camán or slapping it with the open hand (hand pass); to carry it further, the player must bounce or balance it on the stick (solo), and the ball may be handled only twice in one possession. Shoulder-to-shoulder contact with an opponent who is playing the ball is allowed, and a helmet with a faceguard is mandatory in matches. Younger age groups and small-sided formats use adapted rules.

History

Camogie developed alongside hurling in Ireland, whose earliest written references date to 7th-century Irish law tracts. The Camogie Association was founded in 1904 and drew up the first standardized rules of the women's game. The first All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship was played in 1932 and won by Dublin. The sport spread with Irish emigration, and clubs now exist across Europe, North America, Australia and beyond. Cork and Tipperary are among the most successful counties in the championship's history.
