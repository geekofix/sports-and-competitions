Wrestling

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wrestling is one of the oldest Olympic sports, contested at the ancient Games from 708 BC and at every modern Olympics since Athens 1896, and today it is governed worldwide by United World Wrestling (UWW), founded in 1912 and renamed from FILA in 2014 (Olympedia, n.d.). Olympic wrestling comprises two styles — Greco-Roman, in which holds below the waist are illegal, and freestyle, which allows the whole body — plus women's freestyle, introduced at Athens 2004 with four weight classes and expanded to six at Rio 2016; Paris 2024 awarded medals in 18 events, six per style (United World Wrestling, n.d.). At the end of this section, the current leading athletes, women first: the Paris 2024 women's freestyle champions — Yui Susaki (Japan, 50 kg), Akari Fujinami (Japan, 53 kg), Tsugumi Sakurai (Japan, 57 kg), Sakura Motoki (Japan, 62 kg), Amit Elor (United States, 68 kg) and Zaineb Sghaier (Tunisia, 76 kg) — alongside the men's 57 kg champion Rei Higuchi (Japan) (Olympedia, n.d.).

Rules

A bout lasts two periods of three minutes with a 30-second break, and is won instantly by a fall — both of the opponent's shoulder blades held on the mat — or by technical superiority, an eight-point lead in Greco-Roman and a ten-point lead in freestyle (United World Wrestling, n.d.). Scoring rewards takedowns (two to four points depending on amplitude), exposure of the back to the mat, and penalties for passivity, leaving the mat or illegal holds; in Greco-Roman, attacking below the waist and leg holds are forbidden, while freestyle permits holds on any part of the body (United World Wrestling, n.d.). Competitors wrestle on a 12 × 12 m mat with a 9 m diameter circle, are weighed in the evening before competition, and at Paris 2024 contested six weight classes per style — Greco-Roman 60, 67, 77, 87, 97 and 130 kg; men's freestyle 57, 65, 74, 86, 97 and 125 kg; women's freestyle 50, 53, 57, 62, 68 and 76 kg (Olympedia, n.d.). Women compete in freestyle only at the Olympics, while UWW world championships also run women's Greco-Roman-style events in some age groups, and junior categories (U15, U17, U20, U23) apply the same rules with modified scoring (United World Wrestling, n.d.).

History

Wrestling entered the ancient Olympic programme in 708 BC as part of the pentathlon, and the modern Olympic tournament began at Athens 1896 with Greco-Roman only — the sport was absent only in 1900 — with freestyle added at St. Louis 1904 (Olympedia, n.d.). In February 2013 the IOC voted to drop wrestling from the 2020 programme, but the decision was reversed on 8 September 2013 after UWW reformed its governance and added women's events, securing wrestling's place at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the sport's icons are Aleksandr Karelin of Russia, who won Greco-Roman 130 kg gold in 1988, 1992 and 1996 plus silver in 2000 and was named FILA's Best Wrestler of the Century in 2002; Kaori Icho of Japan, the first woman to win four consecutive Olympic wrestling titles (2004–2016); Saori Yoshida, three-time champion (2004–2012); and Mijaín López of Cuba, with four Greco-Roman super-heavyweight golds from 2008 to 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
