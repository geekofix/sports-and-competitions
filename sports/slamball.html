Slamball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

SlamBall is a full-contact hybrid team sport that fuses basketball with elements of American football, ice hockey and gymnastics: four players per side compete on a sprung court with four trampolines set into the floor at each end, surrounded by Plexiglass boards like a hockey rink, and score by putting the ball through the opposition's net after spectacular aerial launches. It was invented in 1999 by American Mason Gordon, debuted on television in 2002, returned to broadcast in 2008 and 2023, and is today a league sport with games aired on ESPN and ESPN+.

Rules

Games are played 4-on-4 on a court roughly the size of (slightly larger than) a regulation basketball court — about 96 by 64 feet — with a sprung floor and four trampolines inside the arc at each end and Plexiglass boards instead of out-of-bounds lines, so play never stops for the ball leaving the court. Substitutions are on the fly as in hockey, and bodily contact is legal within limits: checking is part of the game. Scoring rewards the spectacular — dunks have always been worth three points, and in the modern rules long shots from outside the trampoline area are also worth three, with shots from behind the arc (set at 26.5 feet) worth four. Two significant departures from basketball are that defenders may legally goaltend shots taken from the trampolines, and fouls are settled not by free throws but by one-on-one penalty plays.

History

Mason Gordon invented SlamBall in 1999 while working for the production company Tollin/Robbins, sketching a real-life video-game sport and enlisting producer Mike Tollin to finance a prototype court in an East Los Angeles warehouse; the first players — James Willis, Sean Jackson, David Redmond, Michael Goldman and Jeff Sheridan — tested the game, and two teams, the Los Angeles Rumble and the Chicago Mob, played an exhibition series in 2001. With the backing of former Philadelphia 76ers owner Pat Croce, SlamBall made its television debut in 2002 on The National Network with six teams (the Bouncers, Diablos, Mob, Rumble, Slashers and Steal), and the Los Angeles Rumble won the inaugural championship; after a second season on Spike TV in 2003, a disagreement between Gordon and the producers dissolved the league. The sport returned with a third season in 2008 — airing on Versus and CBS, with one championship weekend on Cartoon Network — and gained an international foothold with its first major tournament in China in 2012; a social-media #BringBackSlamball campaign culminated in Gordon announcing the sport's return in August 2022, and the relaunched SlamBall league played the 2023 season in Las Vegas with television coverage on ESPN and streaming on ESPN+.
