20km Race Walk

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 20 kilometres race walk is the classic road race of athletics: 20 km on a measured loop, walked under the strict judging of race walking rules rather than run. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and has been an Olympic event for men since 1956 and for women since 2000 (the women's 10 km walk was staged from 1992) (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The world records are 1:16:10 for men, set by Toshikazu Yamanishi of Japan in Kobe on 16 February 2025, and 1:23:49 for women, set by Yang Jiayu of China in Huangshan on 20 March 2021 (World Athletics, n.d.-f).

At the end of the 2025 season — the last to award 20 km world titles, with Spain's María Pérez (pictured) completing a historic 'double double' of the 35 km and 20 km in Tokyo and Brazil's Caio Bonfim winning the men's 20 km — the 2026 season moved to half marathon and marathon distances: Italy's Francesco Fortunato won the inaugural men's half marathon race walk in Brasília, and the marathon titles went to Hayato Katsuki's Japan (men) and Paula Torres of Ecuador (women, 3:24:47) (Xinhua, 2025; World Athletics, n.d.-e). Yamanishi (pictured) remains the men's record holder (World Athletics, n.d.-f).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the race-walking family — 35km Race Walk, 50km Race Walk and Half Marathon — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

Race walking is defined by two visible infringements: loss of contact with the ground — the advancing leg must remain straightened from the moment of first contact with the ground until it passes under the body — and any visible loss of contact, each shown with a yellow warning paddle, a red paddle for loss of contact and a red card for a bent knee; three red cards from different judges disqualify the athlete, and the chief judge may award a fourth red card (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Road races are held on closed loops with refreshment stations; at World Athletics and Olympic competitions a panel of race walking judges including the chief judge officiates (World Athletics, n.d.-d). Since 1 January 2026 the 20 km and 35 km have been replaced by the half marathon (21.0975 km) and the marathon (42.195 km) race walk at World Athletics championships — first staged at the 2026 World Race Walking Team Championships in Brasília on 12 April 2026 — and Los Angeles 2028 will stage the half marathon as the only individual walk plus the marathon mixed relay (Xinhua, 2025; World Athletics, n.d.-d).

All competitors in a road race wear bib numbers and are judged by a panel of at least six race walking judges including a chief judge at World Athletics and Olympic events; lapping is allowed, and athletes receive food and water at designated stations (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Men and women race the same 20 km distance under identical rules; junior (U20) competitions use 10 km, and para athletics offers race walks in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

History

Modern race walking emerged from 19th-century pedestrianism — long-distance competitive walking — in Britain, where Captain Robert Barclay famously walked 1,000 miles in 1,000 hours in 1809, and the first championship races were held in the 1860s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Race walking entered the Olympic programme at London 1908 with the 3,500 m and 10-mile walks, the 10 km followed in 1912, the men's 20 km in 1956, the women's 10 km in 1992 and the women's 20 km in 2000 (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Japan's Yusuke Suzuki set the men's world record of 1:16:36 in 2015 before Yamanishi's 1:16:10 in 2025; China's Liu Hong and Yang Jiayu dominate the women's records, and the 2013 World Championships awarding ceremony in Moscow, pictured, honored Liu Hong as 20 km world champion (World Athletics, n.d.-f).
