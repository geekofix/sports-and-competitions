Ball Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ball hockey — also called street hockey or dek hockey in North America — is a team sport played on foot (or on skates in some variants) with a ball and curved hockey sticks, closely derived from ice hockey but without ice, skates or protective padding beyond gloves and helmets in organized play. It is governed internationally by the International Street and Ball Hockey Federation (ISBHF), founded in 1993, which runs the Ball Hockey World Championship — the 16th men's and women's edition was held in Ostrava, Czech Republic, from June 20–28, 2026, at the ČEZ Aréna and Vítkovice arenas, where Czechia contested the men's final at home (finishing with Finland taking bronze and Slovakia sixth) and the USA defeated Czechia in the women's final (International Street and Ball Hockey Federation [ISBHF], n.d.-a). The sport is recognized by the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) as an affiliated discipline (International Ice Hockey Federation [IIHF], n.d.).

Rules at the top level are close to ice hockey: four skaters-equivalent field players plus a goaltender, two 20-minute halves, offside and icing rules, and no body checking — deliberate contact is penalized. The ball is a hard plastic or orange ball, and play is continuous with rolling substitutions (ISBHF, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the ISBHF rules, a team fields four court players and one goaltender; games at the World Championship are two 20-minute halves (running clock), with teams of up to 20 players. The rink is typically 40–60 m long and 20–30 m wide, surrounded by boards about 1 m high, with two goals. Substitutions are rolling, and offside and icing are enforced as in ice hockey (ISBHF, n.d.-b).

No checking is allowed — any deliberate body contact results in a penalty (minor 2 minutes, major 5 minutes, match misconduct for dangerous play). High sticking (raising the stick above the waist) is a penalty, as is slashing, tripping, hooking and fighting. Face-offs restart play after goals and fouls. Overtime at championship tournaments is sudden-death golden goal; penalty shots decide tied knockout games (ISBHF, n.d.-b).

Equipment: players use hockey sticks, a helmet with face cage (mandatory for goalkeepers; recommended for all), gloves, and shin guards are permitted but full ice-hockey padding is not used in the court version; the goaltender wears leg pads, blocker and mask. The ball is an orange plastic ball of about 8 cm diameter. Men's and women's tournaments use identical rules — the ISBHF runs parallel men's and women's world championships since 1996, with the women's event growing steadily; age classes include U16, U18 and U20 world championships (ISBHF, n.d.-a).

History

Ball hockey emerged in the 1930s–1960s in Canada, the United States and Northern Europe as a fair-weather alternative to ice hockey, played in streets, gymnasiums and on roller rinks; the "hokejbal" tradition in Czechoslovakia dates to the 1920s–1930s. The ISBHF was founded in 1993 in Prague, and the first Ball Hockey World Championship was held in 1996 in Bratislava, Slovakia — won by Sweden in the men's event (ISBHF, n.d.-a).

Since then the world championship has been held every two years, alternating with the ISBHF World Cup in off years; hosts have included Finland, Czechia, Switzerland, Canada and the USA. The 2026 edition in Ostrava was the 16th men's and women's championship. On the women's side, the USA and Czechia have been the dominant nations, with the USA winning the 2026 women's title in Ostrava (ISBHF, n.d.-a). The IIHF recognized ball hockey as an affiliated discipline, and the sport is part of multi-sport events such as the World Urban Games demonstrations.
