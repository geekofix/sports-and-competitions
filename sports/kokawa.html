Kokawa

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kokawa (KoKowa) is the Hausa form of West African lutte traditionnelle wrestled in Nigeria and Niger: two bare-chested fighters in a circular sand ring try to throw each other or force any part but the feet to the ground — without blows (the striking element was split off into dambe) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). International kokawa/lutte traditionnelle competition takes place at the Jeux de la Francophonie and the Championship of African Lutte Traditionnelle, coordinated since 2000; the first ECOWAS championship was held in Dakar in 2008 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Within the combat sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Laamb, Dambe, Evala, Wrestling, Belt Wrestling, Oil Wrestling; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

A bout ends when a wrestler falls, touches the ground with hand, knee or back, or leaves the ring; there are no weight classes and bouts are short, with rounds in tournament formats. The sand ring is formally bounded by sandbags at major events; grips, trips and throws are allowed, strikes are not (sans frappe). Men's and women's categories exist at African championships (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Hausa wrestling traditions are centuries old and were part of warrior training and harvest festivals; kokawa was regularised to the West African standard from the 1950s as lutte traditionnelle organised internationally, with the Francophonie Games as its showcase (Wikipedia, n.d.).
