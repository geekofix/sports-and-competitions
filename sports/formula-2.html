Formula 2

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

FIA Formula 2 is the main feeder series to Formula 1, contested with identical Dallara chassis and Mecachrome V6 turbo engines, so all cars are equal and driver skill decides. It is sanctioned by the FIA and raced at Grand Prix weekends on F1 circuits; the champion typically earns a Super License and moves up to F1.

Rules

Under the FIA F2 Sporting Regulations, each of the teams fields two identical Dallara chassis cars powered by the standardized 3.4 L turbocharged Mecachrome V6 engine, with a common hybrid system introduced in 2023. Race weekends include one practice, one qualifying session, a sprint race on Saturday and a feature race on Sunday. The feature race is around 120-170 km and includes one mandatory pit stop; the shorter sprint race has no mandatory stop. Points follow the F1 scale (25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-3-1), with additional points for pole position in the feature race and for the fastest lap. Sprint races have used reverse grids based on qualifying in some seasons.

History

The FIA Formula Two Championship ran from 1967 to 1984 (European F2 with varied engines), won by drivers such as Jacky Ickx, Jean-Pierre Jarier and Patrick Depailler. It was revived in 2009 as GP2 (with Dallara GP2/08 chassis and Renault engines), renamed FIA Formula 2 in 2017. Champions include Nico Rosberg (2005 GP2), Lewis Hamilton (2006), Nico Hülkenberg (2009), Romain Grosjean (2011), Stoffel Vandoorne (2015), Charles Leclerc (2017), George Russell (2018), Mick Schumacher (2020), Oscar Piastri (2021), Felipe Drugovich (2022), Théo Pourchaire (2023) and Gabriel Bortoleto (2024) — most moved straight into F1 seats. The 2024 car is the Dallara F2 2024 with a new turbo-hybrid Mecachrome engine introduced in 2023.
