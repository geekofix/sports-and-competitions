Cliff Diving

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cliff diving is competitive diving from high platforms — 27 m for men and 20 m for women at the elite level — into open water from purpose-built platforms or natural cliffs, judged on execution and multiplied by the degree of difficulty of each dive; at 27 m divers enter the water at roughly 85–96 km/h (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Aquatics, n.d.-c). The premier international series is the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series, running since 2006 with the King Kahekili Trophy for the overall champions, while World Aquatics has included high diving at the World Aquatics Championships since 2013 and runs the High Diving World Cup since 2014 (Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; World Aquatics, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

The 2026 Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series champions were decided in the autumn of 2026: Rhiannan Iffland of Australia (pictured) won her record tenth consecutive women's title — 2016–2019 and 2021–2026, secured at the Mostar stop — and Aidan Heslop of Great Britain took the men's King Kahekili Trophy in a comeback season, his second overall title, with the season finale at Polignano a Mare in September 2026 won by Ukraine's Oleksiy Prygorov (Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). At the 2026 World Aquatics Championships in Singapore the high diving titles were decided from the 27 m and 20 m platforms with scuba rescue teams on site at every session (World Aquatics, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

At World Aquatics Championships men dive from a 27 m platform and women from 20 m; at other international events the platforms range from 22–27 m for men and 18–23 m for women, and the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series uses 27 m (men) and 20 m (women), from platforms or natural cliffs (World Aquatics, n.d.-c; Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Each competitor performs a set number of dives — typically three or four per event as fixed in the event regulations — and five judges award execution points from 0 to 10 in half-point steps, which are multiplied by the dive's degree of difficulty (up to about 5.0); the highest total wins, and in the Red Bull series the winner of each stop receives 200 points toward the overall King Kahekili Trophy (World Aquatics, n.d.-b; Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.).

Safety rules are strict: the water below the platform must be several metres deep (about 5–6 m) with a certified scuba rescue team on site, and competitors must meet the minimum age and medical requirements of the World Aquatics High Diving Rules; divers wear helmets at some venues and must enter feet-first or head-first according to the dive (World Aquatics, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate events under identical judging and safety rules on their own platform heights; junior and age-group events use lower platforms (World Aquatics, n.d.-c).

History

Cliff diving has roots in the Hawaiian tradition of lele kawa — King Kahekili II of Maui is said to have dived from a high cliff around 1770 — and in the famous La Quebrada cliff divers of Acapulco, Mexico, who have leapt from 27–35 m cliffs into a narrow sea inlet since the 1930s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series was launched in 2006 and became the premier 27 m/20 m series; when FINA (now World Aquatics) added high diving to the World Championships in Barcelona 2013, Colombia's Orlando Duque — pictured — became the first men's world champion from 27 m and the USA's Cesilie Carlton the first women's champion from 20 m (Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Britain's Gary Hunt won a record eleven men's series titles between 2009 and 2022, establishing the King Kahekili Trophy era (Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.).
