25m Pistol Shooting

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 25 m pistol (sport pistol) is the ISSF pistol event shot at 25 m with a .22 Long Rifle pistol over 60 shots in two stages — a precision stage and a rapid-fire stage — against turning 25 m targets; it is governed by the International Shooting Sport Federation under its pistol technical rules and is contested by men and women at World Cups and World Championships (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the Olympic Games the women's 25 m pistol is the medal event — at Paris 2024 Yang Ji-in of South Korea won gold ahead of Camille Jedrzejewski of France and Veronika Major of Hungary — while men contest the 25 m rapid fire pistol instead (see the link below) (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At the 55th ISSF World Shooting Championships in Doha, 1–14 November 2026, Yang Ji-in broke the world record in the women's 25 m pistol final, and India's Esha Singh, Manu Bhaker and Divya Thadigol Subbaraju won the women's team gold (ISSF, n.d.-d; Olympics.com, 2026, November; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Among the athletes defining the current era, the women lead: Yang Ji-in — the Paris 2024 and 2026 world champion — together with Veronika Major of Hungary (pictured), the 2024 Olympic bronze medallist, and India's Singh and Bhaker (Wikipedia, n.d.-b, n.d.-d).

Rules

Under the ISSF pistol rules, the 25 m pistol match is 60 shots: a precision stage of six series of five shots (150 seconds per series) followed by a rapid-fire stage of six series of five shots in four seconds per series, fired at five turning targets on the 25 m range; scoring is whole-ring (1–10 plus inner ten) on the electronic target, maximum 600 (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The top eight from qualification advance to the final, where additional scoring shots and shoot-offs decide the medals under the current rulebook (ISSF, n.d.-c; ISSF, 2026).

The pistol must be chambered in .22 LR, weigh no more than the regulated dimensions, have a minimum trigger pull of 1,360 g for the 25 m pistol event and conventional open sights; the shooter may load one cartridge at a time and must shoot standing, unsupported (ISSF, n.d.-c). Men and women compete in separate classifications at World Championships and the Olympics; the women's Olympic event is the 25 m pistol, while the men's Olympic speed event is the 25 m rapid fire pistol (link below), and junior (under 21) categories exist (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Pistol shooting dates to 19th-century parlour and military matches; the 50 m pistol entered the first World Championship programme in 1900 and rapid fire pistol in 1933, while the 25 m sport pistol became a staple of the ISSF programme in the 20th century and the women's event joined the Olympic programme in the late 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Ukraine's Olena Kostevych, pictured, is one of the icons of the discipline — Olympic gold in 10 m air pistol at Athens 2004, silver in the 25 m pistol at London 2012 and gold in the mixed 10 m air pistol team at Tokyo 2020; on the men's side, Germany's Ralf Schumann set the standard in the neighbouring rapid fire event with three Olympic golds (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
