Kokh

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kokh (Armenian: Exported wrestling) is the Armenian national wrestling style, known since the Early Middle Ages and one of the oldest wrestling forms: two wrestlers on a 7–9 m mat try to throw the opponent onto the mat without a boost or turn, or push him out of the circle; bouts last 5–10 minutes. Two styles exist — Lori Kokh (wrestlers wear the chokha caftan and grip the clothing) and Shirak Kokh (shalvar trousers, topless, leg grabs allowed). World Kokh championships are held; Armenia fields strong freestyle and Greco-Roman teams alongside the traditional style. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Wrestling, Guresh, Kuresh, Kurash; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

The winner throws the opponent to the mat cleanly (both shoulders) without boosting/turning him, or pushes him out of the 7–9 m circle; a bout lasts 5–10 minutes with a referee and side judges. Lori Kokh: chokha jacket grips only. Shirak Kokh: shalvar trousers, topless, leg grips allowed. No ground wrestling after the fall. Fouls: passivity, leaving the mat, unsportsmanlike conduct. Men's and women's world championships are organized by the Armenian Kokh federation (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Wrestling was practiced in the Armenian Highlands since antiquity; King Tiridates III of Armenia is recorded as winning the wrestling event at the ancient Olympic Games in 281 AD. Kokh survived through the Soviet era (a 1963 USSR stamp depicts it; a 2012 dram coin shows kokh). Today about 126 Armenian athletes practice kokh alongside 4,000 freestyle and 3,800 Greco-Roman wrestlers (2021 government statistics); world championships are held in Yerevan (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
