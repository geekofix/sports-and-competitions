Silhouette Shooting

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Metallic silhouette shooting is a handgun and rifle discipline in which competitors knock down steel targets shaped like chickens, pigs, turkeys and rams, placed at increasing distances up to 200 m for handguns and 500 m for big-bore rifles (IHMSA, n.d.). It belongs to the target-shooting family and is run internationally under the International Handgun Metallic Silhouette Association (IHMSA), founded in 1976 (IHMSA, n.d.).

Figure: Flicr user davidwhitewolf. (2007-04-27 20:16:43). Metallic silhouette pistols at Boomershoot, Washington State [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Metallic_Silhouette_Pistols_at_Boomershoot_%281%29.jpg

A standard course has 40 handgun targets — five each of chickens (50 m), pigs (75 m), turkeys (100 m) and rams (150 m) for smallbore, with big-bore rams out to 200 m — and rifles shoot a separate course with rams out to 500 m (IHMSA, n.d.). Each target must fall to count; a target that only wobbles scores nothing, and the match score is simply the number of knocked-down silhouettes out of 40 (or 60/80 in combined matches) (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Handgun matches are divided by calibre and position — revolver, semi-auto, smallbore (.22), production and unlimited — and shooting is standing, one-handed, in a defined shooting box (IHMSA, n.d.). Rifle silhouette allows any calibre above .22 with scoped rifles, fired from standing or supported positions depending on the class (Wikipedia, n.d.). The annual IHMSA International Championship draws shooters from many countries, and the famous Boomershoot event in Washington State is the largest silhouette match in the world (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete together; the IHMSA explicitly welcomes shooters of all ages and physical abilities, with a dedicated PC (physically challenged) committee (IHMSA, n.d.).

History

Silhouette shooting began in Mexico in the 1940s–50s, where shooters informally shot at metal animal shapes; the IHMSA was founded in 1976 in Tucson, Arizona, to standardize the steel targets and course (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport spread rapidly to the United States, Australia, South Africa and Europe, and the first world championships were held in the early 1980s (IHMSA, n.d.). The steel targets themselves are standardized: ram 40% scale of a real ram silhouette, turkey, pig and chicken at descending sizes (Wikipedia, n.d.).
