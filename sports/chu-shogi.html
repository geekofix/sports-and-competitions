Chu Shogi

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chu shogi (middle shogi) is the 12x12 medieval Japanese chess variant from the Heian period with 46 piece types, including the powerful dragon king and dragon horse; it is the direct ancestor of modern shogi. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Shogi, Dai Shogi, Tenjiku Shogi; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Figure: Kyoku at Japanese Wikipedia. (2004-10-15). A chu shogi board photo in a Kyoto shop street [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chushogi_photo.jpg

Rules

12x12 board; 46 piece types with promotions; drops allowed as in shogi; checkmate wins. Played at the imperial court from the 10th–12th centuries (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Chu shogi flourished in the Heian period (10th–12th c.) at the Japanese court; it was superseded by dai shogi and then modern shogi in the 16th century. Modern enthusiasts play reconstructions; historic boards are preserved (e.g. the Shutter shogi board photo in Kyoto) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
