Aggressive Inline Skating

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Aggressive inline skating is the discipline of performing technical tricks on inline skates — grinding rails and ledges, jumping stairs and gaps, and combining maneuvers in skate parks and on street courses. It is governed internationally by World Skate as the "roller freestyle" discipline, with park and street disciplines at its World Championships (World Skate, n.d.-a). See also Roller Freestyle, Inline Speed Skating and Roller Figure Skating on this site.

Among the athletes shaping the sport today are the competitors of the 2025 Roller Freestyle World Championships in Sakai, Japan (November 22–27, 2025), which drew 70 athletes from 21 countries across park and street, senior and junior, with Brazil and France dominating the podiums (World Skate, n.d.-b). The 2026 World Skate Games in Asunción, Paraguay, host the next world-level events (World Skate, n.d.-c).

Women compete on equal terms with men, including a dedicated women's division at the world championships.

Within the skate and roller sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Roller Freestyle, Inline Speed Skating, Roller Figure Skating, Skateboarding, Street Skateboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In park and street contests, skaters perform runs or best-trick rounds on a course of rails, ledges, stairs, ramps and bowls, and are scored on difficulty, execution, variety and use of the course. Protective gear — helmet, and often knee, elbow and wrist pads — is mandatory at most contests. The skates are hard-boot aggressive skates with a soul plate and H-block for grinding, typically set up with four wheels in a flat or anti-rocker configuration for stability on tricks (World Skate, n.d.-a). There are separate men's and women's divisions at championships, judged under the same scoring system, plus junior categories (World Skate, n.d.-b).

History

Aggressive inline skating began when Rollerblade, Inc. was founded in 1980 by Minnesota hockey players Scott and Brennan Olson, who adapted inline skates for off-season training; in 1988 the Lightning TRS became the first skate designed specifically for aggressive skating. The first contest series, NISS (National Inline Skating Series), was launched in 1994 by Rick Stark and Mark Billik with ESPN coverage, the Aggressive Skaters Association (ASA) was founded in 1995, and the X Games included aggressive inline from 1995 until 2005. The World Rolling Series (WRS) was founded in 2009 by Brian Shima, Jon Julio and Kato Mateu, and Winterclash — the largest indoor street-style contest — has been held in Eindhoven since 2005, organized by Johannes Jacobi. Today the discipline lives on under World Skate as roller freestyle (World Skate, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.).
