La Soule

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

La Soule (later choule) is the medieval mass ball game of Normandy, Picardy and Brittany: two parish teams try to carry or throw a ball — solid wood or hollow leather stuffed with hay, bran, horsehair or moss — to the opponent's goal, often a church door or a boundary marker; teams could number in the hundreds. Games were played at Shrovetide and other festivals, were notoriously violent, and were restricted by royal decrees (e.g. a 1412 decree limiting ball size). The game influenced the development of football and rugby and has been revived with codified rules in Normandy since 2001 (Coupe de Normandie since 2005). Within the football (soccer) family and its variants on this site, its closest relatives are Cnapan, Football, Rugby Union, Calcio Fiorentino; the related pages are listed under Important Links (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).

Rules

Historical rules were local and fluid — some forms resembled golf, others hockey-like two-base games. The revived Norman choule (Jeux de Normandie; Coupe de Normandie since 2005) uses codified rules: two teams, a heavy leather ball (soule), goals at each end of a pitch, carrying/kicking/throwing allowed, no deliberate dangerous play; matches at Conches-en-Ouche and across the Cotentin, Caen region and Pays de Caux, Jersey. The 1852 painting shows a Basse-Normandie match with a huge crowd (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).

History

The earliest French reference to a ball game (presumably la soule) dates to 1147; Rabelais described "la soule" in Gargantua (1534). Games nearly disappeared by the 19th century and faded by 1945, with church doors (e.g. St Mary's, Haverfordwest in Wales for cnapan) damaged as goals. Revival began in Picardy and Normandy; "la crosse" has been played in Normandy since 2001 with cups since 2005 (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).
