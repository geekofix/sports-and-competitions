Slowpitch Softball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Slowpitch softball is the recreational and competitive version of softball in which the pitcher lobs the ball in a high arc — typically 6–10 feet — toward home plate, giving fielders time to react and making home runs rare; it is governed internationally by the World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC), which publishes the Official Rules of Softball including the slow pitch provisions and organises the WBSC Slow Pitch World Cups (WBSC, n.d.-a). In the United States, the amateur game is run by USA Softball and the United States Specialty Sports Association (USSSA), founded in 1968, each publishing its own 2026 rulebook (USSSA, n.d.). The standard slow pitch ball is 14 inches in diameter in adult leagues, with 12-inch balls common in youth play and 16-inch balls in some men's divisions; bases are 60 feet apart, the pitching distance is 46 feet for women and 50 feet for men in many rule sets, and outfield fences are typically 200–275 feet (WBSC, n.d.-a; USSSA, n.d.). Slow pitch is famously co-ed: mixed divisions require a minimum number of women on the field, and men's, women's and co-ed leagues all exist. Among the current leading women is the American-Japanese pitcher Samantha Show of the Ogaki Minamo team in Japan's league, one of the most dominant pitchers in the professional game (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the WBSC Official Rules of Softball (2026–2029 edition, slow pitch section) and the USA Softball and USSSA rulebooks, the pitcher must deliver the ball on an arc of 6–10 feet (6–12 feet under USSSA), with no wrist snap; a pitch that fails the arc is a ball, and too many illegal pitches can eject the pitcher (WBSC, n.d.-a; USA Softball, n.d.). Bases are 60 feet apart, the pitching rubber is 46–50 feet from home plate depending on the division, and the ball is 14 inches (12-inch in youth, 16-inch in some men's divisions) (WBSC, n.d.-a). Runners may not lead off — in most rule sets they must wait until the pitched ball reaches the plate before leaving the base — and stealing is restricted or forbidden depending on the organisation (USSSA, n.d.). Games are typically seven innings or a 55–60 minute time limit, with a short-field rule and a fielder minimum of ten players per team in co-ed divisions, at least three of whom must be women on the field at all times (USA Softball, n.d.). Men's, women's, co-ed and mixed divisions exist at every level from youth (under-8 upward) through senior amateur and masters, and the WBSC Slow Pitch World Cups crown men's and women's world champions (WBSC, n.d.-b).

History

Slowpitch softball grew out of "kitten ball" and recreational softball played in the Pacific Northwest of the United States in the 1950s, when the lobbed pitch was adopted to let older and less athletic players enjoy the game safely (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Amateur Softball Association, founded in 1933 and today USA Softball, codified the sport's rules, and the USSSA was founded in 1968 to run slow pitch tournaments, becoming the largest amateur organisation in the country (USSSA, n.d.). Slow pitch spread through company leagues, church leagues and municipal parks and recreation programmes across North America from the 1960s onward, and internationally the WBSC took over governance of softball — including slow pitch world cups — after its 2013 formation from the International Softball Federation (WBSC, n.d.-b). The 2026–2029 WBSC rule cycle introduced updated slow pitch regulations released in March 2026 (WBSC, n.d.-a).
