Slowpitch Softball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Slowpitch softball is the recreational-and-competitive form of softball in which the pitcher delivers the ball underhand in a high, controlled arc rather than with the fast windmill of the fastpitch game. It is the dominant adult amateur format in North America's beer, corporate and community leagues, and is played worldwide under the rules of bodies such as USA Softball and the WBSC, typically with ten fielders per side and an emphasis on batting, fielding and teamwork over pitching duels.

Rules

The defining slowpitch rule is the arc: under USA Softball rules the pitch must rise between six and ten feet on its way to the plate, the batter is out if he bunts (bunting is not legal in slowpitch), and stealing bases is not permitted — runners may leave the base only when the pitched ball reaches or passes home plate, games being won by pure batting and fielding. Slowpitch usually plays ten players a side — the extra fielder commonly in the outfield — with games of seven innings. Because every pitch arrives slowly and steeply, batting strategy revolves around placement and power in a game in which scores are high; adult league formats include co-ed rules (often with requirements on the male/female batting ratio and dedicated home-run limits that award walks after a team's home-run quota), and balls and bats are specific certified slowpitch models to control exit speed for safety.

History

Softball itself began in 1887 at Chicago's Farragut Boat Club, where Harvard and Yale alumni waiting for football results turned a wrapped boxing glove and a broom handle into the first game, with reporter George Hancock codifying indoor baseball; from a 16-inch "mush ball" Chicago standard (still played there, famously bare-handed) the game developed through national championships from the 1933 World's Fair. Slowpitch was long the playground-and-recreation face of the sport — fastpitch dominated organised competition — but after the Second World War adult recreational slowpitch leagues exploded in the United States: 16- and 12-inch games became the backbone of company, church and tavern leagues, spilling into mixed and family formats, and by the 1960s\u201380s slowpitch was the world's most-played team sport by weekly participants. Under organisations such as the Amateur Softball Association (ASA, now USA Softball), slowpitch secured national championships, co-ed and senior divisions and world tournaments, and today it thrives everywhere from North American summer corporate leagues to European club circuits — the standard game of millions of recreational players.
