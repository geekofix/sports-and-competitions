Kite Buggying

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kite buggying is land sailing with a traction kite: the pilot sits in a light, single-seat, three-wheeled buggy — one steerable front wheel and two fixed rear wheels — and is propelled across beaches, dry lake beds or grass fields by a large controllable power kite flown on long lines. Kite buggies are classified as "Class 8 land yachts" by FISLY, the Fédération Internationale de Sand et Land Yachting, founded in 1962, which sets the international racing rules and has organized world championships since 1975. Skilled pilots reach speeds around 110 km/h.

Rules

FISLY racing rules for Class 8 buggies govern construction (dimensions, weight, brakes — a buggy must be stoppable by the kite alone, using a wind brake, no mechanical brakes in competition), safety equipment (helmet, harness) and racing on a marked course on sand, dry lake or grass. Races are typically time-trial or mass-start circuit races around buoys, with right-of-way rules adapted from sailing; the fastest time around the course wins. Record attempts and speed events are also held, with speed measured over a timed section. Pilots must be licensed through their national association and meet minimum age requirements.

History

The kite buggy was first promulgated by George Pocock in the United Kingdom in 1827, and commercially available buggies appeared in the US and UK in the late 1970s. The modern sport is generally credited to New Zealander Peter Lynn, whose strong, lightweight and affordable buggy designs in the early 1990s made kite buggying accessible worldwide. FISLY was founded in April 1962 by representatives of Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, France and Great Britain and has organized land yacht world championships since 1975, including Class 8 kite buggy events on venues such as Ivanpah Dry Lake in Nevada and beaches in France, Germany and New Zealand.
