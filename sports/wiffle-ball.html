Wiffle Ball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wiffle ball is a scaled-down version of baseball played with a perforated, hollow, lightweight plastic ball and a long hollow plastic bat; one half of the ball carries eight oblong holes, which let pitchers throw curveballs and risers (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Teams of one to five players each try to advance runners around the bases and score based on where the batter places the ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ball was invented in the summer of 1953 in Fairfield, Connecticut, by David N. Mullany, who watched his 12-year-old son David A. and friends break windows playing baseball with a perforated plastic golf ball and a broom handle, and designed a ball that curved easily; the name drops the "h" from "whiff", the neighborhood word for a strikeout (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.). The patent (No. 2,776,139) was granted in 1957, the first balls sold for 49 cents from the family station wagon, a 1959 order from F.W. Woolworth took the toy national, and production moved to a factory in Shelton, Connecticut, in 1959 (Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.).

Competitive Wiffle ball has grown into real tournaments: David Mullany started an eight-team World Wiffle Ball Championship, and by 2019 some 60 teams from 12 states entered (Baseball Hall of Fame, n.d.). The biggest league is Major League Wiffle Ball (MLW), founded in 2009 in Brighton, Michigan, by the Schultz brothers and neighbors playing on the front lawn ("Cultz Field"); it now fields eight permanent teams at The Meadows in Huron Clinton Metropark, streams on YouTube (around 660,000 subscribers as of October 2026) and has been featured on ESPN and MLB Network (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Western Wildcats are the most successful team with six championships, and the Metro Magic are the most recent champions after defeating the Coastal Cobras in four games; the 2026 MLW World Series was held at Lindsey Nelson Stadium in Knoxville, Tennessee (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Wiffle Ball was inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame in 2017, and more than 100 million balls have been sold (Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.).

Rules

Casual Wiffle ball is played under house rules, but organized play follows official rulebooks: two teams of one to five players, a pitcher throwing the perforated ball (which can be made to curve, rise or sink), a batter, and fielders; runners advance around four bases and score by completing the circuit, with hits valued by where the ball lands — typical local rules assign strike zones, pop-ups and grounders as outs and single/double/triple/home-run zones (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). MLW and the World Wiffle Ball Championship play under their own published rulebooks with fixed innings and tournament brackets (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The light plastic ball and hollow bat make the game safe enough for confined yards, which was the point of the invention (Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.). Games are open to all genders and ages, and mixed teams are common (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

David N. Mullany, a former semi-professional baseball pitcher, created the ball on 14 August 1953 at the kitchen table with plastic half-spheres supplied by a friend at a perfume company; the patent was granted on 1 January 1957 (Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.). Sales began from the family car in 1953–54, moved to Canal Street in Manhattan, and exploded after the 1959 Woolworth contract; the family factory opened in Shelton, Connecticut, in 1959 and remains the company’s home today, now run by the third David Mullany with his brother Stephen (Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.). Tournaments began in the United States and Europe in 1977, the Wiffle Ball entered the National Toy Hall of Fame in 2017, and the Mullanys were honored at the White House Made in America Product Showcase in 2018 (Wiffle Ball, Inc., n.d.). The game has been a fixture of American backyards and barbecues for over 70 years (Baseball Hall of Fame, n.d.).
