Mahjong

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mahjong is a tile-based game for four players that was developed in China in the 19th century. Players draw and discard tiles from a wall of 144 tiles, trying to complete a winning hand of four melds (sets or sequences) and one pair. In competitive play internationally, the standardized ruleset known as MCR (Mahjong Competition Rules, also called Chinese official rules) is used, governed by the World Mahjong Organization (WMO). Popular regional variants include Japanese riichi mahjong and American mahjong with its annual scorecard hands.

Rules

A standard game uses 144 tiles and four players seated at the four winds; each player starts with 13 tiles, draws one tile per turn and discards one, and may claim discards for a chow (sequence, from the previous player only), pung (three of a kind) or kong (four of a kind). A player wins by declaring mahjong when their 14 tiles form four melds and a pair, or special hands such as Seven Pairs. Under MCR, a winning hand must score at least 8 points from a published list of 81 scoring patterns called fan, with point grades from 1 to 88; every non-winning player pays the winner 8 extra points, and on a win off a discard the discarder pays the fan points as well. Competitions at the World Mahjong Championship are played in individual and team events over several sessions.

History

Mahjong emerged in Qing-dynasty China around the mid-19th century and spread around the world from the early 20th century; Joseph Park Babcock, a Standard Oil executive in Shanghai, popularized it in the United States from the 1920s onward. In January 1998 mahjong was officially recognized as a sport by the General Administration of Sport of China, and in September 1998 unified international rules were codified. An unofficial first World Championship was held in Tokyo in 2002; the World Mahjong Organization was founded in Beijing in 2006, and the first official World Mahjong Championship took place in Chengdu in 2007, with the event held roughly every two years since. The Open European Mahjong Championship has been held since 2005.
