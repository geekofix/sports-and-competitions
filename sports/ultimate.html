Ultimate

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ultimate is a non-contact team sport played with a flying disc on a 100 m x 37 m field with end zones, in which seven players per side pass the disc to score by catching it in the opponent's end zone. It is governed by the World Flying Disc Federation (WFDF), founded in 1985 and recognized by the IOC, which runs the World Ultimate Club Championships and the World Championships, and disc sports have been part of the World Games since 2001. Related disc sports include disc golf, played on the disc golf page on this site.

Rules

Rules are discipline-specific across the disc sports. In Ultimate, seven players per side pass the disc to score in the end zone, with no running with the disc and turnovers on incomplete passes; games are to 15 points or timed. In disc golf (see Disc Golf), players throw from tee to basket target. Freestyle is judged acrobatic routines with the disc (catches, rolls, delays) performed to music, scored on difficulty, execution and artistry. Guts is a fast throwing game where the receiver tries to catch a disc thrown at up to 70+ mph. Double disc court (DDC) uses two courts with two players each, aiming to land the disc in the opponent's court or hit their disc. At WFDF World Championships, each discipline has its own rules of play published by the federation.

History

The modern flying disc began in 1948 when Walter Frederick Morrison designed the first plastic "Flying Saucer" (later the Wham-O Frisbee, named after the Frisbie Pie Company whose tins Yale students threw). Ultimate was invented in 1968 by Joel Silver and fellow students at Columbia High School in Maplewood, New Jersey, codifying the "Spirit of the Game" self-officiating principle. The WFDF was founded in 1985 and gained IOC recognition in 2013; disc sports were part of the World Games from 2001 (Akita). Disc golf (see separate entry) grew into its own major sport. Freestyle and guts have been played competitively since the 1960s-70s, with world championships run by the WFDF and the Freestyle Players Association.
