Track Cycling Madison

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Madison (named after the six-day races of Madison Square Garden) is track cycling's two-man relay: pairs race as a team with one rider in the race at a time, exchanging by a handsling or touch of the hip every lap-and-a-half or so, scoring points in intermediate sprints (and for lapping the field). The most tactical of the endurance bunch races, it is an Olympic event for men and women.

Rules

Both partners of each team ride the same race: the resting rider circles high on the track, and the pair exchanges when the racing rider handslings (grasping his partner's hand and slinging him into the race) or draws level for a changeover, with the incoming rider taking up the race position. Points are scored in intermediate sprints (commonly every 10 laps in championship formats, typically 5, 3, 2 and 1 to the first four) and a team gaining a lap on the field ranks ahead on the laps-versus-points system; races run 50 to 60 kilometres or set lap totals, and strict rules govern the exchange itself (a dangerous handsling or a veering changeover draws relegation) and double-stacked track craft, with judges watching every exchange.

History

The Madison descends from the two-man competition format of early twentieth-century six-day racing at Madison Square Garden, where teams of two contested multi-day marathon events before tiredness rules limited individual riding hours and exchanges became the format's signature. The event became a pure track discipline with sprint laps and points and joined the world championship programme, and after decades as a men's world championship event it returned to the Olympic programme (Beijing 2008, later Rio and Tokyo with women) as the marquee endurance race on the velodrome. The modern event is famous for its constant exchanges and controlled chaos, and six-day racing's revival in Europe keeps a winter stage for the discipline's specialist stars.
