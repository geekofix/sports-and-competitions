Ladies Gaelic Football

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ladies' Gaelic football is the women's version of Gaelic football, played 15 v 15 on the same pitches and under essentially the same rules as the men's game, and governed on the island of Ireland by the Ladies Gaelic Football Association (LGFA), founded on 18 July 1974 at Hayes' Hotel in Thurles, County Tipperary, by delegates from Offaly, Kerry, Tipperary and Galway (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first All-Ireland Senior Ladies' Championship was played later in 1974 — won by Tipperary — and the LGFA was formally recognized by the GAA in 1982 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the LGFA runs the All-Ireland Senior, Intermediate, Junior, Under-18, Under-16, Under-14 and Club championships, the Ladies' National Football League and the O'Connor Cup, with the Playing Rules and Official Guide for 2026 published on ladiesgaelic.ie (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-b). Because the sport is women-only by definition, female athletes are the entire field: the 2026 championship season crowns inter-county and club champions across Ireland, and the game is broadcast nationally (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-b).

Rules

Ladies' Gaelic football is played 15 v 15 on a pitch of 130–145 m × 80–90 m with H-shaped goals; a goal scores 3 points and a point over the crossbar 1 point (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-b). A senior inter-county match lasts 60 minutes (2 × 30 minutes), club and under-age games are shorter; the ball may be carried for a limited number of steps and must be soloed or bounced, kicked or hand-passed otherwise, and fouls (holding, pushing, tripping, striking) are punished with free kicks, yellow and red cards (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-b). Goalkeepers play in a distinct jersey and may handle the ball within their own area; the restart rules, penalty kicks and advantage play follow the men's code with the LGFA's 2026 Playing Rules taking precedence (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-b). There are no men's divisions — the sport is exclusively for women — and age grades run from Under-14 through Under-18, Junior and Intermediate to Senior, with club championships parallel to the inter-county structure (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rule source: Ladies Gaelic Football Association, Playing Rules 2026 and Official Guide 2026, https://ladiesgaelic.ie/ (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-b). See also Gaelic football and hurling on this site.

History

Ladies' Gaelic football emerged in the early 1970s as women's teams sought the same facilities and recognition as men's clubs; the Ladies Gaelic Football Association was founded on 18 July 1974 at Hayes' Hotel in Thurles by delegates from Offaly, Kerry, Tipperary and Galway, and the first All-Ireland Senior Ladies' Championship was held the same year, won by Tipperary (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The association grew steadily through the 1970s and was formally recognized by the Gaelic Athletic Association in 1982, which brought funding, insurance and access to county grounds (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Since the 1990s the LGFA has run a full championship structure including the National Football League and under-age grades, and the women's game is now played by tens of thousands of registered players across Ireland and abroad (Ladies Gaelic Football Association, n.d.-a). Internationally, the first Ladies' International Rules series against Australia began in the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
