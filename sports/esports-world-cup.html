Esports World Cup

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Esports World Cup (EWC) is an annual international multi-title esports tournament series run by the Esports Foundation (formerly the Esports World Cup Foundation), a Saudi Arabian nonprofit backed by the Public Investment Fund, and it is regarded as the largest professional esports event in the world by total prize pool and number of game titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Each edition crowns individual champions in dozens of video games and, above all, a cross-game Club Champion: clubs earn points for their placements across every title, and the top 24 clubs share a dedicated Club Championship prize fund (Esports Foundation, n.d.). The 2026 edition was held in Paris, France, from 2 July to 23 August 2026 and featured 25 events across 24 games with a record US$75 million total prize pool — US$39 million across the individual events and US$30 million for the Club Championship, whose winner received US$7 million (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). More than 2,000 players representing 200 clubs from over 100 countries competed, the closing ceremony took place at the Grand Palais with French President Emmanuel Macron and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman in attendance, and broadcasts reached 850 million viewers and 440 million hours watched, with a peak of 4.3 million concurrent viewers during the League of Legends final (PR Newswire, 2026). AG.AL — the unified competition brand of the Chinese organization All Gamers, which includes Anyone’s Legend and international divisions — won the 2026 Club Championship with 5,300 points, ending Team Falcons’ two-year reign as club champion (Team Falcons finished second and Vitality third), while Team Spirit won the Counter-Strike 2 grand final 3–1 against FUT Esports for the US$2 million top prize, with Danil “donk” Kryshkovets named tournament MVP (TalkEsport, 2026). The 2027 edition returns to Riyadh (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The EWC grew out of Saudi Arabia’s gaming festival scene: Gamers Without Borders, a COVID-19 charity series launched in 2020, and Gamers8, the eight-week Boulevard City festival of 2022 and 2023 that hosted the last FIFAe World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the event travels between Riyadh venues — the SEF Arena and the stc Play Gaming Hall in Boulevard City — and, exceptionally, Paris in 2026, where finals were staged at venues including the Accor Arena for the Counter-Strike 2 decider (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Alongside the tournaments, the Foundation runs the Club Partner Program, which supported 30 clubs in 2024 and 40 clubs in 2025 with a combined US$20 million in direct funding, and it has announced the Esports Nations Cup, a biennial national-team competition first planned for November 2026 in Riyadh and postponed to November 2027 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Competition is open to all genders — women compete in mixed rosters across the titles, and several games additionally run women-only circuits — and the event has faced criticism over alleged sportswashing and publisher influence, which has prompted occasional team withdrawals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

There is no single rulebook for the Esports World Cup as a whole: each of the 24–25 titles runs its own tournament under the game publisher’s competitive rules plus the Foundation’s event rulebook, with formats ranging from single- and double-elimination brackets to Swiss-system groups, round-robin leagues and racing heats, played on standardized tournament PCs or consoles with anti-cheat software, referees and replay review (Esports Foundation, n.d.). The cross-game Club Championship aggregates every club’s results across all titles into a points table; the top 24 clubs are paid from the US$30 million fund, and the leader takes US$7 million (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Because Club Championship standings require unified branding, multi-division organizations compete under one name — in 2026 All Gamers’ divisions entered as AG.AL (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Qualification to individual events combines each title’s own circuit rankings, regional qualifiers and wildcard slots, while Club Partner Program membership guarantees funded entries; minimum competitor ages are set per title, commonly 16–18 years (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men and women compete together in open brackets, and publishers may additionally operate women’s-only cups in selected games (Esports Foundation, n.d.).

History

The series traces back to Gamers Without Borders, the 2020 charity tournament series of the Saudi Arabian Federation for Electronic and Intellectual Sport, and to Gamers8, held in Riyadh’s Boulevard City in 2022 and 2023 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On 30 July 2023 Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman announced the creation of the Esports World Cup Foundation as part of Saudi Vision 2030, and the foundation was formally established in September 2023; in October 2023 Ralf Reichert — veteran of the industry and co-founder of ESL, which administers the tournaments — was appointed CEO, and on 3 September 2025 the Crown Prince was named chair of its board of trustees (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The inaugural Esports World Cup in summer 2024 offered US$62.5 million across 22 titles and introduced the Club Championship as a US$20 million cross-game competition, and the 2025 edition in Riyadh expanded to 25 tournaments in 24 games with more than US$71.5 million and over 2,500 participants from 100 countries (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On 31 March 2026 the organization renamed itself the Esports Foundation to reflect its broader remit, including the new Esports Nations Cup, and the Club Championship trophy itself is a bespoke piece designed by the British silversmith Thomas Lyte (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Reichert, who previously built ESL into the world’s largest tournament operator before selling it to Savvy Games Group in 2022, remains the public face of the project (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
