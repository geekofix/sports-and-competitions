Omweso

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Omweso (mweso) is the national mancala of Uganda — the “royal game” of the historic Buganda kingdom, where the kabaka (king) was its patron and court officials were expected to play it well (the okwesa ritual at the crowning ceremony on Buddo hill included a game). It is played on a board of four rows of eight pits (32 pits) with 64 seeds — traditionally empiki seeds from the omuyiki tree — 32 per player, whom each arranges in their own 16 pits at the start. Major tournaments are held in Kampala, the Uganda Mind Sports Federation organizes national championships, and international/world tournaments have been organized since the late 2000s. Although historically associated with men and discouraged for girls by some taboos, women now play competitively. Within the mancala and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are Bao, Kalah, Oware; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Each player first arranges their 32 seeds in the eight pits nearest them (a strategic “opening”), then the first player sows. A move picks a pit with at least two seeds and sows counterclockwise around the player's own two rows; if the last seed lands in an occupied pit, relay sowing continues until it lands in an empty pit. Capture: if the last seed lands in an occupied inner-row pit and both opponent pits in the same column are occupied, their seeds are captured and resown from the capturing pit; reverse (clockwise) sowing is allowed from the four leftmost pits when it produces a direct capture. Winning: the last player with a legal move wins, as do special feats — emitwe-ebiri (capturing at both ends in one move), akawumbi (capturing from every opponent pit in one turn) and akakyala. Referees count “emu, bbiri” — three seconds per move in tournaments.

History

Omweso has been played in Buganda for at least four centuries — European travellers described court officials playing it in the 1860s — and Michael B. Nsimbi's study “Omweso, a Game People Play in Uganda” (Occasional Paper No. 6) documents its rules and history. King Mutesa I (r. 1856–1884) played it with his wives, sisters and ministers in the palace. The game is supposedly of Bachwezi origin (16th century Bunyoro-Kitara). Related East and Southern African games on the same 4×8 equipment include bao (Swahili coast), coro (Lango), aweet (Sudan), hus (Namibia), kombe (Lamu), mongale (Mombasa), mongola (Congo),igisoro (Rwanda) and kiela (Angola).
