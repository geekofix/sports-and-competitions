Public Speaking

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive public speaking comprises judged speech contests in which speakers present original addresses before live audiences and panels of judges, scored on content, structure, delivery and effect (Wikipedia, n.d.). Its largest single event is the Toastmasters International World Championship of Public Speaking, which starts in local clubs in over 100 countries and culminates each August at the International Convention; the 2026 edition was held on 21 August 2026 at the Vancouver Convention Centre and won by Dirceu "Didi" da Silva of Rotterdam, Netherlands, with his speech "Best Decision Ever!" (Toastmasters International, n.d.). The field also includes student circuits such as the World Individual Debating and Public Speaking Championships and national forensics leagues; women and men compete in the same contests on equal terms (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Toastmasters' International Speech Contest, the pathway to the World Championship, is governed by the Speech Contest Rulebook: contests start at club level and proceed through area, division, district and quarterfinal rounds, with two winners per quarterfinal advancing to the semifinals and about eight finalists reaching the World Championship (Toastmasters International, n.d.). Speeches must be entirely the speaker's own original work, between five and seven minutes long — under- and over-time speakers place lower — and the final requires a completely new speech; a three-of-five judge panel disqualifies plagiarized or repeated speeches, and judging weighs content, organization, gestures and style (Wikipedia, n.d.). Student circuits run parallel events — original oratory, extemporaneous speaking and interpretation — with similar time limits and judging ballots, and debate formats score argumentation under their own rulesets.

History

Formal oratory descends from the rhetorical traditions of antiquity, and competitive speaking has been part of school and college life since the 19th century, when intercollegiate debating and declamation were main campus spectator sports (Wikipedia, n.d.). Ralph C. Smedley organized the first Toastmasters club at the Bloomington, Illinois YMCA in 1924 (chartered 1932 at Santa Ana, California) and built the organization that now runs the world's largest speech contest; the World Championship of Public Speaking has crowned champions since 1938 (Toastmasters International, n.d.).
