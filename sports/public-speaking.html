Public Speaking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive public speaking comprises judged speech contests in which speakers present original addresses before live audiences and panels of judges, scored on content, structure, delivery and effect. Its largest single event is the Toastmasters International World Championship of Public Speaking, which starts in local clubs worldwide and culminates each August at the International Convention, but the field also includes debating and oratory circuits for students, such as the World Individual Debating and Public Speaking Championships and national forensics leagues.

Rules

Toastmasters' International Speech Contest, the pathway to the World Championship, is governed by the organization's Speech Contest Rulebook. Contests start at club level and proceed through area, division, district and quarterfinal rounds, with two winners from each quarterfinal advancing to the semifinals and roughly ten finalists reaching the World Championship. Speeches must be entirely the speaker's own original work, between five and seven minutes in length (under and over-time competitors place lower), and every rule requires a completely new and different speech for the final than any speech given at earlier rounds; contestants must submit speech outlines for verification, and a three-of-five judge checklist disqualifies a plagiarized or repeated speech. Judging criteria weigh content, organization, gestures and style. Student circuits run parallel events — original oratory, extemporaneous speaking and interpretation — with similar time limits and judging ballots, and debate formats score argumentation under their own rulesets.

History

Formal oratory descends from the rhetorical traditions of antiquity, and competitive speaking has been part of school and college life since the 19th century, when intercollegiate debating and declamation were the main spectator sports of many campuses. Ralph C. Smedley organized the first Toastmasters club at the Bloomington, Illinois YMCA in 1905 and chartered the lasting organization at Santa Ana, California, in 1924; the international championship now known as the World Championship of Public Speaking began in 1938 and grew from roughly 10,000 annual participants in the 1990s to more than 50,000 in 149 countries in recent years. Champions have included the first non-American winner Glenn E. Carroll of Canada (1960), the first woman, Evelyn Jane Burgay (1977), and, more recently, winners from across Asia, Africa and Europe, reflecting the competition's worldwide growth. High-school forensics grew into organized national circuits, and the World Individual Debating and Public Speaking Championships has brought national school representatives together annually since 1988.
