Hapkido

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hapkido is a Korean martial art meaning "the way of harmony with internal energy," founded in the mid-20th century (traditionally attributed to Choi Yong-sool, who learned jujutsu in Japan and returned to Korea in 1948), combining joint locks, throws, strikes and falls with circular, flowing movements. It is practiced worldwide under several organizations, including the World Hapkido Federation and the Kuk Sul Won (Kuk Sool Won) umbrella, and competitions include forms, self-defense demonstrations and sparring under protective gear.

Rules

There is no single world rulebook; competitions vary by organization. At major hapkido tournaments (e.g., World Hapkido Championships), events include individual and team forms (poomsae-style sequences), one-step and free sparring with protective gear (chest guard, helmet, shin guards), and self-defense (hosinsul) demonstrations judged on realism and technique. Sparring is typically point-based (light contact to scoring zones), with prohibited techniques including strikes to the groin, eyes, and joint manipulations beyond safe limits; referees award points and penalties, and matches are 2-3 minutes. Traditional training also emphasizes falling (nakbeop) and weapon techniques.

History

Choi Yong-sool (1904-1986) developed hapkido after studying Daito-ryu Aiki-jujutsu in Japan under Takeda Sokaku and returning to Korea; he opened the first dojang in Daegu in 1948, and the art spread through his students, notably Ji Han-jae (who founded Sin Moo Hapkido) and Myung Jae-nam. Hapkido became popular globally in the 1960s-70s, aided by 1970s martial arts films, in which Bruce Lee popularized Korean arts. Today hapkido is practiced in over 50 countries with multiple international federations.
