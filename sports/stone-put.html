Stone Put

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The stone put is a heavy athletics event of the Scottish Highland games in which competitors throw a heavy stone for distance — the Braemar stone of 22 lb (about 10 kg) for men from a standing position without a run-up, and the lighter "open stone" of 16 lb (7.3 kg) thrown with a glide or spin from a toe board; women compete with stones of roughly 8–12 lb (3.6–5.4 kg) in their own division (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The related Swiss event Steinstossen at the Unspunnenfest in Interlaken throws an 83.5 kg granite block, with the standing record of 4.16 m set by Urs Hutmacher in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The largest modern gathering is the Scottish Highland Gathering and Games in Pleasanton, California, held since 1866, and the Toledo German-American Festival has staged a steinstossen since 1989 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's competitions are run separately with different stone weights, and masters and youth classes exist at the games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Figure: James Brooks. (2014, August 2). Highland Games stone throw [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Highland_Games_stone_throw.jpg

Among current leading athletes, women such as Sweden's Siri Svensson, who won the women's stone at the Canmore Highland Games in 2023, compete alongside the men's leaders in the international games circuit; the men's open-stone distances at the biggest games regularly exceed 40 feet (12 m) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Within the strength and lifting family on this site, its closest relatives are Steinholding, Scottish Hammer Throw, Strongman, Weightlifting, Olympic Weightlifting Snatch; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In the Braemar stone event the 22 lb (10 kg) stone (heavier stones up to about 28 lb in some games) is thrown from a standing position inside a marked circle, with no preliminary movement or run-up allowed; the throw is measured from the toe board or the front of the circle to the first point of impact, and each athlete gets three attempts with the best counting (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The open stone of 16 lb (7.3 kg) is thrown with a glide, spin or rotational technique from behind a trig (toe board), and distances are measured the same way. Women throw stones of about 8–12 lb (3.6–5.4 kg) under identical rules in the women's division, and masters classes use lighter stones for older athletes (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Fouls include stepping over the board before release, a foul throw that lands out of the sector, or losing balance after release without a controlled "follow-through" step; the sector is marked and judges measure from the nearest break in the ground. The Swiss Steinstossen uses the 83.5 kg stone pushed from a throwing circle, measured to the first point of impact, with three attempts (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Stone putting is one of the oldest Highland games heavies, with roots in the strength contests of the Scottish clans; the Braemar Gathering has staged the event since the 19th century, and the Pleasanton games since 1866 made it a staple of the American Scottish festival scene (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Swiss Steinstossen at the Unspunnenfest dates to the 13th century origins of that festival in Interlaken, where throwing the 83.5 kg Unspunnenstein was a farmers' strength contest; Germany adopted the 15 kg iron block throw as an athletics event in 1860, and the Toledo German-American Festival has continued the tradition in the US since 1989, with Kevin Marx setting 4.66 m there in 2009 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
