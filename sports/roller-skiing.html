Roller Skiing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller skiing is cross-country skiing on asphalt: athletes use short skis fitted with rubber or pneumatic wheels — two or three per ski — and ski poles to train and race on roads when there is no snow (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), which recognized roller skiing as a distinct discipline within cross-country skiing in 1992, introduced the FIS Roller Ski World Cup and staged the first World Championships in Rotterdam in 2001 — whose 25th anniversary fell in 2026 (FIS, n.d.). The 2026 FIS Roller Ski World Championships were held 3–6 September 2026 in Madona, Latvia, combining the Smeceres sils venue with city-centre races: Norway's Ragnhild Aarekol won three gold medals (10 km classic interval start, team sprint, 20 km freestyle mass start), Andreas Bergsland won the men's classic individual, and Latvia's Raimo Vigants took the men's team sprint and the 20 km mass start in front of the home crowd (FIS, n.d.). The 2026 FIS Roller Ski World Cup ran three stages — Trollhättan (Sweden), Madona (Latvia) and Val di Fiemme (Italy, 18–20 September) — with 11 races; in the season-opening sprint in Trollhättan, Sweden's Johanna Hagström won the women's title and Anton Grahn the men's (FIS, n.d.). Women and men compete in identical formats in senior and junior categories (FIS, n.d.).

Rules

Roller skiing follows the race formats of cross-country skiing — sprint (including a 160–200 m super sprint), classic interval start, team sprint in skating technique and mass start in freestyle — but there is no pursuit event (FIS, n.d.). Races take place on closed public roads or dedicated circuits, in classic and freestyle (skating) technique with the same rules as on snow, and safety requires a helmet; roller skis must meet FIS equipment rules for wheel type and length (FIS, n.d.). At the 2026 World Championships in Madona the programme ran 10 km classic interval start (seniors, 3 September), team sprint freestyle 2 × 3 × 1.7 km (4 September), 200 m freestyle city sprint (5 September) and 20 km freestyle mass start for seniors / 15 km for juniors (6 September) (FIS, n.d.). Both women and men race in senior and junior classes, and World Cup and World Championship standings are kept separately per gender (FIS, n.d.).

History

Roller skis were invented in the 1930s — early versions appeared in Italy and Scandinavia as summer training for cross-country skiers, and the first competitions were held in the 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.). FIS recognized roller skiing as a distinct discipline from cross-country skiing in 1992, World Cup events soon followed, and the first FIS Roller Ski World Championships took place in Rotterdam, Netherlands, in 2001 (FIS, n.d.). The sport is now a standard summer training tool for Nordic skiers, with a World Cup circuit across Europe and world championships held roughly annually, most recently in Madona, Latvia, in September 2026 (FIS, n.d.).
