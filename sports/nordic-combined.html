Nordic Combined

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Nordic combined is a winter sport that merges the two disciplines of ski jumping and cross-country skiing into a single competition: athletes first take a jump on a large hill, and the jumping result is converted into a time handicap for the subsequent cross-country race. The athlete with the best combined jumping and skiing performance wins. The sport is governed worldwide by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS) and has been on the Olympic Winter Games program since the first Winter Olympics at Chamonix 1924.

Rules

In the classic Gundersen method, each athlete takes one jump (two jumps in some team events), and the jumping points are converted into seconds on the start list for the cross-country race, so the leading jumper starts first and every following athlete starts as many seconds behind as the point deficit. The athlete who crosses the finish line first wins. The main individual formats at World Cup and World Championship level are the Individual Gundersen (one jump plus 10 km), the Compact (one jump plus 7.5 km) and the Mass Start (10 km cross-country first, with the results converted into a time handicap for a final jumping round). Team events comprise four athletes per nation, each jumping and skiing a 5 km leg, and there is a mixed team event with two women and two men per nation. Races are timed electronically to hundredths of a second, and the athlete with the lowest total time over the combined disciplines wins.

History

Nordic combined originated in Norway in the late 19th century, where soldiers and civilians combined ski jumping and cross-country skiing over the winter terrain; the first recorded competition is the Holmenkollen event of 1892, and the sport debuted at the first Winter Olympics in Chamonix 1924. Norway dominated the early decades, with Thorleif Haug one of the most successful athletes of the era, and later East Germany and Germany became the leading nations. The FIS Nordic Combined World Cup has been held since the 1983-84 season, and women's events at senior World Championship level were introduced in 2021. Stars of the modern era include Germany's Eric Frenzel, Norway's Jarl Magnus Riiber and Johannes Rydzek. In 2025 the International Olympic Committee decided to remove nordic combined from the program of the 2030 Winter Olympics, which sparked a debate about the future of the discipline.
