BASE Jumping

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

BASE jumping is the extreme sport of parachuting from fixed objects — Buildings, Antennas, Spans (bridges) and Earth (cliffs) — using a steerable parachute. There is no single world federation; the biggest competitions are the World BASE Race in Norway's Romsdalen valley and the BASE Jump Extreme World Championship (EWC) in Benidorm, Spain, held since 2008.

Rules

In the World BASE Race, pairs of jumpers race down a steep mountainside course, with the fastest time from the exit point to a landing zone deciding the winner; safety rules require minimum equipment (helmet, wingsuit or tracking suit) and a no-fly list for weather. At the BASE Jump EWC in Benidorm, jumpers perform scored proximity-flying runs from a fixed cliff platform, judged on distance from a target and flight precision. Wingsuit proximity flying adds a separate discipline where pilots race a marked course as close to the terrain as possible.

History

BASE jumping emerged from skydiving in the late 1970s; the acronym was coined in 1981 by Carl Boenish and others, and the first legal fixed-object jumps from El Capitan took place in the early 1980s. The sport grew through the 1990s with wingsuit development. The World BASE Race in Romsdalen, Norway, began in the early 2000s and became the premier team race, while Benidorm hosts the annual EWC individual competition.
