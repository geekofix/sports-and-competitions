Water Polo

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Water polo is the team ball sport played in a swimming pool: two teams of seven — six field players and a goalkeeper — swim, tread water with the eggbeater kick and pass a ball one-handed, aiming to throw it into the opponents' 3 × 0.9 m goal (World Aquatics, n.d.). It is one of the oldest Olympic team sports, in the programme since Paris 1900 for men and since Sydney 2000 for women, governed worldwide by World Aquatics (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women's water polo stands at the very top today: at the Paris 2024 Olympics Spain won its first-ever Olympic women's gold medal, unbeaten through the tournament, and at the 2025 World Aquatics Championships in Singapore Greece defeated Hungary 12–9 in the women's final to take its second world title (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

A match consists of four quarters of eight minutes of playing time, with a 30-second shot clock; teams field seven players (six plus a goalkeeper), and field players may touch the ball with one hand only, while the goalkeeper may use both hands within the 5 m area (World Aquatics, n.d.). Play is physical but regulated: shoulder-to-shoulder challenges and stealing the ball from a holder are legal, while holding, sinking or striking an opponent, and punching the ball, are fouls — ordinary fouls give a free throw, exclusion fouls send the offender to the penalty area for 20 seconds (fourth exclusion is permanent), and a major foul in the attacking 5 m area awards a 5 m penalty throw (World Aquatics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The pool is at least 1.8 m deep so players cannot stand, goals are nets 3 m wide and 0.9 m high, and men's and women's tournaments are run separately under the same Laws of the Game, with different ball sizes historically (men's 710–730 g, women's 400–450 g) (World Aquatics, n.d.). Substitutions are free at any stoppage, and video review (VAR) is used at major tournaments (World Aquatics, n.d.).

History

Water polo emerged in late-19th-century Britain as "aquatic rugby" played in rivers and lakes; the London Water Polo Club drew up early rules around 1870, the first international match was played between Scotland and England in 1890, and men's water polo entered the Olympics at Paris 1900 — making it the oldest Olympic team sport — with the women's tournament added at Sydney 2000 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The early Olympic era was dominated by Great Britain and Hungary; after World War II the Eastern European powers — Hungary's "Golden Team" of the 1950s–70s and the Yugoslav/Serbian dynasty — set the standard, and today the elite scene is led by Serbia (men's Olympic gold at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024), Spain (men's world champion 2025, women's Olympic champion 2024), Greece (women's world champion 2025), Hungary, Croatia and the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.). World Aquatics — founded as FINA in 1908 — governs the sport with World Championships, a World Cup and the Olympic tournament, and professional club leagues thrive in Hungary, Italy, Spain, Greece, Croatia and Serbia (World Aquatics, n.d.).
