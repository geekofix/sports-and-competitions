Beach Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Beach wrestling is Olympic-style wrestling contested in a circular sand arena. It is a recognized discipline of United World Wrestling (UWW), which organizes the World Beach Wrestling Championships (first held in 2006), and it has appeared at the Youth Olympic Games and the World Beach Games.

Rules

Under UWW beach wrestling rules, two wrestlers face each other in a circle 6 m in diameter on sand, wearing wrestling singlets. The objective is to throw or take the opponent down so that a part of the body above the hips touches the ground, scoring a fall (4 points) or a takedown (1-2 points depending on amplitude). Matches last 3 minutes; if no fall occurs, the wrestler with more points wins, and ties are broken by criteria such as last scoring action. Weight classes and age groups are contested at the World Championships, with knockout brackets after the group stage.

History

Beach wrestling was codified by FILA (now UWW) in the early 2000s as part of its associated styles, alongside grappling and pankration. The first World Beach Wrestling Championships were held in 2006 in Turkey, and the discipline has since been contested at the Youth Olympic Games (Buenos Aires 2018) and the World Beach Games. It is popular in countries with strong wrestling traditions, including Turkey, Ukraine, Azerbaijan and Iran.
