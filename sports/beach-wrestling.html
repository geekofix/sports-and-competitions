Beach Wrestling

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Beach wrestling is a sand-based wrestling discipline governed by United World Wrestling (United World Wrestling [UWW], n.d.-b), contested in a circular area with points for falls and for pushing the opponent out, in men's and women's weight classes from 50 kg up to 90+ kg. The 2026 UWW senior World Championships in Katerini, Greece, in October 2026 gathered 91 athletes from 17 nations across eight senior world titles; Azerbaijan won the first-ever men's team title (two golds and three bronzes, with coach Oyan Nazariani named UWW Coach of the Year), Ukraine's Anna Karbovska won the women's 70 kg crown, and Georgia's Luka Chkhitunidze beat Azerbaijan's Sahib Dadashov in the men's 80 kg final (UWW, n.d.-a). At the 2026 U20 World Championships in Katerini on October 2 (95 wrestlers from 14 nations) Ukraine dominated and the US women won the team title with 53 points, and at the U17 World Championships on August 27, 2026 in Baku, Azerbaijan's Eljan Hasanov took gold (UWW, n.d.-a). Beach wrestling made its Youth Olympic Games debut at Dakar 2026 on November 7–8, 2026, where Senegal won three titles (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-a) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-b).

Rules

A beach wrestling bout takes place in a circular sand area 6 m in diameter and lasts three minutes; the objective is to score more points than the opponent (UWW, n.d.-b). Throwing the opponent so that any part of the body other than the feet touches the sand is a fall and scores four points, while forcing the opponent out of the circle scores one point, and the wrestler with the highest score at the end of the bout wins; a fall or a dominant score can also end the bout early under the UWW regulations. Competitors are divided into weight classes — for senior men and women including 50, 70, 80 and 90+ kg — and wear a singlet or rash guard; gripping is free but striking and joint locks are forbidden. Men's and women's bouts are identical in format and duration, and U17 and U20 categories use shorter bouts and lighter circles (UWW, n.d.-a).

History

Beach wrestling as a UWW discipline began with the first world championships in 2006 in Turkey, and it has since become a staple of multi-sport beach events, debuting at the Youth Olympic Games at Dakar 2026 in November 2026 (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.). The United States was an early adopter, staging beach wrestling at its World Team Trials from 2010 — the 2010 Rochester trials featured a lightweight division bout between Anthony Gallton and Robert Teet — and American teams have since competed regularly at UWW world events (UWW, n.d.-a). Women's beach wrestling has been part of the world programme since the early editions, with national squads such as the United States women, who won the 2026 U20 team title in Katerini, and Senegal's women, who took three Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic titles, showing the rapid growth of the women's side (UWW, n.d.-b).
