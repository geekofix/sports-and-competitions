Duathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Duathlon is a multisport race combining running and cycling, governed by World Triathlon (formerly the International Triathlon Union, ITU), which runs the World Triathlon Duathlon Championships. Standard formats are 10 km run - 40 km bike - 5 km run ("standard distance"), as well as sprint (5-20-2.5 km) and power duathlon variants, with draft-legal and non-drafting (draft-illegal, like triathlon) versions.

Rules

At World Triathlon Championships, athletes complete the run-bike-run sequence in transition areas with designated racks; in draft-legal events, riding in another athlete's slipstream (drafting) is prohibited and penalized, while non-drafting (power) duathlons allow it. Age-group and elite races use staggered or mass starts, with wave starts by age group at large events. The clock runs from start to finish including transitions (T1, T2). Standard distances are sprint (5 km run, 20 km bike, 2.5 km run), standard (10-40-5 km), and long distance (14-80-10 km); the World Championships also include relay and mixed relay events. Equipment rules (helmet mandatory on the bike, no drafting in age-group power events) follow the World Triathlon Competition Rules.

History

Duathlon was invented in the early 1980s in France, with the first official races around 1983-1990 as a way to race multisport without swimming; the first World Championship was held in 1990 in Calais, France, under the International Triathlon Union. The sport grew through the 1990s with the ITU (founded 1989) adding duathlon to its program, and it is part of the World Games and the Youth Olympic Games (Buenos Aires 2018). France, Great Britain, the Netherlands, Belgium and the United States have traditionally dominated, with champions such as Rob Woestenborghs, Urs Dolder and Sandra Levenez. Power duathlon (non-drafting) is especially popular in the United States and Germany, where it is raced over longer bike distances.
