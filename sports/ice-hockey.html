Ice Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ice hockey is a fast-paced team sport played on an enclosed ice rink, in which two teams of six players (one goaltender and five skaters) use hockey sticks to shoot a hard rubber puck into the opponent's goal; each goal is worth one point and the team with the most goals wins. The sport is governed internationally by the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF), founded in 1908; the premier professional league is the National Hockey League (NHL), founded in 1917, whose playoff tournament for the Stanley Cup is the oldest professional team trophy in North America. Ice hockey has been part of the Winter Olympic Games since the first Winter Olympics in Chamonix 1924.

Rules

Under the IIHF Official Rules, a game consists of three 20-minute periods on a rink of 60 m x 30 m with rounded corners and a goal at each end; teams dress up to 20 players but only six (or five during overtime) may be on the ice at once. Play starts with a face-off and is stopped for goals, penalties and infractions such as icing (shooting the puck from behind the centre red line down the ice) and offside (a skater entering the attacking zone ahead of the puck). Penalties range from a two-minute minor and a five-minute major to a ten-minute misconduct, and leave the offending team short-handed on a power play; serious foul play can earn a penalty shot. Body checking is permitted in men's elite hockey, and a goaltender who covers the puck or freezes it stops play. In regular-season overtime, teams play three-on-three for five minutes followed by a shootout, while playoff overtime uses sudden-death 20-minute five-on-five periods.

History

Ice hockey grew out of 19th-century Canadian stick-and-ball games played on frozen ponds and rivers. The first documented indoor game was organized by James Creighton of McGill University at the Victoria Skating Rink in Montreal on March 3, 1875. Lord Stanley of Preston, Governor General of Canada, donated the Stanley Cup in 1892 as a challenge trophy for the country's best amateur club, first awarded in 1893 to the Montreal Amateur Athletic Association. The NHL was founded in Montreal on November 26, 1917, with the Montreal Canadiens, Montreal Wanderers, Ottawa Senators, Quebec Bulldogs and Toronto Arenas among the original franchises, and the IIHF was founded in Paris in 1908, making ice hockey one of the first international winter sport federations. Canada won gold at the first Olympic tournament in Chamonix 1924. Legendary figures include Wayne Gretzky, the NHL's all-time leading scorer known as "The Great One", and Gordie Howe, "Mr. Hockey"; famous events include the 1972 Summit Series between Canada and the Soviet Union and the "Miracle on Ice" at the 1980 Lake Placid Olympics, when a US college team defeated the Soviet national team 4-3. Women's ice hockey has been an Olympic medal event since Nagano 1998.
