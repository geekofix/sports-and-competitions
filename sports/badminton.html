Badminton

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Badminton is a racket sport played over a net with a lightweight shuttlecock, in singles or doubles. Two or four players hit the shuttlecock back and forth, scoring when it lands in the opponent's court. It is governed by the Badminton World Federation (BWF) and has been an Olympic sport since Barcelona 1992. It is one of the fastest racket sports: smashes regularly exceed 400 km/h (250 mph).

Rules

Under the BWF Laws of Badminton, the court is 13.4 m long and 6.1 m wide for doubles (5.18 m for singles), with the net 1.55 m high at the posts. Matches at the World Championships and Olympics are best of three games to 21 points, won by two clear points (capped at 30). The serve must be hit below waist level into the diagonally opposite service court; a shuttlecock landing on a line is "in". In singles, the service courts are long and narrow; in doubles they are short and wide.

History

The game descends from battledore-and-shuttlecock, played in ancient China, Greece and India, and from "poona", played by British officers in India in the 1860s. The modern game takes its name from Badminton House in Gloucestershire, England, where the Duke of Beaufort entertained guests in the 1870s. The International Badminton Federation (now BWF) was founded in 1934, the first All England Open was held in 1899, and badminton became a full Olympic medal sport at Barcelona 1992.
