Trap Shooting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Trap shooting is the clay target shotgun sport of the "going away" angle: shooters stand on arcs of stations and shoot clay pigeots launched from a single trap house in front of them, flying away at varying angles. One of the classic disciplines of international shooting sport (part of the Olympic shotgun programme alongside skeet), it is governed worldwide by the ISSF and contested without limit distinction by shotgun shot rounds at 110-millimetre clay targets travelling roughly 100 km/h.

Rules

Standard ISSF trap events shoot 125 targets in qualification (150 in some formats) for men and women across five stations in a squad rotation: each shooter mounts the gun, calls for the target, and the trap machine releases a clay at an unknown horizontal angle within a set arc, with a single shot permitted and hits decided by visible target break. Finals narrow the field to shoot-off rounds for medals, scoring is by broken targets, and rules govern gun and ammunition (shotgun gauge standards, shell limits), hold points (gun position before the call) and the specific trap machine's angle-setting procedures, with judges and referee panels enforcing the standardized ISSF rulebook at world championships, world cups and the Olympic Games.

History

Trap shooting descends from live pigeon releases (the earliest glass-ball and pigeon traps of the early nineteenth century), with the standardized clay pigeon replacing live birds in the sport's late nineteenth-century European and American evolution; governing bodies standardized the traps, arcs and squad formats, and trap joined the first modern Olympic shooting programme era contested from 1900. The ISSF's world championships and Olympic trap events continue as the discipline's apex, with tractor-era changes to voice-release systems and flash targets refining spectacle, and national federations supporting club-level trap along with its sporting skeet, double trap and universal trench cousins.
