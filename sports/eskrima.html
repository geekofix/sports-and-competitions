Eskrima

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Eskrima — also called arnis or kali — is the Filipino martial art of stick (baston) and blade fighting, codified in Republic Act 9850, signed on 11 December 2009 by President Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo, which declared arnis the national martial art and sport of the Philippines (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-e). The international governing body is the World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation (WEKAF), founded on 11 August 1989 in Cebu City with around 40 member federations, which runs the biennial World Championships — the 17th edition was held in Mandaue, Philippines, in 2024 and the 18th takes place in Srinagar, India, 27–31 July 2026 (World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation, n.d.-c; World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-f).

In the Philippines the recognised national sport body is the Philippine Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation (PEKAF), founded in 2017 under Senator Miguel Zubiri, which fields the national team and lobbied arnis back into the Southeast Asian Games in 2019 and 2023 after earlier editions in 1991 (demonstration) and 2005 (Wikipedia, n.d.-d). Among the best-known international competitors is Riaz Amin of Great Britain, a WEKAF world champion who combines eskrima with Shotokan karate (Wikipedia, n.d.-f).

Rules

WEKAF competition uses full-contact padded rattan sticks (baston) of 26–28 inches with protective armour — head guard, gloves, chest, forearm and shin guards — and awards points for clean, controlled strikes to valid target areas; events also include double-stick, single-stick, soft/first-strike categories and blade simulation with wooden or rubber knives, plus disarming (World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-f).

Bouts are divided into age and gender classes — cadet, junior and senior divisions, and separate men's and women's categories — so women compete on equal footing in their own divisions at every WEKAF World Championship; in the Philippines, arnis is compulsory in school physical education for both boys and girls under RA 9850 (World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

History

The art's roots are the fighting systems of the Philippine islands, whose names eskrima/esgrima derive from the Spanish word for fencing brought by the colonisers from 1565; legend credits the chieftain Lapu-Lapu with using kali techniques against Magellan at the Battle of Mactan in 1521, and guerrillas used stick fighting in the 1898 revolution against Spain and later against American rule, which suppressed the art in the early 1900s (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Modern styles crystallised in Cebu: the Doce Pares system of the brothers Eulogio and Cacoy Cañete, taught from their Labangon Fencing Club founded in 1918, and Balintawak, organised by Anciong Bacon in 1952; WEKAF was founded at the Sacred Heart Center in Cebu on 11 August 1989 with Dionisio Cañete as charter president and staged its first World Championship the same week (Wikipedia, n.d.-f; Wikipedia, n.d.-c; World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation, n.d.-c).
