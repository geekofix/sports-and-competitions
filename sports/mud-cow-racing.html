Mud Cow Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mud cow racing refers to traditional cattle races held in muddy, flooded rice fields, best known as pacu jawi in Tanah Datar, West Sumatra, Indonesia, and as the cattle race festival of An Giang in the Mekong Delta of Vietnam. In pacu jawi a jockey stands barefoot on a simple wooden plough that is loosely tied to a pair of bulls and holds their tails while the animals race through a muddy track of about 60 to 250 m in a harvested rice paddy, with mud up to 30 cm deep. Despite the name, the bulls do not race directly against each other and no formal winner is announced; the event celebrates the harvest and lets owners show off their animals to buyers.

Rules

In pacu jawi each jockey takes a turn with a pair of bulls aged about 2 to 13 years on the muddy track; the bulls start running when the plough is on the ground and the jockey steps on it, and the jockey may bite or pull a bull's tail to urge it faster, often being dragged and thrown into the mud. The fastest-looking run wins the crowd's approval and prize money, but the race is judged more on spectacle than on strict timing. In the An Giang festival in Vietnam, held around the 8th lunar month in connection with the Khmer dokteah festival, pairs of cattle race head-to-head on a muddy course in "playoff" heats over a longer track. Both traditions prohibit harming the animals, and veterinary checks apply at organized events.

History

Pacu jawi has been practiced for hundreds of years in the Minangkabau highlands of West Sumatra as a celebration at the end of the rice harvest season, when the fields lie empty and muddy; it also serves as a marketplace where cattle sellers display their animals. The modern event in Tanah Datar, with funding from the local tourism office, has become a major tourist attraction photographed around the world. A similar tradition exists in An Giang Province, Vietnam, where cattle racing accompanies the Khmer community's harvest festival in the Mekong Delta. Both events were paused during the COVID-19 pandemic and have since resumed as cultural festivals.
