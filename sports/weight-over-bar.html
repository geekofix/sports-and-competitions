Weight Over Bar

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Weight over bar (Scottish Gaelic: cuideam thairis air a 'bhàr) is the classic one-arm heavy event of the Scottish Highland games: the athlete heaves a heavy iron weight with a ring handle — 56 lb (25.4 kg, half a hundredweight) in the open men's class — over a horizontal bar, and the bar is raised after every successful round until only one thrower remains (Wikipedia, n.d.). In the classic method the weight is swung between the legs like a pendulum and released at the apex directly overhead; a spin technique is also permitted. Classes adjust the implement — 56 lb open men, 42 lb masters men, 28 lb (12.7 kg) women and 14 lb (6.4 kg) light women — so women and men of every age group compete on equal terms (NASGA, n.d.). The standing world record is 6.20 m (20 ft 4 in) with the 56 lb weight, thrown by Iceland's Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson at the 2026 Sherwood Park Highland Games — his thirteenth world record in the event (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Each thrower gets three attempts at a given height, and the bar is raised in set increments — commonly 6 inches (15 cm) — after each round (NASGA, n.d.). The throw is delivered from behind the trig or throwing line with one hand only, and it counts only if the weight passes cleanly over the bar and lands on the far side without dislodging it. A thrower who fails all three attempts at a height is eliminated; the last athlete standing wins, with the fewest total misses breaking ties. The event has also been contested at World's Strongest Man since 1980 using the same 56 lb weight and the rising-bar elimination format, which is where the modern record heights come from (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

The event traces back roughly 600 years to ancient Scottish market and farm life, where a four-stone (56 lb) iron block with rings served as a counterweight in trade, and athletes hurled such blocks one-handed over a bar at Highland gatherings (Wikipedia, n.d.). The heavy events were formalised in the nineteenth century at Caledonian-club games alongside the caber toss, stone put and hammer throw, and the 56 lb weight over bar became a staple of games from Braemar and Crieff to the diaspora games in North America, Australia and New Zealand. The great icon of the modern era was Scotland's Bill Anderson (1937–2019), 16-time Scottish champion, who broke the world record four times up to 4.90 m (16 ft 1 in) in 1977; the event entered the World's Strongest Man programme in 1980 and has been dominated since by strongman specialists (Wikipedia, n.d.).
