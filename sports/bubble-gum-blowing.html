Bubble Gum Blowing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bubble gum blowing as a competitive activity is the sport of inflating the largest possible bubble from chewing gum; the recognized world records are kept by Guinness World Records. The current Guinness record for the largest bubblegum bubble blown hands-free is 50.8 cm (20 in), set by Chad Fell of the United States on April 24, 2004, at Double Springs High School in Winston County, Alabama, using three pieces of Dubble Bubble — a record still unbeaten more than 20 years later (Guinness World Records [GWR], n.d.-a). The largest bubble ever blown with assistance (measured by a team helping to stretch it) was 58 cm (23 in), set by Susan Montgomery Williams of the United States in Fresno, California, in 1994; she died in 2008 (GWR, n.d.-b) (Guinness World Records, n.d.-a) (Guinness World Records, n.d.-b).

On the women's side, Susan Montgomery Williams remains the record holder in the assisted category, and her 2025 commemorative photograph documents the record attempt; in the hands-free category, women regularly compete at bubble-blowing contests and school events around the world, though the overall record is held by a man (GWR, n.d.-a) (GWR, n.d.-b).

Rules

Bubble gum blowing as a record sport follows the Guinness World Records measurement rules: the bubble must be inflated from a single wad of chewing gum (multiple pieces are allowed if they form one bubble — Chad Fell used three pieces of Dubble Bubble), using only the mouth, with no hands, no straws and no other aids in the hands-free category; the bubble must remain intact and be measured across its widest diameter with a ruler or tape by an adjudicator (GWR, n.d.-a). In the assisted category, helpers may stretch and shape the bubble after it leaves the mouth, and the final diameter is measured the same way (GWR, n.d.-b). In casual contests — school fairs, festivals and party games — the rules are simpler: one piece of gum per contestant, the bubble must stay attached to the mouth, and the largest intact bubble after a countdown wins; popping the bubble ends the attempt.

Technique matters more than lung volume: contestants chew the gum until soft, press it flat against the tongue and teeth, open the mouth wide and blow a steady stream of air to expand the bubble evenly; the world's largest bubbles are blown slowly over several seconds to avoid tearing the gum film. Equipment is minimal — chewing gum only (sugar-free gum stretches less), and no other materials. There are no age or gender classes in the official records: men's and women's attempts are measured under the same rules, and youth categories exist only in informal contests (GWR, n.d.-a).

History

Chewing gum itself was invented in 1928 by Walter Diemer, an accountant at the Fleer Chewing Gum Company in Philadelphia, who developed the first successful bubble gum, marketed as Dubble Bubble — the same brand Chad Fell used for his 2004 record (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). Bubble blowing as a pastime became a fad in the United States in the 1940s–50s, and by the 1990s organized bubble-blowing contests with measured records were common at fairs and on television; Guinness World Records began listing the largest bubblegum bubble in the 1990s. Chad Fell's 50.8 cm bubble of April 24, 2004, remains the benchmark more than two decades later, while Susan Montgomery Williams' 58 cm assisted bubble of 1994 stands as the largest ever measured (GWR, n.d.-a) (GWR, n.d.-b).
