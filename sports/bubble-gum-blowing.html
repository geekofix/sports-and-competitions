Bubble Gum Blowing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bubble gum blowing is the competitive art of inflating the largest possible bubble from chewing gum. There is no world federation; the recognized records are the Guinness World Records titles. The hands-free record is 50.8 cm (20 in) in diameter, set by Chad Fell (USA) on 24 April 2004; the hands-assisted record is 58 cm (23 in), set by Susan Montgomery Williams (USA) in 1994.

Rules

Under Guinness World Records rules for the largest bubblegum bubble blown, the attempt must take place in a controlled environment, be documented on video, and the bubble must be held for five seconds before popping. The diameter is measured in centimetres or inches, and the type of gum and number of pieces used must be specified. In the hands-free category the bubble may not be touched or steadied with the hands at any point; the assisted category allows the hands to stretch and shape the bubble. Chad Fell used three pieces of Dubble Bubble gum.

History

Bubble gum was invented in 1928 by Walter Diemer at Fleer in Philadelphia (Dubble Bubble, pink because that was the available dye). Competitive bubble blowing emerged with TV record shows in the 1970s-90s; Susan Montgomery Williams became famous in the 1990s, setting the assisted record in 1994 and appearing on Johnny Carson and Jay Leno. Chad Fell's hands-free 20-inch bubble at Double Springs High School, Alabama, on 24 April 2004 has stood for more than 20 years. Informal contests are held at fairs and schools, often with smaller "largest bubble" prizes.
