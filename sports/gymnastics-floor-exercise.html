Gymnastics Floor Exercise

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The floor exercise is the artistic gymnastics event performed on a 12 × 12 m sprung carpet with a safety border, where gymnasts combine tumbling passes, dance steps, strength and flexibility elements into a routine of at most 70 s for men and 90 s for women — the women's routine performed to music (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is one of the six men's and four women's apparatus at the Olympic Games and World Championships; at Paris 2024 the men's title went to Carlos Yulo of the Philippines and the women's to Rebeca Andrade of Brazil, with Simone Biles of the USA taking silver (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.).

Among the leading floor workers of the 2026 season, the women come first: Skye Blakely of the United States (pictured), the 2026 world all-around champion, Simone Biles, the most decorated gymnast in World Championship history with her powerful tumbling, Rebeca Andrade, and Kaylia Nemour (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the men's side Carlos Yulo — double Olympic champion on floor and vault at Paris 2024 and the first Filipino Olympic gymnastics gold medallist — and Artem Dolgopyat of Israel, the Tokyo 2020 floor champion, head the lists (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

This belongs to the eight artistic-gymnastics apparatus — Pommel Horse, Rings, Vault, Parallel Bars, Horizontal Bar, Uneven Bars, Balance Beam — within the gymnastics family — the parent Artistic Gymnastics page covers all eight apparatus; full list under Important Links.

Rules

The floor area is a 12 × 12 m sprung carpet surrounded by a safety border of at least 2 m; men's routines are limited to 70 s and women's to 90 s, women must perform to music, and stepping or landing outside the carpet costs deductions (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a). Under the 2025–2028 Code of Points eight elements count toward the D-score, acrobatic series earn connection bonuses, and from 1 January 2026 the dismount is credited first with a bonus for a stuck landing; the E-panel starts at 10.0 and deducts for execution faults (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a).

In qualification each gymnast performs one routine; the top eight advance to the apparatus final, where the highest D + E total wins, with the higher execution score breaking ties (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). Men and women compete separately under identical rules; junior (under-18) categories use the same floor, and the event is also part of the team and all-around competitions (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

History

The floor exercise grew out of 19th-century gymnastic floor routines and became an Olympic apparatus for men in 1896 and for women in 1932; tumbling was formalized in the mid-20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). Olga Korbut of the USSR, pictured, electrified Munich 1972 with her backflip-to-backflip combination on beam and floor and won floor gold, and Nadia Comăneci's perfect 10.0 at Montreal 1976 came in the all-around with her legendary floor routine; the USA's Kurt Thomas, who invented the “Thomas flair”, won the 1978 world floor title, and in the modern era Kohei Uchimura and Simone Biles set the standard for power tumbling (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
