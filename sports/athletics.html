Athletics

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Athletics (track and field) covers running, jumping, throwing and combined events, governed worldwide by World Athletics. It is the centerpiece of the Olympic Games, with events from the 100 m sprint to the marathon, plus hurdles, relays, steeplechase, jumps, throws and the decathlon/heptathlon.

Rules

Track events are timed electronically; sprints and hurdles use staggered lanes, relays use baton exchange zones. In field events, athletes get a set number of attempts (usually three, plus three more for the top eight in finals); the best valid mark wins, with a wind-gauge limit of 2.0 m/s for sprint and jump records. Combined events (decathlon, heptathlon) score each discipline with the World Athletics points tables. Since 2010, a single false start disqualifies. Variants: indoor championships use a 200 m banked track and adjusted field events; road disciplines include the marathon and race walks.

History

Athletics has ancient roots in the Greek games; modern track and field formed in 19th-century Britain (amateur clubs, first championships 1880s). It has been at every modern Olympics since 1896. Icons include Jesse Owens (1936 Berlin), Carl Lewis, Usain Bolt (100/200 m world records), Eliud Kipchoge (marathon) and Armand "Jacko" Duplantis (pole vault).
