Athletics

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Athletics — known internationally as track and field — is the umbrella sport of running, jumping and throwing competitions, plus road running, race walking and cross country. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics, founded in 1912 as the International Amateur Athletic Federation (IAAF), and it has been the centerpiece of the modern Olympic Games since Athens 1896 (World Athletics, n.d.-c; Olympics.com, n.d.). The 2026 season was headlined by the World Indoor Championships in Toruń, Poland (March 20–22, 2026), where Sweden's Armand "Mondo" Duplantis won the men's pole vault with a championship record 6.25 m, and by the European Championships at Alexander Stadium in Birmingham (August 14–16, 2026), where Duplantis cleared 6.15 m for a fourth straight European pole vault title and Britain's Amy Hunt became the first athlete in history to win four gold medals at a single European Championships (100 m, 200 m and both relays) (World Athletics, n.d.-b; European Athletics, n.d.) (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

Among the athletes who shape the sport today, the women come first: the Netherlands' Femke Bol, the dominant 400 m hurdles runner and 2024 Olympic champion; Kenya's Faith Kipyegon, the three-time Olympic 1500 m champion and world-record holder; Britain's Keely Hodgkinson, the 2024 Olympic 800 m champion; the USA's Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone, the 400 m hurdles world-record holder; and Britain's Amy Hunt, the 2026 European sprint champion. On the men's side, Duplantis — who set the indoor world record of 6.31 m in Uppsala on March 12, 2026 and the outdoor world record of 6.30 m in Tokyo 2025 — remains the defining figure, alongside the USA's Noah Lyles (sprints), Norway's Jakob Ingebrigtsen (middle distance) and Kenya's Emmanuel Wanyonyi (800 m) (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

Rules

Under the World Athletics Competition Rules, track events are run counterclockwise on a standard 400 m oval with eight or nine lanes of 1.22 m; races of 400 m and shorter are run in lanes, while longer races and relays use staggered starts, and the finish is judged by the torso crossing the line, with fully automatic timing and photo-finish equipment mandatory at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start — reacting in under 0.100 s — disqualifies the athlete outright. Wind readings matter: a record or qualification mark stands only with a legal wind of at most +2.0 m/s in sprints and horizontal jumps (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

Field events comprise jumps (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault) and throws (shot put, discus, hammer, javelin). In horizontal jumps and throws, athletes get three attempts in qualifying and the best eight advance to a final round of three more attempts, ranked by best mark; in vertical jumps (high jump, pole vault), athletes may pass and the last athlete to clear the winning height wins (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Race walking is judged by two visible rules: the advancing leg must be straightened from the moment of first contact with the ground until in the vertical upright position, and the athlete must always have at least one foot in contact with the ground; three red cards from different judges mean disqualification. Combined events are the men's decathlon (10 events over two days: 100 m, long jump, shot put, high jump, 400 m, 110 m hurdles, discus, pole vault, javelin, 1500 m) and the women's heptathlon (7 events: 100 m hurdles, high jump, shot put, 200 m, long jump, javelin, 800 m) (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

There are no gender-specific differences in distances or equipment at elite level — women and men contest the same events, from sprint to marathon, and since 2024 the marathon race walk mixed relay is a standard championship event. Age and classification differences matter instead: World Athletics runs U20 and U18 championships, and World Para Athletics assigns athletes to T (track) and F (field) classes by impairment, with some events contested over different distances, such as the shorter wheelchair racing distances or seated throws from a throwing frame (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

History

Athletics is the oldest organized sport: the ancient Olympic Games of 776 BC in Greece consisted of a single race, the stadion (about 192 m), and by 708 BC a pentathlon (stadion, long jump, discus, javelin, wrestling) had been added. The modern era began with the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896, and the international federation was founded in Stockholm in 1912, on the eve of those Games, by 17 national federations — originally as the IAAF, renamed World Athletics in 2019 (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

The most iconic figure of the early Olympics is Jesse Owens of the United States, who won four gold medals (100 m, 200 m, long jump, 4 × 100 m relay) at the 1936 Berlin Games in front of Adolf Hitler — a feat that made him the most successful athlete of those Games and a global symbol against racism. Carl Lewis (USA) matched Owens' four golds at a single Olympics only in 1984, winning the 100 m, 200 m, long jump and 4 × 100 m relay as well. Jamaica's Usain Bolt, the 100 m and 200 m world-record holder, won eight Olympic gold medals between 2008 and 2016 and is the most decorated sprinter in history. Kenya's Eliud Kipchoge became the first human to run a marathon in under two hours (1:59:40 in Vienna 2019, an exhibition) and won the Olympic marathon in 2021 and 2024 (Olympics.com, n.d.).

Figure: Le Miroir des sports. (1936, August 11). Jesse Owens at Berlin, JO de 1936 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jesse_Owens_%C3%A0_Berlin,_JO_de_1936.jpg

On the women's side, the American Wilma Rudolph overcame childhood polio to win three gold medals at the 1960 Rome Olympics; her compatriot Florence Griffith-Joyner set the still-standing 100 m (10.49 s, 1988) and 200 m (21.34 s, 1988) world records at the Seoul Olympics; Allyson Felix (USA) won 11 Olympic medals across five Games (2004–2020); and New Zealand's Valerie Adams won four Olympic shot put medals (two gold) between 2008 and 2021. Women first contested at the Olympics in 1928 (Amsterdam), with nine athletics events (Olympics.com, n.d.).
