Wallyball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wallyball — also called rebound volleyball, a portmanteau of "wall" and "volleyball" — is a fast-paced indoor team sport played on a racquetball court with a net strung across the middle: teams of two to six players per side hit a volleyball-sized rubber ball over the net, and unlike volleyball, rebounding the ball off the walls is legal and central to the game (Wikipedia, n.d.). It was created in 1979 by Joe Garcia, a racquetball teaching pro at the Calabasas Racquetball Club in California, who wanted to fill empty court time in summer; he formalized the rules, registered the name and founded Wallyball Inc. the same year, and the American Wallyball Association (AWA) was formed in 1979 to run US championships in men's, women's and coed divisions — an explicit gender structure, with the net set at different heights for men and women (Wikipedia, n.d.; Rules of Sport, n.d.).

Rules

Wallyball is played on a standard racquetball court 40 ft (12 m) long, 20 ft (6 m) wide and 20 ft (6 m) high, divided by a center line with a 3-ft (0.91 m) net hung across the full width; the net top may be no higher than 8 ft (2.44 m) in men's games and 7 ft 6 in (2.29 m) in women's games — the sport's explicit gender difference (Wikipedia, n.d.). The ball is a rubber sphere of 9–10 oz (255–283 g) and 25–27 in (64–69 cm) circumference, like a volleyball (Wikipedia, n.d.). Teams of two to six (the AWA allows two-to-four-player teams) get up to three hits to send the ball over the net; during those hits the ball may touch any number of walls on the serving side, but once it crosses the net it may legally touch only one side wall — never the ceiling or the opponent's back wall (Wikipedia, n.d.; Rules of Sport, n.d.). The serve is struck from the service area, may hit one side wall, and jump serves with legal spin are allowed (Wikipedia, n.d.). Scoring is rally scoring — officially adopted in 1989 — with a "freeze point" three points short of the game target, after which a team must serve to score; side-out and speed scoring are also used, and each team gets two 30-second timeouts (Wikipedia, n.d.). Ball-handling is stricter than in volleyball: hand sets are closely watched and the ball must pop cleanly off the hand; in 2000 rules director Steve Fuhrman worked with USA Volleyball to codify ball-handling guidelines that remain in force (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Joe Garcia devised wallyball at the Calabasas Racquetball Club in 1979 while looking for games to fill court time, experimenting with a volleyball on an empty court; by the end of 1979 he had formalized the rules, registered the name and started Wallyball Inc., and equipment makers (AMF Voit, with the "Official Wallyball" in 1981) followed (Wikipedia, n.d.; Wallyball Information Network, n.d.). The American Wallyball Association was formed in 1979 and has organized US championships for men's, women's and coed teams; the world's first official Wallyball Championships were sanctioned December 5–7, 1980, at the Rollout Racquetball Club in Northridge, California, with an exhibition match featuring basketball legend Wilt Chamberlain (Wikipedia, n.d.). Rally scoring was officially adopted in 1989, and in 2000 Steve Fuhrman codified ball-handling guidelines with USA Volleyball (Wikipedia, n.d.). A related Kansas game, Deckerball (created by a teacher and student Mark Decker), is considered a predecessor (Wallyball Information Network, n.d.).
