Golf Croquet

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Golf croquet (GC) is the fast, attacking code of croquet played between two sides — Blue/Black against Red/Yellow — in which each player takes a single stroke in strict colour order and both sides race to run the hoops in sequence; there are no bonus strokes, so every stroke counts and games typically last well under an hour (World Croquet Federation [WCF], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed worldwide by the World Croquet Federation (WCF), which maintains the Rules of Golf Croquet — currently the sixth edition, published in February 2022 — together with world rankings for individuals and nations and the World Golf Croquet Championship (WCF, n.d.-a; WCF, n.d.-b). The 16th WCF Golf Croquet World Championship takes place in Cairo, Egypt, from 21 to 29 November 2026, with a qualifying event in Alexandria on 14–18 November and an opening ceremony and practice day on 20 November, for a field of 80 players (WCF, n.d.-c). Golf croquet is played in more than 30 countries and has become the predominant competitive form of croquet in continental Europe, while association croquet remains popular in England, Australia and New Zealand (WCF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (World Croquet Federation, n.d.-a) (World Croquet Federation, n.d.-b) (World Croquet Federation, n.d.-c).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the players at the top of the WCF women's world ranking, which the federation publishes alongside the men's ranking — women compete on equal terms in singles, doubles and team events, as they did at the 2026 World Championship in Cairo (WCF, n.d.-a; WCF, n.d.-c). On the men's side the reigning world singles champion is Egypt's Mohamed Nasr, who won the 2026 singles title and captained the victorious Egyptian team in Cairo (WCF, n.d.-c).

Rules

Under the WCF Rules of Golf Croquet (6th edition, 2022), a match is a contest between two sides of one or two players each: in singles each player plays two balls, in doubles each player plays one ball throughout the game, and play progresses in the strict colour order Blue, Red, Black, Yellow painted on the peg (WCF, n.d.-a). A turn consists of exactly one stroke — there are never extra strokes for running a hoop or hitting another ball — and a side scores 1 point when one of its balls first runs the hoop in order, passing through it in the correct direction (WCF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The court is a marked rectangle of grass, 35 by 28 yards (105 by 84 feet) at full size, with six hoops and one central peg; the two inner hoops stand 7 yards north and south of the peg and the four outer hoops 7 yards from the adjacent boundaries, and the peg plays no scoring part — it is an obstacle and the colour-order reminder (WCF, n.d.-a). Hoops are contested in the fixed order shown in the rules' diagram: a standard game is a "best-of-13-point" contest that ends as soon as one side reaches 7 points, and if the score is 6–6 after the 12th hoop the game is decided by contesting hoop 3 again; events may also be played to target scores of 4 or 10 points, or as time-limited and handicap games in which extra strokes are used (WCF, n.d.-a). After a point is scored, any ball lying clearly beyond the halfway line toward the next hoop becomes an offside ball, and the opposing side may then direct that it be played next from one of the two semi-circular penalty areas centred on the halfway points of the east and west boundaries (WCF, n.d.-a). A ball that leaves the court is replaced touching the boundary where it went off; faults — touching another ball with mallet or body, striking the ball more than once, squeezing a ball against a hoop or the peg, or damaging the court surface — end the turn without points, and the opponents may have the balls replaced to their earlier positions (WCF, n.d.-a). Men and women play under identical rules, in separate or mixed events, and the WCF maintains separate world rankings for women and men (WCF, n.d.-a; WCF, n.d.-b).

History

Golf croquet developed from garden croquet in England around 1900 — sets of rules survive from 1902, and H. F. Crowther-Smith published "How to Win at Golf Croquet" in 1913 — and the Croquet Association formalized official rules in 1934 and instituted a golf croquet championship in 1935 (WCF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A parallel tradition grew in Egypt, where the game was introduced in the 1920s among British officials and rest-houses, spread to schools and clubs in the 1930s, and led to the founding of the Egyptian Croquet Federation in 1967; Egyptian players such as Khaled Younis — the first WCF Golf Croquet World Champion, in Milan in 1996 — and later Salah Hassan and the Nasr family made the country the historic powerhouse of the code (WCF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The World Croquet Federation itself was first formed in 1986 as a committee of the English Croquet Association, and its inaugural General Meeting was held at the Hurlingham Club in London on 15 July 1989 alongside the first World Championship (WCF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the WCF counts 12 Full Members, 7 Associate Members and further Recognized Croquet Organisations — Mauritius and the Netherlands joined most recently — and sanctions world championships in both golf croquet and association croquet as well as world team events (WCF, n.d.-b).
