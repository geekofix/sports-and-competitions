Hydrospeed

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hydrospeed is riding river rapids prone on a small foam board (a monohydrospeed), holding on with the arms and steering with fins on the feet; it is a recognised whitewater discipline alongside rafting and kayaking, with competitions on slalom courses and downhill sprint courses (IRF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are Riverboarding, Whitewater Rafting Race, Canoe Slalom, Freestyle Kayak, Playboating, Rafting; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Figure: Marcuscalabresus. (2011-01-29). Hydrospeed rider on a river rapid [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hydrospeed.jpeg

Rules

Competitors descend a marked course wearing a helmet, life jacket, wetsuit and a foam board (typically 60–90 cm) with handgrips; fins are worn on the feet for steering and propulsion. Events are timed runs or head-to-head heats on rapids up to grade IV; slalom formats set gates on the course. Men's and women's categories are run separately (IRF, n.d.).

History

Hydrospeed emerged in France in the 1970s–80s, when river runners began riding rapids on foam boards; the French federation organised the first competitions, and the discipline joined international whitewater programmes. Rémi Camus of France famously descended the Mekong by swimming in 2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
