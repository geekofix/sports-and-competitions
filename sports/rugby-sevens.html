Rugby Sevens

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rugby sevens is a variant of rugby union played seven-a-side in two seven-minute halves, administered worldwide by World Rugby. Conceived in 1883 in the Scottish Borders town of Melrose as a fundraising tournament, sevens has become one of rugby's most widely played formats, with a professional world circuit — the SVNS Series — a World Cup, Commonwealth Games inclusion since 1998 and Olympic status since 2016. Fiji and New Zealand are the format's historic powers, with the sport especially strong across the South Pacific, Africa, Asia and the Americas.

Rules

Sevens is played under the World Rugby laws with sevens variations: each team fields seven players (three forwards and four backs) on a full-size rugby pitch, and matches last two seven-minute halves with a one- or two-minute half-time interval (ten-minute halves are common for tournament finals). All conversion attempts must be drop kicks taken within thirty seconds of the try, and the team that scored kicks off again — a variation that keeps possession changing hands quickly. The shorter match time, open field and limited substitutions in squads of twelve create a game of extraordinary fitness, speed and kicking tactics, and drawn knockout matches go to extra time under the "golden point" style sudden-death rules. Olympic and SVNS tournaments use pool stages followed by knockouts, and the World Rugby Sevens Series awards points across global "legs" each season.

History

Sevens began on 28 April 1883 at the Greenyards in Melrose, when apprentice butcher Ned Haig and his master David Sanderson organised a sports-day tournament — shortened to seven-a-side and fifteen-minute matches — to raise funds for Melrose RFC; Melrose beat Gala in the final for the Ladies Cup, and neighbouring Border clubs copied the format, giving rise to the "Kings of the Sevens" circuit that continues today. The format spread slowly before the 1970s, but the Hong Kong Sevens, launched in 1976, transformed it into an international festival drawing the world's best players and huge crowds. World Rugby (then the IRB) staged the first Rugby World Cup Sevens in 1993 at Murrayfield — England won the Melrose Cup, since held only by England, Wales, Fiji and New Zealand — the sevens circuit became the annual World Sevens Series in 1999, the Commonwealth Games added sevens in 1998, and the IOC's Copenhagen session of October 2009 voted sevens onto the Olympic programme, where it debuted at Rio 2016 with Fiji winning the inaugural men's and Australia the women's titles. The women's World Series launched in 2012, and today the SVNS Series stages men's and women's rounds together at destinations worldwide, with sevens also serving as the game's main development gateway in new rugby nations.
