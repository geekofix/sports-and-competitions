Rugby Sevens

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rugby sevens is a fast-paced variant of rugby union in which two teams of seven players — three forwards and four backs — contest two seven-minute halves on a full-size rugby pitch, governed worldwide by World Rugby, whose Laws of the Game include the standard sevens variations (World Rugby, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It has been an Olympic sport since Rio 2016; at Paris 2024 the men's final at the Stade de France saw hosts France, inspired by two tries from Antoine Dupont, defeat defending champions Fiji 28–7, while New Zealand won the women's gold ahead of Canada and the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Rugby, 2024). The elite international circuit is the HSBC SVNS, rebranded from the World Rugby Sevens Series in 2023; the 2025–26 season crowned South Africa's men (the Blitzboks) and New Zealand's women (the Black Ferns Sevens) as series champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Men's and women's teams play identical laws on the same venues, with equal prize money since 2023 (World Rugby, n.d.-a).

Among current leading players, the women's game is headed by New Zealand's Black Ferns stars such as Michaela Blythe and the 2025–26 series champions, alongside France's Joanna Grisez and Australia's Maddison Levi; on the men's side, South Africa's Blitzboks and France's Antoine Dupont headline the SVNS standings (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; World Rugby, n.d.-a).

Rules

Sevens is played under the World Rugby Laws of the Game with published sevens variations: seven players per side (three forwards, four backs), two halves of seven minutes with a two-minute half-time — since 2017 finals are also seven-minute halves — on a standard rugby field up to 100 m long and 70 m wide with H-shaped goals (World Rugby, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring is the same as in fifteens: try 5, conversion 2, penalty and drop goal 3 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Variations include three-a-side scrums, conversions taken as drop kicks within a time limit, and the non-scoring team restarting with a tap kick; in pool matches a draw shares the points, while knockout draws go to sudden-death extra time, first score wins (World Rugby, n.d.-a). Tournaments typically run over one or two days with cup, plate, bowl and shield competitions so every team wins something (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). From the 2025–26 season the SVNS features eight core men's and eight core women's teams across nine events, the last three forming the World Championship that crowns the world champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Rugby sevens was invented in 1883 by Ned Haig, a butcher from Melrose in the Scottish Borders, who organized a seven-a-side tournament to raise money for his local rugby club; the Melrose Sevens is still played annually (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The format spread through the Scottish Borders and exploded globally with the Hong Kong Sevens, founded in 1976, and was added to the Commonwealth Games in 1998 and the Olympics in 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The IRB World Sevens Series ran from 1999 and the women's series from 2012; the final standalone Rugby World Cup Sevens was held in Cape Town in 2022, won by Fiji (men) and Australia (women), after which the SVNS champions are crowned world champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Retired icons include New Zealand's Ben Gollings and South Africa's Cecil Afrika on the men's side, and New Zealand's Portia Woodman and Australia's Emilee Cherry — Olympic champions — on the women's side (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
