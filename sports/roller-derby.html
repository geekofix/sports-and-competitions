Roller Derby

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller derby is a contact sport played on quad roller skates, in which two teams of five skaters race clockwise around an oval track while designated scoring players ("jammers") lap the opposing pack to earn points. The modern game is played almost entirely on flat tracks under the rules of the Women's Flat Track Derby Association (WFTDA), founded in 2005, which counted 451 full member leagues worldwide in 2020; men's play is governed by the Men's Roller Derby Association (MRDA). A full bout lasts 60 minutes.

Rules

A bout consists of two 30-minute periods divided into "jams" of up to two minutes each. In the dominant WFTDA ruleset each team fields five skaters per jam: one jammer and four blockers, one of whom wears a striped cover as the pivot. Both jammers start behind the pack; the first to fight through the pack legally becomes "lead jammer" and may end the jam early (by repeatedly touching her hips) — a key strategic privilege, since the lead jammer holds it for the entire jam unless sent to the penalty box. The jammer scores one point for every opposing skater passed on each subsequent lap through the pack, blockers assist their own jammer and hinder the opponent with legal hits — shoulder and hip checks to legal target zones above the knees and outside the back — while head blocks, forearm strikes and low blows are penalties. A "star pass" allows the jammer to hand her star cover to the pivot, converting the pivot into the new jammer. The WFTDA track is standardised with an inner circumference of 88 feet and an outer of 118 feet, players wear full safety kit (helmet, mouthguard and quad skates; inline skates are banned), and bouts are officiated by three to seven skating referees plus non-skating officials.

History

Roller derby was invented in 1935 by Chicago promoter Leo Seltzer as a Transcontinental Roller Derby, a Depression-era endurance skating marathon simulating a cross-country race, and it evolved under his and writer Damon Runyon's influence into a full-contact game with collisions, jams and later television stardom in the 1950s. The scripted sports-entertainment era of banked-track derby faded in the 1970s, and the sport was revived in 2001 in Austin, Texas, when women skaters founded Bad Girl Good Woman Productions; that league split in 2002 into the Texas Rollergirls, credited with establishing flat-track derby, and the banked-track TXRD Lonestar Rollergirls. The grassroots revival — with its DIY ethic and punk-influenced culture of self-chosen skater names — grew from 50 all-female leagues in 2005 to over 470 leagues by 2010; the Women's Flat Track Derby Association (founded 2005) standardised the modern ruleset, the Men's Roller Derby Association followed in 2007, junior derby programs emerged in the 2010s, and the Roller Derby World Cup was first held in 2011. WFTDA Championships, held since 2005 in various divisions, are the sport's premier competition, and in 2020 WFTDA membership spanned leagues on six continents.
