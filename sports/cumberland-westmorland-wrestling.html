Cumberland & Westmorland Wrestling

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cumberland and Westmorland wrestling (Cumbrian/Backhold) is the North Country style of England's Lake District: a fixed backhold — chest to chest, chins on the right shoulder, right arm under the opponent's left — from which wrestlers try hipes (lifting throws), buttocks (twisting throws) and clicks (trips) until one falls, loses his grip, or a "dog fall" is called. It is classed with Scottish Backhold as North Country/Celtic wrestling. It is practiced today at the Grasmere Sports (since 1852, August Bank Holiday) and other Cumbrian shows; costume is long johns and an embroidered velvet vest. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Cornish Wrestling, Lancashire Wrestling, Dorset Wrestling, Wrestling; the related pages are listed under Important Links (ResearchGate, n.d.).

Rules

Starting backhold: chest to chest, chin on the opponent's right shoulder, right arm under his left arm; the umpire calls "en guard" then "wrestle". Any body part other than feet touching the ground loses the fall; if both fall together, the last to hit the ground wins; an unclear fall ("dog fall") is void and restarted. Losing one's grip while the opponent holds on also loses. Championship bouts are best of three falls. Leg trips are allowed; no ground wrestling (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

The North Country style is associated with a long Celtic tradition (similarities with Cornish and Gouren styles; FILLC classes it as Celtic). Famous 18th-century wrestlers: Adam Dodd of Langwathby and Abraham Brown of Bampton — Britain's first middle-class wrestling hero. Chief historical bouts: the Melmerby and Langwathby Rounds, held at New Year and Midsummer. Today the annual Grasmere Sports and Show (since 1852, unbroken except world wars and 2020–21) hosts the main championships; postcards by C. G. Mason document the 1900 Grasmere championship (Wikipedia, n.d.).
