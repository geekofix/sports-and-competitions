Boule Lyonnaise

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Boule lyonnaise is the throwing variant of boules played mainly in southeastern France (the Lyon region), in which the ball is in motion when released after a run-up, unlike pétanque's standing throw. It is the French name of the international specialty "volo", governed by the Fédération Internationale de Boules (FIB) under the IOC-recognized World Pétanque & Bowls Federation (WPBF).

Rules

Under FIB rules, players take a run-up of up to four steps and throw the ball through the air from behind the throwing line (the C-C1 line) on a long lane (about 27.5 m) with side boards. To score a valid hit, the ball must first touch the ground within 50 cm of the target (the jack or an opponent's ball). After each throw, positions are measured and points awarded — one point per ball closer to the jack than the opponent's best. Matches are played to a set score (commonly 15-18 points) in singles, doubles and team formats, for men and women, at the FIB World Championships.

History

Boule lyonnaise developed in the Lyon area in the 19th century as a more athletic, run-up version of boules, distinct from pétanque's standing throw invented in Provence in 1910. The FIB was one of the three federations that founded the CMSB (now WPBF) in Monaco in 1985, and the international specialty volo (Italian for "flight") shares the same rules. Lyon remains the heartland, with thousands of licensed players and clubs.
