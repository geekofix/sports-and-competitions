Tomahawk Throwing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tomahawk throwing is the sport of throwing axes, tomahawks and hatchets at wooden targets so the blade sticks: from historical gatherings and mountain-man rendezvous to modern axe-throwing venues and leagues with standardized targets, tournaments and world championship events.

Rules

Standard play is target-based: throwers cast the axe or hawk at a wooden target (typically end-grain boards) from a set distance (usually 4 to 6 metres, or up to 10 for tomahawks), with scoring rings (bullseye) awarding points per zone and matches played to a set total or head-to-head throw-offs. The weapon must rotate a defined number of times to stick properly (the half-turn or full-turn rules by distance) and points require the blade to remain stuck; league formats such as the World Axe Throwing League's govern game types (including around-the-world and kill-shot variations) and safety standards (lane discipline, handling and prohibited trick throws). Historical rendezvous events also run double hawk and knife-and-hawk formats for style and speed.

History

The tomahawk was a trade and war implement of early America, and throwing it at a target evolved as a skill demonstration at historical reenactments and mountain-man rendezvous of the twentieth century, where black-powder gatherings standardized throwing distances and point rings. A modern wave brought the sport to the mainstream through urban axe-throwing venues (founded in Canada in the 2000s and multiplied across North America and beyond in the 2010s), and the World Axe Throwing League now sanctions tournaments, rankings and world championships, while knife-and-hawk games remain the historical community's expression of the same skill.
