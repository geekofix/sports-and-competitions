Tomahawk Throwing

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tomahawk throwing — competitive axe and hatchet throwing at a wooden target — is an urban sport in which throwers score points for sticks that land and stay in concentric target zones, with the highest total after a fixed number of throws winning the match (World Axe Throwing League, n.d.). Two international bodies govern the sport: the World Axe Throwing League (WATL, founded 2017), which describes itself as the global governing body with 19 member nations and 300+ affiliated venues in 20 countries, and the International Axe Throwing Federation (IATF, established 2016 in Canada, 20,000+ competitive throwers), each running its own leagues, national championships and world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; International Axe Throwing Federation, n.d.). Women and men compete together in the same open divisions under identical rules, and both leagues crown women's as well as men's world champions at their annual championships — the WATL's 9th World Axe Throwing Championship took place in Tulsa, Oklahoma, in 2026, and the 10th World Axe & Knife Throwing Championship follows in Appleton, Wisconsin, in April 2027 (World Axe Throwing League, n.d.).

Rules

The target is a wooden end-grain board (typically cottonwood, poplar or pine) mounted on a wall with the bullseye at roughly 63 inches; the thrower stands behind a fault line 12 ft (3.7 m) from the board for a standard hatchet and 15 ft for the two-handed "big axe" (1–1.5 kg blade), with lanes required to be at least 12 ft wide, 15 ft long and 12 ft high (World Axe Throwing League, n.d.). On the WATL target the bullseye scores 6 points and the rings 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 outward, plus two called "killshot" blue dots worth 8 points; a standard match is 10 alternating throws, the axe must remain stuck to score, stepping over the fault line before impact scores 0, and a tied match goes to sudden-death killshot throws (World Axe Throwing League, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). IATF rules use a simpler 5-3-1 target with a 7-point "clutch" that must be called on the final throw, matches run as three rounds of five axes with the first player to win two rounds taking the match, and only axes with wooden handles are allowed (International Axe Throwing Federation, n.d.). Practice throws are made on a separate board — throwing at the competition target before the event means disqualification — and both organizations sanction hatchet, big-axe and duals formats plus a separate knife-throwing circuit (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Axe Throwing League, n.d.).

History

Throwing axes and tomahawks at targets has roots in 19th- and 20th-century North American lumberjack competitions, while the tomahawk itself is the signature tool and weapon of Indigenous peoples of North America (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Modern competitive axe throwing was pioneered in Canada in 2006 by Matt Wilson and friends, who ran the Backyard Axe Throwing League's "Champions League" from 2012 to 2016 before founding the National Axe Throwing Federation in 2016 (Matt Wilson and Brian Simmons) — restructured in 2019 as the International Axe Throwing Federation, whose season culminates in the International Axe Throwing Championship and the Wilson Cup named after commissioner Matt Wilson (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In parallel, the World Axe Throwing League was founded in 2017 by representatives from Canada, the United States, Brazil, Ireland and Denmark, held its first World Championship the same year, put the 2018 edition on ESPN, created International Axe Throwing Day (13 June) and launched the sister World Knife Throwing League in 2021; the 7th Worlds were held in Tulsa in 2024, the 8th in Appleton in 2025 and the 9th again in Tulsa in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Axe Throwing League, n.d.).
