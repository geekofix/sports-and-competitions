Freediving

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freediving is the sport of diving on a single breath without breathing apparatus, judged by depth, distance or time held; it is governed internationally by two bodies, AIDA International (founded in 1992) and CMAS, which ratify world records and organize world championships (AIDA, n.d.-a; CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). AIDA's pool disciplines are static apnea (STA, holding breath), dynamic apnea with fins (DYN) and without fins (DYNB), and dynamic no fins (DNF), while the depth disciplines are constant weight (CWT), free immersion (FIM), variable weight (VWT) and no limits (NLT); CMAS adds constant weight without fins (CNF) (AIDA, n.d.-b). In 2026 the AIDA Pool World Championship ("World Apnea 2026") was held in Budapest, Hungary, May 31 – June 2, where Magdalena Solich-Talanda of Poland set the DYNB world record of 270 m on June 2, 2026, and the AIDA Depth World Championship took place September 30 – October 8 in Limassol, Cyprus — the fifth time Cyprus hosted it, with more than 180 athletes from over 50 countries — where Alexey Molchanov of Russia set a pending-ratification CWT world record of 137 m on October 9 and Zsófia Törőcsik of Hungary set the women's FIM world record of 108 m, beating Natalia Zharkova by 5 m (AIDA, n.d.-c). CMAS held its 2026 depth world championship in Roatán, Honduras, in August, where Kateryna Sadurska set the CNF record of 88 m and Mateusz Malina the DYN record of 326.5 m (CMAS, n.d.). Freediving has been a World Games sport since Chengdu 2025 (AIDA, n.d.-c) (AIDA International, n.d.-a) (AIDA International, n.d.-b) (AIDA International, n.d.-c).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Zsófia Törőcsik (HUN) — 2026 FIM world record holder and 2025 World Games champion — and Alenka Artnik (SLO) / Alessia Zecchini (ITA) at 123 m in CWT, while on the men's side Alexey Molchanov (RUS, 137 m CWT world record) and Petar Klovar (CRO, 137 m FIM in April 2026) head the rankings (AIDA, n.d.-c; CMAS, n.d.).

Freediving is the breath-hold foundation of the underwater aquatics — Finswimming, Open Water Swimming, Underwater Hockey, Underwater Rugby, Underwater Orienteering, Underwater Cycling — listed under Important Links.

Rules

AIDA competition rules govern safety and judging: in depth disciplines the athlete is pulled down and up by a lanyard attached to the wrist (except NLT, which uses a lift bag) and must surface with the tag, remove the nose clip and give the OK signal within a set time; white card = valid, yellow = penalty (e.g., early start, lanyard issues), red = disqualification (AIDA, n.d.-b). In pool disciplines, the face must be immersed at the start of STA and the athlete must remain at the surface after the performance until the protocol is complete; blackout or loss of motor control means immediate disqualification and medical attention (AIDA, n.d.-b). In CWT the athlete descends and ascends under their own power with a constant weight; FIM allows pulling on the rope; DYN is measured by horizontal distance in a pool with fins; STA by breath-hold time; records are ratified after video review and anti-doping controls (AIDA, n.d.-b; CMAS, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same disciplines with separate records, and there are junior (U18) and masters age classes at world championships (AIDA, n.d.-b). The central safety rule across all freediving is that no athlete ever trains or competes alone — a buddy system with a trained safety diver is mandatory (AIDA, n.d.-b).

History

Freediving's modern competitive era began in the 1950s–60s, when Italian naval officer Raimondo Bucher and Hungarian-born Enzo Maiorca pushed depth records, and the Frenchman Jacques Mayol (1949–1987) pioneered deep diving and yoga-based training in the 1970s–80s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). AIDA International was founded in 1992 by Roland Specker, Claude Chapuis and Natalia Molchanova to organize depth competitions safely; Molchanova (1962–2015), who disappeared in a training dive near Formentera in 2015, set 41 world records and remains the most decorated female freediver in history, while Austria's Herbert Nitsch set the AIDA no-limits record of 214 m in 2007 (AIDA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Other icons include Italy's Natalia's compatriot Alessia Zecchini, the USA's Tanya Streeter, France's Loïc Leferme and New Zealand's William Trubridge, who together with Molchanov set the modern depth records in the 2000s–2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
