Roller Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller hockey in its traditional form — also known as rink hockey, quad hockey or hardball hockey — is a limited-contact team sport played on quad roller skates, in which two teams of five use curved sticks to drive a hard ball into the opponent's goal. It is played in nearly sixty countries and governed internationally by World Skate, which stages the world championships as part of the biennial World Skate Games; in Europe the sport is run by World Skate Europe — Rink Hockey. A second major variant, inline hockey, is played on inline skates with a puck and rules closer to ice hockey.

Rules

Each team fields five players — four skaters and a goalkeeper — on a rink with a halfway line and an "anti-play" line painted 22 metres from each end wall. Matches consist of two 25-minute halves with a 15-minute interval, the clock stopping when the ball becomes dead; if tied, up to two five-minute golden-goal overtime periods follow, and a penalty shootout decides persistent ties. The ball is pressed vulcanised rubber, 23 cm in circumference and weighing 155 grams; sticks are 90 to 115 cm long and may not be wider than 5 cm or heavier than 500 g, resembling the curved sticks of bandy and shinty rather than ice hockey sticks. The ball may be moved only by the stick; playing it with the skate is a foul, and excessive body contact is prohibited, making the game closer in style to basketball or football than to ice hockey — indeed the goal area is a 9 by 5.40 m rectangle protected by a half-circle of 1.5 m radius from which goalkeepers play, and rosters run from a minimum of six to a maximum of ten players. Inline hockey, by contrast, is played four skaters plus a goalie on inline skates with a puck, in three 15-minute (or, at higher levels, 20-minute) periods, without icing rules and without body checking.

History

Roller hockey's roots in England run back to roller polo of the 1880s, and the first organised world governing body for roller sports, the International Roller Sports Federation lineage, published the first rink hockey rules in 1924; the first world championship of the sport was held in Stuttgart, Germany, in 1936, making rink hockey the oldest international roller-team championship. The sport took its modern shape in the Iberian peninsula and Latin America — Spain, Portugal, Argentina and Italy have dominated the world championships, with Spain winning 18 world titles, Portugal 16, Argentina 6 and Italy 4 — while England's clubs declined despite an early lead. The sport was a demonstration event at the 1992 Barcelona Olympic Games, and its world championship was integrated into the World Skate Games from 2017, when the former FIRS championships were reorganised under the World Skate umbrella; women's world championships and under-17 and under-20 championships have since become established parts of the international calendar. Inline hockey evolved separately in North America from the 1990s inline skating boom, developing its own world championships under the same World Skate structure and feeding off the ice hockey world — in the US especially, young players train on inline surfaces for ice hockey.
