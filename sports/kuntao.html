Kuntao

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kuntao (kuntaw) is the Southeast Asian hand-combat art practiced in Indonesia and the southern Philippines, regarded as one of the ancestral roots of Filipino martial arts (together with silat): fluid hand techniques, trapping, joint locks and low stances, practiced today especially in Mindanao and among Chinese-Filipino communities. It shares its name with the Indonesian kuntao school and influenced the vocabulary of kali/eskrima/arnis. Practiced by men and women, often alongside silat; national and international kuntao/silat championships are held. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Eskrima, Arnis de Mano, Dumog, Muay Thai; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Pekiti-Tirsia.cz, n.d.).

Rules

Kuntao has no single codified sport rule set; competitive kuntao follows the point-sparring rules of the national Pencak Silat/kuntao federations (Philippines: Sikaran? no — see Pencak Silat rules), typically three two-minute rounds, protective gear, points for clean hand techniques, and knockdown rules in full-contact silat variants. Traditional forms (jurus) are judged in forms competition. Weapons ranges exist in related FMA styles (Pekiti-Tirsia.cz, n.d.).

History

Kuntao arrived in the archipelago with Chinese and Malay traders from the 9th century onward; the 9th-century Tang trade and the later Brunei/Malacca sultanates carried Malay-Indonesian silat-kuntao into Mindanao and the Visayas. Spanish chroniclers described the fighting methods of the Muslim Moro south and the Christian Visayans. In the 20th century kuntao merged into the Philippine FMA scene alongside eskrima; Dan Inosanto and others popularized it in the West from the 1970s (Pekiti-Tirsia.cz, n.d.).
