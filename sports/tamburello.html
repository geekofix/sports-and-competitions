Tamburello

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tamburello is the traditional Italian ball game played with a round tambourine-like paddle (the tamburo): teams of five hit a small ball back and forth across a 90-metre field, scoring points when opponents fail to return it. One of Italy's historic pallone family of ball games, it is played in regional leagues and national championships, with variants including tamburello indoor on smaller courts.

Rules

The outdoor game is played on a long rectangular court of roughly 90 by 25 to 30 metres divided by a centre line: using the cord-wound tamburello paddle, players serve and volley the ball (a small, hard rubber ball) into the opponents' half, and a team scores the point when the opponents fail to return the ball cleanly, allow it to fall twice in their half, or commit serving or line faults. Each match is scored to a set number of points in a format similar to tennis's old-style games; teams of five (three in some indoor formats) alternate serve and court position as in the pallone tradition, the outdoor field offers walls in some historical formats (tamurello a muro) and the indoor variant substitutes a net and smaller paddles for winter play. Federations govern paddle dimensions, ball specifications and court sizes, with umpires calling lines and faults at every level of the sport.

History

The tamburello paddle traces back to the pallone family of Italian street games attested since the sixteenth century, which were once Italy's most popular spectator sport; the tamburello form itself sharpened in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries through organized play in Piedmont, Lombardy and Emilia-Romagna, where the Federazione Italiana Palla Tamburello standardized the modern game and its championships. The sport survived the rise of football by building a devoted regional base, and the twentieth century added the indoor and beach variants to keep play year-round. Today tamburello remains a living piece of Italian sporting heritage: national championship series run in several regions with dozens of clubs, the Italian federation stages cups in multiple formats, and the International Ball Game Confederation has carried the tambourine paddle to showcase events abroad.
