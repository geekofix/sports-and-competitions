Risk

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Risk is the world-conquest board game invented by Albert Lamorisse and published in France in 1957 (Milton Bradley in the US 1959): 2–6 players deploy armies on a world map, attack adjacent territories with dice, and collect card sets for reinforcements; eliminate all opponents to win. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Diplomacy, Stratego, Avalon; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Hasbro/Milton Bradley, n.d.).

Figure: K.olsen. (2010-05-29). Probability-of-conquest chart used in Risk analysis [Diagram]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Probabilities_of_victory_3_3.png

Rules

World map of 42 territories in 6 continents; setup by territory allocation; each turn: reinforce (cards + territory/3), attack (up to 3 dice vs 2) until resolved, fortify (one move); card sets (3 of a kind or one of each) trade for escalating armies; eliminate all enemies to win. World Risk Championship exists (Hasbro/Milton Bradley, n.d.).

History

Invented by French film director Albert Lamorisse in 1957 ; published by Miro in France; Milton Bradley 1959 US; the probability plot photo documents its math; world championships are held (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
