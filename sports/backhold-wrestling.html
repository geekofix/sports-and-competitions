Backhold Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Backhold Wrestling (Scottish Backhold) is the indigenous folk wrestling style of Scotland: two wrestlers grip each other in a closed "backhold" around the chest and shoulders, and the first to touch the ground with anything except the soles of the feet loses the fall. It is governed in Scotland by the Scottish Wrestling Bond and internationally linked through the Fédération Internationale des Luttes Celtiques (FILC, since 1985), which also covers Breton gouren and other Celtic styles.

Rules

Wrestlers take the closed backhold grip (right hand under the opponent's left arm, left arm over, chin on the opposite shoulder). When both hold firmly, the referee shouts "HOLD" and the bout starts. There is no ground work: the loser of a fall is the one who touches the mat with any body part other than the soles of the feet; if both fall simultaneously it is a "dog fall" and the fall is re-wrestled. A fall is decided by a majority of the referee and two judges. Bouts are usually best of five falls. Variants: sister styles exist across Europe — Cumberland and Westmorland wrestling in England, Cornish wrestling, Irish barróog, Breton gouren — with slightly different grips and rules.

History

Backhold wrestling has been practiced in Scotland since at least medieval times and was a staple of Highland Games and country fairs in the 18th and 19th centuries, with challenge matches for cash in music halls. It survives today at Highland and Border Games every summer, organized into district, national and European championships.
