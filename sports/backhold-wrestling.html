Backhold Wrestling

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Backhold wrestling — in Scotland also known as the barrúog (Gaelic for "back hug") — is a traditional folk style of wrestling in which two competitors stand upright, each gripping the other around the back with interlocked hands ("the backhold" or "the hug"), and the first to throw the opponent so that any part of the body other than the soles of the feet touches the ground loses the bout. It is one of the "heavy events" of the Scottish Highland Games and is governed competitively by the Scottish Wrestling Bond (wrestling.scot) and internationally by the International Federation of Celtic Wrestling (FILC), founded in 1985 (Scottish Wrestling Bond, n.d.-a; FILC, n.d.) (Scottish Wrestling Bond, n.d.-a).

A bout is decided by falls: a "fall" is scored when any part of the loser's body except the soles of the feet touches the ground, a "dog fall" (both shoulders down) ends the bout immediately, and matches are typically best of five falls or first to two clean falls. The traditional call is "HOLD" when the grip is taken, and competitors must start with their chins over the opponent's shoulder. Backhold wrestling remains a living Highland Games event at gatherings such as the Cowal Highland Gathering and the Braemar Gathering, where royal attendance is traditional (Scottish Wrestling Bond, n.d.-b).

The women's side of the sport is small but growing: women's backhold events are now staged at several Scottish gatherings, and the FILC sanctions mixed-national championships that include women's divisions in the sister styles (International Federation of Celtic Wrestling [FILC], n.d.).

Within the combat sports and martial arts family on this site, its closest relatives are Wrestling, Freestyle Wrestling, Greco Roman Wrestling, Arm Wrestling, Boxing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Under the FILC and Scottish Wrestling Bond rules, competitors begin standing, foreheads together, and take the backhold: the right arm over the opponent's left shoulder with hands clasped behind the back, the left arm under the opponent's right armpit gripping the opponent's right biceps or the backhold. Referees call "HOLD" and the wrestlers may begin. A fall is any touch of the ground by any part of the body other than the soles of the feet; a dog fall — both shoulder blades touching — is an immediate win; a slip where a competitor touches with hand or knee is a fall for the opponent (FILC, n.d.).

Bouts are usually first to two falls or best of five, on a grass circle or sawdust ring. Weight classes exist at some championships, but at Highland Games the open-weight format dominates. Competitors wear shorts and a vest (or bare chest in traditional dress events), wrestle barefoot, and may not strike, kick, gouge or twist fingers; dangerous throws are penalized.

There are no separate women's rules — women compete under the identical grip and fall rules, though women's events are often run at lighter weight classes at FILC championships. Youth divisions exist at some gatherings with modified grips for safety. Sister styles with nearly identical rules — Cumberland and Westmorland wrestling and Cornish wrestling in England, and gouren in Brittany — fall under the same FILC umbrella (Scottish Wrestling Bond, n.d.-a; FILC, n.d.).

History

Backhold wrestling is depicted on early medieval stone carvings, most famously on the 9th-century High Cross of Durrow in County Louth, Ireland, where two figures are shown in a backhold grip — one of the earliest visual records of organized wrestling in the British Isles. The style has been part of Scottish Highland Games since at least the 18th century; the Braemar Gathering, founded in 1832 and patronized by Queen Victoria, included wrestling among its events, and the Cowal Highland Gathering (from 1894) made the heavy events famous worldwide (Scottish Wrestling Bond, n.d.-b).

Figure: Thousand Holds. (2021, May 23). Backhold wrestling on High Cross of Durrow (Co. Louth, Ireland) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Backhold_wrestling_on_High_Cross_of_Durrow.jpg

The modern competitive era began in the 20th century with the founding of the Scottish Wrestling Bond and, in 1985, the International Federation of Celtic Wrestling (FILC), which groups the backhold style with Cumberland, Cornish and Breton gouren wrestling. Famous Scottish backhold champions include the 19th-century strongman William Donald and modern Highland Games athletes who also competed in the professional wrestling world. On the women's side, the first women's backhold events at Scottish gatherings date to the late 20th century, and the FILC now lists women's competition among its aims (FILC, n.d.).
