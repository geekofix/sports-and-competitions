Trophy Truck

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Trophy Truck is the premier unlimited off-road racing class of SCORE International and BITD in North America: a tube-frame, long-travel pickup-style truck with 900+ hp, built to race the Baja 1000 and desert events at over 200 km/h (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the desert/off-road racing family with rally raid and Ultra4.

Figure: Brian Snelson from Hockley, Essex, England. (2009-07-05 11:01). 2009 Trophy Truck at speed [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/2009_Trophy_Truck_-_Flickr_-_exfordy.jpg

Trophy Trucks (SCORE "Trophy Truck" class, formerly Class 8) have no body-shell requirement — a space frame with composite body panels — but must look like a production truck; suspension travel of 36+ inches and 40-inch tyres handle whoops at speed (Wikipedia, n.d.). Races are point-to-point desert races (Baja 1000, Baja 500, Vegas to Reno) with no closed course; crews of driver/co-driver race against the clock with chase teams (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Rules are minimal: safety cage, fuel cell, race seats/harnesses, window nets, fire suppression, and a minimum weight; engines are unlimited V8s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — female co-drivers and drivers have won class titles.

History

The class evolved from the 1970s–80s Class 8 "funco" sand rails and pickups; by the 1990s purpose-built Trophy Trucks dominated SCORE racing, with legends like Ivan "Ironman" Stewart (16 Baja wins), Larry Ragland (5 Trophy Truck Baja 1000 wins), and Kyle LeDuc (photo, 2009 winner) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today the class fields 30–50 trucks at the Baja 1000.
