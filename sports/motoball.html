Motoball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Motoball — also called motorcycle polo — is a team motorsport played on a football pitch: five players per team, four of them on 250 cc motorcycles and one on foot as goalkeeper, contest the ball with feet and machines toward the opponent's goal (FIM Europe, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed through the FIM and FIM Europe, whose Motoball Rules (Appendix 075, 2026 edition) define the game, and the annual Motoball European Championship for national teams is its pinnacle event, with France and Germany the traditional powerhouses (FIM Europe, n.d.). A match is split into four 20-minute periods, the ball is nearly 40 cm (16 in) in diameter, the field is about 100 m long, and the semicircle in front of each goal is forbidden to riders (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport is open to women and men alike; in 2012 Pascale Reschko Jacquot became president of the Commission of French Motoball and was later the first woman elected president of a FIM Europe commission, the Motoball Commission (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Under the FIM Europe Motoball Rules (Appendix 075), a team fields five players — four riders and one goalkeeper, who is the only player allowed on foot inside the goalkeeper's semicircle; a squad may have up to ten players, two mechanics and one manager, and at most ten motorcycles per match (FIM Europe, n.d.). The game is played on a football field about 100 m long with goals at each end; a match consists of four periods of 20 minutes. The ball is nearly 40 cm in diameter, and riders pinch it between bike and leg using specially mounted side frames on their 250 cc two-stroke machines (GasGas). Kick-offs, free kicks (opponents 9.15 m away), penalties and corner kicks follow football conventions adapted to the bikes; a goal counts only if the ball fully crosses the goal line and no attacker was in the forbidden semicircle; the team with the most goals wins, with extra time or penalties to break ties in championship matches (FIM Europe, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Riders wear helmets and shin guards against collisions, and dangerous riding is penalized by the referee. There are no gender restrictions in the rules — women's and men's teams compete under identical regulations (FIM Europe, n.d.).

History

Motoball began as an officially organized sport in France in the mid-1920s to mid-1930s, when motorcyclists began playing football on bikes on ordinary pitches, and it spread through Europe, remaining strongest in France and Germany (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Motoball European Championship for national teams has been held annually under FIM Europe, which publishes the official Motoball Rules, and the sport was included in the inaugural Goodwill Games in Moscow in 1986 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Archival photographs from 1950s Haarlem show Dutch club matches, when the game was already a well-established spectator sport in the Netherlands (Wikipedia, n.d.).
