Bed Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bed racing is a novelty team race in which a bed — complete with a passenger lying on it — is pushed, carried or ridden by a team of up to six people over a road course. The most famous event is the Great Knaresborough Bed Race in Yorkshire, England, held every August since 1966, alongside other races such as the Great Bed Races at the Kentucky Derby Festival in the United States.

Rules

At the Great Knaresborough Bed Race, teams of six push a decorated bed with one person riding on it from the start line through the town to the finish, with the river crossing included; the fastest team wins. Beds must have working wheels and brakes, and riders must wear helmets; teams also compete for the best fancy-dress prize. Rules vary at other events — at the Kentucky Derby Festival's Great Bed Races, teams race beds around a course at Churchill Downs — but the core format is the same: a crew propels a bed with a rider as fast as possible.

History

The Great Knaresborough Bed Race began in 1966 when the local Lions Club organized it to raise charity funds, inspired by a landlady's remark that the town needed better beds; since then it has raised millions for charity. Similar bed races spread internationally, including the Kentucky Derby Festival's Great Bed Races, which have been held for decades in Louisville. Today bed races are popular charity and festival events in the UK, the US and beyond.
