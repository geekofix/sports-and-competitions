Wushu Taolu

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wushu taolu is the forms half of Chinese wushu competition, in which athletes perform choreographed routines drawn from traditional Chinese martial arts styles — combining expressive technique, acrobatic jumps and weapon routines judged on the quality of movement, difficulty and presentation. Competitors perform barehand routines (such as changquan long fist and nanquan southern fist) and weapon routines including straight sword, broadsword, staff and spear.

Taolu competition is governed internationally by the International Wushu Federation (IWUF) with world championships and Olympic-code pathways — wushu featured as an Olympic demonstration sport at Beijing 2008 and Nanjing 2014 — and events for men's and women's changquan, nanquan, taijiquan and weapon categories.

Rules

Each competitor performs a routine of roughly one to two minutes on a marked carpet, combining required technical content with jumps, balances and linked difficulty movements; judges score execution (technique quality and errors) and difficulty points (the combined value of performed acrobatic and flexibility elements) plus an overall performance dimension covering rhythm, spirit and choreography. Routine content is specified by IWUF compulsory and optional-rules system, with time limits, carpet area requirements, deduction rules for mistakes such as stepping out of the area and falls, and scoring combining judge panels with video-review provisions at major championships.

History

Competitive taolu emerged from the modern wushu movement consolidated in twentieth-century China, which standardised traditional styles into a sporting curriculum in the 1950s and beyond, establishing national wushu competitions with standardised routines. The International Wushu Federation, founded in 1990, staged the first world wushu championships in 1991 and grew the sport to national federations across every continent, with sanda and taolu as its two halves. Wushu featured as an Olympic demonstration or branded support sport at Beijing 2008 and Nanjing 2014, international games and multi-sport continental events include it widely, and annual world junior and senior championships keep expanding the athlete base.
