Working Cow Horse

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Working cow horse (reined cow horse) is a rodeo and Western riding event that tests a horse's "cow sense" — its instinct and training for reading, separating and controlling a single cow — across three scored parts: a reined work pattern demonstrating the horse's manners, a herd work marking and cutting a cow from the herd, and a cow work boxing the animal along the fence and circling it both directions in the open.

It descends from the practical work of ranch horses in the American West and is celebrated as the most complete test of the stock horse, governed at the highest level by the National Reined Cow Horse Association in the United States with Snaffle Bit Futurity championship tradition.

Rules

Each competitor performs three parts in one run. In the dry work (reining pattern) the horse is judged on circles, spins, lead changes and sliding stops. In the herd work, the rider cuts a cow from the herd and shows the horse can hold it working on its own with minimal rein contact. In the cow work (fence work), the horse boxes a single cow against the short fence, then runs it along the fence, turns it back both ways, and finally circles the cow in the open field — scored for control, courage and correct form. Judges score each part on a points basis with penalties for errors such as losing the cow, visible rein resistance or a horse refusing to turn the animal.

History

The event grew out of the everyday work of Spanish-colonial California and the Great Basin buckaroo tradition, where reins and hackamore training shaped the spade-bit bridle horse — a descent traced to Spanish vaquero horsemanship. Competitive reined cow horse shows emerged over the twentieth century preserving that progression of training stages (snaffle bit, hackamore, bridle), and the National Reined Cow Horse Association, founded in 1949, formalised the event in the United States, with its Snaffle Bit Futurity for three-year-olds becoming the sport's marquee championship today.
