Bodyboarding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bodyboarding is wave riding on a short, rectangular foam board (about 40–42 inches / 100–107 cm long) that the rider wears with swim fins and holds with their arms while riding prone or drop-knee. It is governed by the International Surfing Association (International Surfing Association [ISA], n.d.-a), which runs the ISA World Bodyboard Championship as a standalone event since 2011. The 2026 men's world title went to Tristan Roberts of South Africa — his third crown after 2019 — secured at the Gran Canaria Frontón King / El Frontón in the Canary Islands in 2026 (ISA, n.d.-b).

On the women's side, Natasha Sagardia of Puerto Rico is the most successful female bodyboarder in history with multiple IBA world titles, and the women's division is a full medal event at every ISA World Bodyboard Championship; the 2026 ISA World Surfing Games in Surf City El Salvador (an LA28 Olympic qualifier) and the 2026 World Junior Surfing Championship there, both swept by Australia, included bodyboarding divisions (ISA, n.d.-b).

Rules

In competition, each heat lasts 20–30 minutes; riders are judged on the best waves (usually a maximum of 10 counting waves, best 5 or 6 scored) on a 0–10 scale per wave based on difficulty, maneuvers, speed, power and flow, with aerial maneuvers (air reverses, rolls, spins) scoring highest (ISA, n.d.-a). Riders must catch the wave in the take-off zone and ride it to the closeout; wipeouts and interference penalties reduce scores. Equipment rules fix board length, core and shape, and riders must wear fins; leashes are mandatory. Men's and women's events use identical judging criteria and heat formats; junior (U18) and masters divisions use shorter heats (ISA, n.d.-b).

History

Bodyboarding was invented in 1971 by Tom Morey in Hawaii, who created the "Boogie Board" — a half-size foam surfboard ridden prone; the sport exploded in the 1970s–1980s, and Hawaii's Mike Stewart became its most dominant champion with nine world titles and a record number of Pipeline bodysurfing/bodyboarding wins. The IBA (International Bodyboarding Association) ran the world tour from the 1980s, and the ISA took over the world championship as a standalone event in the Canary Islands in 2011. The 2026 title for Tristan Roberts at El Frontón, the wave that also hosts the Frontón King, continued the Canary Islands' status as the spiritual home of the sport (ISA, n.d.-b).
