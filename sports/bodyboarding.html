Bodyboarding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bodyboarding is a wave-riding sport in which the rider lies prone (or drops to one knee) on a short rectangular foam board and rides breaking waves toward the shore, using swimfins for propulsion. It is governed by the International Surfing Association (ISA), which runs the ISA World Bodyboarding Championship, and it is part of the surfing family alongside shortboard and longboard.

Rules

Under ISA rules, bodyboarders compete in heats of four to six riders, each with a set time (typically 20-30 minutes). Judges score each wave out of 10 for difficulty, speed, power and flow, as well as the rider's commitment; the best two (sometimes three) waves count. Riders must catch waves in the designated contest area; the ISA World Bodyboarding Championship crowns men's, women's and junior world champions. Equipment rules limit board length (typically max 42-45 inches depending on class) and materials.

History

Bodyboarding was invented in 1971 by Tom Morey in Hawaii, who cut a foam mat in half and added a nose grip; the sport exploded in popularity in the 1970s-80s, especially in Hawaii, California, Australia and Brazil. The first world championships were held in the 1980s, and the ISA took over governance of bodyboarding in the 1990s. Hawaiian Mike Stewart, a nine-time world champion, is among the sport's legends. Olympic surfing (ISA) does not include bodyboarding.
