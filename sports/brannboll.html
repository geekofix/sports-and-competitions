Brännboll

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Brännboll (Swedish for "burn-ball", also rundboll in Denmark, Brennball in Germany) is a Scandinavian bat-and-ball game similar to baseball and rounders, played at amateur level in parks, fields and school PE classes throughout Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland and Germany. There is no central governing body and rules vary regionally; an unofficial world championship, Brännbollscupen, is held annually in Umeå, Sweden.

Rules

Unlike baseball and cricket, brännboll has no pitcher: the batter throws or bounces the ball (usually a tennis ball) themselves and then hits it with a bat — a regular baseball bat or a paddle-like "klappträ" for beginners. After a valid hit the batter runs around four bases; fielders try to get the ball and either catch the hit on the full or "burn" the runner by touching them with the ball while they are between bases. A runner completing the full lap scores a point; a "free lap" (frivarv, like a home run) scores extra. If the batting team fails to get anyone home in a round, the teams switch sides. Local point systems vary widely.

History

The game has been known in Scandinavia since at least the 18th century and became a schoolyard staple in Sweden in the 20th century. The German school variant Brennball (played indoors without a bat, throwing instead) spread through PE classes. Brännbollscupen, the annual student tournament in Umeå, was first arranged in 1974 and is treated as the de facto world championship. Related games include Danish longball (rundbold) and Russian lapta.
