Volleyball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Volleyball is the team sport played over a net: two teams of six hit a ball with hands and arms — at most three touches per side — to send it over the net and ground it in the opponent's court, with rally scoring making every rally a point (FIVB, n.d.). It was invented in 1895 by William G. Morgan, a YMCA physical education director in Holyoke, Massachusetts, as a gentler indoor alternative to basketball for businessmen's classes, and the FIVB — the Fédération Internationale de Volleyball — was founded in Paris in 1947 and is today headquartered in Lausanne (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women's volleyball stands at the top of the world game: at the 2025 FIVB Women's World Championship in Thailand (August 22–September 7, 2025), Italy defeated Turkey in the final to win its second title, with Alessia Orro named MVP, while at the 2025 men's championship in the Philippines Italy beat Bulgaria for a record fifth title (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

The court is 18 × 9 m, divided by a net set at 2.43 m for men's competition and 2.24 m for women's — one of the sport's explicit gender differences (FIVB, n.d.). Six players per side rotate clockwise on service; play starts with a serve from behind the end line, each side may touch the ball at most three times (a block does not count) before returning it, and a rally ends when the ball lands in the court, goes out, or a fault — catch, double touch, net touch — is committed (FIVB, n.d.). Matches use rally point scoring in a best-of-five set format: sets are played to 25 points, must be won by two, and the deciding fifth set goes to 15 (FIVB, n.d.). The libero is a back-row defensive specialist in a distinct jersey who may replace any back-row player and cannot attack above net height; the FIVB rulebook governs rotation, substitutions (six per set), the attack line, the libero rules and the video challenge system used in international play (FIVB, n.d.).

History

William G. Morgan created volleyball in 1895 at the International YMCA Training School in Holyoke, Massachusetts, originally calling it "Mintonette"; the game spread through the YMCA network to Asia and Europe in the early 1900s, and the FIVB was founded in Paris in 1947, with world championships beginning in 1949 for men (Prague) and 1952 for women (Moscow) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Volleyball joined the Olympic programme at Tokyo 1964, and beach volleyball followed at Atlanta 1996 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The modern era has been shaped by the great national teams — the Soviet Union's men, Brazil's men and women, the United States women's dynasty of the 1980s–2000s and today's dominant Italian squads, whose men and women both won world titles in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
