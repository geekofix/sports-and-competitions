NASCAR

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

NASCAR (National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing) is the American sanctioning body and operator of stock car racing, best known for its premier NASCAR Cup Series. Founded by Bill France Sr. in Daytona Beach, Florida, on 21 February 1948, NASCAR sanctions more than 1,500 races a year at over 100 tracks in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Brazil and Europe. The calendar's centerpiece is the Daytona 500, "The Great American Race", first run in 1959. The other national series are the Xfinity Series and the Craftsman Truck Series.

Rules

Cup Series races are run over 36 points-paying events plus exhibition races, mostly on ovals (superspeedways such as Daytona and Talladega, intermediate tracks and short tracks) but also on road courses and street circuits. Races are divided into stages, and a playoff system (introduced in 2004 as the "Chase") determines the champion: a group of drivers is seeded into the playoffs and competes in elimination rounds, with the last remaining driver winning the final race at Phoenix (formerly Homestead). Cars are strictly regulated "stock" machines built on a common chassis with manufacturer bodies (Chevrolet, Ford, Toyota), and points are awarded for race and stage finishes. Safety rules include mandatory head-and-neck restraints, SAFER barriers and cockpit safety nets.

History

Stock car racing traces its roots to Prohibition-era moonshine runners who modified their cars to outrun police and raced each other on beaches and dirt roads, especially at Daytona Beach. Bill France Sr. organized beach races from the mid-1930s and, after seeing dishonest promoters, convened drivers and owners at the Streamline Hotel in Daytona Beach in December 1947; NASCAR was incorporated on 21 February 1948 and its first race was held at the Daytona Beach Road Course that same year, won by Red Byron. The first Strictly Stock race (the forerunner of today's Cup Series) was run at Charlotte in June 1949, won by Jim Roper. The series became the Grand National Series in 1950, the Winston Cup in 1972, and the modern Cup Series, with the first Daytona 500 in 1959 won by Lee Petty. Richard Petty holds the record with 200 Cup wins; Petty, Dale Earnhardt and Jimmie Johnson share the record of seven championships.
