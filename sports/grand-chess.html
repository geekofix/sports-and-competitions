Grand Chess

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Grand chess is the 10x10 variant (with chancellor and archbishop) that Capablanca preferred from 1929 and that Christian Freeling refined in 2002; the 10x10 board is used in modern grand chess with world championships. Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are Capablanca Chess, Chess; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Figure: Ihardlythinkso. (2012-02-01). Grand chess starting position diagram [Diagram]. Wikimedia Commons. CC0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grand_Chess_init_config.PNG

Rules

10x10 board; pieces as in Capablanca chess (chancellor, archbishop); pawns promote on the 10th rank; castling along the back rank with corner rooks (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Capablanca introduced Grand Chess on a 10x10 board in 1929; Christian Freeling's 2002 rules are the modern standard, played at international grand chess tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
