Duplicate Bridge

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Duplicate bridge is the competitive form of contract bridge in which the same set of hands (boards) is played by several pairs, so that the cards cancel out and skill decides the result (World Bridge Federation [WBF], n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the WBF, which organizes the world team championships — the Bermuda Bowl for men, the Venice Cup for women and the d'Orsi Senior Bowl for seniors — together with the quadrennial World Bridge Series; the 2026 World Bridge Series was held in Katowice, Poland, from 20 August to 3 September 2026 (WBF, n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in women's bridge — England's Samantha Punch, one of the world's leading women's pair players, pictured below, and Hungary's Rixi Markus, and — in men's bridge — the open pairs and national teams contesting the 2026 World Bridge Series in Katowice (WBF, n.d.-b) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (World Bridge Federation, n.d.-a) (World Bridge Federation, n.d.-b).

Rules

Duplicate bridge is played under the Laws of Duplicate Contract Bridge, maintained by the WBF Laws Committee (current edition: 2017) (WBF, n.d.-a). In a pairs match, each board is played twice — once by each pair in each direction — so that both pairs bid and play the same cards from both sides; the two results at each board are compared and converted into IMPs (International Match Points), and the IMP totals are converted into victory points (VPs) that decide the match (WBF, n.d.-a). At championship level the boards are computer-dealt and play is hidden behind screens, with bidding boxes and mandatory alerting and explaining of calls, so that no unauthorized information passes between partners (WBF, n.d.-a). Team events such as the Bermuda Bowl and Venice Cup use round-robin and knockout formats in which matches consist of multiple sessions of boards with carry-over scoring, and the Laws regulate irregularities, penalties, unauthorized information and the appeals procedure (WBF, n.d.-a). The contract is the level and strain (trump suit or no-trump) a pair commits to make a number of tricks; the declarer plays the hand for both pairs from the combined 26 cards, and the defense tries to defeat the contract (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; WBF, n.d.-a). The WBF runs separate world championships for open (men's), women's and senior categories, while most national and club events are open to all; men's and women's teams compete in their own premier events, the Bermuda Bowl and the Venice Cup (WBF, n.d.-b).

History

Contract bridge evolved from whist in the nineteenth century and was standardized as auction bridge by the American Edward Culbertson in the 1920s; duplicate formats developed in the United States in the 1920s and 1930s, with early showcase events such as the Vanderbilt knockout and the famous 1932 world championship match in New York between Culbertson's American team and the European team led by Austria's Karl von Lenz (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Bridge League was founded in 1932, and the European Bridge League, founded in 1947 in Copenhagen, dissolved it at its inaugural meeting; on 18 August 1958 delegates of the EBL, the United States and South America formed the World Bridge Federation in Oslo, Norway, electing Baron Robert de Nexon of France as first president, with Harold S. Vanderbilt named first honorary member (WBF, n.d.-b; European Bridge League, n.d.). The Bermuda Bowl, the men's world team championship, has been played since 1950, the Venice Cup for women since 1974 and the d'Orsi Senior Bowl since 2000 (WBF, n.d.-b). Icons of the game include the Italian Blue Team of the 1960s and 1970s — Giorgio Belladonna (born 8 June 1923 in Naples, died 16 May 1995) and Benito Garozzo (born 1927 in Rome) — who dominated the Bermuda Bowl for a decade; on the women's side the Venice Cup has documented elite women's team bridge since 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
