Baja 1000

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The SCORE Baja 1000 is a legendary off-road endurance race across Mexico's Baja California Peninsula, covering roughly 1,000 to 1,200 miles of desert, pavement and whoops depending on the course. Motorcycles, ATVs, cars, trucks and UTVs compete in separate classes. It is organized by SCORE International and is the flagship of the SCORE desert racing series alongside the Baja 500, San Felipe 250 and SCORE 400.

Rules

The Baja 1000 is a point-to-point race, traditionally from Ensenada to La Paz (the direction is occasionally reversed). Entrants must pass every checkpoint and time clock within the allotted time limits; missed checkpoints or late arrivals bring penalty time. Outside assistance is restricted in many classes — help is only allowed in designated areas or at checkpoints, and "pre-running" the course is forbidden. The overall winner is the vehicle with the least elapsed time, not simply the first to cross the line.

History

The race traces back to 1967, when Ed Pearlman rode a motorcycle from Tijuana to La Paz in under 28 hours, inspiring the first organized "Mexican 1000" run by SCORE International, founded by Mickey Thompson and Pearlman the same year. The Baja 1000 became the most famous desert race in North America and anchored the growth of off-road racing; SCORE was sold and the series evolved, but the Baja 1000 remains the crown jewel of desert racing.
