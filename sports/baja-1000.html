Baja 1000

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Baja 1000 is the flagship event of SCORE International's World Desert Championship, an off-road race across the Baja California Peninsula in Mexico for trophy trucks, buggies, motorcycles and UTVs. The 2026 edition, the 59th, ran from November 9–15, 2026 as the first-ever "Cabo-to-Cabo" course — entirely within Baja California Sur, from Cabo San Lucas to La Paz, 950.70 miles with four checkpoints. Bryce Menzies won overall in the #7 Trophy Truck in 16:28:29 after a speed-zone timing correction relegated Alan Ampudia, who had crossed the line first, to second, with Luke McMillin third; 279 vehicles started and 209 finished (75%) (SCORE International, n.d.-a; SCORE Results, n.d.). In the motorcycle classes, Tyler Lynn won Pro Moto Unlimited on a Honda — the 35th motorcycle overall victory of the 2026 SCORE World Desert Championship, which SLR Honda swept — and Joe Terrana took Pro UTV Open (SCORE International, n.d.-b).

SCORE International was founded in 1967 by Mickey Thompson and Ed Pearlman after Pearlman's 1967 solo motorcycle run from Tijuana to La Paz; the first official race followed in November 1967 (SCORE International, n.d.-a). Today the series also includes the Baja 500, San Felipe 250, Mint 400 and the NORRA Mexican 1000 rally.

Women compete as drivers and co-drivers in the Baja 1000, including in the top Trophy Truck classes.

Rules

Under SCORE rules, the race is a timed point-to-point event: the clock starts at the green flag and stops at the finish line, with mandatory speed zones through towns and checkpoints where vehicles must slow to the posted limit (typically 50 mph in zones) and stop briefly for tracking. Each vehicle carries a GPS tracker, and missing a checkpoint or exceeding speed limits draws time penalties. Classes range from unlimited Trophy Trucks and Class 1 buggies through production-based classes, motorcycles (Pro Moto Unlimited down to 125cc classes) and UTVs (Pro UTV Open, etc.) (SCORE International, n.d.-b).

Vehicles must pass technical inspection, carry safety equipment (roll cage, fuel cell, racing seats, harnesses, window nets, fire suppression, helmets, HANS devices for cars), and have a chase crew; motorcycles race with a support team but no pace vehicle allowed on course. There are no gender-specific classes — women compete in all vehicle classes, including as drivers of Trophy Trucks and UTVs — but there are age/ability-based classes such as Ironman (single rider/driver, no pit support) and the Sportsman divisions.

History

The Baja 1000 was born in April 1967, when Ed Pearlman rode a Honda 90 motorcycle from Tijuana to La Paz in 39 hours 54 minutes, and Mickey Thompson — off-road racing legend and tire mogul — challenged him to a race, which Thompson won in a Chevrolet trophy truck in 27 hours 38 minutes; SCORE (Southern California Off-Road Enthusiasts) was founded that year and the first official race ran from Tijuana to La Paz in November 1967 (SCORE International, n.d.-a).

Legendary winners include Parnelli Jones, who won the first two runnings (1967, 1968) with Bill Stroppe, and Ivan "Ironman" Stewart, who won multiple times solo without pit support. The race became a cultural phenomenon through the 1970s "Baja 1000" documentary films, and after a hiatus it returned in the 1990s under SCORE's reorganization. Women have been part of the race since the early years — Louise Slocum and others competed in the 1970s — and today female drivers and riders regularly win classes, with all-female teams competing in the top Trophy Truck ranks (SCORE International, n.d.-b).
