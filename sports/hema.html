HEMA

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

HEMA (Historical European Martial Arts) is the umbrella term for the reconstruction and competitive practice of martial arts from European historical manuals (fechtbücher), fought with blunted steel swords, rapiers, longswords, daggers and other weapons. The main international body for competitive HEMA is the HEMA Alliance, which runs the annual HEMA Alliance Tournament (the world's largest HEMA event, held in the US) with divisions for longsword, rapier, sword & buckler, dagger and others.

Rules

At HEMA Alliance and most tournament organizers (e.g., Swordfish in Sweden, Longpoint in the US), bouts are fought with blunted steel weapons under a ruleset such as the HEMA Alliance Rulebook: points are scored for clean, controlled hits to valid target zones (typically the whole body for steel, with afterblow rules — a double hit scores only for the first attacker within a short time window); matches are fought to a point cap or a time limit, with protective gear mandatory (fencing mask, gorget, gloves, elbow and knee protection, and chest or arm protection for steel). Judges and video review oversee exchanges; grappling may be allowed depending on the ruleset. Safety rules govern blade flex, thrusts and control.

History

HEMA reconstructs the fighting arts described in medieval and Renaissance treatises, from the German Liechtenauer school (Johannes Liechtenauer, 15th century) and the Italian Fiore dei Liberi and Filippo Vadi to the Spanish Destreza and English backsword; the modern revival began in the 1990s-2000s with online communities sharing translations of the manuals (e.g., Wiktenauer). The HEMA Alliance was founded in the early 2010s as a member organization for clubs and tournaments. Today thousands of practitioners train worldwide, with major events including Longpoint (US), Swordfish (Sweden), the HEMA Alliance Tournament and numerous European events. The related living-history groups practice the same sources without full-contact sparring.
