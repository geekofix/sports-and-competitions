World Indigenous Games

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Indigenous Games (Jogos Mundiais dos Povos Indígenas) are the international multi-sport event of indigenous peoples: the first edition was held 23 October–1 November 2015 in Palmas, Tocantins, Brazil (~2,000 athletes, 23 countries, ~50 ethnic groups; opened by President Dilma Rousseff; organized with the Intertribal Committee of chiefs Carlos and Marcos Terena), the second in 2017 at Maskwacis, Alberta, Canada (where the "Friendship Spear" was passed on). The programme mixes Western sports (football, athletics, swimming, wrestling, tug of war) with indigenous disciplines (archery with bow-hunting-fish, spear toss/chaza, corrida de tora, xikunahati head football, 100 m rustic race, traditional canoeing). The World Council of Indigenous Games (WIG), with Chief Willie Littlechild, now stewards the event (100+ member countries). Within the multi-sport events and games festivals on this site, its closest relatives are World Nomad Games, Arctic Winter Games, World Eskimo Indian Olympics, Dene Games; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Cultural Survival, n.d.).

Rules

Participation is by invitation/registration of indigenous nations and communities; each edition's programme is set by the host organizing committee and the WIG, combining standard sports rules (FIFA football, World Athletics sprint, canoe sprint) with indigenous-games rule sets (spear throw accuracy, log race, head-ball, rustic 100 m). Medal table by nation/people; cultural programme (body paint, dance, forums) runs alongside, non-competitive (Cultural Survival, n.d.).

History

Conceived by the Intertribal Committee (Terena brothers) and the Municipality of Palmas with Brazil's Ministry of Sports; the 2015 Games in Palmas drew 3,000 athletes per some counts and 30 participating countries in the wider events, covered by 300+ media outlets. The 2017 Maskwacis Games passed the Friendship Spear to Canada (Chief Littlechild). WIG was established in India as the permanent council (mastersindigenousgames.ca, n.d.).
