Static Apnoea

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Static apnoea (STA) is the pool discipline of competitive freediving: the athlete floats face-down on the surface and holds their breath as long as possible on a single inhalation of air, with no swimming (AIDA, n.d.). It belongs to the freediving family with dynamic apnoea and constant weight.

Figure: jayhem from Antibes, France. (2006-11-30 12:13). Herbert Nitsch at the Freediving Team World Championships 2006 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Nitsch_at_the_Freediving_Team_World_Championships_2006_%28317185613%29.jpg

The world governing bodies are AIDA International (founded 1992) and CMAS, which ratify separate world records under strict protocols — video evidence, doping control, and medical safety with a rescue team and oxygen at the venue (AIDA, n.d.). The AIDA men’s world record is 11 min 35 s, set by France’s Stéphane Mifsud in 2009; the women’s record is 9 min 22 s by Germany’s Heike Schwerdtner, set in Stockholm in May 2025, breaking Natalia Molchanova’s 9:02 from 2013 (Freediving Base, 2026).

Rules

Competition rules: the athlete may hyperventilate only with air, must not use pure oxygen beforehand (pre-oxygenation is forbidden — the 29:03 Guinness mark of Vitomir Maričić in 2025 was achieved with O2 and is not a sporting record), and the clock starts when the face enters the water and stops when the face leaves it or the athlete signals (AIDA, n.d.). Blackouts are disqualified and medically treated (AIDA, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate events at the AIDA World Championships, held in pool (depth disciplines) and pool-only (STA/DYN) editions.

History

Freediving as a sport was formalized by AIDA in 1992, inspired by the exploits of Enzo Maiorca and Jacques Mayol in the 1960s–70s; Natalia Molchanova (1962–2015), who held the women’s static record for years with 9:02 in 2013 and whose winning time once exceeded the men’s at a world championship, is the discipline’s tragic icon (Wikipedia, n.d.). Herbert Nitsch — the photo above shows him at the 2006 team world championships — holds the 214 m no-limits record of 2007 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
