Pig Olympics

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Pig Olympics is the popular name for novelty events in which trained pigs and piglets compete in races and games, typically as an attraction at agricultural shows and county fairs. There is no single world governing body; rather, a number of traveling troupes stage pig races at fairs across the United States, Britain, Australia and elsewhere, while events such as the annual "pig Olympics" in Russia have pitted piglets against each other in racing, swimming and "pigball" competitions, complete with an opening ceremony and the naming of a champion.

Rules

Because pig racing is an exhibition novelty, there are no unified international rules and the details vary from show to show. The most common formats are a short sprint, of about 100 yards, from starting gates to a finishing area where food is waiting, and a low hurdle race of about 50 yards over makeshift fences a few inches high. Trained pigs, usually of small breeds or young Gloucestershire Old Spot piglets, run in heats of three or four animals several times a day, with a championship round at the end of each show. Vendors and fair organizers assign the audience to cheer for individual pigs, and at some events spectators place small novelty bets. Animal welfare rules apply as at any livestock show: the pigs are trained from about ten weeks of age with food rewards, checked by veterinarians, and raced only a few times a day for short distances.

History

Pig racing originated as a novelty attraction at county and state fairs in the United States, where it has become one of the most reliable family draws on the midway. Troupes such as the All-Alaskan Racing Pigs, which has staged races in the northwestern United States since the early 1990s with 100-yard flat and hurdle courses, the "Ham Bone Express", and others travel the fair circuit with teams of trained piglets and hype men who assign cheering sections to the crowd. The concept spread to other agricultural shows in Britain, Canada and Australia. In Russia, an annual "pig Olympics" was staged from the mid-2000s, in which 12 piglets raced, swam and played "pigball" in front of hundreds of spectators. Novelty "Olympig" races remain a fixture of fair entertainment.
