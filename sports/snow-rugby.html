Snow Rugby

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snow rugby is the winter form of rugby union adapted for deep-snow conditions: full-body contact is softened, matches typically use seven-a-side formats, and players wear thermal gear and traction-friendly footwear on pitches cut into the snow. It is played in the world's colder rugby outposts — Russia, Canada, Finland, the Baltic states, northern USA, the Kashmir region of India, and Argentine ski resorts such as Las Le\u00f1as — usually as a festival or tournament game rather than a regular league season.

Rules

Snow rugby is essentially rugby sevens played under modified winter conditions: seven players a side, with the number of players, duration, rules of play and scoring generally matching sevens, but with adaptations — softer contact, shorter matches in extreme cold, and clothing suited to the elements (thermal layers, gloves and footwear chosen for grip rather than studs, since ordinary cleats clog with snow). A standard rugby ball is used, though snow and ice make it slippery; matches begin with a kickoff from the centre, and running, passing and tackling all require adjusted technique on the low-grip surface, with falls into deep snow making for soft but exhausting collisions. Tournament formats vary by organiser — from "20 Below Rugby in the Snow" sevens events to demonstration tournaments at winter festivals — with women's, men's and mixed categories common.

History

Rugby has long been played in remarkable cold: a New Zealand Ranfurly Shield match was played in snow in 1939, and a match in Russia in 1978 went ahead at \u221223 \u00b0C, while a generation of players in Russia's freezing league weekends made snow pitches a familiar challenge. Organised snow rugby as its own format, however, is a modern creation: India's first snow rugby tournament was staged at Gulmarg in Kashmir in 2009 (the format had appeared as a demonstration event in the 2008 Indian Winter Games), the 2011 Rugby World Cup in New Zealand featured the "20 Below Rugby in the Snow" seven-a-side tournament at Mount Dobson, and Russia hosted its snow-rugby championships in the 2010s — the Russian and Eurasian winter versions becoming the sport's strongest circuits. Today snow rugby thrives as a winter attraction and development tool: it is part of winter games programmes across Europe and Asia, draws rugby-playing communities outdoors for snowbound sevens, and serves as one of the most visible forms of rugby in cold-climate growth markets.
