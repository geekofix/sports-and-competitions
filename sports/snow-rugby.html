Snow Rugby

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snow rugby is rugby union adapted to deep snow: teams play on a snow-covered pitch where running and tackling are slowed dramatically and the ball is hard to handle, producing a heavy, physical, short-passing game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is played recreationally and competitively in Canada, Kashmir, the Baltic states, Russia, the northern United States, Finland, Argentina (Las Leñas) and Belgium (Boitsfort, Brussels) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The main organised competition is the Snow Rugby European Championship of Rugby Europe, first held on 21–22 November 2019 in Moscow with eight teams of six players: Russia beat Romania 4–2 in the men's final and Russia beat Norway 8–0 in the women's final, establishing women's snow rugby at championship level (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Other landmark events include the 2009 first snow rugby tournament in Gulmarg, Kashmir, staged during the 2008 Indian Winter Games demonstrations, and the 2011 "20 Below Rugby in the Snow" sevens event at Mount Dobson in New Zealand during the Rugby World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Finland plays a seven-player version. Among the current leading women, the Russian women's team won the inaugural 2019 European title (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Snow rugby follows the laws of rugby union — 15-a-side in summer, but six players per side in the 2019 European championship format and seven per side in the Finnish version — with the adaptations that deep snow demands: matches are shortened (typically two halves of 10–15 minutes), the offside line and ruck rules apply unchanged, but kicking is rarely effective and play is concentrated in close quarters (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Teams wear normal rugby kit plus warm layers; studded boots or snow boots are permitted depending on the surface regulations of the event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Rugby Europe's Snow Rugby European Championship is the organised continental competition, with men's and women's tournaments, and informal club tournaments are organised locally by rugby clubs and winter-sports associations such as A.S.D. Alp Rugby Tarvisio in Italy, which runs snowrugby.com (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). There is no single world federation for the snow variant; World Rugby's laws govern the adapted game (World Rugby, n.d.).

History

Rugby in snow has sporadic historical precedents — New Zealand's 1939 Ranfurly Shield match was played in snow, and a Russian international was played at −23 °C in 1978 — but organised snow rugby began in the 21st century (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2008 Indian Winter Games included snow rugby demonstrations, leading to the first snow rugby tournament in Gulmarg, Kashmir, in 2009, and during the 2011 Rugby World Cup New Zealand staged "20 Below Rugby in the Snow", a sevens event at Mount Dobson (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rugby Europe formalised the discipline with the first Snow Rugby European Championship in Moscow on 21–22 November 2019, where eight teams of six competed and Russia swept both the men's final (4–2 over Romania) and the women's final (8–0 over Norway) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Since then the championship has grown, with Baltics, Nordics and North American teams joining the circuit (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
