Rugby League Sevens

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rugby league sevens is a seven-a-side derivative of rugby league, usually a thirteen-a-side sport, played with the same fundamental laws but shorter matches and smaller scrums so that a tournament can be finished within a day. It is played by clubs and by social teams worldwide and is valued as a development tool for the sport's skills and for emerging rugby league nations. Its historical showpiece was the World Sevens, played before the NRL season in Sydney, and sevens remains a staple of festival rugby league from the Scottish Borders tradition of Ned Haig's invention to Australia and the Pacific.

Rules

Sevens follows the rules of thirteen-a-side rugby league with variations: matches consist of two seven-minute halves, squads total twelve players with unlimited substitutions, and scrums are formed from three forwards per team. All kicks at goal must be drop kicks, and after points are scored the non-scoring side restarts play with a tap kick. Teams are composed of three forwards, one half and three backs on a standard rugby league field, and individual national federations may adjust the international rules for their own domestic competitions, so local tournament formats can vary. Festival events such as the Orara Valley Sevens use knockout draws with national teams and visiting clubs alongside local sides.

History

Rugby league sevens traces to the very first sevens tournament of all — Ned Haig's fundraiser at Melrose in 1883, more than a decade before the 1895 schism created rugby league and rugby union as separate sports — and the shortened format was adopted naturally by the league code, where a famous 1933 sevens match between Australia and England at Roundhay Park in Leeds drew 80,000 spectators and English royalty. The first rugby league sevens tournament was played in Australia in 1961, and in 1988 the World Sevens was launched in Sydney as the code's premier short-format event, played before each NRL season with international and club teams; it ran until the mid-2000s, suspended in 2005 after NRL clubs grew reluctant to field stars and crowds dwindled. Sevens continues worldwide at junior, social and international levels — from the Orara Valley Axemen tournament at Coffs Harbour to Latino Rugby League Sevens — and is credited as one of the ways developing nations learn the game's core skills of open running, passing and tackling play.
