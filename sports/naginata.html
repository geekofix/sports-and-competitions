Naginata

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Naginata is the modern competitive Japanese martial art of the naginata, a polearm with a curved blade similar to a European glaive or halberd (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport form is called Atarashii Naginata ("new naginata"), codified after World War II by the All Japan Naginata Federation (AJNF), and it uses kendo-style protective armor (bogu) with additional shin guards (sune-ate) and a practice weapon with an oak shaft and bamboo blade (All Japan Naginata Federation, n.d.). Internationally the art is governed by the International Naginata Federation (INF), which organizes World Championships every four years — the 8th edition was held in Boulder, USA, in 2024 — and women compete on equal terms with men in the same shiai divisions (International Naginata Federation, n.d.-b). It is closely related to kendo and jodo.

Rules

Competition has two forms: shiai (free sparring) and engi (forms performed by pairs, judged on execution) (International Naginata Federation, n.d.-a). In shiai, two competitors in full bogu fight for a set time — three minutes at international events — and the first to score two valid points (ippon) wins; otherwise the three referees decide on overall merit (Wikipedia, n.d.). Valid target areas are men (head), kote (wrists/forearms), do (trunk), sune (shins) and tsuki (throat thrust); a point requires a decisive strike with the correct part of the blade, correct posture, spirit and a kiai shout, confirmed by the referees (Wikipedia, n.d.). In engi competition, pairs perform preset shikake-oji techniques or the Zen Nihon Kata, and judges award the win to the better-executed pair (International Naginata Federation, n.d.-b). Women's and men's competitors follow identical rules; the World Championships include women's shiai and engi, and age divisions from juniors to seniors apply equally to both genders (International Naginata Federation, n.d.-b).

History

The naginata was used by samurai and ashigaru infantry in Japan from the medieval period onward, with classical schools (koryu) such as Tendo-ryu and Jikishinkage-ryu preserving the art (Wikipedia, n.d.). After the Meiji Restoration and the sword ban, and again after World War II when martial arts were restricted by the occupation authorities, the modern sportive form Atarashii Naginata was codified with standardized bogu and rules; the All Japan Naginata Federation was founded in 1955 to nurture youth (All Japan Naginata Federation, n.d.). The International Naginata Federation was founded in 1989 and held its first World Championships in 1995, bringing together practitioners from Japan, Europe, North America and beyond; today the INF counts member federations across several continents, including the European Naginata Federation (International Naginata Federation, n.d.-a; European Naginata Federation, n.d.).
