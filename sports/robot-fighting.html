Robot Fighting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Robot fighting, also known as robot combat, is a sport in which teams build remote-controlled, armoured machines fitted with weapons such as spinning discs, hammers, flippers and flamethrowers and pit them against each other inside a bullet-resistant polycarbonate arena. Its most visible platform is the American television series BattleBots, while the British series Robot Wars carried the sport through the 1990s and 2000s; other organisers include the National Havoc Robot League (NHRL) and RoboGames. Robot combat is as much an engineering discipline as a sporting contest, with design, materials science and driving skill deciding matches.

Rules

When BattleBots was televised on Comedy Central (2000–2002) robots were separated into four weight classes, but since the 2015 revival the competition has used a single heavyweight class with a maximum weight of 250 pounds (about 113 kg) "ready to fight," with extra allowance — up to 500 pounds — permitted for walking robots to encourage design diversity. Matches take place inside the BattleBox, a 48-by-48-foot arena with active hazards and spiked bumpers along the inner wall designed to damage or overturn bots, and are decided by knockout, immobilisation or, if time expires, judges' decision. Fights are contested as two-minute (historically) or three-minute bouts in a single-elimination tournament, with strict safety rules: weapons must not fire entanglement devices, electromagnets are restricted, no EMPs/radio-jamming, no outside intervention after the match starts, and every robot must pass a safety inspection, including failsafes that shut down all weapons and drive on signal loss. Forbidden weapons include electricity, liquids, glues, nets and high-speed untethered projectiles by rule of most modern rulebooks.

History

Robot combat grew from early US events in the mid-1990s: the Robot Wars competitions founded by Marc Thorpe in San Francisco from 1994, followed by BattleBots (founded by Trey Roski and Greg Munson as a spin-off after a 1999 Long Beach event). BattleBots was televised on Comedy Central from 2000 to 2002, producing legendary machines such as the three-time middleweight champion Hazard, and the same era also seeded education: the BattleBotsIQ (later BotsIQ) school competition, using the 120-pound middleweight class, ran from 2002 to 2011. The sport went global through the UK's Robot Wars (broadcast from 1998), and BattleBots was revived on ABC in 2015 before moving to Discovery in 2018, with each season's knockout tournament crowning a new "world champion" of robot combat. Amateur organisations such as the National Havoc Robot League and RoboGames now give the sport a broad grassroots base.
