HEMA Longsword

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Longsword is the most popular weapon in HEMA (Historical European Martial Arts) competition: a blunted steel "federschwert" (approx. 1.2-1.3 m, ~1.5-1.8 kg with flexible blade) fought according to the German Liechtenauer tradition and other European longsword sources. See HEMA for the general rules, protective gear and major organizations (HEMA Alliance rulebook is the common standard).

Rules

Longsword bouts at HEMA tournaments follow the HEMA Alliance Rulebook or event-specific variants: valid targets are the whole body (some events restrict the legs or head), points for clean cuts and thrusts with control; afterblow rules apply (a double exchange scores only for the first hit within the ~300 ms window); bouts are usually fought to a point cap or a time limit, best of three, in a ring or on a piste. Thrusts must be controlled (no lunging with full force); grappling (wrestling at the sword, half-swording) is allowed at most events. Mandatory gear: fencing mask (with overlay), gorget, reinforced gloves, elbow and knee protection, and a padded jacket for steel.

History

The longsword tradition is documented in the German fechtbücher from the 14th-16th centuries — Johannes Liechtenauer's glosses (c. 1389), the Zedel of the Brotherhood of St. George, Sigmund Ringeck, and Joachim Meyer's 1570 treatise — codifying cuts, parries (the "hut"), the master strikes (five "meisterhäue" — Zornhau, Krumphau, Zwerchhau, Schielhau, Scheitelhau) and wrestling. The modern sport revived these sources from the 1990s onward (Wiktenauer translations), and longsword is now the largest division at the HEMA Alliance Tournament, Longpoint and Swordfish, with world-class practitioners such as the champions of the annual Longpoint longsword events.
