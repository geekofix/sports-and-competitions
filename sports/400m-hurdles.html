400m Hurdles

≈ 7 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 400 metres hurdles is the one-lap hurdle race of athletics: ten hurdles are cleared around the full 400 m lap of the track, 45 m from the start, 35 m apart and 40 m before the finish, combining sprint speed with rhythm and endurance. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic programme since 1900 for men and since 1984 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.). The men's world record is 45.80 s, set by Alison dos Santos of Brazil at the 2026 Weltklasse Zürich on 27 August 2026, taking the 45.94 s of Karsten Warholm of Norway (Tokyo 2021); the women's world record is 50.37 s, set by Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone of the USA in the 2024 Olympic final in Paris (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was headlined by Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone (pictured), the 2024 Olympic champion and world record holder, Femke Bol of the Netherlands, the 2025 world champion, and Jasmine Jones of the USA, who won the 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship in 52.45 s; the men were led by 2026 world record holder Alison dos Santos (pictured), Karsten Warholm, who ran a world-leading 46.61 at the 2026 London Diamond League, Rai Benjamin and Matheus Lima of Brazil (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

Rules

The 400 m hurdles is run in lanes around the full lap from a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks; a single false start disqualifies since 2010, and the finish is judged by photo finish when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-b). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

Ten hurdles are set 45 m from the start, 35 m apart and 40 m before the finish, at 0.914 m for men and 0.762 m for women; hurdles may be knocked down without disqualification, but leaving the lane to impede a rival is an offence (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Wind readings do not apply to the 400 m hurdles for record purposes, but the +2.0 m/s rule governs all track records up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Junior and para athletes compete with adjusted hurdle heights and in classification classes; a new 300 m hurdles event debuted on the international circuit in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

History

The 400 m hurdles has been an Olympic event for men since 1900 and for women since 1984 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Edwin Moses of the USA, pictured, dominated the men's event, winning Olympic gold in 1976 and 1984 and setting world records en route to 122 consecutive victories in finals between 1977 and 1987 (Olympics, n.d.). Kevin Young broke the 47-second barrier and set the world record of 46.78 s at the 1992 Barcelona Olympics, a record that stood for nearly 29 years until Karsten Warholm ran 46.70 in Oslo in July 2021 and then 45.94 s for the first sub-46 in the Tokyo Olympic final; dos Santos's 45.80 in 2026 is the latest mark (World Athletics, n.d.-e). On the women's side, Sally Gunnell and Dalilah Muhammad (world record 52.20 in 2019) paved the way for McLaughlin-Levrone, whose 50.37 s in Paris 2024 is the current record (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-e).
