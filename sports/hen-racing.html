Hen Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hen racing is a traditional British novelty sport in which hens race short sprint courses (about 15-30 m / 30 ft) in heats, encouraged by their owners with food and shouts. The "World Hen Racing Championships" are held annually at the Barley Mow pub in Bonsall, Derbyshire, every August since 1991, covered by the BBC and Sky Sports (BBC News, 2012).

Women take part as handlers and owners on the same terms as men.

Rules

Hens are released at a start line and race down a marked track (about 15-20 m) in heats of several birds, with a time limit of about 3-4 minutes to cross the finish line; the first hen across wins the heat, and the fastest hens reach the grand final. Hens must start with their feet on the ground; a hen leaving the track is placed back at the start. Fighting between hens is forbidden and can disqualify. Anyone can enter with their own hen or hire one on the day; pets (other than the racing hens) are prohibited at the event.

History

Village hen racing dates back over a century in Derbyshire, where neighbouring villages competed informally in summer. The modern World Hen Racing Championships were devised in 1991 by Alan Webster, former landlord of the Barley Mow in Bonsall, and have run every first Saturday of August since, attracting hundreds of entrants and coverage from the BBC's Countryfile and Sky Sports. The event has drawn competitors from Spain, Finland and the US, and a competitor once earned a Guinness World Record for training a hen.
