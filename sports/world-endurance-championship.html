World Endurance Championship

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Endurance Championship encompasses the top level of endurance racing for prototypes and grand touring cars sanctioned by the FIA, most prominently expressed in the FIA World Endurance Championship — a global series of long-distance races lasting from 6 hours to a full 24 hours, contested by Hypercar and LMGT3 categories. Endurance racing tests reliability, efficiency and teamwork over sustained stints, with driver changes, refuelling strategy and tire management deciding races.

The series' marquee round is the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the world's oldest active endurance sports car race, first held in 1923 and still regarded as the discipline's ultimate prize.

Rules

Championship events are staged over timed distances ranging from 6-hour races to the 24 Hours of Le Mans, with points awarded for finishing position at each round and title standings for drivers, teams and manufacturers across the season. Competition classes run together on track but are scored separately, each class balancing performance through rules on power, weight and energy use, with hybrid Hypercar prototypes at the top level and Balance of Performance arrangements keeping multi-manufacturer fields close. Sporting regulations govern driver stints and rest, fuel allocation or hybrid deployment windows, and safety interventions such as slow zones and full-course yellows, while technical scrutineering enforces the class rules at every event.

History

Endurance racing grew out of the interwar boom in sports car racing centred on Le Mans from 1923, joined by classic races such as the Mille Miglia and later Sebring and Daytona, and through the 1950s the world championship for sports cars formalised the discipline under the FIA. The modern FIA World Endurance Championship was founded in 2012 by the FIA and the Automobile Club de l'Ouest, unifying Le Mans and international rounds, first with the LMP1 hybrid era of Porsche, Audi and Toyota, and later with the Hypercar class introduced in 2021 that drew major manufacturers back to prototype racing. Le Mans remains the season's centrepiece, together with further-flung rounds in Asia, the Middle East and the Americas.
