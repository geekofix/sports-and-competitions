Schwingen

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Schwingen — Swiss folk wrestling, colloquially called Hosenlupf ("breeches-lifting") — is one of Switzerland's national sports alongside Hornussen and Steinstossen: two wrestlers (Schwinger) grapple in a sawdust ring wearing jute breeches (Schwingerhosen), and the first to force both of the opponent's shoulders to the ground while holding on to the breeches wins the bout; the sport is governed by the Eidgenössischer Schwingerverband (ESV, founded 1895), and its pinnacle is the Eidgenössisches Schwing- und Älplerfest (ESAF), held every three years (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the ESAF 2025 in Mollis, Glarus, 29–31 August 2025 — the first ESAF in the Glarus region, with 274 wrestlers competing in a temporary arena of 56,500 seats — Armon Orlik of Graubünden was proclaimed Schwingerkönig (wrestling king) and received the prize bull named Zibu; he was the first king since 1926 not to have contested the Schlussgang (final round) (ESAF 2025, n.d.; Schlussgang, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Women's wrestling has grown steadily in Switzerland: the Eidgenössischer Frauenschwingverband (EFSV) organises its own festivals, and at the Eidgenössisches Frauen- und Meitlischwingfest in Huttwil on 24 August 2025 Jasmin Gäumann of Bern was crowned Schwingerkönigin 2025, with Isabel Egli and Vroni Brun sharing the festival victory; on the men's side the 2025 king Armon Orlik (born 1995) heads the rankings ahead of Werner Schlegel, Samuel Giger and the 2022 king Joel Wicki (EFSV, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

A bout takes place in a circular sawdust ring 12 m in diameter; the Schwinger wear short jute breeches over their clothes, grip each other at the back of the belt and try to throw the opponent so that both shoulder blades touch the ground while the winner keeps at least one hand on the breeches — by tradition the winner then brushes the sawdust off the loser's back (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Three referees officiate, one of them standing in the ring, awarding up to 10 points for a clean winning throw; if no fall occurs, the more active wrestler receives the higher score, and named holds include kurz, übersprung, wyberhaagge, hüfter (almost identical to judo's koshi guruma) and brienzer (basically uchi mata) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At a Schwingfest every wrestler faces six opponents — eight at the ESAF; the two leaders after five rounds (seven at the ESAF) contest the Schlussgang, top finishers receive the Kranz (wreath), and the ESAF winner is crowned Schwingerkönig and traditionally receives a live bull as the main prize (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There are no weight classes — the sport favours tall, heavy athletes, typically over 180 cm and 100 kg, often craftsmen such as carpenters, butchers and cheesemakers (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's Schwingen follows the same ring and rules at EFSV festivals; because the competitive field is still small, the Schwingerkönigin is decided by the season ranking rather than a single final (EFSV, n.d.).

History

Wrestling records in Switzerland date back to the 13th century — a relief in Lausanne Cathedral shows the typical grip — and the Entlebuch pastor Franz Josef Stalder recorded early rules in his 1797 "Fragmente über Entlebuch"; the modern sport was institutionalised from the Unspunnenfest of 1805 on, and Schwingen became a Nationalturnen (national gymnastics discipline) at the Eidgenössisches Turnfest in Lausanne in 1855 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Eidgenössischer Schwingerverband was founded in 1895 and the first ESAF was held the same year in Biel (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Record crowds include 52,016 spectators per day at Estavayer-le-Lac 2016 and about 56,000 at Zug 2019, and the next ESAF follows in Zug in 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Kings of past eras include Jörg Abderhalden (ESAF winner 1998, 2004 and 2007), Kilian Wenger (2010) and Matthias Glarner (2016), all now retired (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
