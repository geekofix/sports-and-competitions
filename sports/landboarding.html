Landboarding

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Landboarding is riding a mountainboard — an all-terrain skateboard with large pneumatic tires and bindings — on asphalt, gravel paths and skateparks; the World Landboarding Federation (WLF) governs the sport and runs international championships with downhill, slalom, boardercross and freestyle disciplines (WLF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Speeds over 90 km/h are reached on steep paved descents, so full protective gear (helmet, slide gloves, pads) is mandatory at events; men and women compete in separate categories.

Figure: Thesupermat. (2009-08-22). Kite landboarding on a beach in Brittany, France [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kite_landboarding_en_Bretagne.JPG

Within the board-sports family on this site its closest relatives are Mountainboarding, Dirtboarding, Kite Landboarding, Longboarding, Skateboarding and Downhill Skateboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

WLF competition rules: downhill races are timed single runs with full-face helmets and slide gloves mandatory; slalom is a timed run through gates on pavement; boardercross is a four-rider elimination race on a course with jumps and berms; freestyle is judged on tricks in a park. Equipment: boards with trucks and 8–10 inch tires, bindings optional by discipline; categories by age and gender (WLF, n.d.).

History

The mountainboard was invented in the early 1990s in the United States, and landboarding as a road sport spread in the UK and Europe in the 1990s; the World Landboarding Federation was formed in the 2000s and the first world championships ran in that decade, with strong scenes in the UK, Germany, France and Australia (Wikipedia, n.d.).
