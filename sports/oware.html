Oware

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Oware (awale, awari, ayò, wari, ouril, warri) is the most widespread mancala game of West Africa and the Caribbean — the national game of Ghana's Bono and Ashanti regions and of Antigua and Barbuda — played on a board of two rows of six pits with 48 seeds (four per pit), usually cowrie shells or caesalpinia seeds. The competitive “abapa” rules are codified by the Oware Society (UK), which has run international tournaments and championships since the 1990s; oware is also a World Nomad Games discipline (136 athletes in 2018; intellectual-games category in 2026). Men and women play on equal terms, and the game is widely used to teach arithmetic to children. Within the mancala and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are Ayoayo, Dara, Kalah; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Players sit opposite and sow counterclockwise, skipping the hole they picked up. If the last seed lands in an opponent's pit making it two or three seeds, those seeds are captured (with chain captures backward if the preceding pits also end on two or three). A grand-slam move that would leave the opponent with no seeds is legal but captures nothing — and if the opponent's side is empty, you must make a feeding move if possible. The game ends when a player has no legal move; remaining seeds go to the other player, and the player with more than 24 seeds wins.

History

Oware is widely believed to be of Ashanti (Akan) origin — the name means “he/she marries” in Twi, from the legend of two lovers who played endlessly and married to stay together. Robert Sutherland Rattray, an early Western scholar, called it wari. It spread across West Africa under many names (awalé in Côte d'Ivoire, wari in Mali, ouril in Cape Verde, ayoayo among the Yoruba) and was carried to the Americas by enslaved Africans (warri in the Caribbean, ouril by Cape Verdean immigrants in New England). A commercial version, Oh-Wah-Ree, was marketed by 3M in 1964. Oware was a medal sport at the 2018 World Nomad Games in Cholpon-Ata.
