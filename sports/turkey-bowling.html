Turkey Bowling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Turkey bowling is the supermarket and holiday tradition that turns a frozen turkey into a bowling ball: participants slide a frozen bird down a supermarket aisle (or a hockey ice surface or party room lane) at a set of plastic bottles stacked as pins, and score as in ten-pin bowling.

Rules

The setup is deliberately improvised but constant in its elements: ten bottles (commonly one-litre soda bottles, partly filled for stability) are arranged in the familiar ten-pin triangle at the end of a "lane" (a smooth aisle, gym floor or ice surface), and bowlers slide a frozen turkey (typically a 5-kilogram-class bird, still frozen and wrapped) from a throwing line. Players take their turns in bowling-style frames (adapted to two or three throws each) and are scored by the number of bottles knocked down, with the highest total after the agreed frames winning; house rules govern sliding technique (no throwing, the bird must slide), lane boundaries and resets, and events are staged at supermarket holiday promotions, ice-rink intermissions and office or community parties.

History

Turkey bowling appears to have been born in American supermarket aisles in the second half of the twentieth century, a stock-clerk innovation at holiday time whose grainy era of local-news segments and store promotions made it folk-famous; the activity spread as a Thanksgiving-season staple of TV news, office parties and charity events, and staged exhibitions (including hockey intermission turkey bowling on ice with frozen birds) have kept the game an annual holiday joke-sport. It has never organized into a serious competition structure: the sport lives in its seasonal, improvised form, with occasional attempts at world records and town tournaments part of the general landscape of holiday novelty games.
