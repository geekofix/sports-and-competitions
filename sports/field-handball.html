Field Handball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Field handball (German: Feldhandball) is the original outdoor form of handball: two teams of eleven players play on a grass pitch roughly the size of a football field, throwing a ball by hand into a large football-style goal, with only the goalkeeper allowed inside the goal area (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was an Olympic sport exactly once, at the Berlin 1936 Summer Olympics, where six nations competed and Germany won the gold medal, and it was a demonstration sport at Helsinki 1952 (Olympics.com, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the game is governed within the International Handball Federation (IHF, founded 1946), which organizes the modern successors — indoor Handball and Beach Handball — including the 2026 IHF Men's and Women's Beach Handball World Championships in Zagreb, Croatia, 23–28 June 2026, won by Germany (men) and Argentina (women) (IHF, n.d.-a).

At the 2026 Beach Handball World Championships in Zagreb, the leading women's team was Argentina under coach Leticia Brunati, who won their first world title by defeating Denmark in the final and became only the third nation after Brazil and Germany to hold the world, continental and World Games titles at once; on the men's side Germany, coached by Marten Franke, beat Brazil in the final with Argentina taking bronze (IHF, n.d.-a).

Rules

Field handball is played 11 against 11 (plus two reserves) on a grass field of 90–110 m × 55–65 m with goals of 7.32 m × 2.44 m, the same dimensions as a football goal; matches last two halves of 30 minutes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two parallel lines 35 m from each goal line divide the field into three sections, and no more than six players of a team may stand in any one section (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The goal area is a semicircle of 13 m radius in front of each goal, and only the goalkeeper may stand inside it; a player may not receive or pass the ball inside the opponent's goal area, and a foul there is punished with a penalty throw from the mark 14 m in front of the goal (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Players may run with the ball for up to three steps, may hold it for three seconds, and must pass, dribble or shoot; double dribble, travelling and holding an opponent are fouls punished with free throws (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Throw-ins, goal kicks and corner throws restart play after the ball crosses a boundary or the goal line, and the offside rule of the 1936 Olympic tournament was abolished in later revisions of the outdoor rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women played under the same rules; women's field handball existed at club and national-team level, although the Olympic tournament of 1936 was men-only, and the women's game is today carried on mainly in indoor handball and beach handball, which are played under the IHF rules with full gender parity (IHF, n.d.-b).

History

The modern rules of field handball were published on 29 October 1917 by the German gymnastics teachers Karl Schelenz, Max Heiser and Erich Konigh, who combined elements of the Danish and German ball games into a fast team game for eleven players; the rules were designed for the training of soldiers and quickly spread through German schools and clubs (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Amateur Handball Federation (IAHF) was founded on 11 July 1928 in Amsterdam by eleven nations, with the American Avery Brundage and the Finn Einar Pihkala among the driving forces, and the first men's outdoor World Championship was held in 1938 in Germany, which won the title on home soil (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the Berlin 1936 Olympics the tournament was played 11-a-side on grass fields with six teams, and hosts Germany defeated Austria in the final; after a demonstration appearance in 1952 the outdoor game disappeared from the Olympic program, while indoor handball, invented in Scandinavia and Germany in the 1950s–60s, became the Olympic discipline from Munich 1972 (men) and Montreal 1976 (women) (Olympics.com, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The IAHF was reconstituted as the International Handball Federation (IHF) on 11–13 July 1946 and today governs handball, beach handball and wheelchair handball worldwide (IHF, n.d.-b).
