Trampoline Dodgeball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Trampoline dodgeball — branded Ultimate Dodgeball or "3-D Dodgeball" by its creator — is dodgeball played on a court whose floor and surrounding walls are all trampolines: two teams of five bounce on the rebounding surfaces and run on the angled walls (set at roughly 50 degrees) while throwing soft foam balls, eliminating opponents with a direct hit, while a clean catch eliminates the thrower (Wikipedia, n.d.; WBUR, 2012). The game was created around 2004 by Rick Platt, an entrepreneur who founded the Sky Zone trampoline-park chain, opening its first park in Las Vegas in 2004 with a 17,000 sq ft (1,600 m²) arena built for US$2 million (Wikipedia, n.d.). Sky Zone organized the first Ultimate Dodgeball events in 2011 and the first national championship in Los Angeles on 9–12 November 2012 with a $25,000 prize; a league championship series followed from 2015 under a dedicated director of leagues (WBUR, 2012; Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches last four minutes, going to sudden death if both teams still have players standing, and the game is played in park leagues and tournaments worldwide — including mixed-gender and women's divisions — with the World Dodgeball Federation and USA Dodgeball also sanctioning events at trampoline parks (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Dodgeball Federation, n.d.). There is no single world federation for the trampoline variant itself; it is governed by the park operators and the general dodgeball bodies (World Dodgeball Federation, n.d.).

Rules

Play follows classic dodgeball elimination adapted to the rebound court: teams of five start on opposite sides, contest the center balls in an opening rush, and may hold only one ball at a time; a player is eliminated by being hit with a thrown ball (headshots are excluded or eject the thrower, depending on the rule set) or by having their throw caught, and the game is won by eliminating the entire opposing team (Wikipedia, n.d.). Trampoline-dodgeball matches are four minutes long, with sudden death — first elimination — if time expires with both teams alive (WBUR, 2012). The rebound surface changes the tactics: players leap several metres to throw, dodge by bouncing, and run up the angled walls, so venue rules govern safe jumping conduct, no intentional head-hunting throws and boundary play on the wall-to-wall courts (WBUR, 2012). Park venues additionally enforce jump-safety standards — grip socks, court supervision and single-jumper rules on unrestrained surfaces — under the International Association of Trampoline Parks (IATP) guidelines, and the World Dodgeball Federation publishes court adaptations for trampoline-park tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Dodgeball Federation, n.d.). Women's, mixed and junior divisions follow the same elimination rules with league-adapted team sizes and match lengths (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

The game grew out of the commercial trampoline-park boom that began in 2004, when Rick Platt opened the first Sky Zone park in Las Vegas and conceived a team sport for its patchwork trampoline arena — "3-D Dodgeball," first played around 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.; WBUR, 2012). Sky Zone organized Ultimate Dodgeball competitions from 2011, and the first national Ultimate Trampoline Dodgeball Championship was held in Los Angeles on 9–12 November 2012 with a $25,000 prize, attracting teams from across the United States (WBUR, 2012). In May 2015 Sky Zone hired a Director of Leagues and launched the Ultimate Trampoline Dodgeball Championship Series as a franchise league across its parks, and the activity became one of the signature offerings of trampoline parks worldwide, alongside freestyle jumping, foam pits and basketball (Wikipedia, n.d.). Mainstream dodgeball bodies followed suit: the World Dodgeball Federation, formed in 2011 and running the Dodgeball World Championship since its first edition in Kuala Lumpur in 2012, and USA Dodgeball now hold tournaments at trampoline parks (World Dodgeball Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The trampoline variant remains a venue-based recreational league sport rather than a federation-governed international one (Wikipedia, n.d.).
