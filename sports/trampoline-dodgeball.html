Trampoline Dodgeball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Trampoline dodgeball is the Dodgeball variant on courts of connected trampolines: two teams bounce on the rebounding floor and walls of a purpose-built arena, throwing soft balls to eliminate opponents by hit or catch while flying. A creation of the commercial trampoline-park era of the 2010s, it is played recreationally worldwide and in organized local leagues and tournaments at park venues.

Rules

Standard play follows dodgeball's classic elimination logic, adapted to the trampoline court: teams start with an equal number of players on separate sides of the arena, soft foam or fabric-covered balls are released (a center rush or initial throw-off gives opening possession), and players eliminate opponents by hitting them with a thrown ball (headshots generally excluded) while a clean catch eliminates the thrower. The trampoline surface changes the game: players can leap to throw and dodge, and court rules govern safe jumping conduct, no intentional head-hunting throws, and boundary/out rules on the wall-to-wall courts. Games play to elimination or a time limit, with leagues adapting rules for juniors, mixed play and park formats, and venues typically enforce jump-safety standards (grip socks, court supervision, single-bounce restraint zones) alongside the game rules.

History

The sport grew directly from the commercial trampoline park boom of the 2010s (the first large-format parks opened in the late 2000s and multiplied worldwide), when park operators sought structured games for their wall-to-wall rebound courts and dodgeball's simple elimination format proved the natural fit. Leagues, tournaments and corporate team-building formats followed, and the activity became one of the signature trampoline-park offerings alongside freestyle jumping, with dodgeball courts now standard at parks across the world. The game remains a recreational and venue-based discipline rather than an international federation sport, contested in local leagues and park tournaments with a devoted subculture.
