Kho Kho

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kho kho is a traditional Indian tag game in which a chaser (wazir) pursues defenders who try to evade being tagged; the name comes from the cry "kho!" shouted when a defender is touched (International Kho Kho Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game is played on a rectangular court of 29 × 16 m with a central lane and eight cross lanes at right angles, marked by poles 120 cm high at each end, with 12 players per side — nine active and three substitutes (Kho Kho Federation of India, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match consists of two innings, each side taking turns of seven minutes as chaser and defender, and every tag scores one point (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Kho Kho Federation (IKKF) governs the sport globally and, with the Kho Kho Federation of India (KKFI), staged the inaugural Kho Kho World Cup at the Indira Gandhi Indoor Stadium in New Delhi, 13–19 January 2025, with 23 nations fielding 20 men's and 19 women's teams in a seven-a-side fast format (International Kho Kho Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the top of the women's game stand the 2025 World Cup champions of India, captained by Priyanka Ingle and including star raider Nasreen Shaikh, who received the Arjuna Award in 2023; India beat Nepal 78–40 in the women's final, while the men's team of captain Pratik Waikar beat Nepal 54–36 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the IKKF/KKFI rules, the court is 29 × 16 m with a central lane about 23.5–24 m long and 30 cm wide, crossed at right angles by eight cross lanes; at each end of the central lane stands a pole 120 cm high, with a free zone of 1.5 m behind each pole (Kho Kho Federation of India, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each team fields 12 players, of whom nine are active: one chases (wazir) while eight defenders sit in the cross lanes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The wazir enters the opponents' half, tags a defender between the shoulder and the waist while shouting "kho," and the tagged defender leaves the field; the wazir then passes to the next seated defender of his team by touching his back, and play continues until the seven-minute turn expires (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each tag scores one point for the chasing side; a match has two innings in which each side chases and defends in alternating turns, with a two-minute break between turns and a three-minute break between innings (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Fouls include the wazir crossing the central lane, defenders leaving their cross lane, and wrong-direction play, which give the opposing side points or free tags (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The professional Ultimate Kho Kho (UKK) league, launched in India in 2022, plays a faster seven-a-side format with power plays during which two wazirs chase simultaneously (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's kho kho are played under the same rules, with national championships for men, women and juniors in India and separate men's and women's World Cups (International Kho Kho Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rule source: International Kho Kho Federation, https://www.khokho.net/ (International Kho Kho Federation, n.d.).

History

Kho kho's origins are traced to the ancient chariot game rathera, and the modern rules were standardized in 1914 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game was demonstrated at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, and the Asian Kho Kho Championships were first held in Kolkata in 1996, with editions in Dhaka (2000), Indore (2016) and Assam (2023) — India won every edition (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Kho Kho Federation of India (KKFI) was founded in 1955 and held the first National Championship in Vijayawada in 1959; the 58th Senior National Championship took place in Kazipet in January 2026 (Kho Kho Federation of India, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Ultimate Kho Kho franchise league debuted in 2022, and the inaugural Kho Kho World Cup in New Delhi in January 2025 made India the first men's and women's world champion, beating Nepal in both finals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). India also hosted the first Commonwealth Kho Kho Championship in March 2026, and the next World Cup is scheduled for Birmingham, England, in 2027 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons of the modern women's game include Nasreen Shaikh, Arjuna Award winner 2023 and 2025 World Cup champion, and captain Priyanka Ingle (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
