Kho Kho

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kho Kho is India's pursuit team sport: on a rectangular court, two teams of nine alternate between chasing and running, with chasers tagging runners in the shortest time while the runners dodge and weave around the field. The chaser who tags must shout kho and pass the pursuit to a teammate sitting in the centre lane, giving the game its distinctive relay-of-chasing rhythm.

The sport is governed in India by the Kho Kho Federation of India, featured at national games, and professionalised by the Ultimate Kho Kho league; internationally, South Asian and Asian federation championships bring together a growing field of nations.

Rules

Matches consist of two innings, with each team alternating attacking (chasing) and defending (running) turns: chasers line up on the centre lane between stanchions and may pursue in one direction around the field in the central lane structure, only one chaser active at a time; the active chaser may go in any direction exiting the lane, but the chase passes to the next chaser with a kho while touching the seated teammate's back from behind. Runners enter the field in batches of three, scoring for how long they survive untagged; each team alternates chasing and running turns of a set length, and the team with the better tag and survival totals wins.

History

Kho Kho comes from the Indian tradition of running and tagging games, formalized with rules in the 20th century in Maharashtra gymnasiums and taken up nationally through school sports and state associations. The Kho Kho Federation of India organised national championships from the mid-20th century, the game entered the South Asian Games, and Asian kho kho championships emerged. In 2022 the Ultimate Kho Kho league launched with franchise teams, television coverage and indoor mats, professionalizing the sport and boosting its profile among India's traditional games.
