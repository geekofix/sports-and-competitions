Artistic Swimming

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Artistic Swimming (formerly synchronized swimming) is an aquatic discipline in which athletes perform choreographed routines to music in a pool, combining swimming, dance, gymnastics and strength. It is governed by World Aquatics and has been an Olympic sport since 1984. Events are solo, duet and team (eight athletes).

Rules

Under World Aquatics rules, routines have a set time limit and must include required elements (figures, eggbeater kicks, acrobatic moves called "acrobatic movements"). A panel of judges scores execution, artistic impression and difficulty, and the totals are combined. Routines are performed above and below the water; athletes wear nose clips and may use goggles underwater. Technical and free routines are both contested, plus a free combination in some events. Variants: at the Olympics since Paris 2024, the team event includes an acrobatic routine with up to three highlighted acrobatic movements.

History

Artistic swimming grew out of water ballet shows in the early 20th century (Annette Kellerman popularized it around 1910) and became an Olympic sport at Los Angeles 1984. The US and Russia/Soviet Union dominated the early decades; today China, Spain and Japan are among the strongest nations.
