Snowkiting

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snowkiting is kite-powered skiing or snowboarding across flat snow, ice and mountain terrain: a steerable power kite pulls the rider on skis or a snowboard, enabling upwind travel, jumps and speed runs on frozen lakes; snowkiting speed records on ice above 100 km/h are documented (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitions include racing on a marked course, freestyle, and long-distance expeditions and ice crossings, with events such as the Snowkite Masters in Finland (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Figure: Peszo. (2007-05-08 22:42:04). Snowkiter on snow (sprzęt/equipment photo) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Snowkiting_sprzet.jpg

Within the kite-sports and winter family on this site its closest relatives are Power Kiting, Kite Landboarding, Kitesurfing, Kite Foil, Cross-Country Skiing and Snowboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Snowkiting competition rules: race courses are set with gates/buoys on snow or ice, ridden upwind and downwind in heats; freestyle judged on jumps and tricks; safety: helmet, impact vest, harness with quick release, avalanche gear in mountain terrain; kites checked for line condition; categories by kite size, age and gender (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Snowkiting emerged in the late 1990s–2000s in Scandinavia, North America and the Alps as kite technology improved; the Snowkite Masters in Finland and races on frozen lakes in Scandinavia and North America have run since the 2000s, and speed records on ice above 100 km/h are documented (Wikipedia, n.d.).
