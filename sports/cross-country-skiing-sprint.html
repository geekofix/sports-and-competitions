Cross-Country Skiing Sprint

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cross-country sprint is the knockout format of cross-country skiing: athletes first ski a short individual time-trial qualification (classic technique, about 1.2-1.8 km), and the fastest advance to head-to-head heats (quarterfinals, semifinals, final) skied in freestyle technique with mass starts of four to six skiers on courses of about 1-1.8 km. It is governed by the FIS and has been an Olympic event since Turin 2006; the team sprint pairs two skiers in alternating legs.

Rules

Under the FIS International Competition Rules, sprint qualification is an individual start in classic technique, and the top qualifiers advance to the knockout rounds. Each heat is skied freestyle with a mass start, and the fastest finishers advance; the final decides medals. Courses are 1-1.8 km with climbs, turns and a finish sprint, and skiers may change skis once in the race. False starts and obstruction are penalized; the team sprint alternates legs between two teammates.

History

The sprint format was developed in the early 2000s to make cross-country skiing more spectator- and TV-friendly, debuting in the World Cup in 2000 and at the Olympics in Turin 2006, where Bjørn Lind of Norway took the men's title. Stars of the modern era include Emil Iversen and Johannes Høsflot Klæbo (Norway) and Federico Pellegrino (Italy).
