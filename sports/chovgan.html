Chovgan

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chovgan (also spelled chowgan, chovgen or chobgan) is a traditional horseback ball game from Azerbaijan, often described as the ancestor of polo: riders on horseback use a long-handled wooden mallet to hit a wooden ball into the opposing goal on a large field. It is deeply rooted in Azerbaijani culture and was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013 as "Chovgan, a traditional horse riding game on the territory of the Republic of Azerbaijan".

Rules

In chovgan, two teams of riders (traditionally five per side) compete on a field similar to a polo pitch, driving a small wooden ball through the opponent's goal with a mallet. Riders may use the mallet to block opponents, and the game combines horsemanship, speed and team play. Modern regulated versions set team sizes, match length and safety rules (helmets, boots), and the game is played at national festivals and in the annual Chovgan championship organized by the Azerbaijan Equestrian Federation. There is no goalkeeper in the traditional form.

History

Chovgan is one of the oldest known horseback ball games, with depictions and references dating back over a millennium; it is mentioned in Persian and Azerbaijani literature and was played by nobles and commoners alike. It influenced the development of polo, which spread from Persia to the West. In 2013 UNESCO inscribed chovgan on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity during the committee session in Baku, recognizing its role in Azerbaijani identity and equestrian heritage.
