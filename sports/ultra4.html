Ultra4

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ultra4 racing is the unlimited off-road class that combines high-speed desert running (over 160 km/h in the open) with extreme rock crawling — the cars are purpose-built tube-frame 4x4s with portal axles and 100:1 crawl ratios (Wikipedia, n.d.). Its flagship is the King of the Hammers at Johnson Valley, California.

Figure: Bureau of Land Management California. (2019-02-04 13:44). King of the Hammers 2019 — cars on the course [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Hammers_2019_%2846934615182%29.jpg

The King of the Hammers race week includes the Ultra4 vs Rock Bouncer shootout, the Every Man Challenge, the Toyo Desert Invitational and the 4400 Race of Kings — the main event combining a desert loop with the Hammers trails (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2025 edition (23 Jan–8 Feb) drew over 80,000 spectators; Kyle Chaney won the 2025 Ultra4 4400 race, and in 2026 Loren Healy took the 4400 Race of Kings (GearJunkie, 2026; Ford Racing, 2026).

Rules

Ultra4 rules: unlimited class 4400, mandatory roll cage, fuel cell, safety gear; the race is a single loop with a neutralized section, timed from a Le Mans-style start (Wikipedia, n.d.). Other Ultra4 series run across the US and Europe (Ultra4 Europe). Men and women compete — women have finished and won in UTV and Ultra4 support classes.

History

The King of the Hammers began in 2007 when Jeff Kincaid and a small group of rock-crawling enthusiasts combined desert racing with the Hammers trails at Means Dry Lake; the first race had 12 cars, Shannon Campbell winning from last place (Wikipedia, n.d.). Hammerking Productions formalized the event, and Ultra4 Racing was born; by 2026 it was a 20th-anniversary spectacle with 50+ Ultra4 starters (Wikipedia, n.d.).
