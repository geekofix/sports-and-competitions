Para Cross-Country Skiing

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para cross-country skiing is cross-country skiing for athletes with a physical impairment or visual impairment at the Paralympic Winter Games, governed by the International Paralympic Committee (IPC) on FIS courses; it has been on the Paralympic programme since the first Winter Paralympics at Örnsköldsvik 1976 (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Paralympics, 20 medal events were held at the Tesero Cross-Country Skiing Stadium in Val di Fiemme (pictured) from 10 to 15 March 2026 — sprint, middle- and long-distance races in classic and free technique plus 4 × 2.5 km relays (mixed and open) (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the 2026 champions, the women lead: the United States' Oksana Masters won the women's sprint sitting in Tesero for her 21st Paralympic medal of a career total of 24, South Korea's 19-year-old Kim Yunji won the women's 20 km sitting free race as one of two golds at her debut Games, Norway's Vilde Nilsen won the women's sprint classic standing and the United States' Sydney Peterson the women's 20 km free standing; on the men's side China's Liu Zixu won the sprint sitting, Belarus' Raman Svirydzenka the sprint standing and the United States' Jake Adicoff four golds including the 20 km visually impaired with his guide (Olympics, 2026, March 10; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Athletes compete in three categories — standing, sitting (sit-ski with outriggers) and visually impaired (with a sighted guide ahead, results adjusted by factored times) — in sprint (about 1.2–1.5 km), middle-distance (around 10 km) and long-distance (up to 20 km) events, using the classic technique (diagonal stride, double poling) or the free technique (skating) (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.-a; FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Races are mass-start or interval-start, timed electronically; relay teams of four cover 4 × 2.5 km with mixed or open categories; equipment follows FIS rules with adaptations for the class — standing skiers use two skis, sitting athletes a monoski — and helmets are recommended per FIS rules; men and women compete in separate events in each class (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

History

Cross-country skiing was among the sports of the first Winter Paralympics at Örnsköldsvik, Sweden, in 1976, together with alpine skiing; the programme has grown from a handful of events to 20 medal events at Milano Cortina 2026, with sitting events added from the 1980s and visually impaired events with guides using factored times (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

The United States' Oksana Masters — a dual-sport Paralympic medallist across summer and winter Games — became one of the most decorated winter Paralympians with 24 career medals after Milano Cortina 2026, while Canada's Brian McKeever won Paralympic gold as an athlete and later as a guide for visually impaired skiers (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
