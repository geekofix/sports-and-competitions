Shin Kicking

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shin kicking is the combat sport of the Cotswold Olimpick Games on Dover's Hill near Chipping Campden, Gloucestershire, in which two competitors dressed in white smocks grip each other squarely by the shoulders and try to kick each other's shins until one of them falls or concedes — the loser traditionally cries "sufficient" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olimpicks, n.d.). At the 2026 games on 29 May, Andrew Bailey was crowned champion after bravely competing without the customary straw padding in his trousers, the title being awarded by the stickler, three-time champion Mike Newby, better known to the crowds as "Shindiana Jones"; the 2026 edition marked the 414th anniversary of the games and drew more than 2,500 spectators (BBC, 2026). A women's shin-kicking competition is also held at the games, so female and male competitors each have their own championship bouts at Dover's Hill (Olimpicks, n.d.; BBC, 2026).

The current men's champion is Andrew Bailey of 2026, crowned by stickler Shindiana Jones (Mike Newby), and the women's event crowns its own champion at the same games each year, keeping the tradition alive for both genders (BBC, 2026; Olimpicks, n.d.).

Rules

Competitors wear soft-soled shoes and traditionally tuck straw padding into their trouser legs for protection; steel toe caps are banned, and both combatants hold each other by the shoulders throughout the bout (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olimpicks, n.d.). The objective is to deliver genuine, forceful kicks to the opponent's shins until pain or loss of balance brings him or her to the ground; the referee — the stickler — watches each exchange and counts a fall only after confirming real contact (BBC, 2026). A competitor who can no longer continue concedes by calling "sufficient", and the modern championship at the Olimpicks is run as a tournament of bouts in which the last unbeaten competitor is crowned World Shin-Kicking Champion; a women's tournament is staged alongside the men's (Olimpicks, n.d.). A related Cornish tradition of clog fighting, long associated with rural fairs, shares the same shoulder-grip, kick-until-down format (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

The Cotswold Olimpick Games were founded around 1612 by the lawyer and courtier Robert Dover (c. 1575/1582–1652), who staged "Olympick" rural games on Dover's Hill at Chipping Campden to revive ancient Greek athletics; shin kicking was among the original events and a woodcut titled Annalia Dubrensia depicted the contest in 1636 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The games lapsed in 1852, were revived in 1951, and have been held annually since 1965, with shin kicking billed as the World Shin-Kicking Championship; in 2012 the organisers were even contacted by the London Olympic Committee to feature in a promotional video for the home Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; BBC, 2026). The 2026 event on 29 May was the 414th anniversary of Dover's original games (BBC, 2026).
