Shin Kicking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shin kicking is an English folk combat sport, also historically known as "purring" or "shin diggings", in which two contestants hold each other by the collar or shoulders and kick each other's shins until one is felled. It is the signature event of the Cotswold Olimpick Games held on Dover's Hill near Chipping Campden, Gloucestershire, a festival founded in 1612 by the lawyer Robert Dover, and it has been described as an English martial art. At the modern games it is contested as the World Shin-Kicking Championships, drawing crowds of thousands.

Rules

The combatants face each other and hold on to each other's collar or shoulders — at the Cotswold Olimpicks they traditionally wear white coats, representing shepherds' smocks. Each tries to strike the opponent's shin with the inside of the foot and the toes; success demands both agility and the ability to endure pain, and traditionally the loser cried "Sufficient" when he had had enough. In modern competitions the combatants are required to wear soft shoes and to stuff their trouser legs with straw for padding, and ambulance crews attend the events in case of injury. Matches are observed by a referee, known as a stickler, who determines the score; a modern contest is won by the combatant who wins the majority of a series of bouts against his competitor, with the fallen man losing each bout.

History

Shin kicking was one of the most popular events at Robert Dover's Cotswold Olimpick Games, which the lawyer founded on Dover's Hill above Chipping Campden in about 1612 with the approval of King James I; the sport is known to have been contested there since at least 1636, alongside cudgel-playing, wrestling, hammer-throwing and horse-racing, until the games ended in 1852 when the common land was enclosed. The games were revived on 17 May 1951 for the Festival of Britain, and after a further break became an annual fixture again in 1965 with the foundation of the Robert Dover's Games Society; since 1966 they have been held each year on the Friday after Spring Bank Holiday, with shin kicking staged as the World Shin-Kicking Championships. Beyond the Cotswolds, shin kicking was a popular pastime among Cornish miners as a subset of Cornish wrestling, and as "purring" it flourished as a combative means of settling disputes in the mill towns of Lancashire until the 1930s — associated spectator gambling was illegal. The British Olympic Association has recognised the Cotswold Olimpick Games as "the first stirrings of Britain's Olympic beginnings", and the straw-stuffed spectacle is now covered regularly by national and international media.
