Free Solo Climbing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Free solo climbing is rock climbing (or buildering) with no rope, harness or protection of any kind — only climbing shoes and chalk — so a fall is fatal from most heights (Wikipedia, n.d.-d). It is not a judged competition: there is no federation that sanctions free solos, and the IFSC governs the roped competition disciplines — sport climbing, bouldering, lead, speed and ice climbing — to which free soloing is adjacent (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-f).

The landmark ascent was Alex Honnold's free solo of El Capitan's Freerider route (5.13a / 7c+, 880 m) in Yosemite on 3 June 2017, completed in 3 hours 56 minutes and documented in the Oscar-winning film Free Solo (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Still active today, Honnold extended the discipline to architecture on 25 January 2026, when he free soloed the 508 m Taipei 101 tower in 1 hour 31 minutes, graded around 5.11 — the tallest buildering free solo ever (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The women's side of the sport is documented by Catherine Destivelle of France, the leading female climber of the late 1980s, who was the first woman to climb the Bonatti Pillar and made iconic free solos such as El Matador and the 1987 Bandiagara cliffs in Mali filmed for the documentary Seo (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

There is no rulebook for free soloing — the "rules" are the absence of rules: no rope, no harness, no protection, no second chance. Climbers rehearse routes roped for months or years to memorize every move, then descend on foot or solo downclimb (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Routes are graded on the Yosemite Decimal System (5.10–5.15) or the French scale (5a–9c+); difficulty grades describe technical movement, not consequence. In competition climbing (IFSC), by contrast, athletes are roped and clipped into quickdraws, and falls are routine — free solo sits deliberately outside that framework (Wikipedia, n.d.-f).

Sub-disciplines include free solo rock climbing on cliffs and big walls, free solo bouldering (lower heights, but still unroped), deep-water soloing over water, and buildering (free soloing buildings and towers, e.g. Taipei 101 in 2026) (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Free soloing entered public consciousness in the late 1970s and 1980s through the Canadian Peter Croft, who pioneered big-wall free soloing with Astroman and The Rostrum in Yosemite, and through Americans such as John Bachar (died 2009) and Patrick Edlinger, the "Dark Avenger". Dean Potter took big-wall soloing further and established Easy Rider (5.11d) on El Capitan in 2012 before dying in a 2015 BASE-jumping accident (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

On the women's side, Catherine Destivelle made the era's most famous female free solos, including El Matador and the Bandiagara escarpment in Mali in 1987; Swiss speed soloist Ueli Steck (died 2017) and the French buildering icon Alain Robert completed the pantheon of the era (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Alex Honnold's 2017 El Capitan free solo — the first big-wall free solo at 5.13a — became one of the most-watched athletic feats in history through the 2018 documentary Free Solo, which won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Canadian Marc-André Leclerc, profiled in the 2021 film The Alpinist, died in 2018 (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).
