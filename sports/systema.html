Systema

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Systema (literally the system) is a Russian martial art of close-quarters self-defence built on breathing, relaxation, structure and movement: practitioners learn to survive weapons, grabs and strikes through fluid, adaptable responses rather than fixed kata-like patterns. Associated historically with Russian military and security training, it is taught today in civilian schools across Russia, Europe, the Americas and beyond, and its practice is organized through seminars, camps and affiliate federations.

Rules

Training centres on the four pillars the tradition emphasizes: continuous breathing under stress, relaxation of body and mind, correct posture and skeletal alignment, and constant movement in a relaxed, non-anticipatory way, taught through slow-motion sensitivity drills, breath work, ground movement and freestyle work that adapts to strikes, grabs and weapons. Practitioners typically train in street clothes rather than uniforms, without competition sparring, so unlike the judo or MMA rule-set there is no sporting competitive branch and no submission scoring in the conventional sense. Progression is by depth and versatility of skill rather than colored belts (rank and certification are school-specific), and instruction emphasizes psychology, multiple-opponent scenarios and weapon defence.

History

Russian hand-to-hand traditions reach back through Cossack fighting methods and old Slavic combat games, and modern Systema developed out of twentieth-century Soviet military close-combat research, originally passed through those who studied and taught it for the military and security services of the USSR and later the Russian Federation. After the Soviet era, exponents began teaching openly in Russia and abroad, with the practice spreading widely through the 1990s and 2000s via seminars, videos and affiliate schools, and the style's distinctive breathing-and-relaxation approach drawing a global following among law-enforcement trainers and civilians. Systema remains a living tradition without a single world governing structure, organized around lineages, schools and an international network of annual camps.
