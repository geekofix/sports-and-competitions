Lokotxa

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lokotxa is the Basque corn-cob collecting race: teams or individuals strip as many corn cobs (lokoak) from a set pile as possible within a time limit, traditionally at harvest time (artoa) in Gipuzkoa and Nafarroa. It is a harvest-festival herri kirolak closely tied to maize-shelling work. Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are Lasto Altxatzea, Zaku Lasterka, Segala; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

Figure: Rtr PK. (2026-02-11). Manual maize shelling — the labour behind the lokotxa race [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Local_maize_shelling_in_Uganda.jpg

Rules

A pile of corn cobs is set per competitor; only cobs fully stripped of husk count; the highest count within the time limit (usually a few minutes) wins. Traditionally played in separate men's and women's heats at village fêtes; modern rules fix the cob count and pile size (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

History

Maize (artoa) arrived in the Basque Country in the 17th century and became a staple; the communal harvest (arto biltzea) was ritualized in the lokotxa race. Photographs of manual maize shelling show the same labour in farming communities today (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
