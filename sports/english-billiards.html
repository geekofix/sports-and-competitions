English Billiards

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

English billiards is a cue sport played on a 12 ft × 6 ft snooker-sized table with three balls — one red and two white — in which points are scored by cannons (hitting both other balls) and by pocketing the red or an opponent's ball after a cannon (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Its top competition is the World Billiards Championship (World Professional Billiards Championship), organized by World Billiards, a subsidiary of the World Professional Billiards and Snooker Association (WPBSA), and one of the oldest sporting world championships, contested since 1870 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). At the 2026 World Billiards Championship at the Landywood Snooker Club in Great Wyrley, England, on 22–23 October 2026, India's Pankaj Advani defeated England's Robert Hall 1,906–1,206 in a five-hour final to win his 21st billiards world title and 29th career world title, ending David Causier's unbeaten run that stretched back to 2023 (WPBSA, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in the women's game — the reigning World Ladies Billiards champion, and — in the men's game — 2026 world champion Pankaj Advani of India (b. 24 July 1985, Bangalore) and runner-up Robert Hall of England (WPBSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Rules

English billiards is played under the rules maintained by World Billiards and the WPBSA: on a table of 12 ft × 6 ft (3.7 m × 1.8 m) with pockets, the cue ball is the player's own white ball — distinguished by a spot — and the object balls are one red and the opponent's white (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A cannon — striking both object balls with the cue ball in one shot — scores one point, and a pot — pocketing the red ball (5 points) or an opponent's white (3 points) after a cannon — adds to the score, with a red pot followed by playing the red as cue ball for up to 15 consecutive points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). World championship matches are played over a handicap or points-up format, typically to a target score or over a set time — the 2026 final was a timed points match of five hours — with group stages feeding a knockout bracket (WPBSA, n.d.). Fouls — failing to hit a ball, pushing the cue ball, or a pot without a prior cannon — score no points and end the break; the highest break in the 2026 final was Advani's 587 (WPBSA, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate world championships — the World Billiards Championship and the World Ladies Billiards Championship, played since 1931 — with identical rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Billiards reached England in the fifteenth century and evolved through bagatelle and bar-billiards into the pocketed carom game played today; the first known rules date to the sixteenth century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The World Championship was first contested in 1870, won by William Cook — then known as the champion of the world — and the early twentieth century saw the great English professionals John Roberts Jr. and Tom Reece dominate, with Roberts' 1885 world record break of 100,000 still unmatched in concept (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The women's game has its own history: Mme Strebor and Eva Collins played a famous match at Burroughes Hall, London, in 1906, pictured below, and the World Ladies Billiards Championship has been played since 1931 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Modern icons include England's Mike Russell, a nine-time world champion, and India's Pankaj Advani, who won the "Grand Double" of timed and points world titles three times (2005, 2008, 2014) and the 2026 title at age 41 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
