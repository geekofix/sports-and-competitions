English Billiards

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

English billiards is a cue sport played on a 12 ft x 6 ft (3.7 m x 1.8 m) snooker table with three balls (two white cue balls and one red object ball), combining cannons (caroms) and pot attempts with the aim of scoring as many points as possible in a single visit. It is governed in the professional game by the World Professional Billiards and Snooker Association (WPBSA), which runs the World Billiards Championship, and internationally by the World Billiards & Snooker Federation for amateur play.

Rules

Each player has their own cue ball (one dotted, one spotted). A cannon (hitting both object balls with the cue ball in one shot) scores 2 points; a pot (potting the red or an opponent's cue ball) scores 3 points, and potting your own cue ball scores 2. Points can be chained in a single visit, and a century (100+ points in one visit) is a major achievement. At the World Billiards Championship, matches are played over a set number of points (e.g., first to 2,000 or 3,000) or time, with the time rule: in the last frame, play is under the clock and the player with the lead at the interval can force the opponent to play on. Fouls (missing both object balls, potting the red without a cannon in some variants, or off-table shots) end the visit and may add penalty points to the opponent. Cue ball and table dimensions, cloth and ball specs follow WPBSA standards.

History

English billiards developed in the 18th century from earlier bat-and-ball and cue games in Britain and became the dominant cue sport of the 19th century, with world championships from 1870 (won by William Cook, then John Roberts Jr., "the Emperor of Billiards," who held the title for 50 years). The WPBSA (founded 1968 as the Billiards and Snooker Control Council, renamed 1986) professionalized the game, and legends include Joe Davis (who also dominated snooker), Tom Newman and Pankaj Advani, the Indian player who has won multiple World Billiards titles in both English billiards and snooker. The World Billiards Championship is traditionally held at the Southport Theatre & Convention Centre and in India (Leeds/ Bengaluru editions). Modern stars set centuries regularly, with top players like Advani and Mike Russell competing for titles over 3,000-point matches.
