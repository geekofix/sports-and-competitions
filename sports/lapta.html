Lapta

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lapta is a Russian folk bat-and-ball game, first documented in the 14th century, in which one team serves a ball toward a batter who tries to hit it far and then run between two end lines while the fielding team tries to hit the runners with the ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is widely regarded as a precursor of both baseball and cricket, and is today organized in Russia by the All-Russian Lapta Federation, which grew out of an interregional federation founded in 1997 and was registered in 2003, coordinating 46 regional federations and annual national championships held since 1990 (All-Russian Lapta Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The game is played by men and women alike in modern Russia — women's and mixed teams compete in the national championships and school tournaments (All-Russian Lapta Federation, n.d.-a).

Rules

Lapta is played outdoors on a field about the size of half a football pitch — 42.7 × 53.3 m (20 × 25 sazhens) — with two parallel side lines (salo), a batter's area (gorod) and an end line (kon) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The fielding team has five players on the field plus a pitcher/server who stands near the batter and serves the ball; the batting team has six players (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each batter gets up to two chances to hit the served ball beyond the 10 m line; a successful hit lets the batter run to the kon line and, if possible, back to the gorod line, scoring for each safe completed run without being hit by the ball thrown by the fielders (All-Russian Lapta Federation, n.d.-a). A game lasts one hour, split into two equal halves, and the team with more points wins — or the batting team wins immediately if all its players complete runs (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment is a tennis-sized ball and a flat, spade-shaped wooden bat (the lapta) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's matches follow identical rules, with junior divisions in school and club competition (All-Russian Lapta Federation, n.d.-a). Rule source: All-Russian Lapta Federation rules, https://ruslapta.ru/ (All-Russian Lapta Federation, n.d.-a). See also baseball, cricket and softball on this site.

History

Lapta is documented as early as the 14th century: mentions appear in medieval manuscripts, and balls and bats were found in 14th-century layers during excavations in Novgorod (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Peter the Great encouraged his elite guard — the Preobrazhensky regiment — to play lapta for physical training, and writer Aleksandr Kuprin praised the game's demands of speed, accuracy and resourcefulness (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). During the Soviet period the game was promoted as a "Soviet-type sport": thousands of teams were registered in the 1950s, Russian championships were held from 1958, and lapta entered the Spartakiad programme in 1959; a USSR decree of 11 November 1986 promoted baseball, softball and lapta together (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). After a decline, annual Russian championships resumed in 1990, an interregional federation was founded in 1997 and the All-Russian Lapta Federation was established in 2003 (All-Russian Lapta Federation, n.d.-a).
