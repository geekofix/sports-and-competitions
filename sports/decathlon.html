Decathlon

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The decathlon is the premier combined track-and-field contest, in which athletes compete in ten events over two days and the winner is the athlete with the highest combined score from the official points tables (World Athletics [WA], n.d.-b). It is governed by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic program for men at every Games since Stockholm 1912; women contest the seven-event heptathlon, covered on Heptathlon on this site (WA, n.d.-b; Olympics.com, n.d.). At the Paris 2024 Olympics, Norway's Markus Rooth won the men's decathlon with a national record 8,796 points — the first Norwegian Olympic decathlon champion since 1920 — ahead of Germany's Leo Neugebauer (8,748) and Grenada's Lindon Victor (8,711) (Olympics.com, n.d.). At the 2025 World Championships in Tokyo, Neugebauer won gold with 8,804, twenty points ahead of the USA's Owens-Delerme; at the 2026 European Championships in Birmingham in August 2026, Neugebauer retained the title with 8,611, 38 points ahead of Estonia's Kaul (WA, n.d.-a). In women's combined events, Belgium's Nafissatou Thiam — the 2024 Olympic heptathlon champion and three-time world champion — and the USA's Anna Hall, second on the all-time heptathlon list after her 2025 performance, are the leading current names (WA, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current men are — pictured below — 2024 Olympic champion Markus Rooth (Norway) and two-time world champion Leo Neugebauer (Germany) (WA, n.d.-a) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (World Athletics, n.d.-a) (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the combined-events family — Heptathlon and Athletics — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

Under the World Athletics competition rules, the men's decathlon runs over two days: day one — 100 m, long jump, shot put, high jump, 400 m; day two — 110 m hurdles, discus throw, pole vault, javelin throw, 1,500 m (WA, n.d.-b). Each event is converted into points by the official World Athletics combined-events scoring tables, and the athlete with the highest total wins; ties are broken by the athlete with the higher number of first-place event wins, then second places, and so on (WA, n.d.-b). The women's heptathlon comprises 100 m hurdles, high jump, shot put and 200 m on day one, and long jump, javelin and 800 m on day two (WA, n.d.-b). Age classifications distinguish senior and under-20 (U20) athletes, who have their own world championships, while para combined events are organized separately under World Para Athletics (WA, n.d.-b). The decathlon is contested by men and the heptathlon by women at elite level; both use identical equipment specifications and scoring tables within their event sets (WA, n.d.-b).

History

The modern decathlon was created for the 1912 Stockholm Olympics by Pierre de Coubertin, inspired by the ancient Greek pentathlon of 708 BC, and the American Jim Thorpe won the first Olympic decathlon in 1912, receiving his gold medal from King Gustav V of Sweden (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Since 1912 the decathlon has been part of every Olympic Games (Olympics.com, n.d.). Icons of the event include Bob Mathias, who won gold at just 17 in 1948 and again in 1952, Daley Thompson, Olympic champion in 1980 and 1984 and twice world champion, Roman Šebrle of Czechia, the first man over 9,000 points (9,026 in 2001), and Ashton Eaton, Olympic champion in 2012 and 2016 with the world record 9,045 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the heptathlon greats are Jackie Joyner-Kersee of the USA, whose world record of 7,291 from 1988 still stands and who won Olympic gold in 1988, and Sweden's Carolina Klüft, Olympic champion in Athens 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
