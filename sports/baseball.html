Baseball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Baseball is a bat-and-ball team sport played between two teams of nine players who alternate between batting and fielding over nine innings; the batting team scores runs by hitting a pitched ball and circling four bases, while the fielding team tries to record three outs per inning. The top professional league is Major League Baseball (MLB) in North America, governed with the players' association under the commissioner of baseball, and the international game is governed by the World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC), which runs the World Baseball Classic (World Baseball Softball Confederation [WBSC], n.d.-a). The 2026 MLB season ended with the World Series (October 23–28, 2026), in which the Milwaukee Brewers defeated the Tampa Bay Rays 4 games to 1 for the franchise's first championship — the first World Series played league-wide under the ABS automated strike-zone system introduced in 2026. William Contreras was named World Series MVP (Willie Mays Award) after batting .381; the Brewers had posted a 103–59 regular season, swept the Dodgers in the NLCS and beat the Padres in the NLDS, and pitcher Jacob Misiorowski won the NL Cy Young Award (Major League Baseball [MLB], n.d.-b). In the 6th World Baseball Classic (March 5–17, 2026), Venezuela defeated the USA 3–2 in the final at loanDepot park in Miami on March 17, 2026 — Venezuela's first WBC title — with Maikel García named MVP after Venezuela had beaten defending champion Japan in the quarterfinals and Italy in the semifinal (WBSC, n.d.-a) (Major League Baseball, n.d.-a) (Major League Baseball, n.d.-b) (World Baseball Softball Confederation, n.d.-a) (World Baseball Softball Confederation, n.d.-b).

On the women's side, women's baseball has grown rapidly: the Women's Baseball World Cup (WBSC) is the premier international tournament, and professional women's leagues operate in Japan, the USA and elsewhere; women also play the sport at the Olympics in the form of softball, its close sister (WBSC, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the Official Baseball Rules (maintained by MLB), each team fields nine players — pitcher, catcher, four infielders (first, second, third base, shortstop) and three outfielders. The game lasts nine innings; each inning has a top and bottom half, and the visiting team bats first. The pitcher throws the ball toward home plate, and the batter tries to hit it into fair territory; a batted ball that lands in fair territory or is caught after a fair fly allows the batter to run. Three strikes (swinging or called, in the strike zone) is a strikeout; four balls outside the zone is a walk; three outs end the half-inning (MLB, n.d.-a).

Runs are scored when a batter safely reaches first base and subsequent runners advance around all bases to home plate. Fielding outs include strikeouts, fly outs, ground outs and force outs/tag outs. Foul balls count as strikes except on a third strike. Since 2026, MLB uses the ABS challenge system league-wide: the automated strike zone is used with each team allowed two challenges per game, retaining a challenge if successful (MLB, n.d.-b).

Equipment and field: the diamond has 90 ft (27.4 m) between bases, a pitcher's mound 60 ft 6 in (18.4 m) from home plate, and a 400 ft+ center-field fence at MLB parks; bats are wood at MLB level, aluminum in amateur play. Men's and women's baseball use identical rules at the international level (the WBSC Women's Baseball World Cup plays standard rules, often with a slightly smaller ball); youth and amateur leagues modify pitching distance, mound height and innings. Designated hitter rules differ by league (DH universal in the AL, optional in the NL since 2022).

History

Baseball evolved from older bat-and-ball games (rounders, cricket) in 18th-century England and was codified in the United States: the Knickerbocker Rules of 1845 in New York established the 90-ft diamond, nine innings and three-outs rules, largely through Alexander Cartwright; the Cincinnati Red Stockings became the first professional team in 1869, and the National League was founded in 1876, the American League in 1901 (MLB, n.d.-a).

The most iconic player is George Herman "Babe" Ruth of the New York Yankees, who revolutionized the game with the home run in the 1920s, holding the career home run record (714) from 1927 until 1961 and the single-season record (60 in 1927) until 1961. Other legends include Lou Gehrig ("the Iron Horse"), Joe DiMaggio (56-game hitting streak, 1941), Jackie Robinson (broke MLB's color barrier with the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1947) and Hank Aaron (755 career home runs, 1974). On the women's side, the All-American Girls Professional Baseball League (AAGPBL, 1943–1954) — the league of "A League of Their Own" — featured stars like Dottie Green; women's baseball was revived internationally with the Women's Baseball World Cup from 2004 (WBSC, n.d.-b).
