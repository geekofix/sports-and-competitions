Baseball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Baseball is a bat-and-ball team sport in which two teams of nine players alternate between batting and fielding. The batting side scores runs by hitting a pitched ball and running around four bases; the fielding side tries to get batters and runners out. It is governed internationally by the World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC) and is the national sport of the United States, where Major League Baseball (MLB) is the top professional league. Baseball returned to the Olympic program in 2020 (Tokyo 2020, held 2021).

Rules

Under the Official Baseball Rules used by MLB and the WBSC World Baseball Classic, each game has nine innings; the visiting team bats first. The pitcher throws from the mound 60 ft 6 in from home plate, and the batter tries to hit into fair territory, then runs counterclockwise around first, second and third base to home plate. A team records three outs per inning (strikeouts, fly outs, ground outs, tags). A batter gets three strikes (swinging or looking in the strike zone) or four balls (outside the zone) for a walk. At the World Baseball Classic, extra innings and tie-breaker rules apply after a set number of innings.

History

Baseball evolved from older bat-and-ball games (rounders, cricket) in 18th-century England and was codified in the United States in the 19th century — the Knickerbocker Rules of 1845 are a landmark. The first professional team, the Cincinnati Red Stockings, was founded in 1869, and MLB's National League began in 1876. Baseball was an Olympic sport from 1912 (demonstration) and 1952-1956 officially, and returned in 1992; it was dropped after 2008 and reinstated for Tokyo 2020. Legends include Babe Ruth, Jackie Robinson (who broke the color barrier in 1947) and Hank Aaron.
