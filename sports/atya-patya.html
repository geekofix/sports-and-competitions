Atya Patya

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Atya patya is a traditional South Asian tag sport — a “game of feints” and “militant chase” — most popular in rural Maharashtra: two sides of nine players, a court split by a central trench with nine perpendicular trenches on either side; the attacking players score by crossing as many trenches as possible without being tagged by the nine defenders standing in the trenches. It is governed by the Atya Patya Federation of India (founded 1982, Nagpur; recognized by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports in 2013), and men's and women's national championships are held; the first South Asian Atya Patya Championship was held in Bhutan in June 2013 and won by India.

Figure: Snehasis1990. (2012-01-28). Two teams contesting atya patya on a clay court in Hyderabad, India [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Atya_patya_hyderabad.jpg

Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are gilli-danda, kancha and seven-stones; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The playing area is a rectangle divided into two halves by a central trench, with nine trenches perpendicular to it on each side. Teams of nine alternate offense and defense in four innings of 7 minutes each (each team gets two scoring innings). Attackers try to cross as many trenches as possible without being touched by the defenders, each of whom stands in one trench; a touched attacker is out for that inning. The team crossing more trenches (scoring more points) wins. Related games: killithattu in Tamil Nadu, killithaddu in Sri Lanka, wanjh wati in Sindh, gobak sodor in Indonesia, galah panjang in Malaysia and patintero in the Philippines.

History

Atya patya is mentioned in the Natyashastra, written before 300 AD, and was used as combat practice by soldiers of the Chola dynasty; kalaripayattu was practiced in tandem with it in Kerala. The Atya Patya Federation of India was formed in 1982 and is headquartered in Nagpur; the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports listed it among recognized national sports federations in 2013. The first South Asian championship took place in Bhutan in June 2013; national championships have been held since the 1990s (e.g., the 6th National Senior championship in New Delhi, July 1994).
