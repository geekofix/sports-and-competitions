Canoeing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canoeing is the umbrella term for all paddling sports with canoes and kayaks, governed worldwide by the International Canoe Federation (ICF), founded in Copenhagen in 1924. The ICF oversees ten disciplines: canoe sprint, canoe slalom, kayak cross, paracanoe, canoe marathon, canoe polo, wildwater canoeing, canoe freestyle, canoe ocean racing and dragon boat, plus recognized disciplines such as stand-up paddling. Canoeing has been on the Olympic program since Berlin 1936 (sprint), with slalom added at Munich 1972.

Rules

Each discipline has its own ICF rule book, published on the ICF rules page: sprint and marathon are timed races on flat water, slalom is a timed gate course on whitewater, polo is a team ball game, freestyle is judged trick riding, and ocean racing is downwind sea paddling. Common elements are standardized boat classes (K for kayak, C for Canadian canoe, with crew sizes 1, 2 or 4), safety equipment requirements, anti-doping rules under WADA, and qualification pathways from national championships to ICF world championships and the Olympic Games.

History

Paddled craft have been used for transport for millennia, and competitive paddling emerged in 19th-century Europe, especially in Britain, Scandinavia and Central Europe. The ICF was founded on 24 January 1924 in Copenhagen by national federations from Denmark, Sweden, Austria and Germany, among others, and held its first sprint world championships in 1938. Olympic canoeing debuted at Berlin 1936, slalom joined in 1972, and paracanoe was added at Rio 2016. Today the ICF has over 170 member national federations.
