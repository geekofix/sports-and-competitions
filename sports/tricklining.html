Tricklining

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tricklining is the acrobatic branch of slacklining: athletes bounce on a tensioned two-inch webline anchored low between trees or posts, performing flips, tricks and combinations at height (jumps, rotations, combos like the butt-bounce and chest-bounce families). From park sessions to the Gibbon-hosted world championships, it is the sport's explosive young branch.

Rules

Standard trickline competition judges athletes on a set performance time: competitors bounce and throw tricks that are scored on difficulty, execution, height and landing quality, with athletes combining aerial tricks (from basic chest and butt bounces to rotations and moves from gymnastics/breakdance vocabulary) in runs; a heavy-bounce, powerful line setup gives maximum height. Judging panels score each trick on set criteria with deductions for falls or incomplete landings, formats include solo finals and battles, and equipment rules govern line type (two-inch polyester webbing, standard tension), anchor safety and competition setups at a fixed height, with events from festival showcases to world championship series.

History

Slacklining itself derives from the climbing-camp tightrope of Yosemite's 1970s (ropewalkers walking loose climbing webbing between trees), with the modern slackline scene building the specific discipline of tricklining in the 2000s as short, highly tensioned lines transformed walking lines into bouncing trampolines. Commercial kit makers (above all Germany's Gibbon) and YouTube clips popularized the discipline worldwide, and the major international championships have run trickline competitions since the early 2010s. The modern scene spans park jam sessions to a competitive world circuit, and trickline culture sits at the heart of urban movement festivals alongside parkour and freerunning.
