Sand Skiing

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sand skiing is skiing on sand dunes using regular skis (often with a waxed or plastic base) on specially prepared sand slopes; the best-known venue is Monte Kaolino in Hirschau, Bavaria, Germany — a 70 m high sand dune with lifts — and the FIS has run a sand ski World Cup there. Speeds reach 60–80 km/h on the steepest lines, and the sport is a summer training and competition option for alpine skiers (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). At competitions, men and women race in separate categories on the same course.

Figure: State Library and Archives of Florida. (2014-02-07 05:18:05). Woman sand skiing on a sand slope at Fort Meade, Florida, with spectators [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sand_skiing_in_Fort_Meade,_Florida_(12362993375).jpg

Within the skiing family on this site its closest relatives are Alpine Skiing Downhill, Grass Skiing, Speed Skiing, Sandboarding and Water Skiing as the water surface-swap; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

FIS sand ski rules mirror alpine skiing: timed single runs on a marked sand course with gates for slalom formats, or speed runs on a straight line; equipment is standard alpine skis with sand-suitable wax; helmet and pads recommended; falls stop the run; qualification and final rounds; men's and women's categories and junior classes (FIS, n.d.).

History

Sand skiing began in the 1960s–70s at coastal dunes (e.g., Cronulla Beach near Sydney, Australia, and Fort Meade, Florida), where lifeguards skied for fun; the FIS recognized sand skiing in the 1980s and the first World Cup ran at Monte Kaolino in the 1990s, with the venue hosting the FIS Sand Ski World Cup regularly since (Wikipedia, n.d.).
