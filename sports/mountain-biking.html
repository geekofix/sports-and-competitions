Mountain Biking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mountain biking is cycling on unpaved terrain — forest trails, mountains, fields and bike parks — on specially built bicycles with wide tires, suspension and powerful brakes. Competitive mountain biking is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) and comprises the disciplines cross-country Olympic (XCO), cross-country short track (XCC), cross-country marathon (XCM), downhill (DHI), enduro, eliminator (XCE) and trials (see Mountain Bike Trials). Cross-country has been an Olympic sport since Atlanta 1996. The top events are the annual UCI Mountain Bike World Cup and the UCI Mountain Bike World Championships.

Rules

In cross-country races, riders start in a mass start and complete several laps of a 3-5 km circuit with climbs, descents and technical sections; the first across the line wins, and elite races last about 80-100 minutes. Downhill is an individual timed run down a prepared course with jumps and rock gardens, where the fastest time wins. Enduro combines timed downhill stages with untimed liaison sections. Marathon is a long-distance mass-start race of 60-120 km. Eliminator is a knockout race on a short urban circuit where the top riders advance heat by heat. At the World Championships, the winners wear the rainbow jersey for a year and represent national teams rather than trade teams.

History

Mountain biking was born in the 1970s in Marin County, California, where riders such as Gary Fisher, Tom Ritchey, Joe Breeze and Keith Bontrager modified old cruiser bikes with wide tires, motocross handlebars and stronger brakes to ride down the fire roads of Mount Tamalpais; the Repack Race of 1976 is considered the first organized mountain bike race, and the Crested Butte-to-Aspen race of 1978 the first cross-country event. The first UCI Mountain Bike World Cup series ran in 1988 (sponsored by Grundig, cross-country only), and the first UCI Mountain Bike World Championships were held in Durango, Colorado in 1990 with cross-country and downhill. Cross-country became an Olympic discipline at Atlanta 1996. Riders such as John Tomac, Ned Overend, Juli Furtado, Nino Schurter, Rachel Atherton and Mathieu van der Poel shaped the sport's history.
