Taekwondo ITF

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

ITF Taekwon-Do is the branch of taekwondo founded by General Choi Hong-hi in 1966 as the International Taekwon-Do Federation: the traditional-pattern form of the art, characterized by the Chang Hon tul patterns, sine-wave movement and semi-contact sparring in which hand techniques to the head are permitted. It is one of the two great international systems of taekwondo, alongside the Olympic WT style.

Rules

The ITF curriculum is built on the Chang Hon patterns (tul), 24 choreographed sequences named for Korean history and philosophy, executed with the system's distinctive sine-wave motion "+E+" a deliberate rise and fall of the body that loads techniques with dropping body mass. Sparring (matsogi) is semi-contact with protective gear, scoring punches and kicks to permitted target areas including head strikes with the hands, in bouts refereed for control and clean technique; belt progression runs through geup grades to dan degrees under ITF examiners, and competitions feature patterns, sparring, breaking, special technique and self-defence events across age and belt divisions at national and world championships run by ITF-affiliated organizations.

History

General Choi Hong-hi, one of taekwondo's principal founders, created the International Taekwon-Do Federation in Seoul in 1966 to promote the unified art internationally as he systematized it (he authored its encyclopedic technical canon), and after his political break with the South Korean government of the day he moved ITF headquarters abroad, an exile history that left the federation strongest in Europe, North Korea and the former Eastern bloc and with northern-influenced politics long attached to its name. After General Choi's death in 2002 the ITF fragmented among competing successor organizations, several of which continue to run world championships under variations of the original structure and curriculum, and ITF-style taekwondo remains a thriving global martial art with a distinctive identity: closer to its karate roots, emphasizing practical self-defence, and unified by tul patterns anyone in the world can recognize on sight.
