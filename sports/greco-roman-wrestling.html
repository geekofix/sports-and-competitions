Greco-Roman Wrestling

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Greco-Roman wrestling is one of the two Olympic styles of wrestling — the other being freestyle — in which two wrestlers grapple on a circular mat and may only attack above the waist; it is governed worldwide by United World Wrestling (UWW), founded in 1912 as the International Amateur Wrestling Federation (FILA) and renamed UWW in 2014 (United World Wrestling [UWW], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Bouts consist of two three-minute periods, start standing and continue on the ground, and are decided by a fall (both shoulder blades on the mat), technical superiority (an eight-point lead) or points when time expires; the senior men's programme at the World Championships comprises ten weight classes from 55 kg to 130 kg (UWW, n.d.-b). The 2026 World Wrestling Championships were held at Barys Arena in Astana, Kazakhstan, from 24 October to 1 November 2026, with around 900 wrestlers competing for 30 titles across Greco-Roman, men's freestyle and women's freestyle — in the 130 kg Greco-Roman final, Fardin Hedayati of Iran defeated Dárius Vitek of Hungary 8–0 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; UWW, n.d.-a) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-a) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-b).

Greco-Roman is a men-only discipline at the elite level — women compete in freestyle wrestling at the World Championships and the Olympic Games (see Freestyle Wrestling on this site) — so the leading athletes of the style are the reigning world champions, among them Vakhtang Lolua of Georgia (55 kg), Aidos Sultangali of Kazakhstan (60 kg), Malkhas Amoyan of Armenia (77 kg) and Gholamreza Farokhi of Iran (82 kg), while Riza Kayaalp of Türkiye chased a record sixth world title at 130 kg in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Within the combat sports and martial arts family on this site, its closest relatives are Wrestling, Freestyle Wrestling, Sumo, Judo, Boxing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The official rulebook is the UWW Wrestling Rules, published by United World Wrestling; the bout takes place on a mat with a 9 m diameter competition circle, wrestlers wear a one-piece singlet, and each bout consists of two periods of three minutes with a 30-second break (UWW, n.d.-b). Each period starts standing; after a score the bout resumes in the standing or parterre (kneeling) position as decided by the referee. Scoring: a takedown is worth 1–2 points, turning the opponent's back to the mat with exposure 2 points, a throw with great amplitude 4–5 points, and a penalty point is awarded for fouls such as illegal holds below the waist, leg attacks, fleeing the mat, passivity or blocking — cautions also award points to the opponent and repeated offences can lead to disqualification (UWW, n.d.-b). A fall — both shoulder blades held on the mat simultaneously — wins the bout immediately, and an eight-point lead ends the bout by technical superiority in Greco-Roman (against ten in freestyle); if neither occurs, the wrestler with more points wins, with the more active wrestler preferred in case of a tie (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; UWW, n.d.-b). The competition programme includes the Olympic Games (men's Greco-Roman since Athens 1896), the annual World Championships with repechage, continental championships, the UWW ranking series, and U23 and U17 age groups with adapted weight classes and rules; weigh-ins take place the day before competition under medical supervision and WADA anti-doping rules apply (UWW, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.). Gender and age differences: Greco-Roman is contested by men only at senior level, while women's wrestling at UWW events is freestyle only — women have been Olympic wrestlers since Athens 2004; youth categories (U17, U20, U23) use modified rules and lighter mats for the younger classes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; UWW, n.d.-b).

History

Wrestling is among the oldest sports, depicted in ancient Greek art and part of the ancient Olympic programme from 708 BC, but the "Greco-Roman" label dates from the 19th century, when the style was codified in France and the German lands and named to distinguish it from the looser "catch-as-catch-can" freestyle; it was the only wrestling style at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896, and freestyle was added in 1904 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Amateur Wrestling Federation (FILA), the predecessor of today's UWW, was founded in 1912, and World Greco-Roman Championships have been held since 1904 (UWW, n.d.-a). Among the icons of the style are Ivan Poddubny (1871–1949), the Russian strongman who won six world titles in the early 1900s and became a national legend, Aleksandr Karelin of the Soviet Union/Russia (b. 1967), who won three consecutive Olympic gold medals (1988, 1992, 1996) and nine world titles at heavyweight — the "Experiment" — and Riza Kayaalp of Türkiye (b. 1989), Olympic champion at London 2012 and five-time world champion (2011, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2022), who moved up to 130 kg and was chasing a sixth world title at the 2026 championships in Astana (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). On the women's side, which competes freestyle, Kaori Icho of Japan won four consecutive Olympic gold medals from 2004 to 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
