Greco-Roman Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Greco-Roman wrestling is the Olympic wrestling style in which holds below the waist are forbidden — only the upper body may be attacked — and takedowns, throws and exposing the opponent's back score points; a fall (pin) wins immediately. It is governed by United World Wrestling (UWW), and it was the only wrestling style at the first modern Olympics in Athens 1896; it has been at every Games since.

Rules

Under UWW Greco-Roman rules, a match is two 3-minute periods. Scoring: takedown 2-5 points depending on amplitude, exposure (rolling the opponent's back to the mat) 2-5 points, reversal 1-2, penalties for passivity/fleeing 1 point to the opponent. A fall — both shoulder blades on the mat — ends the match; technical superiority (10-point lead) also wins. Leg attacks, holds below the waist, leg locks and chokeholds are prohibited and penalized (warning, then 1-2 points to the opponent, possible disqualification). Matches are on a 9 m diameter mat (12 m including the safety area), with a 1 m red zone; stepping into the red zone while standing gives a point to the opponent. Video review (challenge) is used at major events.

History

Greco-Roman wrestling revives the ancient Greek and Roman style and was included at the 1896 Athens Olympics (won by Germany's Carl Schuhmann). The FILA (founded 1924, now UWW) has organized World Championships since 1922. The Soviet Union/Russia dominated historically with legends such as Aleksandr Karelin (three-time Olympic champion, 887-2 career record), while Sweden, Finland, Hungary, Turkey and Cuba also produced greats. Women's Greco-Roman is not an Olympic event (women compete freestyle), but UWW holds World Championships. Greco-Roman specialists include Aleksandr Karelin (three Olympic golds, an 887-2 career record) and Roman Vlasov of Russia (Olympic golds 2012 and 2016).
