Mountainboarding

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mountainboarding is off-road riding of a mountainboard on grass, dirt, gravel and downhill courses, governed internationally by the ATB (All Terrain Boarding) scene with the International Mountainboard World Championships; disciplines include downhill (timed), boardercross (mass start with jumps), slopestyle and kite-assisted riding (ATB, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Grass and dirt courses are built at dedicated centers, and top downhill speeds exceed 60 km/h; men's and women's categories are contested.

Figure: Ricardo Villela from Belo Horizonte, Brazil. (2005-08-19 17:53). Mountainboarder on a downhill course (Biagioni by Villela) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mountainboarding_Biagioni_by_Villela.jpg

Within the board-sports family on this site its closest relatives are Landboarding, Dirtboarding, Kite Landboarding, Skateboarding and Snowboarding — the mountainboard is the summer snowboard; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Competition rules (ATB/World Championships): downhill timed runs with full-face helmet, gloves and pads; boardercross four riders per race over jumps and berms, top two advance; slopestyle judged on tricks; equipment: mountainboard with 8-inch wheels, optional bindings; age and gender categories; course inspection before runs (ATB, n.d.).

History

Mountainboarding emerged from landboarding in the late 1990s in the UK, where dedicated downhill centers opened on hills and in forests; the first world championships were held in the 2000s and the sport is strongest in the UK, with scenes in New Zealand, the USA and Germany (Wikipedia, n.d.).
