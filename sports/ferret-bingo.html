Ferret Bingo

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ferret bingo is a traditional British pub game in which numbered ferrets are placed in a cage or container and "called" like bingo numbers, with players holding cards marked with ferret numbers; the owner of the called ferret wins a prize. It is organized and promoted by the British Ferret Club and played at ferret shows, country fairs and charity events across the UK.

Rules

In the standard game, a set of ferrets (each marked with a number, traditionally by a colored tag or by identification) is placed in a bingo cage or box. The "caller" draws or announces numbers, and each ferret's number is called as it is identified (or as the numbers are drawn). Players hold bingo cards with numbers corresponding to the ferrets; the first player to complete a line (or full house, depending on the house rules) shouts "Bingo!" and wins the prize, usually a ferret-themed prize or cash at charity events. House rules vary between pubs — some versions call the ferrets by name rather than number, and some use the ferrets themselves drawn from a bag. The British Ferret Club promotes fair play and ferret welfare standards at such events.

History

Ferret bingo became a staple of British pub entertainment in the mid-20th century, particularly in East Anglia and the Midlands where ferret keeping is traditional (ferrets have been used for rabbiting in Britain since the Middle Ages). The British Ferret Club, founded to promote ferret welfare and showing, sanctions ferret games including bingo at its events and shows. The game is also played at charity fundraisers, where the prizes can be substantial, and has been featured in BBC regional programming and local newspapers. It remains a nostalgic feature of village fêtes and working-men's clubs.
