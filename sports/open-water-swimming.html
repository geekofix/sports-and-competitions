Open Water Swimming

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Open water swimming, also called marathon swimming, is competitive swimming in natural bodies of water such as lakes, rivers, bays and the sea, over distances from about 1,500 m up to 25 km. It is governed worldwide by World Aquatics and became an Olympic discipline with the 10 km race at the Beijing 2008 Games. The elite calendar includes the World Aquatics Championships, the Open Water Swimming World Cup and a 3 km knockout sprint format. It is closely related to pool swimming and to the open-water leg of triathlon.

Rules

Championship races are held over 5 km, 10 km and 25 km, plus a 3 km knockout sprint and a mixed team relay in which two men and two women each swim 1.25 km. Races are started with a mass start from a pontoon or the beach, timed electronically at start and finish, and swimmers must follow the marked course; touching another swimmer, a boat or a buoy out of the course, or receiving assistance, leads to disqualification. Feeding stations are allowed in races of 5 km and longer. Water temperature must be between 16 and 31 degrees Celsius; between 16 and 18 degrees the swimmer must wear a foam suit covering the shoulders, and above 18 degrees standard racing suits are worn, with high-performance suits not approved since 2024. Wetsuits are permitted only within the temperature bands set by World Aquatics.

History

Long-distance open water swimming has roots in endurance feats such as Captain Matthew Webb's first crossing of the English Channel in 1875 and Gertrude Ederle becoming the first woman to cross from England to France in 1926. Open water swimming became an official FINA discipline at the 1991 World Championships in Perth, where men and women raced 25 km, and the 10 km event entered the Olympic program at Beijing 2008. The Netherlands has been the most successful Olympic nation, with Maarten van der Weijden (2008), Ferry Weertman (2016) and Sharon van Rouwendaal (2016 and 2024) winning gold, while Italy's Gregorio Paltrinieri dominated the 2020s with 5 km and 10 km titles plus the 3 km knockout sprint at the 2025 World Championships. Tunisia's Oussama Mellouli made history at London 2012 by winning Olympic gold in both the pool 1500 m and the 10 km open water race.
