Open Water Swimming

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Open water swimming, also called marathon swimming, is competitive swimming in natural bodies of water such as lakes, rivers, bays and the sea, over distances from about 1,500 m up to 25 km (World Aquatics, n.d.). It is governed worldwide by World Aquatics and became an Olympic discipline with the 10 km race at the Beijing 2008 Games; men and women compete in separate events under identical rules (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). The elite calendar includes the World Aquatics Championships, the Open Water Swimming World Cup and a 3 km knockout sprint format (World Aquatics, n.d.). It is closely related to pool swimming and to the open-water leg of triathlon.

Together with Swimming, this is an open-water discipline, alongside Marathon Swimming and pool Swimming — and the full aquatics list is under Important Links.

Rules

Championship races are held over 5 km, 10 km and 25 km, plus a 3 km knockout sprint and a mixed team relay in which two men and two women each swim 1.25 km (World Aquatics, n.d.). Races are started with a mass start from a pontoon or the beach, timed electronically at start and finish, and swimmers must follow the marked course; touching another swimmer, a boat or a buoy out of the course, or receiving assistance, leads to disqualification (World Aquatics, n.d.). Feeding stations are allowed in races of 5 km and longer, where support crews hand food and drinks from boats (World Aquatics, n.d.). Water temperature must be between 16 and 31 degrees Celsius; between 16 and 18 degrees the swimmer must wear a full foam suit, and below 16 degrees the race is cancelled for safety (World Aquatics, n.d.). Men's and women's races use the same distances, rules and temperature limits, differing only in the start order and heat allocation (International Olympic Committee, n.d.).

History

Long-distance swimming in open water has been practiced for centuries — the first recorded crossing of the English Channel was by Captain Matthew Webb in 1875 — and marathon swimming became an Olympic event at Beijing 2008 with the 10 km race in open water, won by Maarten van der Weijden in the men's and Britta Steffen in the women's race (International Olympic Committee, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). World Aquatics (formerly FINA), founded in 1908, governs the sport and runs the World Championships and the World Cup circuit; the 25 km is the longest championship distance (World Aquatics, n.d.). Record crossings such as the Channel, the Tsugaru Strait and the Oceans Seven challenge series keep the endurance tradition alive alongside the competitive program (Wikipedia, n.d.).
