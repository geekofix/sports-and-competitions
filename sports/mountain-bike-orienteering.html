Mountain Bike Orienteering

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mountain bike orienteering (MTB-O or MTBO) is one of the four official disciplines of orienteering, in which competitors navigate by map and compass on a mountain bike along trails and forest tracks instead of on foot. Riders may not leave the trail network, carry their map on a map board fixed to the handlebars, and must punch a series of control points in the correct order in the shortest time. The discipline is administered by the International Orienteering Federation (IOF) and started at club level in the late 1980s in countries with a strong mountain biking culture.

Rules

In individual races (sprint, middle distance and long distance), each competitor starts at intervals, follows a course of around 25 control points marked on a specialized MTBO map, and punches each control electronically; the fastest valid time wins, and mis-punching or missing a control means disqualification. The mass start discipline starts all riders together, with "forkings" on the course forcing individual route choices, and a relay involves teams of three legs. IOF rules forbid using electronic navigation aids during the race; GPS devices may only log the route for later analysis. At the annual World MTB Orienteering Championships, the program includes sprint, middle, long, mass start and relay races for men and women.

History

MTB orienteering grew out of foot orienteering in the late 1980s, mainly in the Nordic countries and Central Europe, as mountain bikes became popular for off-road recreation. The IOF, founded in 1961 and recognized by the IOC since 1977, made MTBO an official discipline with its own competition rules, and the first World MTB Orienteering Championships were held in 2002. The sport is strongest in Europe (Scandinavia, the Czech Republic, Poland, Lithuania), but world championships have also been held in Australia and elsewhere, attracting around 25 national teams. European and Junior World Championships are held annually alongside the senior world event.
