Intercrosse

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Intercrosse (also called soft stick lacrosse, softcrosse, modcrosse or pop lacrosse) is a non-contact variant of lacrosse played with a plastic-headed stick (the "cross") and a larger, softer, hollow ball. Two teams of five players (four field players plus a goalkeeper) face each other on a court of about 40 m x 20 m, and the aim is to score goals in the opponent's 1.2 m x 1.2 m net. The international governing body is the Fédération Internationale d'Inter-Crosse (FIIC), founded in Paris in 1985, and the sport is popular in the Czech Republic, Switzerland, France and Quebec, Canada.

Rules

Intercrosse is played without any body contact: a player may carry the ball for at most five seconds and a team must shoot at goal within 30 seconds of gaining possession. Players cover the ball with the basket of the stick and must then pass; opponents must keep a distance of 3 m from a player covering the ball, and zone defense is not allowed. A match lasts four periods of 12 minutes, substitutions are allowed on the fly, and the goalkeeper wears protective pads and a helmet with a visor. Mixed-gender teams are common, especially at the FIIC World Games, which are played annually with randomly mixed teams.

History

Intercrosse was codified in Quebec, Canada, in the 1980s as an educational, non-violent version of lacrosse, using a plastic stick and a soft ball so it can be played safely in school yards and indoors. The FIIC was formed in Paris in 1985 and organizes three international competitions: the annual World Games (since 1987, mixed teams), the World Championship for national teams (men since 1999, women since 2001, biennial since 2006) and the European Cup for clubs (since 2010). The Czech Republic and Quebec have traditionally been the strongest nations. The "pop lacrosse" form is also widely used to introduce children to lacrosse in physical education.
