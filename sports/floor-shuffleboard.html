Floor Shuffleboard

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Floor shuffleboard (also called deck shuffleboard) is a cue-sport-like game played on a long wooden court in which players slide weighted discs ("disks" or "biscuits") toward a scoring triangle at the far end, trying to land closest to the "10-off" area or knock opponents' discs out. It is governed internationally by the International Shuffleboard Association (world-shuffleboard.org), founded in 1979, which runs the World Shuffleboard Championship.

Rules

The court is 6 ft (1.8 m) wide and 39 ft (11.9 m) long between scoring triangles, with 6 ft shooting areas at each end. Each team uses four discs (traditionally yellow and black) of 6 in (15 cm) diameter weighing about 15 oz (425 g). Play alternates: one player slides a disc down the court, then the opponent, until all eight discs are played (an "end"); only the discs in the scoring triangle count. Scoring is like curling: only the team with the disc closest to the head (the 10-off apex) scores, one point per disc closer than the opponent's best (10, 8, 7, 5, 3, 2, 1 and 10-off if the opponent has no disc in). A disc touching the foul line or hanging over the triangle edge ("hanger") may count depending on rules. Games at the World Championship are to 75 points (or 50 in some formats), best of three games in some events. The 42nd World Championship was held in 2025 in Hohenroda, Germany.

History

Shuffleboard originated in 15th-century England as "shovelboard" (coins slid on a table), and the floor version developed in the US in the late 19th century, becoming a staple of resorts, cruise ships and Florida communities. The National Shuffleboard Association of the US was founded in 1913, and the International Shuffleboard Association was founded in 1979 in Canada/Japan/USA to run world championships. The World Championship has been held annually/biennially since 1981. The sport is especially popular in the US (Florida), Canada, Japan, Germany and the UK, with tournament rules published by shuffleboard.net and the ISA.
