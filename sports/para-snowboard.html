Para Snowboard

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para snowboard is snowboarding for athletes with physical impairments, governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation since July 2022 (previously by the World Snowboard Federation and then World Para Snowboard) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Athletes are classified SB-LL1 (more significant lower-limb impairment, e.g. above-knee amputation; prostheses allowed), SB-LL2 (less significant lower-limb impairment) and SB-UL (upper-limb impairment); at Milano Cortina 2026 the women's SB-LL1 riders competed in the SB-LL2 category (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

The FIS programme lists five events — snowboard cross, team snowboard cross, banked slalom, dual banked slalom and giant slalom; at the Paralympics the programme since PyeongChang 2018 (where the sport became a stand-alone discipline after Sochi 2014 within Para alpine skiing) is snowboard cross and banked slalom per class. Milano Cortina 2026 hosted eight medal events at the Cortina Para Snowboard Park (SBX on 7–8 March, banked slalom on 13 March 2026) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c; Olympics, n.d.).

Current stars include Italy's Emanuel Perathoner, 39, who won double gold at his Paralympic debut in Milano Cortina 2026 — men's SBX SB-LL2 ahead of Ben Tudhope of Australia and Jehyuk Lee of Korea, then banked slalom SB-LL2 — becoming one of the few athletes to compete at both the Olympics and Paralympics; among the women, Kate Delson of the United States, 20, won the banked slalom SB-LL2 gold ahead of Lisa Bunschoten-Vos and Brenna Huckaby (2026, March 8; 2026, March 16). Rules

FIS snowboard rules apply with para adaptations: snowboard cross is raced in heats and then bracket finals with four riders per heat on a course of banked turns, rollers and jumps; banked slalom is a timed run through a banked gate course (two runs, best time counts at major events) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Riders use standard snowboards; SB-LL1 athletes may race with prostheses. Men and women compete separately in each class, and the World Para Snowboard Championships (first held in 2015 at La Molina, Spain; 2025 at Big White, Canada) and the Para Snowboard World Cup sit below the Paralympics (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

History

Snowboarding itself traces back to 1960s pioneers such as the Snurfer and became an Olympic sport at Nagano 1998; athletes with disabilities campaigned for Paralympic inclusion from the early 2000s, were added within Para alpine skiing at Sochi 2014 and became a separate Paralympic sport at PyeongChang 2018, where Australia's Simon Patmore (pictured) won the men's SBX SB-UL gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The most decorated Paralympic snowboarders are Brenna Huckaby of the United States (three gold, four medals) and Matti Suur-Hamari of Finland (two gold, four medals); the United States are the most successful nation with 21 medals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
