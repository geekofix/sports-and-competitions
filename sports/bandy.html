Bandy

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bandy is a winter team sport played on a large ice rink — up to 110 × 65 m, roughly the size of a football pitch — by two teams of 11 players who use curved sticks to hit a ball into the opponent's goal (3.5 × 2.1 m). It is governed by the Federation of International Bandy (FIB), founded in 1955, which runs the Bandy World Championship: the 2026 edition, the 42nd men's championship together with the women's championship, was held in Pori, Finland, from January 12–18, 2026 (with some Group B matches at Serwent Arena in Uppsala, Sweden), and Sweden won both the men's and the women's titles — a historic double (Federation of International Bandy [FIB], n.d.-a). Bandy is an Olympic demonstration sport (Oslo 1952) and is seeking full medal status (Federation of International Bandy, n.d.-a) (Federation of International Bandy, n.d.-b).

The elite club scene is centered in Sweden, Russia and Finland, with the Swedish Elitserien the strongest league; the women's game has grown rapidly since the first women's world championship in 2004, and Sweden's 2026 double shows how far it has come (FIB, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the FIB Rules of Bandy, a match is two halves of 45 minutes (90 minutes total) on a rink 90–110 m long and 45–65 m wide, marked with a center line, penalty areas and corner arcs. Teams field 11 players including a goalkeeper; substitutions are unlimited and rolling. The ball (about 63 mm diameter, orange) may be played with the stick, skate or body except the arms and hands, and offside does not exist. Free strokes are awarded for fouls, and a penalty stroke is taken from 12 m with only the shooter and goalkeeper (FIB, n.d.-a).

A goal is scored when the whole ball crosses the goal line between the posts and under the crossbar. Matches tied after 90 minutes go to extra time (golden goal) in knockout rounds; in group play a draw stands. Corners and free strokes follow ice-hockey-style set pieces.

Equipment: players wear skates, helmets are mandatory for goalkeepers and recommended for all, and the stick must have a curved blade of defined dimensions. Men's and women's bandy use identical rules and rink dimensions — the women's world championship has used the same rules since 2004, typically on the same-sized rinks; age classes include U17 and U19 world championships, and para bandy is developing (FIB, n.d.-b).

History

Bandy's modern form was codified in England in the 1880s — the Bandy Association was founded in 1891 in London, and the first international match was played in 1891 between England and the Netherlands — but the game became a national sport in the Nordic countries, especially Sweden, Russia and Finland, where frozen lakes and ponds provided natural rinks. Bandy was a demonstration sport at the first Winter Olympics in Oslo in 1952 (won by Sweden), and the FIB was founded in 1955 in Stockholm with Sweden, Finland, Norway and the Soviet Union as founding members (FIB, n.d.-a).

The men's world championship has been held annually since 1955 (first in Finland), and the women's championship since 2004. Record champions include the Soviet Union/Russia and Sweden in the men's game. On the women's side, Sweden's dominance — including the 2026 title in Pori — has been challenged increasingly by Finland and Russia; the first women's world championship in 2004 was won by Sweden (FIB, n.d.-b). Club bandy's biggest stage is the Swedish Elitserien final, traditionally played in Uppsala before huge crowds, as in the 2021 Villa-Lidköping BK vs AIK final.
