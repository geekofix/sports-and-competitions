Gutte

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gutte (also panch gitti, meergati) is the five-stone juggling game of South Asia: a player tosses five small stones into the air, catches them on the back of the hand, flicks them one by one into the palm and finally catches all five — completing the sequence without dropping a stone scores points. It is played by girls and boys across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Nepal, often together with other stone games, and panch-gitti contests are held at school festivals. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are Kancha, Seven Stones, Chindro; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Five small stones (gutte), pebbles or knucklebones are used. The classic sequence: pick up all five, toss them, catch them on the back of the hand, then flick them off one at a time into the palm, catching each; finally toss all five and catch them all in one hand. Dropping a stone ends the attempt; players repeat rounds and score per completed sequence, with bonus points for catching all five in one hand. House rules vary widely on the exact sequence and on whether the non-playing hand may help.

History

Stone juggling is one of the oldest children's games of the subcontinent and is found from the Punjab to Tamil Nadu and in Nepal and Bangladesh; it is part of the same family of stone games as seven stones and chindro. Girls have traditionally been the most practiced players. The game needs no equipment beyond five pebbles and is still a staple of rural schoolyards and village festivals across South Asia.
