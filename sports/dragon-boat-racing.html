Dragon Boat Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dragon boat racing is the competitive racing form of dragon boat paddling: teams of 10 or 20 paddlers synchronized by a drummer race decorated boats over 200 m, 500 m and 2,000 m. The discipline is governed by the IDBF and also contested on the ICF world circuit.

Rules

Racing follows the IDBF competition rules: 20-paddler boats (10 for small boats) with a drummer and steerer, lane starts over 200 m, 500 m and 2,000 m, heats to finals, and a bow-crosses-the-line finish. False starts, lane infringements and equipment violations are penalized, and mixed crews must meet minimum female-paddler quotas. ICF-sanctioned races run under canoe sprint rules over 200 m and 500 m. Race distances and boat classes vary slightly between national federations, but the 200/500/2,000 m standard is used at world level.

History

Competitive dragon boat racing has its roots in the ancient Chinese Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu) held in honor of the poet Qu Yuan. Modern international racing was standardized by the IDBF (founded 1991, Hong Kong), which held the first World Championships in 1995, and by the ICF, which integrated dragon boat into its sprint program. The sport is now raced on every continent, with the IDBF Club Crew World Championships as the club-level world title event.
