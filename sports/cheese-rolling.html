Cheese Rolling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cheese rolling is the sport of chasing a wheel of cheese down a steep hill: at Cooper's Hill in Brockworth, Gloucestershire, England, a 3-4 kg wheel of Double Gloucester cheese is released at the top of the 200-yard (about 180 m) hill and the first person to reach the bottom wins the cheese. The Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling event takes place every Spring Bank Holiday Monday, organized by the local community, and has been run for over 200 years, with organized races documented since the 19th century.

Rules

In each race (men's, women's and children's heats, plus a down-and-up race), one wheel of cheese is sent rolling down the hill and competitors sprint after it; the first to cross the finish line at the bottom wins the cheese (or the cup in later races). There is no touching the cheese before the start, and the hill is extremely steep and often muddy, which makes falls frequent. Spectators stand behind barriers, and the event has been cancelled or restricted on safety grounds in some years; the official organizers publish the rules and safety instructions.

History

The origins of cheese rolling are unclear; theories include a pagan fertility rite or a custom of marking parish boundaries. Organized races at Cooper's Hill are documented from the early 19th century, and in the late 20th century the event grew into a major attraction drawing thousands of spectators and international media. After safety concerns, the 2010s saw the event run unofficially by locals, and since 2023 the landowner has restricted access, so recent editions are smaller community-run races organized under the official Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling banner.
