Shinty

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shinty is Scotland's indigenous stick-and-ball field game, played 12-a-side with a caman (a curved laminated ash or hickory stick) and a sliotar (a cork-centred ball) on a pitch of 140–170 by 70–80 yards, and governed by the Camanachd Association (Camanachd, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Goals are the only way to score, body tackling is allowed shoulder-to-shoulder, and the men's premiership has been dominated in the 2020s by Kingussie, who completed a fifth successive league title in 2025, while Scotland won the dramatic 2026 shinty–hurling international against Ireland under composite rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The women's game, run by the Women's Camanachd Association since the 1990s, plays identical rules, and Badenoch won the Valerie Fraser Camanachd Cup in 2025 (2–0 over Lochaber, player of the match Rona Stewart) and again in 2026 (5–3 after extra time over Kinlochshiel, player of the match Elizabeth McGregor) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-d) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Among the current leading players, the women's game is headed by Badenoch & Strathspey's Elizabeth McGregor, player of the match in the 2026 Valerie Fraser Cup final, and Rona Stewart, who won the same award in 2025, while the men's elite is led by Kingussie's 2025 league champions and by the Scotland side that won the 2026 shinty–hurling international (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The pitch measures 140–170 yards (128–155 m) long and 70–80 yards (64–73 m) wide, with a goal at each end, and each team fields 12 players over two 45-minute halves; only goals count, one goal being one point (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Camanachd, n.d.). The caman must be made of laminated ash or hickory with a head small enough to pass through a 2.5-inch (6.4 cm) ring, and the sliotar is a small cork ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Players may tackle shoulder-to-shoulder and block with the body, but high sticks, hacking and off-the-ball contact are fouls; the goalkeeper may handle the ball with an open hand inside his own penalty area, and penalties are struck from a spot 20 yards (18 m) out (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Restarting play uses throw-ins, free hits and corner hits under the Camanachd Association's rules of play, and there is no offside (Camanachd, n.d.). Women's shinty follows the identical laws, with 12-a-side in representative games and 8- or 10-a-side in most league fixtures, administered by the Women's Camanachd Association (WCA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

History

The Camanachd Association was founded in October 1893 at the Victoria Hall in Kingussie, with Lord Lovat as its first president, and the first Camanachd Cup final was played on 25 April 1896 in Inverness, where Kingussie beat Glasgow Cowal 2–0 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The game's roots reach back centuries in the Scottish Highlands, and the annual Scotland–Ireland shinty–hurling international under agreed composite rules has been played since the late 19th century — Scotland won the dramatic 2026 edition (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Newtonmore hold the record with 34 Camanachd Cup wins, and Kingussie have dominated the 2020s, completing a fifth consecutive premiership title in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Women's Camanachd Association was set up in the 1990s when Glengarry, Oban and Dunadd began a league, and the Valerie Fraser Camanachd Cup — the women's premier trophy — was first played for on 28 July 2002, won 7–0 by Glengarry over Kingussie (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
