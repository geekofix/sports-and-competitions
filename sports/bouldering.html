Bouldering

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bouldering is rock climbing without ropes at "boulder height" — typically up to about 4-5 m — over padded landing mats, on natural rock or artificial walls. It is governed by the International Federation of Sport Climbing (IFSC), which runs the IFSC Boulder World Cup and World Championships, and bouldering has been an Olympic discipline (combined with lead and speed as sport climbing) since Tokyo 2020.

Rules

Under IFSC bouldering rules, each climber gets a set number of attempts (usually four) on each of four to five boulder "problems" within a 4-minute time limit per problem. A zone hold (usually halfway up) and the top hold determine scoring: a "top" is worth full points, a "zone" fewer, with fewer attempts and fewer zones breaking ties. At the World Championships and World Cup, climbers advance from qualification to semifinal and final rounds. At the Olympics, bouldering was combined with lead into one event at Tokyo 2020 and became a standalone medal event at Paris 2024.

History

Bouldering grew out of roped climbing in the 1970s-80s in Fontainebleau (France) and Yosemite (USA), where climbers practiced on low boulders; "bouldering" as a term comes from the Fontainebleau sandstone blocks. Artificial climbing walls and gyms spread the sport worldwide in the 1990s-2000s. The IFSC (founded 2007 as the International Council for Competition Climbing) governs it internationally, and bouldering joined the Olympic program at Tokyo 2020. Stars include Adam Ondra (CZE), Janja Garnbret (SLO) and Natalia Grossman (USA).
