Throwball

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Throwball is a non-contact court ball sport played between two teams of seven players on each side of a net: the attacking team throws a volleyball-like ball over the net, and the defending team must catch it with both hands and throw it back — points are scored only by the serving side (International Throwball Federation, n.d.-a). The game originated in India, where the YMCA brought it to Chennai in the 1940s as a women's sport, and by 1990 it was played by both men and women; today it is governed in India by the Throwball Federation of India (TFI, formed 1985) and worldwide by the International Throwball Federation (ITF, founded August 2002), with the Asian Throwball Federation (ATF, 1996) as the continental body (Throwball Federation of India, n.d.). Women's teams dominate the international scene — the Indian women's national team are the leading side in senior national championships such as the 44th Senior National Throwball Championship at Ara, Bihar, in March 2024 — and the game is played at senior, junior, sub-junior and mini levels for both women and men (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

The court for senior, youth and junior categories is 18.30 × 12.20 m (60 × 40 ft) with a neutral zone of about 2 m on either side of the center line, and 15.30 × 9.20 m for sub-junior and mini categories; the net is 2.30 m high for senior men and women, 2.20 m for juniors and 2.00 m for sub-juniors (International Throwball Federation, n.d.-a). Teams may have up to 15 players, of whom 7 are on the court at any time; a match is the best of three sets, each set played to 15 points (International Throwball Federation, n.d.-b). The server must serve from the service zone within 5 seconds; the ball must be caught with both hands, may be held for at most 3 seconds, and must be thrown back one-handed over the shoulder — dribbling, dubbing or throwing with two hands is a foul, and the ball may touch the net on the way over but not the antenna (International Throwball Federation, n.d.-b). A maximum of three substitutions per set and two 30-second time-outs per team per set are allowed; faults include the ball landing outside the court, stepping over the center line, or a thrown ball failing to cross the net, each awarding a point to the opponent (Wikipedia, n.d.). Age and gender classifications — senior, junior, sub-junior and mini, each for men's and women's teams — use the different court and net dimensions listed above, and national championships are organized separately for women and men (Throwball Federation of India, n.d.). The official rulebook is published by the International Throwball Federation, and the TFI is the sole authority to frame or modify the rules in India (International Throwball Federation, n.d.-a; Throwball Federation of India, n.d.).

History

Throwball is believed to derive from a recreational ball game popular among women in England and Australia in the 1930s; the YMCA brought it to the YMCA College of Physical Education in Chennai (Madras) in the 1940s, where it was played as a women's sport (Throwball Federation of India, n.d.). Dr. Harry Crowe Buck, the American-born founder of the YMCA College of Physical Education in Madras in 1920 and a pioneer of scientific physical education in India, drafted the first guidelines and rules of throwball in 1955 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game spread to Bangalore in the 1950s, the first national-level championship was organized there in 1980, and the Throwball Federation of India was formed in 1985 under the leadership of T. Ramanna, gaining recognition from the Government of India's Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports in 1989 (Throwball Federation of India, n.d.). By 1990 throwball had become a sport for men and women of all ages, the Asian Throwball Federation was established in 1996 with nine nations, and the International Throwball Federation was established in August 2002 through T. Ramanna's vision, today counting around 72 member nations; the ITF runs the World Cup and the Asian Championships, and the TFI's 34th Sub-Junior National Championship is scheduled for Kolkata, 30 October to 1 November 2026 (International Throwball Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.).
