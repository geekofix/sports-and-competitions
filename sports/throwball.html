Throwball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Throwball is the Indian women's team sport that mirrors volleyball in setup and netball in touch: two teams of seven (originating from a school and college game in India) throw a ball over an eight-foot net, catching and returning it in a rally with no spiking allowed. Popular across India's schools, colleges and federations and in other parts of Asia, it is a recognized national championship sport especially played by women, as its formal description highlights.

Rules

Each team fields seven players on a court of roughly 12 by 18 metres with a net at 2.28 metres: the serving side puts the ball in play from behind the baseline, and the receiving team must catch the ball with both hands and return it over the net within a defined number of passes and seconds, typically two or three passes and three seconds, without letting it touch the ground. Spiking, dribbling, or holding the ball beyond the permitted time are faults; games are scored to a set total of points (commonly 15 or 25 a set, with best-of-set match formats), players rotate positions, and rules govern net touches, line calls and the catching form that gives the game its character. Federation rules standardize the ball, court and match formats for school, university and national championship play.

History

Throwball began as a recreational school and college game in India in the middle decades of the twentieth century, descendent of the wider family of catch-and-throw games; by the 1950s-1980s the sports organization of Indian physical education bodies developed it as a competitive team game for women, with intercollegiate and national championships giving it a formal structure. The Throwball Federation of India governs the sport today and stages national championships for girls and women, and the game spread to Sri Lanka, Singapore, Malaysia and other Asian schools and associations. It remains a fixture of Indian school sport, with university and senior national tournaments each year.
