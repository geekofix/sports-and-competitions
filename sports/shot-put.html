Shot Put

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The shot put is the shortest of the four athletics throws: from a 2.135 m circle with a stop board, the athlete puts — pushes, never throws — a heavy metal ball from the neck/jaw area, using the glide or the rotational (spin) technique, into a 34.92-degree sector. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and, with the discus, was one of the two throwing events at the first modern Olympics in 1896 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world records are 23.56 m for men, set by Ryan Crouser of the USA in Los Angeles on 27 May 2023 (indoor record 22.82 m, 2021), and 22.63 m for women, set by Natalya Lisovskaya of the Soviet Union in Moscow on 7 June 1987 — both standing in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by 2025 world champion Chase Jackson of the USA (pictured), with Sarah Mitton of Canada, Jessica Schilder of the Netherlands (20.69 m indoors in Berlin, March 2026) and Axelina Johansson of Sweden; the men's event remained the domain of three-time Olympic champion and world record holder Ryan Crouser (pictured at Rio 2016), with Joe Kovacs, Tom Walsh and Leonardo Fabbri of Italy (22.95 m in Savona 2026) (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the throws family — Discus Throw, Javelin Throw and Hammer Throw — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The shot is a solid metal sphere of iron, brass or steel, 7.26 kg (16 lb) for men and 4 kg for women, thrown from a circle 2.135 m in diameter with a stop board 1.21 m wide, 14 cm deep and 10 cm high; the sector is 34.92 degrees (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Throws at major championships begin with a qualification round; in the final each athlete has three attempts, and the best eight receive three further attempts in reverse order of placings, with a one-minute time limit per attempt (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The implement must land completely inside the marked sector, the athlete must not touch the ground or the stop board before the implement lands, and must leave the circle from its rear half after the throw (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The distance is measured to the nearest centimetre from the mark made by the implement to the inside edge of the throwing circle's line (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

A foul is recorded if the shot lands outside the sector, if the athlete touches the top of the stop board or the ground outside the circle before the shot lands, or leaves the circle from the front half; a foul voids the attempt (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women use different shot weights; U18/U20 athletes use lighter shots (5 kg/3 kg), and para athletics contests the event in classification classes with adjusted weights (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

Putting heavy stones was part of the ancient Greek games and of Highland games; the modern shot put took shape in the 19th century, and the 1896 Olympic champion was Robert Garrett of the USA (Wikipedia, n.d.). Parry O'Brien of the USA revolutionized the event with the 16–17 m glide technique in the 1950s and became the first man over 18 m; Ulf Timmermann of East Germany threw 23.06 m with the glide in 1988 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Randy Barnes of the USA set the world record of 23.12 m in 1990 — a mark that stood for 31 years until Crouser's 23.37 m in 2021 (World Athletics, n.d.-e). On the women's side, Tamara Press of the USSR won Olympic gold in 1960 and 1964, Ilona Slupianek of East Germany set the still-standing Olympic record of 22.41 m in 1980, and Natalya Lisovskaya, pictured, threw the world record 22.63 m in Moscow 1987 (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-e).
