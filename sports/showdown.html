Showdown

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Showdown is the IBSA table sport for blind and vision-impaired players: two players at a rectangular table bat an audible ball (it contains small BBs) under a centre screen toward goal pockets at each end, using flat paddles. It combines elements of air hockey and table tennis in an inexpensive game playable in any room.

Invented in Canada in 1977, showdown is governed by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA), with world championships since 2009 and its strongest scenes in Europe and North America; sighted players play recreationally, but IBSA tournaments are reserved for vision-impaired athletes.

Rules

The ball must be served so that it bounces off the side wall exactly once before passing under the centre screen; serves alternate two at a time. A player scores two points for driving the ball into the opponent's goal pocket, and one point when the opponent hits the ball into the screen, off the table, or touches the ball with anything but the bat or batting hand, or traps the ball making it inaudible. Players wear eye protection and a batting glove; sets are played to 11 points (win by two) in best-of-three matches, with sides changed between sets and spectators silent during play, and referee-certified balls are standardized by IBSA regulations.

History

Showdown was designed in 1977 by Joe Lewis, a totally blind Canadian, so vision-impaired people could play a competitive sport without sighted assistance, with refinements by Patrick York. It debuted internationally as a demonstration at the 1980 Olympiad for the Physically Disabled in Arnhem, and IBSA ran its first official world championship in Stockholm in 2009. National and continental championships have grown since, especially across Europe, and the game is played from school programmes to IBSA world games as a fast, low-cost sport for blind athletes.
