Pizza Dough Throwing

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Pizza dough throwing, also called pizza acrobatics or pizza freestyle, is a competitive performance sport in which pizzaiolos toss and spin stretched pizza dough, performing choreographed tricks with one or two doughs at a time (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competition formats exist within the World Pizza Championship in Salsomaggiore Terme near Parma, Italy, organized by the magazine Pizza e Pasta Italiana, and the World Pizza Games at the International Pizza Expo in Las Vegas (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitions are open to men and women in separate and open categories; the women's freestyle category has grown steadily at the World Pizza Championship, and today's leading women compete alongside the men for the acrobatics titles. Its most famous champion is the American Tony Gemignani, who has won several world titles for dough tossing along with his cooking championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Competitors perform a self-choreographed routine, typically two to three minutes long and set to music, in front of a live audience, using 10 to 12 rounds of dough (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A panel of expert judges scores each performance on dexterity, difficulty, showmanship, synchronization and creativity; points are deducted for dropped dough. Common elements include rolls, selena (double dough throws), multi-dough spins and signature catches such as Gemignani's "two doughs across the shoulders." Alongside freestyle acrobatics, pizza championships include speed events such as the largest dough stretch and fastest pizza maker, judged on preparation, taste, bake and presentation. Men's and women's freestyle routines are judged with the same criteria; age-group and junior categories are offered at the major championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Spinning dough to stretch it is as old as pizza-making itself, but the performance art developed as entertainment in American pizzerias in the 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tony Gemignani began tossing dough at his brother's pizzeria in Castro Valley, California, in 1991, practicing with sewn-together beach towels, and between 1995 and 2007 he won eight world championships for pizza acrobatics, earning the nickname "the Michael Jordan of pizza throwing" (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The World Pizza Championship in Salsomaggiore Terme near Parma, Italy, organized by the magazine Pizza e Pasta Italiana, has staged freestyle acrobatics since the 1990s alongside its culinary titles, and the World Pizza Games at the International Pizza Expo in Las Vegas run the largest American competitions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport remains a showcase discipline at pizza expos and championships worldwide, with routines now including multi-dough spins of four or more pizzas (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
