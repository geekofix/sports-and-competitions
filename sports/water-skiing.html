Water Skiing

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Water skiing is the sport of being towed behind a motorboat on one or two skis across lakes and rivers, and at the elite level it is a judged sport of three disciplines — slalom, tricks and jump — combined into an overall title; it is governed worldwide by the International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, founded in 1946 and headquartered in Lausanne, which runs the biennial IWWF World Waterski Championships and ratifies the world records through its Tournament Council (Wikipedia, n.d.; International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-d; International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-c). The 2025 IWWF World Waterski Championships were held in Recetto, Italy, from 26 to 31 August 2025, and the 2026 season included the World Over 35 Championships at Predator Bay, Calgary, from 25 July to 2 August 2026 (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-a; International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-e).

Among the athletes defining the current era, the women lead: Regina Jaquess of the USA holds the women's slalom world record of five buoys at the shortest rope length of 10.25 m (2023, after the 4.5-buoy record ratified in 2019), Erika Lang of the USA the tricks record (11,260 points, 2019), Jacinta Carroll of Australia the jump record (61.3 m, 2021) and Natalia Berdnikova of Belarus the overall record (3,177.26, 2012) — and Alesha Naima Putri of Indonesia (pictured) won the 2025 East Java provincial water ski title (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.). On the men's side Nate Smith of the USA holds the slalom record (2.5 buoys at 9.75 m, 2013, with four world titles 2013–2025), Patricio Font of Mexico the tricks record (12,950 points, 2022), Ryan Dodd of Canada the jump record (77.4 m, 2017) and Joel Poland of Great Britain the overall record (2,660.12, 2021) (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-c).

Rules

In all three disciplines the skier is towed by a certified competition boat on a straight course; at international elite level the boat speed is up to 58 km/h for men and 55 km/h for women in slalom, and up to 58/55 km/h in jump with a 1.8 m ramp (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-d). Slalom: the skier on one ski weaves around six buoys on a 259 m course, starting with a 22 m tow rope that is shortened stepwise (18.25 m, 16 m, 14.25 m, 13 m, 12 m, 11.25 m, 10.75 m, 10.25 m, and 9.75 m for men) as the boat speed is raised; a pass score is the number of buoys rounded, and a world record requires homologation by the IWWF Tournament Council with official witnesses and video (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-d; International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-c). Tricks: two passes of 20 seconds each on a fixed tricks list scored by a judges' panel. Jump: three jumps per round from the ramp, the best counting, measured in metres (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-d).

The overall title combines the three disciplines with normalized scoring, and the IWWF also runs age-group divisions (junior, Over 35, Over 45 and older), barefoot and disabled (world disabled championships) events, show skiing and — under the same federation — wakeboarding and wakesurfing; men and women compete in separate divisions under identical rules (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-d; International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-b).

History

Water skiing was invented in 1922 by Ralph Samuelson, an 18-year-old from Minnesota, who tied himself to a laundry line and was towed across Lake Pepin on a pair of boards — he later refined the sport with barrel staves and a ski tow, and the first competitions were held in the 1930s, with show skiing developing at Cypress Gardens, Florida, where Evie Wolford (pictured) pioneered the single-ski slalom course around 1950 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The international federation was founded in 1946, the first world championships were held in 1949, and the sport has been part of the World Games programme since 1981 (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). Rolf W. Schnyder of Switzerland, pictured, was among the dominant all-round champions of the 2000s, and the modern era has been shaped by record chasers such as Regina Jaquess and Nate Smith (International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation, n.d.-c).
