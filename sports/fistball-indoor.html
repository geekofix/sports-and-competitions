Fistball Indoor

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Indoor fistball is faustball played in a gymnasium on a 40 m x 20 m court with a net about 2.00 m high, using the same fist-and-forearm striking rules as the outdoor game. It is governed by the International Fistball Association (IFT) and by national federations (e.g., the German Turnerschaft and Austrian Fachverband), with indoor leagues and national championships in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Brazil.

Rules

Indoor fistball uses a 40 x 20 m court with a 2.00-2.10 m net, teams of five, and the standard faustball rules: the serve is thrown up and struck over the net with a closed fist or forearm, each side may touch the ball up to three times (net block counts), and points are scored when the ball lands in the opponent's court or a fault occurs. Matches are best-of-five sets to 11 points (win by two, capped at 15). The indoor ball is similar in size to the outdoor ball (circumference 65-68 cm, weight 340-380 g, though lighter indoor balls exist in some leagues). Substitutions are allowed per national rules, and the IFT Laws of the Game apply. Indoor play is faster with harder surfaces and rebound walls in some halls.

History

Indoor fistball developed in Central Europe in the mid-20th century as a winter training and competition format for outdoor faustball, with indoor leagues established in Germany and Austria from the 1950s-60s. The IFT coordinates international indoor competitions, and the German Bundesliga (men and women) is the strongest club league, with top German and Austrian clubs dominating the indoor title races. National indoor championships crown winter titles, and the sport is especially popular in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, the Czech Republic and Brazil.
