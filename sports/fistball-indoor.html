Fistball Indoor

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Indoor fistball is the hall variant of Fistball: played in a sports hall on a smaller hard court with a net, it keeps the outdoor game's core rules — fist and forearm only, three contacts, one bounce — while allowing play off the hall walls in some rule sets and fitting the winter season in Central Europe (IFA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed by the International Fistball Association (IFA) together with the outdoor and beach variants (IFA, n.d.). Indoor fistball is strongest in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, the Czech Republic and Brazil, where winter hall leagues and championships are organized by the national federations (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Men's and women's teams compete in identical formats with separate classifications, and youth categories are standard; at the end of 2026 the leading indoor nations remain Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Czech Republic, with the Brazilian clubs close behind (IFA, n.d.).

Rules

Indoor fistball is played 5 against 5 on a court of about 40 × 20 m inside a sports hall, divided by a net with a top edge of 2.00 m (men) and 1.90 m (women), the same as outdoors (IFA, n.d.). The ball is the standard fistball of 340–360 g, and the rules mirror the outdoor game: a maximum of three team contacts, at most one bounce of the ball between contacts, only the fist and forearm may play the ball, no double hits, no catching or carrying, and the attack line 3 m from the net applies (IFA, n.d.). In hall play the ball may additionally be played off the walls and ceiling in some national rule sets, which speeds up rallies; where walls are used, a ball touching a wall remains in play (IFA, n.d.). The serve is taken from behind the back line, and players may not touch the net or cross the center line under it (IFA, n.d.). Matches are played in two 20-minute halves or three 15-minute periods depending on the competition regulations, with sets to 11 points in tournament play (IFA, n.d.). Fouls award a point to the opponent, and substitutions and timeouts follow the IFA regulations; men and women play under the same rules apart from net height and youth ball weights, and U14–U18 classes exist (IFA, n.d.). Fistball has no goalkeeper; all five players defend, which distinguishes it from Handball (IFA, n.d.).

History

Indoor fistball arose in mid-20th-century Central Europe as the winter training form of outdoor fistball, when clubs in Germany, Austria and Czechoslovakia moved the game into gymnasiums; the IFA later codified the indoor variant alongside the outdoor rules (Wikipedia, n.d.). Winter hall championships became a staple of the Central European fistball calendar, and the variant spread with emigrants to Brazil and Paraguay (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today indoor fistball is organized by the national federations under IFA sanction, with national indoor championships in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Czech Republic and growing participation in South America (IFA, n.d.). The women's indoor game has developed strongly since the 2000s, with women's hall leagues running parallel to the men's in several countries (Wikipedia, n.d.).
