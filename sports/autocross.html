Autocross

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Autocross (Solo in the US) is a timed motorsport in which one driver at a time navigates a course marked by traffic cones on a large paved area — a parking lot, airfield or stadium lot. The fastest clean time wins. In the US it is run by the Sports Car Club of America (SCCA) as "Solo"; internationally the FIA runs a European Autocross Championship.

Rules

Each driver gets multiple runs (typically 3 to 6) to learn the course, and the best time counts. Touching a cone adds a 2-second penalty (some organizations use 1 s). Missing a gate or spinning out results in a "DNF" (did not finish). Cars are grouped into classes by level of preparation (Stock, Street, Prepared, Modified). The driver walks the course beforehand; there is no wheel-to-wheel racing. Variants: ProSolo adds a drag-race-style start with Christmas-tree lights; RallyCross is the gravel, multi-car version.

History

Autocross originated in the United States in the 1950s as an affordable entry into motorsport using everyday cars on temporary cone courses. The SCCA formalized Solo events nationally, and the FIA European Autocross Championship has run since the 1960s. It remains the cheapest form of competitive driving.
