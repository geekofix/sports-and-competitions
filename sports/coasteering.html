Coasteering

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Coasteering is movement along the intertidal zone of a rocky coastline on foot or by swimming, without boats or boards. It combines scrambling over rocks, climbing, jumping into deep water and swimming in tidal currents, and is most developed in the UK (Wales, Cornwall, Pembrokeshire, Scotland), where over 100 activity centres offer guided trips. In the UK the National Coasteering Charter (NCC), formed in 2011, advises providers and publishes safety guidance, and the activity is licensed by the Health and Safety Executive.

Rules

There is no formal competition rulebook; coasteering is primarily a guided outdoor activity, and the NCC's "Safety Advice for Coasteering Providers" is the reference standard. Participants wear a wetsuit, buoyancy aid, helmet and grippy closed-toe footwear; jumps into water are only made where depth and submerged hazards have been checked. Routes are planned around tide, swell and weather, groups stay together under a qualified guide, and adventure-race formats sometimes include coasteering stages. The National Water Safety Forum publishes public guidance on coasteering and tombstoning risks.

History

The term coasteering was first used in the 1973 book "Sea Cliff Climbing" by John Cleare and Robin Collomb, and in 1985 Andy Middleton of Twr-y-Felin Outdoor Centre in Pembrokeshire, Wales, turned it into a commercial guided activity along the St Davids coast. It spread across the rocky coasts of the UK in the late 1980s and 1990s; estimates put around 120,000 coasteering trips per year in the UK. The NCC was formed in 2011 by around 80 providers together with the RNLI and RoSPA, and now has more than 400 members.
