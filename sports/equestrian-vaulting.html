Equestrian Vaulting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Equestrian vaulting is gymnastics and dance performed on a moving horse: individuals, pairs (pas-de-deux) or squads of up to three vaulters execute compulsory and free exercises on a cantering horse that is led in a circle by a lunger (Fédération Equestre Internationale [FEI], n.d.-a). It is governed by the FEI and contested at the FEI Vaulting World Championships; at the FEI World Championships Aachen 2026, held 11–23 August 2026, the German squad won the team gold on home soil, with individual and pas-de-deux titles decided in the compulsory, technical and free tests (FEI, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current vaulters are the 2026 Aachen world champions in the individual female, individual male and pas-de-deux competitions, with Germany's squads and the Dutch and Swiss teams behind them (FEI, n.d.-a). Men and women compete together — in individual, pas-de-deux (mixed pairs) and squad events — under identical rules (FEI, n.d.-b).

Rules

Vaulting is conducted under the FEI Vaulting Regulations: the horse canters on a 15 m circle, controlled by a lunger on the ground, while vaulters mount via a springboard, perform their exercise and dismount (FEI, n.d.-b). Competitions comprise a compulsory test of set figures and a technical test of self-chosen elements, each scored out of 10 for execution, difficulty and artistry, plus a free test performed to music; individual vaulters perform all three tests, pas-de-deux and squads perform compulsory and free tests (FEI, n.d.-a). At least two of three squad members must remain in contact with the horse, or the element is not scored (FEI, n.d.-a). The vaulter must wear a helmet, boots and a close-fitting unitard, and the horse must be suitably equipped with a vaulting surcingle and handles (FEI, n.d.-b). Falls and dangerous situations are penalized, and the FEI veterinary regulations apply to the horse's welfare throughout (FEI, n.d.-b). Individual male, individual female, pas-de-deux and squad events are held at world championships, and junior classes run alongside the seniors; men's and women's individual titles use identical tests (FEI, n.d.-a).

History

Acrobatics on horseback are ancient — depicted in Minoan and Greek art and practiced by Roman cavalry — but modern vaulting was revived as a sport in Germany in the 1950s and 1960s, with the first German national championships held in the early 1960s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FEI recognized vaulting as a discipline and has run the FEI Vaulting World Championships since 1986; the discipline was part of the FEI World Equestrian Games program from 1990 to 2018 and remains a championship discipline today (FEI, n.d.-a). The French Cadre noir of Saumur — the national riding school founded in its present form in 1830 — has long showcased vaulting as part of its public presentations (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women have been central to the sport since its revival, with individual women's world titles awarded since the first championships, and the pas-de-deux — a mixed pair of one man and one woman — makes vaulting one of equestrian sport's most visibly mixed disciplines (FEI, n.d.-a).
