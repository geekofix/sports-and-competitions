Equestrian Vaulting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Equestrian vaulting is gymnastics and dance performed on a moving horse, in which vaulters execute compulsory and freestyle routines while the horse is lunged in a circle by a handler (the lungeur). It is governed by the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI), which runs the FEI Vaulting World Championships and includes vaulting in the FEI World Equestrian Games.

Rules

At FEI Championships, competition is in three categories: individual (men and women), Pas-de-Deux (pairs, mixed or same-sex) and teams (six vaulters plus the horse). Each vaulter performs a compulsory set of standardized moves (mount, basic seat, flank, mill, scissors, shoulder stand, free stand, dismount) and a freestyle routine of about 1 minute set to music, judged on difficulty, execution, artistry and the horse's quality of movement. Scores are out of 10 from a panel of judges, and the team score combines the three best individual scores plus the Pas-de-Deux. The horse must be suitably trained, at least 6 years old, and is lunged on a 15 m circle at canter; protective equipment (helmet, body protector, vaulting boots) is mandatory for vaulters, and the FEI Vaulting Rules define safety requirements, horse welfare and anti-doping.

History

Vaulting has roots in ancient Roman equestrian acrobatics and in the Renaissance cavalry training of circles and figures on horseback; it was revived as a competitive sport in Germany in the 1920s, with the first German championships in 1920 and the founding of the German Vaulting Federation. The FEI (founded 1921) recognized vaulting as a discipline, and the first FEI World Vaulting Championships were held in 1986 in Bulle, Switzerland; vaulting has been part of the World Equestrian Games since 1990. Germany, the Netherlands, Switzerland, the United States and France dominate internationally, with legendary vaulters such as Lala Cerovac, Nadia Zülow and the German team squads of the 2000s. The sport combines gymnastics, dance and horsemanship and is popular at riding clubs worldwide.
