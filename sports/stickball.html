Stickball

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Stickball — ishtaboli in Choctaw, kapucha toli, “the little brother of war” — is the traditional two-stick ball game of the Choctaw and other Southeastern Woodlands nations, played with two hickory sticks (kabocha) and a small woven leather ball (towa) that is never touched by hand; it is the forerunner of modern lacrosse and is still played at tribal gatherings (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Wikipedia, n.d.-d). The premier event is the World Series of Stickball, held annually since 1975 at Pearl River, Mississippi, by the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians, with divisions for Pushmataha, Tulli Okchi Ishko, women, open men and men 35+, and the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma has fielded teams since 2009 (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

The game remains central to Choctaw cultural life: the 76th Annual Choctaw Indian Fair in Durant, Oklahoma, runs 14–18 July 2026 with stickball among its headline events, and the Choctaw Nation Labor Day Festival in Tuskahoma hosts a stickball tournament from 4 to 6 September 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma, n.d.-a).

Rules

Each team fields around 30 players on a field roughly the size of a football pitch, with a square wooden goalpost (fabvssa) about 12–14 feet high set at each end; a point is scored when the ball — carried, passed and thrown only with the sticks — is thrown or hit against or through the opponent's upright, and the ball may never be touched with hands or feet (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

A match is divided into four quarters, and the women's division plays the same rules as the men's, with separate women's and men's tournaments at the World Series; age divisions (youth, men 35+) run alongside the open events, so all ages and both genders compete (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

History

Stickball was invented by the Choctaw long before European contact and was used to settle disputes between villages as a ritualised alternative to war; the painter George Catlin depicted a Choctaw ball play in 1834, and his 1846–1850 painting “Ball-play of the Choctaw — Ball Up”, shown here, is among the earliest images of the game (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

The game spread to neighbouring nations such as the Cherokee and Seminole, but 19th-century U.S. government policies suppressed tribal games; the modern revival began in the 1970s, leading to the first World Series of Stickball in 1975 and the establishment of formal divisions, and the sport's influence is visible in lacrosse — the Northeastern one-stick game versus the Southeastern two-stick original (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
