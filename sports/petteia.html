Petteia

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Petteia ('lines game') is the ancient Greek board game played on a grid (8x8 or larger) where pieces move along lines and are captured by custodial enclosure (flanking on opposite sides); it is a candidate ancestor of latrunculi and chess. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Latrunculi, Alquerque, Nine Men's Morris; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology, n.d.).

Figure: Eunostos. (2022-07-13). The ostomakhion (ancient Greek mathematical game), Kotsanas Museum [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ostomakhion,_ancient_Greek_board_game,_Museum_of_ancient_Greek_technology.jpg

Rules

Grid board; pieces move orthogonally along lines until blocked; capture by surrounding an enemy on two opposite sides. Different Greek cities used different board sizes (8x8 and larger) (Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology, n.d.).

History

Petteia was played across ancient Greece (Athens, Sparta, Thessaly) from the 8th century BC; reconstructions at the Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology (2022/2025 photos) show the 'enneas' (nine lines) variant; it influenced Roman latrunculi (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
