Hantis

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hantis — a mash-up of "hand" and "tennis" — is a table sport that combines table tennis with four square: four tables are arranged in a 2×2 square, teams of two play with a tennis ball (called a "Loach"), and any body part counts — hands, head, knees or feet (Hantis.net, n.d.). A team may hit the ball twice per possession and must get it onto the opponents’ side of the square; the other team tries to keep it up, and possession flips when the ball touches the opposing team or their tables (Hantis.net, n.d.). Hantis started in 2005 at Scott County High School in Georgetown, Kentucky, when students in a media class — Thaxton Marshall, Ben Fatheree and Jason Johns — got bored, first building a makeshift ping-pong table from two tables and binders, then spreading the tables apart until four tables became one game (Hantis.net, n.d.; Lexington Herald-Leader, n.d.). Today it is played in PE classes, college intramurals, backyards and beaches, from Kentucky to Western Australia (Hantis.net, n.d.).

The official rulebook at hantis.net defines two modes: freestyle, a rotation game for groups of any size, and competitive, fixed teams of two playing for points — almost every rule applies to both (Hantis.net, n.d.). The most common ball is a fresh tennis ball; other balls can be used on less bouncy tables (Hantis.net, n.d.). "X-Factors" — walls, ceilings, even the legs of the tables — are in play, and the ball may bounce off them with no penalty (Hantis.net, n.d.). Games are mixed-gender and open to all ages, and in 2010 the creators sent 250 Action Packs (brochure, two tennis balls, DVD) to K-12 schools in 15 U.S. states (Bleacher Report, 2012).

Rules

Competitive Hantis: four tables of the same size are placed in a 2×2 square, 3–5 feet apart depending on skill level, teammates side by side and opponents across the middle (Hantis.net, n.d.). Each team gets one bounce on each of its two tables per possession, and a player may hit the ball twice during a possession before sending it over (Hantis.net, n.d.; Lexington Herald-Leader, n.d.). Teams of two play matches of one, three, five or seven games (Hantis.net, n.d.). A point is scored when the ball hits an opposing table twice, or bounces once and hits the ground on the opponents’ side; contact is allowed but excessive roughness is a penalty awarded to the other team, basketball-style (Bleacher Report, 2012). Walls and ceilings can be used as X-factors without penalty (Hantis.net, n.d.). There are no separate gender divisions — mixed teams are standard (Hantis.net, n.d.).

History

Hantis was born in 2005 in a media class at Scott County High School in Georgetown, Kentucky, when Thaxton Marshall, Ben Fatheree and Jason Johns improvised a ping-pong game from two tables and binders and then moved the tables apart, discovering the four-table game (Lexington Herald-Leader, n.d.). The name combines "hand" and "tennis" because the game mixes table tennis and four square (Lexington Herald-Leader, n.d.). A Bleacher Report feature in 2012 introduced the sport nationally, and in April–May 2010 the creators sent 250 Action Packs to K-12 schools across 15 states including Kentucky, Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan and California (Bleacher Report, 2012). The official rulebook is maintained at hantis.net, and the game is now played in PE classes and intramurals from Kentucky to Western Australia (Hantis.net, n.d.).
