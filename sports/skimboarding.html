Skimboarding

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Skimboarding is the sport of skimming a thin board across a thin layer of water at the shoreline — either flat water on the sand (flatland skimboarding) or out to meet an incoming wave, riding it back to shore (wave skimboarding) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport was founded in 1976 in Laguna Beach, California, when Tex Haines and Peter Prietto of Victoria Skimboards renamed and formalised it and held the first World Skimboard Championship at Aliso Beach the same year (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The most prestigious event, The Vic at Aliso Beach, has been held annually since 1976 — the 2026 edition took place 22–23 August 2026 with Johnny Weber winning the men's and Chabe White the women's title (UST, n.d.). The 44th Zap World Championships of Skimboarding were held 7–9 August 2026 in Dewey Beach, Delaware, with 228 entries across the divisions; Timmy Gamboa of Laguna Beach won Pro Men and Casey McCullough of Stuart, Florida, won Pro Women (UST, n.d.). The 2026 United Skim Tour comprises five stops — including Cabo San Lucas and Santa Cruz, Portugal — with the best four results counting. Among the current leading women are Pro Women world champion Casey McCullough and Chabe White; among the men, Timmy Gamboa and Johnny Weber (UST, n.d.).

Rules

Skimboarding competitions are run by the United Skim Tour (UST) and the world championship bodies in divisions by age and level: Pro Men, Pro Women, semi-pro and junior classes, with men's and women's titles awarded separately (UST, n.d.). In a contest, each rider takes a set number of judged runs — typically one run out to the wave and back, plus flatland runs in flatland events — and the best-scoring run counts; judges score on criteria such as speed, difficulty, variety and style of turns and tricks (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Riders start by running with the board on the wet sand, drop onto the thin wash left by the receding wave, and skim in a straight line before throwing the board down at speed for wave riding (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment is simple: a foam or composite board roughly 120–135 cm long with grip pads, and no leash in wave riding; wetsuits and board wax are optional. Because the sport has no single world federation, the UST tour standings and the annual world championships serve as the de facto world ranking (UST, n.d.).

History

Skimboarding was invented in 1976 at Laguna Beach, California, by Tex Haines and Peter Prietto, who had been making skimboards under the Victoria Skimboards label and gave the activity its name and its first contest — the World Skimboard Championship at Aliso Beach in the same year (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport spread along the Southern California coast in the late 1970s and 1980s and gained international reach in the 1990s and 2000s with tours in the United States, Brazil, Portugal and elsewhere (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Victoria-sponsored "Vic" contest at Aliso Beach became the unofficial world championship and has been held every year since 1976, while the Zap World Championships at Dewey Beach, Delaware, grew into the formal world championship, reaching its 44th edition in 2026 (UST, n.d.). Founders Haines and Prietto remain the sport's defining figures (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
