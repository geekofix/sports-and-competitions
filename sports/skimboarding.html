Skimboarding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Skimboarding (skimming) is the boardsport of riding a small, finless board from the wet-sand shoreline out across the thin wash of waves \u2014 and, in the wave-riding style, onto the face of a breaking wave to ride it like surfing. Boards are towelled-down fibreglass or carbon-fibre shapes smaller than surfboards without fins; the sport is strongest on the shorebreak beaches of California and Florida in the United States, Mexico, Brazil and Europe, with a professional tour and a celebrated world championship.

Rules

A wave-riding run begins on dry or wet sand: the rider sprints after the wash of a receding wave, drops the board onto the thin film of water and jumps aboard at speed, skimming out across the shorebreak to catch an oncoming wave, and then riding it \u2014 using the board's rail, body and the wave's energy \u2014 back to the beach or along the wave face, with rail-to-rail carves like a shortboarder. In competition, wave-riding skim is judged like surfing on commitment, degree of difficulty, combinations of manoeuvres, variety, speed, power and flow over a heat, with falls ending a wave. Flatland skimboarding, by contrast, is the skateboard-like branch: tricks \u2014 ollies, shove-its, spins, flips and slide rails placed in the wash \u2014 are executed on the thin water film without wave-riding, judged in trick-competition format; the newer urban style uses fountains, canals and shallow water anywhere in the city. Equipment rules are informal: board shapes and materials (wood for flatland, foam-and-glass for waves) differ by style, and riders wear wetsuits in cool water.

History

Skimboarding's roots are usually traced to the Polynesian pioneers of surfing, and the sport in its modern form surged in Laguna Beach, California, whose steep shorelines and frequent shorebreak were ideal. In the 1970s Tex Haines and Peter Prietto founded Victoria Skimboards there, developing foam-and-fibreglass boards and launching the annual Victoria ("Vic") World Championship at Aliso Beach in 1976 \u2014 the longest-running professional skimboarding contest. Florida developed its own scene and industry around the same time, alongside the first tour series Skim USA, and the United Skim Tour (UST) now crowns professional world champions each season. The 1990s\u20132000s brought the modern wave-riding revolution \u2014 riders like Bill "Sponge" Bryan, Paulo Prietto, Sam Stalder, Brad Domke and Blair Conklin pushed skim waves further than ever, with Conklin and Domke also bringing the discipline mass attention, and the 2010s an urban/flatland boom led by stars like four-time European champion Adrien Raza \u2014 while skimboarding remains both a beach-lifestyle sport and an organised competitive discipline from the Americas through Europe to Asia.
