Basketball

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Basketball is a team sport in which two teams of five players score points by throwing a ball through the opposing team's elevated hoop, which hangs 3.05 m (10 ft) above the floor; the international governing body is the International Basketball Federation (International Basketball Federation [FIBA], n.d.-b), founded in Geneva in 1932, while the top professional league is the National Basketball Association (NBA) in North America. The sport has been on the Olympic programme since Berlin 1936 for men and Montreal 1976 for women, and the 3x3 variant has been played since Tokyo 2020. In 2026 the women's game was headlined by Gabby Williams of the Golden State Valkyries, MVP of the 2026 WNBA Finals after the Valkyries beat the Atlanta Dream 4–3 in the first Game 7 in WNBA history (Williams scored 30 points, 28 of them in the second half), and by Breanna Stewart of the New York Liberty, MVP of the 2026 FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup in Berlin (September 4–13, 2026), where the USA under coach Kara Lawson won its fifth consecutive title at the 20th edition — the first staged with 16 teams (Women's National Basketball Association [WNBA], n.d.; FIBA, n.d.-a). On the men's side, the New York Knicks won the 2026 NBA Finals 4–1 over the San Antonio Spurs — the franchise's first championship since 1973 — with Jalen Brunson the unanimous Finals MVP after scoring 45 points in Game 5, and the WNBA expanded to 15 teams in 2026; the next men's FIBA World Cup is the 2027 edition in Qatar, where Germany defends the 2023 title (National Basketball Association [NBA], n.d.) (International Basketball Federation, n.d.-a) (International Basketball Federation, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the FIBA Official Basketball Rules, a game is played on a court 28 m × 15 m with the hoop 3.05 m high, in four 10-minute quarters; each team may attempt a shot within 24 seconds of gaining possession, and a successful field goal counts two or three points depending on the spot (three points beyond the 6.75 m line) while free throws count one (FIBA, n.d.-b). A player is disqualified after five personal fouls under FIBA rules (six in the NBA), teams may call a limited number of timeouts, and a tied game goes to one or more 5-minute overtime periods. The ball differs by gender: men play with a size 7 ball (75–78 cm circumference), women with a size 6 ball (72–73 cm), and youth competitions use smaller courts, lower hoops and smaller balls appropriate to the age group. The 3x3 variant is played on one basket with teams of four (three on court plus one substitute), a 12-second shot clock and a game to 21 points or the highest score after 10 minutes (FIBA, n.d.-b).

History

Basketball was invented on December 21, 1891, by Dr. James Naismith, a Canadian physical-education instructor at the International Training School (now Springfield College) in Massachusetts, who nailed two peach baskets to a gymnasium balcony and wrote 13 original rules for a game to keep his students active indoors in winter; the first match was played that day between two squads of nine. The game spread rapidly through the YMCA movement, FIBA was founded on June 18, 1932, in Geneva by eight national associations (Argentina, Czechoslovakia, Greece, Italy, Latvia, Portugal, Romania and Switzerland), and the NBA was created in 1946 as the Basketball Association of America before merging with the National Basketball League in 1949. Basketball entered the Olympics at Berlin 1936 (men) and Montreal 1976 (women), and the 1992 Barcelona "Dream Team" globalized the professional game. The most iconic player is Michael Jordan of the Chicago Bulls — six NBA titles, five MVP awards and the 1988 Slam Dunk champion — while on the women's side the WNBA (founded 1996) was shaped by stars such as Lisa Leslie and Sheryl Swoopes, both four-time Olympic gold medalists.
