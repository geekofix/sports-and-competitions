Sheepshead

≈ 1 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sheepshead is a three- or five-player American trick-taking/melding game with a 32-card deck (7–A) and a 'widow' (kitty); the Queens and Jacks are the trump 'barn' (Q♣Q♠Q♦Q♥J♦J♠ = the 'barn'); it was called 'the national card game of America' in the 19th century. It is the namesake of Sheepshead Bay, Brooklyn. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Euchre, Pinochle, Schnapsen; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Pagat.com, n.d.).

Figure: Krenakarore. (2009-09-19). A perfect Sheepshead hand ('Gradma hand') [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheepshead_Card_Game.JPG

Rules

32-card deck (7,8,9,10,J,Q,K,A); 3–5 players; the Q♣ is the highest trump (the 'Gradma', the big queen); marriages and other melds score points; trick-taking with the barn as trump; losers 'sheep' the pot (Pagat.com, n.d.).

History

Sheepshead came to America with German immigrants (Pennsylvania Dutch) in the 18th century and dominated in the 19th century (Wisconsin especially today); the 'Gradma Hand' photo shows a perfect hand (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
