Steeplechase

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The steeplechase is the 3000-metre obstacle race of track and field: athletes run seven laps of a 400 m track, clearing 28 fixed barriers and seven water jumps — four barriers and one water jump per lap, 35 obstacles in total — with the water jump placed on the inside of the track in the standard layout (World Athletics, n.d.). It is governed by World Athletics under the Competition Rules, standardized in its modern form in 1954, and contested at the Olympics for men since Antwerp 1920 and for women since Beijing 2008 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The world records are 7:52.11 for men, set by Kenya's Lamecha Girma at the Paris 2024 final, and 8:44.32 for women, set by Kenya's Beatrice Chepkoech in Monaco in 2018; at the Paris 2024 Olympics, Bahrain's Winfred Yavi won the women's gold and set the Asian record of 8:44.39 in Rome on 29 August 2024, adding the 2026 Asian Games title in Nagoya with 9:04.36 as a Games record, while Morocco's Soufiane El Bakkali took the men's Olympic gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the current leading women, Winfred Yavi (Olympic champion, Asian record holder), Beatrice Chepkoech (world record holder), Faith Cherotich, Peruth Chemutai and Kenya's Norah Jeruto top the world list; among the men, Lamecha Girma (world record holder), Soufiane El Bakkali (Olympic champion) and Kenya's Kibiwott Kandie lead (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The 3000 m steeplechase is run on a standard 400 m track with 28 barriers and 7 water jumps; each barrier is 91.4 cm (3 ft) high for men and 76.2 cm (2 ft 6 in) for women, 3.66 m wide and weighs 80–100 kg, and the water jump is a 3.66 m long barrier followed by a 3.66 m by 3.66 m pit with a sloping bottom to 70 cm depth at the barrier (World Athletics, n.d.). Athletes may hurdle or step on the barrier, and knocking it down is not a foul, but obstructing an opponent can be; the race is timed like any track event, with the first three placings decided by finish order, and qualifying heats lead to the Olympic and world championship finals. The event is part of the World Athletics programme with men's and women's divisions at every championship, different barrier heights per gender, and youth (under-18/under-20) categories at junior championships; there are no para-steeplechase classes at Paralympics, where the T54 wheelchair 1500 m serves as the counterpart (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Athletics, n.d.).

History

The steeplechase derives from the cross-country and horse-racing steeplechases of 19th-century Britain, where the 3-mile race with 30 barriers and a water jump was run at the 1900 Paris Olympics in a form later abandoned; the modern 3000 m event with 35 obstacles was standardized by the IAAF in 1954 and entered the Olympic programme for men at Antwerp 1920 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the event now retired include Kenya's Ezekiel Kemboi, twice Olympic champion (2004, 2008) and four-time world champion; Qatar's Saif Saaeed Shaheen, who set the world record of 7:53.63 in 2004; and Russia's Gulnara Samitova-Galkina, the 2008 Olympic champion who was later stripped for doping (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
