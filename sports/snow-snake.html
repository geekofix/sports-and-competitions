Snow Snake

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snow snake is a Dene game played at the Arctic Winter Games: a greased wooden stick (the 'snake', traditionally made of hardwood, 1–3 m) is thrown with an underhand sliding motion so it travels as far as possible across smooth snow or ice; the longest throw wins (AWG, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Long and short snake divisions exist; men and women compete separately at the Games. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are Blanket Toss, Snow Snake, Two-Foot High Kick, Finger Pull, Hand Games, World Eskimo Indian Olympics; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The snake is thrown from a marked lane; it must slide (not tumble) and the throw is measured from the starting line to where the head of the snake stops. Grease (traditionally seal fat) reduces friction. Short snakes (~1 m) and long snakes (up to 3 m+) are separate events (AWG, n.d.).

History

Snow snake comes from Dene hunting and travel culture — the game mimics sliding a spear or a sled runner over snow. It has been a Dene Games event at the Arctic Winter Games since 1990 and appears at WEIO (Wikipedia, n.d.).
