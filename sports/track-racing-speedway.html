Track Racing Speedway

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Speedway is motorcycle track racing's great broadslide: four riders on 500cc single-cylinder bikes with no brakes race four anticlockwise laps around an oval dirt or shale track, power-sliding the corners at full throttle. A European-born, worldwide spectating sport with professional leagues (above all in Poland, Sweden, Denmark and Britain) and a world championship grand prix series, it is motorcycling's purest four-lap duel.

Rules

Riders race from a tapes start over four laps, drifting the bike with the rear wheel spinning through the corners (the speedway slide) on machines stripped to a steel frame and methanol-burning engine: first across the line wins the heat, and matches use the scoring of place points per heat to decide the team or individual winners. Grand prix events run heat-race meetings toward semifinals and a final, and team leagues use pairs of riders alternating races under points-per-place scoring; rules govern the start (breaking the tapes is a warning or exclusion), track conduct (no dangerous riding in corners, exclusion for bringing down opponents) and machine specifications (no brakes, minimum weight, controlled fuel and specified tires).

History

Speedway was born on the dirt ovals of Australia and New Zealand in the 1920s and crossed to Britain, where the first meetings at High Beech in 1928 launched the sport that became a national and European institution. Its postwar golden age built the sport's classic strongholds: the British leagues, Poland's enormous spectator crowds, Sweden, Denmark and the Soviet bloc, and a world championship from 1936 through to today's speedway grand prix series. The sport's highlights (the world championship, speedway of nations and the great league derbies) continue through the modern era, and its identifiably sideways style of cornering remains the sport's signature spectacle.
