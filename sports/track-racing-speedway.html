Track Racing Speedway

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Speedway is motorcycle racing on a flat oval of shale or crushed stone: four riders (sometimes up to six) race four anti-clockwise laps on specialist 500 cc machines with a single gear and no brakes, sliding the rear wheel with the left foot through the turns (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.). The world championship is decided by the FIM Speedway Grand Prix (SGP) series, held since 1995 — the 2025 season was the 80th FIM Speedway World Championship, won by Bartosz Zmarzlik of Poland, who claimed a record-equalling sixth title (with Ivan Mauger and Tony Rickardsson) and the first four consecutive titles, one point ahead of Australia's Brady Kurtz, who set a new record with five consecutive Grand Prix wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete in their own FIM-sanctioned championships, including the Women's European Championship, alongside the men's series, and top female riders race on the same ovals under the same rules (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.).

Rules

FIM regulations require a level oval track of 260–425 m measured 1 m from the inner boundary, at least 10 m wide on the straights and 14 m in the turns, surfaced with shale, granite or crushed brick with grains no larger than 7 mm (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each race ("heat") is four anti-clockwise laps taking about a minute, with four riders (up to six in some meetings) starting from a standstill behind a taped gate; riders score 4, 3, 2 and 1 points by finishing order (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.). The machines are single-cylinder 500 cc methanol-fueled engines with one gear and no brakes — riders brake by sliding the rear wheel with a steel shoe on the left boot (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the SGP, 15 permanent riders plus a wild card and two track reserves contest each Grand Prix; heats, semifinals and a final award points toward the season standings, and the rider with most points after the series is world champion (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.). Safety gear — helmet, leathers, gloves, boots and chest protector — is mandatory, and women race under the same rules in dedicated women's events (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.).

History

Speedway began in Britain in the 1920s and grew around iconic venues such as Wembley Stadium, which hosted its first meeting in 1929 and major internationals from 1936 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The individual world championship ran from 1936 to 1994; its icons include New Zealander Ivan Mauger (six titles, 1976–1989) and Swede Tony Rickardsson (six titles, 1994–2005), alongside Poland's Jerzy Szczakiel (1973), Tomasz Gollob (2010), Australia's Jason Crump (three titles, 2006–2011, and a then-record 23 Grand Prix wins) and Denmark's Nicki Pedersen (2003, 2007, 2008) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Since 1995 the title has been decided by the Speedway Grand Prix series, promoted by Discovery Sports Events since 2015; Bartosz Zmarzlik won in 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, breaking Crump's Grand Prix win record in 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The team competition — the Speedway World Cup (1960–2017, Poland a record eight-time winner) — was replaced by the Speedway of Nations in 2018; Australia won the 2025 edition (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
