Bikejoring

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bikejoring is a dog sport in which one or two dogs pulling in harness tow a cyclist along a cross-country course (usually dirt tracks or forest trails), with the rider steering, pedalling to assist and directing the dog by voice — a summer counterpart to dog sledding and skijoring, using the same pull harnesses and lines with a bicycle as the vehicle.

It is popular as off-season training for mushing teams and as a competitive sport in its own right, with national federations (especially in Europe and North America) running dryland championships, with mid-distance races under canicross rules families (bikejoring run alongside canicross and scooter races).

Rules

Competition formats typically have one or two dogs per bike racing against the clock on marked courses over distances from a few hundred metres to several kilometres, with the dog in a padded pulling harness attached by an inline bungee line to the bike's frame or a specialised antenna (for two dogs), and the rider required to stay with the bike and behind the dog(s). Races forbid riding ahead of the dog, loose dogs, choke or prong corrections and harsh handling, rules on visible race numbers and veterinary checks apply, with age minimums for dogs (commonly 18-24 months depending on the federation), with timings decided at the finish line.

History

Bikejoring emerged from the tradition of sled dog mushing as European mushers sought off-season training and racing in the 1990s and 2000s, transferring the pull-harness line and commands of sledding to mountain bikes on dirt trails. It formed part of the family of "dryland" dog sports that federations from the International Federation of Sleddog Sports (IFSS) formalise in their regulations and weekend race schedules; European countries like Germany, Italy, France and the Czech Republic run national championships, and the sport continues to grow as both a competitive discipline and an accessible way to exercise high-energy dogs.
