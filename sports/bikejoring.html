Bikejoring

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bikejoring is a dryland dog sport in which a rider on a bicycle is pulled by one or two harnessed dogs attached to the bike with a shock-absorbing bungee line. It belongs to the dryland disciplines governed by the International Federation of Sleddog Sports (International Federation of Sleddog Sports [IFSS], n.d.-a), alongside canicross, scooter and wheel-rig classes. Races are held on natural trails and roads over roughly 4.5 to 6.5 km; the rider stays behind the dog at all times and may never ride ahead of it. The 2026 season included bikejoring at the ICF Canicross World Championship in Owińska, Poland (November 5–8, 2026), which also crowned bikejoring and scooter world champions in men's, women's, junior and veteran classes (IFSS, n.d.-b) (International Federation of Sleddog Sports, n.d.-a) (International Federation of Sleddog Sports, n.d.-b) (International Federation of Sleddog Sports, n.d.-c).

The women's side is fully integrated: every bikejoring class exists for both genders (Bikejoring Women and Men about 6 km, Juniors about 4.5 km, Masters about 6 km), and the IFSS continental directors for 2026–2028 include Alena Zbožíková (Europe) and Sylvain Lefebvre (North America) (IFSS, n.d.-c).

Rules

Under the IFSS dryland rules, one or two dogs run in harness in front of the rider, connected to an antenna on the bike by a bungee line of about 2–3 m; the rider may push the bike but must never pass the dog or ride in front of it (IFSS, n.d.-a). Choke chains and prong collars are prohibited; only well-fitting harnesses are allowed. Dogs must pass a veterinary check before and after racing and meet minimum ages (usually 18 months for one-dog classes, 24 months in longer classes). The course is marked, and the fastest time from the mass start wins; classes are divided by gender, age (junior, masters) and dog number, so women and men compete in their own heats over the same course (IFSS, n.d.-a).

Animal welfare is central to the rulebook: mandatory vet inspections, water stations, maximum temperatures above which races are cancelled, and anti-doping controls for dogs and athletes under the World Anti-Doping Code (IFSS, n.d.-b).

History

Bikejoring developed from sleddog and skijoring traditions as a snow-free training and racing variant, popularized in Europe and North America from the 1950s onward; it became a formal dryland discipline when the IFSS was founded in 1985 by the American International Sled Dog Racing Association and the European Sled Dog Racing Association, with headquarters in Brussels since 2013 (IFSS, n.d.-c). Dryland world championships have included bikejoring classes since the 1990s, and the 2023 IFSS Dryland World Championships in Olvega, Spain, drew record entries. The women's classes have grown steadily and are now standard at every major dryland championship, including the 2026 world event in Poland (IFSS, n.d.-b).
