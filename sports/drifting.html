Drifting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Drifting is a motorsport in which drivers deliberately oversteer, losing rear-wheel traction while maintaining control through a corner at high speed, judged on speed, angle, line, style and proximity to opponents. The premier professional series is the D1 Grand Prix (D1GP) in Japan, and internationally the FIA runs the FIA Intercontinental Drifting Cup (since 2017) and the FIA Drifting World Cup.

Rules

In D1GP, the format is tanso (solo qualifying) followed by tsuiso (head-to-head battles): the lead car is judged on line, angle and speed through designated zones (3+ sectors on the course), and the chase car is judged on how closely it follows without contact. A panel of judges scores each run using the DOSS (D1GP Official Scoring System); contact, spinning out or going off course costs points, and the higher-scoring car advances in a knockout bracket. Cars must be production-based with rear-wheel drive (four-wheel-drive conversions are banned), run on a spec tire, and pass technical inspection. At FIA events (Intercontinental Drifting Cup, Drifting World Cup), similar qualifying and battle formats apply with FIA sporting regulations, and national championships follow the same tanso/tsuiso structure. Judging criteria are speed through zones, maximum angle, style/smoke and closeness in battles.

History

Drifting originated in Japan in the 1970s, when street racers (notably Kunimitsu Takahashi, "the Drift King") slid corners on mountain passes (touge); it became an organized sport with the founding of the D1 Grand Prix in 2000 by Daijiro Inada and Seiji Nagashima, with the first event at Tokyo's Odaiba. The series expanded globally, and the FIA launched the Intercontinental Drifting Cup in 2017, with events in Japan, the US, Europe and China. International stars include Takahashi, Keiichi Tsuchiya (the other "Drift King"), Vaughn Gittin Jr. (first American D1GP champion), Fredric Aasbø and James Deane. Drifting is also part of the Goodwood Festival of Speed and X Games-style exhibitions.
