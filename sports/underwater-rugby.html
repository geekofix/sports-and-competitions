Underwater Rugby

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Underwater rugby is the full-contact team sport played entirely under water on one breath: two teams of six divers battle for a slightly negatively buoyant ball at the bottom of a 3.5-to-5-metre-deep pool and score by placing it in the opponents' basket goal fixed to the pool floor, while opponents may hold, drag and swim through one another in all three dimensions (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The game was invented in 1961 by the German diving instructor Ludwig von Bersuda in Cologne as a winter training game for diving clubs, and it is governed worldwide by CMAS — the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques — which has staged world championships since 1980 in men's, women's and youth categories (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women sit at the very top of the sport: at the 12th CMAS Underwater Rugby World Championship in Montreal in July 2023, the Colombian women's national team won its first world title with a clean sheet across ten competing nations, while the Colombian men defended their title for the second consecutive time (CMAS, n.d.).

Rules

A match runs in two halves of 15 minutes in a pool 3.5–5 m deep; each team fields six players plus up to five substitutes, who may enter freely, and wears mask, snorkel, fins and ear protection (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The ball is a rubber sphere filled so that it sinks slowly toward the bottom, and each goal is a rigid basket with a net fixed to the pool floor at the end of the pitch; a point is scored only by placing the ball into the opponents' basket, and an opposing player may hold the rim while defending (Wikipedia, n.d.). Full three-dimensional body contact is legal, but dangerous play — strangling an opponent, pulling off mask, fins or snorkel, and holding a player for an excessive time — is penalized with free throws, penalty shots and temporary exclusion (CMAS, n.d.). Men and women play under identical technical rules in separate men's and women's world championship categories, alongside youth divisions, and the CMAS rulebook fixes pool dimensions, goal and ball specifications, substitution mechanics and the foul catalogue (CMAS, n.d.).

History

Underwater rugby was invented in 1961 by Ludwig von Bersuda, a diving instructor in Cologne, who devised the game so his diving-club members could stay fit and entertained through the winter; the German diving federation VDST formalized early rules and organized the first club matches in the 1960s (Wikipedia, n.d.). From the German clubs the game spread through the diving federations of Europe — and into North and South America — until CMAS adopted it as an international championship sport, holding the first world championship in 1980 and editions since at Essen 1999, Fredericia 2003, Cali 2015, Graz 2019 and Montreal 2023 (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport's modern heartlands are Germany, the Nordic countries (Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland), Central Europe and — since its breakthrough in the 1990s — Colombia, whose men's and women's national teams have dominated the world championships of the 2020s (CMAS, n.d.). What keeps the game unique is its combination of apnea sprinting, grappling at the bottom and split-second surface breathing: it remains the world's only genuinely three-dimensional full-contact ball sport (Wikipedia, n.d.).
