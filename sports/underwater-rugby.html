Underwater Rugby

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Underwater rugby is the full-contact underwater team sport: two teams of six divers wrestle for possession of a slightly negatively buoyant ball at the bottom of a 3-to-5-metre-deep pool, scoring in basket goals fixed at each end of the bottom while opponents drag, hold and swim through each other in all three dimensions. Invented in Germany in the 1960s as a diving club training game, it is contested at CMAS world championships in men's, women's and youth categories.

Rules

The ball is a filled rubber sphere packed so it sinks slowly, and the goals are rigid metal baskets (with nets) fixed to the pool bottom at each end, roughly 3.5 to 5 metres deep in a standard pool. Six players per side (plus up to five substitutes, exchanging freely) play in mask, snorkel, fins and ear protection, and full three-dimensional contact is legal: players may hold, pass and swim with the ball, score only by putting it into the opponents' basket (an opponent may hold the rim), and the rules penalize dangerous play (strangling, pulling equipment off an opponent, excessive duration of hold) with penalties, exclusion and free throws. Games run two halves of 15 minutes, the play cycles between apnea sprinting, grappling at the bottom and quick surface breathing, and championship rules fix pool depth, goal specifications, ball buoyancy and substitution mechanics.

History

Underwater rugby was invented in 1961 by the German diving instructor Ludwig von Bersuda in Cologne as a training game to keep divers fit over winter, formalized quickly by the German diving federation, and spread through the diving clubs of Europe (and national champions crowned in the Central European leagues); CMAS adopted it as an international world-championship sport in 1978 and has run the world championship circuit since, with men's, women's and youth titles. The sport's modern heartlands are Germany, the Nordic countries (Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland; the Nordic championships are the sport's fiercest rivalries), Central Europe and Colombia, and the game's appeal remains unique: it is the world's only genuinely three-dimensional full-contact ball sport, played in breath-holds of seconds and liters of resolve.
