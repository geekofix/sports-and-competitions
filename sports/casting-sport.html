Casting Sport

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Casting sport (sport casting or fly casting) is the competitive version of the angler’s cast: competitors throw hookless flies or plastic plugs with fly, spinning or multiplier reels, scoring for accuracy onto targets and for distance, on water or on grass (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed internationally by the International Casting Sport Federation (ICSF), founded in 1955, which today has member associations in more than 30 countries, sanctions the World Cup and ratifies world records (ICSF, n.d.).

Figure: Adam Sharron. (2009-04-11 13:01). Caster at the casting pools of the Golden Gate Angling and Casting Club, San Francisco [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Fly_Casting_Workshop_at_the_Golden_Gate_Park_casting_pools_%283431879413%29.jpg

The ICSF event program — the "pentathlon" — combines five disciplines: trout accuracy (scored 0–80 points on concentric targets), trout distance, spinning accuracy on the Arenberg and Skish targets, and single-hand spinning distance, with 1 m of distance worth 1 point and plug distance weighted at 1.5 points per metre (ICSF, 2025). At World and Continental Championships these are combined into individual, combination and team scoring (ICSF, n.d.). The sport was on the World Games program from 1981 until 2005 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Equipment is standardized per event: for trout accuracy a fly rod of any brand up to 9 ft with a standard reel, a 2.5 m monofilament leader and a hookless size-10 dry fly; distance events allow longer rods and weighted plugs (ICSF, 2025). Accuracy events are shot from a raised stand (maximum 50 cm above the water) within a 6-minute time limit, in two rounds of three minutes; distance casts must land in a defined sector at least 50 m wide, and the caster must leave the starting point within 2 minutes of the finished cast or score zero (ICSF, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate categories, with junior and senior classes; tackle classes cover fly, fixed-spool and multiplier reels (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Competitive casting grew out of 19th-century angling clubs in Britain and the United States; the American Casting Association has held annual championships at the Golden Gate Angling and Casting Club since the early 20th century (its 100th annual was in 2008) (Wikipedia, n.d.). The ICSF was founded in 1955 to standardize international competition, and casting was a World Games sport from 1981 to 2005 (ICSF, n.d.). Icons of the sport include American Steve Rajeff — four gold medals at the 1981 World Games and long-time all-round champion — his compatriot Chris Korich, and Norway’s Øyvind Førland (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today the World Cup tour and world records are published on the ICSF site.
