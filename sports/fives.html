Fives

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Fives (Eton Fives) is a historic British handball game played in a walled court: two pairs hit a small hard ball against the front wall with a gloved hand, and only the serving pair can score (EFA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The game takes its name and court shape from the buttresses of Eton College Chapel; it is played at Eton and other public schools — Rugby Fives (singles and doubles, four-wall court with a bar), Winchester Fives and Wessex Fives are the main variants — and is governed at Eton by the Eton Fives Association and at Rugby by the Rugby Fives Association (RFA, n.d.). Matches at Eton are doubles-only, played to 12 points, and the national singles and doubles championships are organized by the RFA; as of November 2025 the English national singles champion is Ben Beltrami and the doubles champions are Ed Kay and Dan Tristao (RFA, n.d.).

Fives is traditionally a boys' and men's game at the great public schools, but women's and girls' fives has grown at schools and universities, and mixed pairs play at several clubs; the rules are identical for all genders (EFA, n.d.).

Rules

An Eton Fives court is an enclosed space of roughly 9 × 6 m (30 × 20 ft) with a front wall, side walls and a stepped rear wall; the left wall carries a sloping buttress (the "pepper-box") and there is a step — two levels — at the back of the court, plus ledges along the walls (EFA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The front wall is marked with a horizontal line and a short vertical line; a served ball must strike the front wall above the marked line before bouncing, and during a rally the ball must be hit "up" — above the ledge level on the front wall — before its second bounce, otherwise the rally is lost (EFA, n.d.). Players wear padded gloves and use a small, hard leather-cork ball about the size and weight of a golf ball (EFA, n.d.). A point is scored only by the serving pair: if the server wins the rally the score advances, if the receiver wins it the serve changes ("hand out"); games are played to 12 points, and at 10–10 or 11–11 a "set" or "step" is played to decide the game (EFA, n.d.). Doubles only is the rule at Eton; Rugby Fives is played as singles or doubles on a four-wall court with a horizontal bar across the front wall instead of the buttress, and Winchester Fives is a singles game on a three-wall court (RFA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Volleying the ball before the bounce is allowed; faults are the ball not going "up", the ball striking the buttress or a ledge illegally, one player hitting the ball twice, or obstructing an opponent (EFA, n.d.). Matches are officiated by a marker who calls the score; there are no formal cards (EFA, n.d.).

History

The game is named after the buttresses of Eton College Chapel, where schoolboys are said to have played a handball game against the walls from the 18th century; the first purpose-built fives courts were constructed in 1840 under headmaster Edward Craven Hawtrey, who built four courts on the Eton Wick road (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first written rules — "Rules of the Game of Fives as played at Eton" — were codified in 1877 by a committee including A. C. Ainger (EFA, n.d.). The game spread to other public schools with local court adaptations: Rugby Fives (with the bar) arose at Rugby School under Thomas Arnold in the 1830s–40s, Winchester College developed its own singles court, and Wessex Fives was codified at Warminster School in 1787 (RFA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). "Bat fives", played with a wooden bat rather than the hand, was popular in the 19th century but died out around 1903 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Eton Fives Association runs the school's league and the annual Fives matches, and the Rugby Fives Association organizes the national championships for the Rugby code; women's fives has been played at several schools since the late 20th century and is growing at university level (EFA, n.d.).
