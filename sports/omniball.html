Omniball

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Omniball is a four-team ball game played on a rectangular trampoline court divided into four quadrants, one per team: teams serve the ball into an opponent’s quadrant and score when the ball cannot be returned (Omniball, n.d.). It belongs to the net/court team ball family with volleyball, bossaball and kin-ball — the trampoline surface is what makes it distinct, similar to bossaball’s trampolines.

Figure: Eric Koch for Anefo. (1968-11-30). Players bouncing on the trampoline court used for Omniball [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wereldkampioenschappen_trampoline-springen_te_Amersfoort_aktie-opnamen,_Bestanddeelnr_921-9037.jpg

A team is four players (up to seven in casual play); the court is a grid of trampolines with a net or line dividing the four fields (Omniball, n.d.). The serve is thrown from behind the back line into any opponent’s quadrant; during play a team may touch the ball up to three times before it must cross into an opponent’s field, and a player may not hold the ball (Omniball, n.d.). A point is scored when the ball lands in an opponent’s quadrant and cannot be played, or on an opponent’s error (Omniball, n.d.).

Rules

Games are played to 11 or 15 points, win by two, best of three sets at tournament level (Omniball, n.d.). Safety: the trampoline frames are padded, players wear grippy shoes, and the surrounding area is clear (Omniball, n.d.). Mixed and single-gender teams play; school and club tournaments are widespread in Germany, where the game was developed.

History

Omniball was developed in Germany in the early 2010s as a school and club sport combining trampolining with a team ball game; the official rules and equipment are published by the Omniball association (omniball.de), and the game is now played in schools, clubs and tournaments across Europe (Omniball, n.d.).
