Swimming

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Swimming is both the essential life skill and one of the oldest and largest Olympic sports: athletes race freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke and butterfly over distances from 50 metres in the pool to open-water marathons of 10 kilometres. Governed internationally by World Athletics's aquatic counterpart World Aquatics (formerly FINA), it is the centerpiece sport of the Games and one of the world's most participated recreations.

Rules

Pool racing runs four strokes over 50 to 1500 metres (plus the individual medley combining all four), with starts from blocks, strict stroke rules enforced by turn judges, and finishes judged to the hundredth of a second by electronic touchpads. Competition formats use heats and finals, world records require water-depth, temperature and timing standards, and the rules permit underwater phases limited to fifteen metres per lap off the starts and walls; relays (medley and freestyle) complete the program, while open-water events race 5, 10 and 25-kilometre courses in seas, rivers and lakes under escort and safety rules. Masters age-group swimming extends competition across the whole of life, and doping-control, swimsuit and equipment regulations are standardized worldwide by World Aquatics.

History

Humans have swum since prehistory and antiquity, but modern competitive swimming organized in nineteenth-century Britain and Australia, where the first swimming associations founded the sport's first clubs and codified freestyle, breaststroke and backstroke racing. The modern stroke set was complete by the mid-twentieth century (butterfly split from breaststroke in the 1950s after a decade of loophole arguments over its legality and records) and the first World Aquatics championships followed in 1973. The Olympic era made swimming the Games' marquee sport: champions from Johnny Weissmuller and Dawn Fraser through Mark Spitz's seven golds and Mark Phelps's eight at Beijing 2008 to the freestyle dynasties of today are legends of the sport, and federations at every level, from club meets to the World Aquatics Championships and the open-water World Cup circuit, keep racing growing worldwide.
