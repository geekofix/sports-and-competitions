Swimming

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Swimming is the sport of racing through water in a pool or open water over set distances in four strokes — freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke and butterfly — plus individual medley and relays. It is governed worldwide by World Aquatics (founded in 1908 as FINA, renamed in December 2022, headquartered in Budapest), which also oversees diving, high diving, open water swimming, artistic swimming and water polo (World Aquatics, n.d.). Swimming is a core Olympic sport with 35 pool events at Paris 2024 at Paris La Défense Arena, where the women's sprint stars led the way: Sweden's Sarah Sjöström won gold in both the 50 m and 100 m freestyle — the first woman to complete the sprint double since 1988 — setting an Olympic record of 23.66 in the 50 m semi before winning in 23.71 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The USA's Katie Ledecky is the most decorated female swimmer in Olympic history with 14 medals and 9 golds, and holds 18 individual world titles (Olympics, n.d.). On the men's side, China's Pan Zhanle set a 100 m freestyle world record of 46.40 at Paris 2024, and China's 4×100 m medley relay won in 3:27.46 — the first non-USA victory in the event since Rome 1960 — while France's Léon Marchand took four golds, including world records in the 200 m and 400 m individual medley (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Current world records include Cameron McEvoy's 50 m freestyle 20.88 (2026), set at the China Open (World Aquatics, n.d.).

Rules

Pool racing takes place in 50 m long-course (LCM) or 25 m short-course (SCM) pools certified by World Aquatics, with eight lanes, starting blocks (backstroke starts from in-water grips) and electronic touchpads; events range from 50 m to 1500 m freestyle, 100/200 m in each stroke, 200/400 m individual medley and 4×100/4×200 m freestyle and medley relays (World Aquatics, n.d.). Races run as heats, semifinals and finals; a false start disqualifies, turns must be touch-turns, and swimmers may not swim underwater more than 15 m off the start and each turn (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Open water swimming — 5 km, 10 km marathon and 25 km — is raced in lakes, rivers and the sea with feeding stations and GPS-checked courses (World Aquatics, n.d.). Men and women contest identical events and distances; the mixed 4×100 m medley relay was added at Tokyo 2020, and junior world championships run separate age groups (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Competitive swimming emerged in 19th-century Britain — the National Swimming Society held races from the 1830s — and swimming has been on the Olympic programme since Athens 1896 for men and since Stockholm 1912 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). FINA was founded in London in 1908 during the Olympics, and the sport's Olympic icons include the USA's Mark Spitz (nine golds, Munich 1972), the German women of the 1970s–80s, Alexander Popov, Ian Thorpe, Michael Phelps (23 Olympic golds, the most decorated Olympian ever) and, on the women's side, Dawn Fraser, Kornelia Ender, and the sprint queens Inge de Bruijn and Sarah Sjöström (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
