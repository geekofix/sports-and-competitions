Dock Diving

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dock diving is a competitive sport in which dogs run down a dock and leap into a pool, judged on distance jumped, height of the leap or speed of retrieval. The leading organization is DockDogs, which runs the DockDogs World Championship and regional events across North America and Europe, while the USDAA (United States Dog Agility Association) also sanctions dock jumping competitions as part of its performance program.

Rules

The dog starts at the end of a dock (typically 24 feet / about 7.3 m long, 8 feet / 2.4 m high) and jumps after a toy or bumper thrown by the handler. Distance events measure the jump from the dock edge to where the dog's tail base breaks the water surface, with separate classes by dog size (e.g., big air over 24 ft, 20 ft, 16 ft and smaller). Speed events (Splash & Dash) time the dog's run, jump and retrieval of a toy from the water. Dogs compete in heats, usually with two jumps each, and the longest or fastest valid attempt counts. Dogs must be a minimum age (usually 12 months at national events), and safety rules cover water conditions, non-slip surfaces and veterinary checks at major championships.

History

Dock diving grew out of casual dock jumping by retrievers and sporting dogs in the United States in the early 2000s. DockDogs was founded in 2003 by Grant Suskie and quickly standardized the sport with size classes and a world championship, which has been held annually in Decatur, Alabama, drawing hundreds of dogs. The USDAA added dock jumping to its agility program, broadening participation in Europe and North America. The sport has since spread internationally with events in the United Kingdom, Germany and elsewhere, and televised competitions brought it mainstream attention. Top dogs regularly clear 30 feet (9 m), with world records above 36 feet.
