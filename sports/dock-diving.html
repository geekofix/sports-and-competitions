Dock Diving

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dock diving is a competitive dog sport in which a dog runs down a raised dock and leaps into a pool of water, judged on the distance of the jump, the height of the leap or the speed of a toy retrieval (DockDogs, n.d.-b). The leading sanctioning body is DockDogs, founded in 2003 by Grant Suskie, which runs more than 200 events a year across North America and Europe and stages the annual DockDogs World Championship — the 2026 edition in Madison, Wisconsin — while the USDAA (United States Dog Agility Association) also sanctions dock jumping within its performance program (DockDogs, n.d.-b; DockDogs, n.d.-a; USDAA, n.d.). The main divisions are Big Air (a long jump measured from the dock edge to where the dog's tail base breaks the water surface), Extreme Vertical (height of the leap after a suspended bumper), Speed Retrieve (retrieving a toy within 60 seconds) and the Iron Dog all-around title combining the three (DockDogs, n.d.-b). Junior divisions allow young handlers to compete with their dogs under supervision (DockDogs, n.d.-a) — the sections below cover rules, history and more.

Within the dog sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Canicross, Canine Freestyle, Agility Dog Sport, Bikejoring, Skijoring; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Competitions are run under the official DockDogs Rules and Policies (DockDogs, n.d.-b). The standard competition dock is 40 ft (12.2 m) long and 8 ft (2.4 m) wide, raised about 2 ft above the water surface; in Big Air the dog and handler start at the far end and the jump is measured from the edge of the dock to the point where the base of the dog's tail breaks the water surface, with size classes such as over-24-ft, 20–24 ft and under-20 ft jumps (DockDogs, n.d.-b). Each dog typically gets two or more attempts per round and the longest valid jump counts; in Speed Retrieve the clock runs for 60 seconds and the toy may be placed up to 20 ft from the dock, and in Extreme Vertical the height of the bumper is raised until the dog fails (DockDogs, n.d.-b). Dogs must be at least 12 months old at national events, and major championships enforce veterinary checks, non-slip dock surfaces, water-quality standards and humane handling rules; outside assistance, doping and mistreatment are prohibited (DockDogs, n.d.-b; USDAA, n.d.). Men and women compete together as handlers in the same divisions, and youth handlers compete in dedicated junior classes (DockDogs, n.d.-a).

History

Dock diving grew out of casual dock jumping by retrievers and sporting dogs in the United States in the early 2000s, when handlers began timing and measuring their dogs' leaps off lake docks (Wikipedia, n.d.). DockDogs was founded in 2003 by Grant Suskie and standardized the sport with size classes, a rulebook and an annual World Championship, long held in Decatur, Alabama, which drew hundreds of dogs and helped the sport reach television audiences (DockDogs, n.d.-a). The USDAA added dock jumping to its performance program and broadened participation in North America and Europe (USDAA, n.d.). The sport has since spread internationally, with events in the United Kingdom, Germany and elsewhere; elite dogs now regularly clear 30 ft (9 m) and world records stand above 36 ft (11 m) (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 World Championship in Madison, Wisconsin, brought together the top distance, speed and vertical dogs of the season (DockDogs, n.d.-a).
