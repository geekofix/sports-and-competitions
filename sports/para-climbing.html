Para Climbing

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para climbing is the Paralympic and IFSC competition form of sport climbing for athletes with vision impairment (B1–B3) and physical impairments, contested in lead, speed and boulder disciplines. At the Paris 2024 Paralympics climbing was lead-only for the vision-impaired classes; from the Los Angeles 2028 Games paraclimbing debuts as a full Paralympic medal sport with lead, speed and boulder events, and the IFSC Para Climbing World Championships (held with the able-bodied worlds since 2012, e.g. Innsbruck 2018) crown world champions in the classes RP1, RP2, RP3 (physical) and B1–B3 (vision impaired) (IFSC, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Women compete in the same classes as men — at the 2018 Innsbruck worlds the RP3 podium included female medallists — and the IFSC runs a dedicated para pathway from youth to elite level (IFSC, n.d.).

Within the climbing family on this site, its closest relatives are Sport Climbing, Lead Climbing, Speed Climbing and Rock Climbing; among the Para sports it is grouped with Para Archery and the other Paralympic disciplines; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

IFSC para competition uses the same walls, routes and timing systems as able-bodied climbing, with class-specific adaptations: vision-impaired climbers receive a sighted guide who reads the route before the attempt, lower grades of holds may be marked, and speed climbing uses the standardized 15 m IFSC speed wall with auto-belay. Ranking is per class, with qualification, semifinal and final rounds; ties are broken by count-back of previous rounds. Gender classes: men's and women's events in every class — there are no mixed divisions (IFSC, n.d.).

History

Paraclimbing grew out of adaptive climbing programs of the 1990s; the first IFSC Paraclimbing World Championships were held in Arco, Italy, in 2011, and the biennial series has run alongside the able-bodied worlds since (Innsbruck 2018; Seoul 2025 was the 9th edition with 206 athletes in 19 classes, lead-only). The IPC included climbing at the Paris 2024 Paralympics (lead, vision-impaired classes), and the Los Angeles 2028 Games will add speed and boulder for paraclimbers. At Seoul 2025, Japan's Yamashita Kazuhiko won men's RP3, Brazil's Marina Dias defended women's RP3, Aita Sho took his sixth men's B1 title and Emily Seelenfreund (USA) won women's RP2 (IFSC, n.d.; IPC, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
