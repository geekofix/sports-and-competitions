Invisible Dog Show

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The invisible dog show is a humorous novelty "competition" in which handlers present invisible dogs to a panel of judges, parodying the conformation shows of the dog fancy, where purebred dogs are judged against breed standards for Best in Show. Owners walk, groom and "command" dogs that are not there, and judges award prizes for categories such as best gait or best smile, with the comedy lying in the deadpan seriousness of it all. It is a stunt or entertainment event rather than a recognized sport, and it is performed at festivals, fairs and television shows, often in aid of animal charities.

Rules

There are no formal rules: handlers typically present their invisible dog in a show ring or on a stage, describing its breed and virtues to the judges, demonstrating obedience commands, tricks or a gait, and accepting the judge's verdict and any rosette. Performances are judged on commitment, timing and audience reaction, and the fun is heightened by the handler reacting to things only they can see, such as a frisbee being caught or a squirrel in the distance. Real dog shows, by contrast, are strictly regulated: dogs are examined for teeth, coat, structure and movement against the written breed standard, with winners progressing from Best in Breed to group and Best in Show.

History

Organized dog shows began in Newcastle, England, in 1859, when a sporting dog show for pointers and setters was held at the Town Hall; the Westminster Kennel Club in New York staged its first show in 1877 and it remains the second-oldest continuously held sporting event in the United States after the Kentucky Derby. The American Kennel Club, founded in 1884, set the rules and breed standards used at US shows. The invisible dog show is a modern comic invention that sends up this tradition — the joke works precisely because real conformation shows are so earnest, and parodies of them have appeared in comedy films and at charity events ever since dog shows became a fixture of popular culture.
