Invisible Dog Show

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The invisible dog show is a novelty and comedy format built around the idea of showing and handling an imaginary dog on a leash — no real animals are involved at all, so there is no betting on animals and no animal-welfare concern; it is purely a humorous performance game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The concept grew out of the "invisible dog on a leash" toy invented in 1972 by S. David Walker at his workshop at 51 Bergen Street in Brooklyn, New York — the Invisible Dog Art Center — which sold around two million units (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the best-known form is "hobby dogging": enthusiasts run agility courses, obedience trials and beauty shows with their invisible dogs, a trend that gained media attention when the German TV channel ZDF featured the 65-year-old Barbara Gerlinger walking her invisible dog through agility circuits (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On 4 October 2009 the prank collective Improv Everywhere staged its famous mission in Brooklyn, in which more than 2,000 people walked invisible dogs through the streets (Improv Everywhere, n.d.). In 2026 the format lives on in comedy shows, team-building events and viral videos, with women such as Barbara Gerlinger among the most visible hobby doggers (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

There is no official rulebook — the invisible dog show has no federation, because it is a parlour-game and performance format rather than a regulated sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the informal "show" version, a handler presents an invisible dog on an invisible leash to a judge or audience: the dog must be kept at heel, walk a set course, perform tricks such as sit, stay, roll over and jump, and is rated on obedience, elegance and the handler's convincing control of the leash (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the hobby-dogging variant, participants run their invisible dogs through agility equipment — jumps, tunnels and weave poles — and are judged on time and faults, exactly like real dog agility, except that the only faults that count are the handler's visible reactions to a dog that does not exist (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Scoring is deliberately arbitrary: judges award rosettes for categories such as "best coat" (invisible), "best tail wag" and "most convincing bark," and disqualification can occur if the handler is seen talking to a real dog during the run (Improv Everywhere, n.d.). Men and women participate equally; there are no age or gender classes, and children's versions are popular at parties (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The invisible dog on a leash was invented in 1972 by S. David Walker, who produced the toy — an empty collar and leash — at 51 Bergen Street in Brooklyn, New York, where his Invisible Dog Art Center sold roughly two million of them (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The toy became a cult gag gift of the 1970s and 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The performance version was popularized on 4 October 2009, when Charlie Todd's Improv Everywhere organized a mission in Brooklyn in which more than 2,000 participants walked invisible dogs of every imagined breed through the city (Improv Everywhere, n.d.). In the 2010s the "hobby dogging" trend turned the gag into a structured pastime, with agility circuits and shows — notably the 65-year-old Barbara Gerlinger, whose invisible dog was featured in a ZDF television report — and since the early 2010s the format has circulated worldwide as viral video content (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
