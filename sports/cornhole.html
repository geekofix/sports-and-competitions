Cornhole

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cornhole (bean bag toss) is a throwing game in which players toss fabric bags filled with corn or beans at a raised wooden board with a hole, trying to land bags in the hole or on the board. It is played casually in American backyards and professionally under the American Cornhole League (ACL) and the American Cornhole Organization (ACO), whose rules govern the ACL World Championships and the World Series of Cornhole; professional matches are broadcast on ESPN.

Rules

Under ACL/ACO rules, the two boards (2 ft by 4 ft with a 6-inch hole 9 inches from the top) are placed 27 feet apart, front edge to front edge. Each player or doubles team has four bags and alternates throws with the opponent in a frame until all eight bags are thrown. Scoring is by cancellation: a bag in the hole is worth 3 points, a bag on the board 1 point, and only the difference between the two teams' frame scores is added. The first side to reach 21 points at the end of a frame wins (no need to hit 21 exactly); matches are best of three frames. Junior play uses 21 feet.

History

Cornhole's origins are debated, with roots in 19th-century ring-toss games; the modern board game became popular in the US Midwest in the 20th century, helped by a 1903 patent for a parlor game. Organized play grew from the 1970s-1990s with the formation of the American Cornhole Association (1994) and the ACO, and the ACL launched in 2015 and now dominates the professional scene with its World Championships. Cornhole has been pushed for Olympic inclusion and is played worldwide, with clubs in Europe and Asia.
