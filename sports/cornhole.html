Cornhole

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cornhole — also called bean bag toss — is a throwing game in which players or pairs alternate tossing fabric bags filled with corn feed at a raised wooden board with a hole in its far end; a bag through the hole scores 3 points, a bag resting on the board scores 1, and bags knocked off or through by the opponent's throw are removed, so scores cancel (American Cornhole Organization [ACO], n.d.). Two bodies govern competitive play in the United States: the American Cornhole Organization (ACO), which maintains the official rules, player rankings and the World Series of Cornhole with its annual World Championships, and the American Cornhole League (ACL), the worldwide governing body for professional, competitive and recreational cornhole, which runs its own tour and the ACL World Championships (ACO, n.d.; American Cornhole League [ACL], n.d.). The 2026 ACL World Championships were held at the Rock Hill Sports & Event Center, South Carolina, from 27 July to 2 August 2026, with more than 50 events and over $800,000 in payouts across five professional divisions — Top 100, USA Pro, Women's Pro, Senior Pro (55+) and Junior Pro (U18); in Pro Singles, Jamie Graham "Teddy G" won his fourth title (2020, 2023, 2025, 2026) for $15,000, ahead of runner-up Ethan Walker, while Pro Doubles split $16,000 (ACL, n.d.). ACL events are broadcast on ESPN, and the sport is played casually in backyards and at tailgates across North America and beyond (ACL, n.d.). Cornhole is a fully mixed-gender sport: women and men compete in the same events and in dedicated Women's Pro and mixed doubles divisions under identical rules (ACL, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are ACL Pro Singles champion Jamie Graham "Teddy G" in the men's game and the reigning ACL Women's Pro champion in the women's division, with Ethan Walker the 2026 runner-up (ACL, n.d.).

Rules

Under the official ACO rules — followed worldwide — a regulation court is 8 ft wide and 40 ft long, with the front edges of the two boards 27 ft apart; each board is 2 ft × 4 ft of plywood with a 6-inch-diameter hole centered 9 inches from the top edge and 12 inches from each side, inclined at about 12 degrees (ACO, n.d.). Bags are 8 × 8 inch fabric squares filled with corn feed; ACO-certified equipment is required at sanctioned events, and ACL inspects players' bags, with forfeiture of winnings for disallowed equipment (ACL, n.d.). In singles, each player throws four bags alternately from behind the foul line at the front of the board; stepping over the line is a foot fault and the bag is removed. Scoring: 3 points for a bag through the hole ("cornhole"), 1 point for a bag on the board, cancellation scoring — opposing bags on the board or in the hole cancel each other — and a game is played to 21 points, win by 2; doubles use the same scoring with two players per side alternating throws (ACO, n.d.). Formats are round-robin and double-elimination brackets at ACL events, with divisions by age and gender — Junior Pro (under 18), Senior Pro (55 and over), Women's Pro, USA Pro and the elite Top 100 — but the playing rules are identical in every division; there are no gender- or age-specific rule differences, only separate competitive categories (ACL, n.d.).

History

Cornhole's roots lie in 19th-century American tossing games; the modern game with plywood boards and corn-filled bags was standardized in the United States in the late 20th century, and the game exploded in popularity from the 2000s onward as organized leagues formed (Wikipedia, n.d.). The American Cornhole Organization was founded to certify equipment, maintain official rules and run the World Series of Cornhole culminating in the ACO World Championships, while the American Cornhole League emerged in the 2020s as the professional tour and self-described worldwide governing body, drawing hundreds of thousands of players, ESPN television coverage and a 2026 World Championships with more than $800,000 in prize money (ACO, n.d.; ACL, n.d.). The most successful professional of the 2020s is Jamie Graham "Teddy G", whose fourth Pro Singles world title at the 2026 ACL World Championships in Rock Hill made him the most decorated champion of the league era (ACL, n.d.).
