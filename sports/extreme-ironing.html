Extreme Ironing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Extreme ironing is a novelty sport in which participants iron clothing in extreme or unusual locations — cliffs, mountains, underwater, forests, while skydiving or on a tightrope — combining ironing with adventure sports. It is organized by the Extreme Ironing Bureau (EIB), which runs the Extreme Ironing World Championships, first held in 1997 in the Forest of Dean / Snowdonia, Wales.

Rules

At the World Championships, competitors choose an ironing location of their own and must iron a minimum number of items (traditionally at least one garment) while performing the chosen extreme activity, judged on the difficulty and danger of the location, the quality of the ironing and showmanship. Categories have included mountain/cliff ironing, underwater ironing (with diving equipment), forest ironing and ironing while performing other sports (e.g., abseiling, mountain biking). Safety rules require participants to be qualified for the activity (e.g., certified divers, climbers), to use a safe ironing setup (cold iron or properly ventilated board), and to have event insurance; the EIB provides safety guidelines and judges. Points are awarded for location difficulty, technique and style, and the highest total wins the world title.

History

Extreme ironing was invented in 1997 by Phil Shaw ("Phil the Floss") and a group of friends from Leicester, England, who ironed a shirt in their back garden before deciding to take it to more extreme places; the first World Championship was held that year at the Forest of Dean with around a dozen competitors. The Extreme Ironing Bureau was founded to organize events, and championships have been held at Snowdonia in Wales, in the Italian Dolomites, and with underwater and freefall categories. The sport has been covered by the BBC, CNN and international media, and charity versions (e.g., ironing for fundraising) are common. Notable feats include ironing at the summit of Kilimanjaro, underwater in the Red Sea, and while skydiving.
