Extreme Ironing

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Extreme ironing is an extreme sport in which participants iron clothing with a hot iron in unusual and often dangerous locations — on cliffs, underwater, in trees, on mountaintops or while paragliding — combining ironing with an adventure activity (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is organized by the Extreme Ironing Bureau (EI), which sanctions the Extreme Ironing World Championships; the sport began in England and the first world championship was held in 2002 (Extreme Ironing Bureau, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the current scene is centered on the annual EI world championships and national extreme-ironing events, with men and women competing on equal terms (Extreme Ironing Bureau, n.d.).

Within the folk and festival games family on this site, its closest relatives are Cheese Rolling, Egg Tapping, Shovel Racing, Lawn Mower Racing, Bed Racing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Competitors iron a single item of clothing — typically a shirt — with a working, heated iron at a location of their choice, while judges score the difficulty and danger of the location, the quality of the ironing and the overall presentation (Extreme Ironing Bureau, n.d.). The iron must be a real, heated iron — gas or electric — and safety rules require a spotter, a first-aid kit and, for water or height disciplines, appropriate safety equipment; dangerous or environmentally harmful stunts are penalized (Extreme Ironing Bureau, n.d.). Events are run in categories — mountain, underwater, forest, urban and "classic" — with points awarded per category and an overall world champion crowned at the annual championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There are no separate men's and women's classes; all competitors are judged together (Extreme Ironing Bureau, n.d.).

History

Extreme ironing was invented in 1997 in Leicester, England, by Chris Mills, who — faced with a pile of ironing and a desire for adventure — combined the two on a cliff near Hartington in Derbyshire; the first competition followed in 1999, and the Extreme Ironing Bureau was set up to organize the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first Extreme Ironing World Championship took place in 2002, and the sport spread internationally with events in Jamaica, South Africa, Nepal and elsewhere (Extreme Ironing Bureau, n.d.). Notable stunts include ironing at the summit of Mount Kilimanjaro, underwater off the coast of Turkey and while skydiving; the sport has been covered by mainstream media as a comic but genuine extreme sport, and the EI explicitly discourages dangerous or environmentally harmful ironing (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
