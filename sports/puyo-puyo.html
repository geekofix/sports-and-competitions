Puyo Puyo

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Puyo Puyo is Sega's 1991 chain-matching puzzle game (colored puyos drop in pairs; chains of four or more pop and send garbage to the opponent); it is played competitively 1v1 at the Puyo Puyo World Championship and as a main title at EVO (via Puyo Puyo Tetris, on the EVO main stage since 2018) (Wikipedia, n.d.) (Sega, n.d.). Matches are best-of-3 (best-of-5 finals); open to all genders.

Figure: Crazysleepygirl. (30th December 2025). Puyo Puyo Japanese logo [Logo]. Wikimedia Commons. CC0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Puyo_puyo_Japanese_logo.png

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Tetris, Pac-Man, Donkey Kong and E-Sports; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Competitive rules per Sega's official ruleset: four or more connected puyos of one color pop; chains send garbage (ojama) to the opponent; a player loses when their stack reaches the top marker; all-clear (zenkeshi) bonuses; hard drops allowed; best-of-3 sets (best-of-5 finals); open brackets, all genders (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Puyo Puyo debuted in 1991 on Sega's Mega Drive and arcade hardware; it became a Japanese esport staple and joined EVO's main stage in 2018 via Puyo Puyo Tetris (2017) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
