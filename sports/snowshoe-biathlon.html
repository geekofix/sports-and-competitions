Snowshoe Biathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snowshoe biathlon is the small winter sport that combines snowshoe running with rifle shooting: competitors race on snowshoes over a snow trail, stopping at a range to fire at targets in the biathlon pattern, with penalties for missed shots. It is a niche cousin of Olympic biathlon \u2014 which employs cross-country skis \u2014 staged at winter festivals, club events and traditional snowshoe meetings, mainly in North America and the nordic countries.

Rules

Formats follow the familiar biathlon architecture: a snowshoe race over a loop (laps of a kilometre or two) with shooting stops \u2014 prone and standing \u2014 at a target range, with each miss punished by added penalty time, a penalty loop or a deducted point, and the lowest total time (or best points total) winning. As in International Biathlon Union-style biathlon, smallbore .22 rifles are standard in organised events (air rifles at club level), with targets at 50 metres under IBU target sizes; snowshoes are running-style lightweight models with traction crampons, and athletes carry or rack the rifle as in ski biathlon. Events vary from sprint formats (one shooting bout, short loops) to pursuit-style and relay races, often run by snowshoe-racing clubs alongside straight races of the same weekend.

History

Snowshoe racing was an organised sport more than a century before ski biathlon entered the Olympics \u2014 the Canadian snowshoe clubs of the 1840s\u20131880s staged championships, and the Montreal Snow Shoe Club codified the sport \u2014 but the shooting-snowshoe hybrid is a much later arrival, emerging as a festival and club discipline in the later twentieth century combining the fastest-growing winter running sport with the Shooting tradition of winter festivals such as the biathlon-styled celebrity invitationals of North America's snowshoe events. With the growth of organised snowshoe racing under the United States Snowshoe Association and the World Snowshoe Federation's world championships, some event weekends added biathlon-style races (and archery biathlon counterparts exist in the same family), giving runners a target-tested winter multisport. The discipline has no Olympic aspirations of its own \u2014 attached as it is to the recreational snowshoe-racing scene \u2014 but it keeps alive the historic bond between winter travel, hunting and marksmanship celebrated by the old snowshoe clubs of Canada and the French-Canadian trappers who invented the racing snowshoe itself.
