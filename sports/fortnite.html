Fortnite

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Fortnite is Epic Games' battle-royale game with building mechanics, played in solos, duos and trios across the Fortnite Champion Series (FNCS); the FNCS 2026 Global Championship (duos) was held 26–27 September 2026 in Belgium with 50 qualified duos and a $2 million prize pool, won by the European duo Swizzy and Pixie ($200,000 each) (Wikipedia, n.d.; esports.gg, n.d.) (Epic Games, n.d.). The 2019 Fortnite World Cup in New York remains the biggest single esport prize pool ever: $30 million, won by Bugha (16, Kyle Giersdorf) in solos (Wikipedia, n.d.). Peterbot (Peter Kata, Hungary/USA) won the 2024 FNCS Global Championship and is regarded as one of the best of all time, with nearly $1 million in tournament earnings.

Figure: Epic Games. (2017-12-09). Fortnite logo (padded) [Logo]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fortnite_logo_padded.svg

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Apex Legends, PUBG: Battlegrounds, Overwatch, Call of Duty and E-Sports; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

FNCS rules 2026: open qualifiers (Play-Ins from Division 1, plus Last Chance Qualifier) feed two Majors; Major 1 culminated in the in-person Major 1 Summit LAN in Düsseldorf ($1M, 50 duos) whose top 15 qualified for the Global Championship; the Global Championship is a 12-game LAN points leaderboard (placement + eliminations); best-of series not used — cumulative points decide; open to all genders (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Fortnite's competitive scene began with the August 2019 inaugural FNCS ($10M across seven regions) after the World Cup; duos/trios formats rotate by chapter (2026 returned to duos after trios in 2025), and Epic announced in September 2026 that the 2027 team size would be decided by a community vote between duos and trios (Wikipedia, n.d.).
