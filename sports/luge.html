Luge

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Luge is a winter sliding sport in which one or two athletes ride a small sled feet-first down an artificial ice track, steering with subtle shifts of the shoulders and calves. Luge has been an Olympic sport since the first Winter Olympics at Innsbruck 1964 and is governed worldwide by the International Luge Federation (FIL), founded in 1957. Tracks are up to about 1,400 m long with steep drops and banked curves, and sleds reach speeds well above 140 km/h.

Rules

In singles, one athlete lies on the sled and starts by rocking back and forth on the handles, then propels with gloved hands on the ice; in doubles, two athletes ride the same sled, the front steering with the feet and the rear with the shoulders. Timing is electronic from the start handles to the finish line, to the thousandth of a second, and the lowest combined time over two runs (singles) wins; doubles race a single run, and the fastest time wins. Athletes wear aerodynamic suits, helmets with visors and special spiked shoes for the start. Tracks must meet FIL homologation standards, with a minimum number of curves and a controlled ice temperature; false starts and equipment violations bring penalties.

History

Sledding on snow has ancient roots, but competitive luge began in the 1880s in Switzerland and Germany, with the first international rules in the 1890s; the FIL was founded in 1957 and luge debuted at the 1964 Innsbruck Winter Olympics. Artificial tracks with refrigerated ice replaced natural tracks from the 1960s onward — the famous Königssee track (1968) and the Whistler Sliding Centre (2010) are among the fastest. East Germany and later Germany dominated the early decades, with Germany being the most successful nation, and stars like Felix Loch (multiple Olympic gold) and Natalie Geisenberger have defined the modern era. Luge is also contested at the Youth Olympic Games.
