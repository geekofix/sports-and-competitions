Bog Snorkelling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bog snorkelling is a sporting event in which competitors race through a water-filled trench cut into a peat bog, wearing a snorkel, mask and flippers, using flipper power alone — no swimming strokes allowed. The World Bog Snorkelling Championships have been held every August Bank Holiday since 1985 at the Waen Rhydd peat bog near Llanwrtyd Wells, Wales, organized by Green Events as part of the World Bog Series.

Rules

Under the Green Events rules, the organisers cut a 60-yard (55 m) water-filled trench through the peat bog. Competitors must wear a snorkel, diving mask and flippers (a wetsuit is optional), and must complete two consecutive lengths — 120 yards (110 m) in total — touching the post at each end, in the shortest time possible. Conventional swimming strokes are forbidden; only flipper power may be used. The event also has a popular fancy-dress category. The men's world record is 1:18.81 (Neil Rutter, 2018); the women's record is 1:22.56 (Kirsty Johnson, 2014).

History

The World Bog Snorkelling Championships were conceived in a pub — the Neuadd Arms in Llanwrtyd Wells — and first held in 1985; Llanwrtyd Wells, Britain's smallest town, has become famous for the event and other "World Bog Series" contests such as the Man versus Horse Marathon. Competitors now travel from over a dozen countries, and proceeds go to a local charity each year. Related events include the Bog Snorkelling Triathlon (120-yard snorkel, 19-mile bike ride, 7.5-mile run) held in Ireland and elsewhere.
