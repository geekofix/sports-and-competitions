Gaelic Handball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gaelic handball — known in Ireland simply as "handball" — is one of the four Gaelic games, played by hitting a small cork-cored ball against a wall with the hand (no racket), in singles (two players) or doubles (four players) (Gaelic Athletic Association [GAA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The main code is 40 × 20 (four-wall) handball, played in a walled court 40 ft × 20 ft (12.2 × 6.1 m) with a high front wall; the related one-wall code (wallball) uses a single 20 × 16 ft (6.1 × 4.9 m) wall, and 60 × 30 (softball and hardball) courts are also played (GAA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In Ireland the game is organized by GAA Handball (the Irish Handball Council, founded in 1924, rebranded in 2009) with its National Handball Centre at Croke Park, opened in December 2021; internationally, the World Handball Association (WWBA) was founded in 2012 at the World Handball Championships in Dublin's Citywest venue, with 30+ countries and 2,000+ competitors (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). World championships were held in Dublin 2012, Calgary 2015 and Limerick 2024 (18–23 August 2024); the 2026 World Wallball Championships took place in New York City in September 2026, and the 2026 Irish Wallball Nationals were held in County Galway (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The softball used in 60 × 30 is 2.2–2.25 in (5.6–5.7 cm) in diameter and weighs 61–62 g (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the top-ranked players of the 2026 World Wallball Championships in New York, with Ireland — the home of the game — and the United States the dominant nations in both the 40 × 20 and one-wall codes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the men's side, the Irish and American players have long headed the world rankings in 40 × 20 and softball handball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

In 40 × 20 handball, the ball is served alternately into the service box and must strike the front wall before bouncing once on the floor; the opponent returns it with any part of the hand (either hand may be used), and a rally continues until a player fails to return the ball legally — failing to hit the wall on the fly, letting it bounce twice, or hitting it out of bounds; a point is scored when the opponent cannot return (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; GAA, n.d.-a). A match is typically best of three games to 15 points (or one game to 21 in some tournaments); in doubles, partners alternate shots (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). One-wall (wallball) is played on a single 20 × 16 ft wall with a boundary line 20 ft in front of the wall: the server hits the ball against the wall, the receiver must return it before it bounces twice within the court, and a point is scored if the ball is not returned or lands out of bounds (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 60 × 30 code is played on a larger court with a softer ball (softball) or a harder, faster ball (hardball), goals being scored when the ball cannot be returned before the second bounce (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate categories under identical rules in all codes; youth grades exist, and the World Handball Championships include 40 × 20, one-wall and softball events for both sexes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Handball courts have existed in Ireland since the Middle Ages — the ball alley at Good Counsel College in New Ross, County Wexford, photographed in 1931, is still standing — and the game was played by all classes of society, with records of matches from the 18th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Irish Handball Council was founded in 1924 to govern the game and was rebranded as GAA Handball in 2009, moving into the National Handball Centre at Croke Park in December 2021 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Internationally, the game spread with the Irish diaspora to the United States, Canada and Australia, and the World Handball Association was founded in 2012 at the World Handball Championships at Citywest in Dublin, which drew more than 2,000 competitors from over 30 countries (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Subsequent world championships were held in Calgary 2015 and Limerick 2024 (18–23 August), and the 2026 World Wallball Championships in New York City in September 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
