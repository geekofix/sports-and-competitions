Gaelic Handball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gaelic handball (handball in the GAA sense) is the Irish sport of hitting a small, hard ball against a wall (the "front wall") with the open hand, in singles or doubles, aiming to make the opponent fail to return it. It is governed by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), which runs the World Handball Championship, held regularly since 1969, and the All-Ireland championships in 40x20 and 60x30 formats.

Rules

In the 40x20 (softball) and 60x30 (hardball) formats, the court is a walled alley 40 ft x 20 ft or 60 ft x 30 ft with a front wall; the server hits the ball against the front wall so it bounces once in the service box, and the opponent must return it before the second bounce, striking it with the open hand only (no gloves in hardball). Points are scored when the opponent fails to return the ball, hits it out of the court, or commits a fault (double hit, obstruction). Games are played to a set number of points, commonly 21 at championship level, with the serve changing every five points. The ball is a small cork/leather ball (softball ~ for 40x20, hard leather for 60x30). One-handed 4-wall handball (American handball) is a related but separate sport.

History

Gaelic handball arrived in Ireland with returning emigrants and was formalized when the GAA included it in its remit from 1884; All-Ireland handball championships have been organized by the GAA since the 1920s. The World Handball Council (now GAA Handball) organized the first World Championships, held in Ireland in 1969. Ireland and the United States (where Irish emigrants built handball alleys in New York, Boston, Chicago, San Francisco and Los Angeles) dominate; stars include Ireland's Robbie McCarthy, a multiple world champion. The GAA runs four codes: Gaelic football, hurling, handball and rounders.
