Breaking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Breaking (also called breakdance in media use) is the acrobatic hip hop dance style born in the Bronx in the 1970s: b-boys and b-girls battle in solo cyphers and crews, combining toprock, downrock, power moves like windmills and flares, and freezes. Judged battles score across technique, variety, performance, musicality and creativity.

Breaking made its Olympic debut at Paris 2024 with b-boy and b-girl events, is governed internationally by the World Dance Sport Federation (WDSF), and top events include the Red Bull BC One world championship and the WDSF breaking series.

Rules

Competitive formats are solo or crew battles judged to a criteria sheet: in WDSF events an odd-numbered judge panel scores each dancer on technique (foundation and execution), variety (vocabulary and originality), performance (confidence and charisma), musicality (response to the beat and hits) and creativity (personality and style), with best-of rounds deciding ties through the bracket. Olympic rules use a battle format of a set number of rounds with judging criteria panels; competing dancers alternate rounds of a set length, and no-repeat, no-crash norms favour clean rounds.

History

Breaking emerged from block parties in the Bronx in the early 1970s as part of hip hop culture with DJ Kool Herc's breaks, spreading on the streets and through the Rock Steady Crew (founded 1977). The 1980s brought global attention with films and the Rock Steady Crew's international tours, and the following decades built jams, world series and Red Bull BC One (from 2004) crowning solo world champions. The WDSF adopted breaking and steered it onto the Olympic programme for Paris 2024, where b-boys and b-girls from all continents battled in one of the Games youngest sports, the athletes distinctively introduced by their B-Boy or B-Girl names.
