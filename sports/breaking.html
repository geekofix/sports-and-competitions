Breaking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Breaking (breakdancing) is the acrobatic, street-dance-based sport discipline of hip-hop culture in which two athletes — b-boys and b-girls — take turns performing in a circle (the cypher), judged on technique, variety, musicality, performance and creativity, and battle head-to-head in knockout rounds. It is governed internationally by the World DanceSport Federation (WDSF), which runs the WDSF World Breaking Championships and integrated breaking into the Olympic movement; breaking made its Olympic debut at Paris 2024 and will also be on the programme at Los Angeles 2028 (World DanceSport Federation [WDSF], n.d.). The parallel commercial circuit is Red Bull BC One, whose 2026 World Final took place in Toronto on November 29, 2026, won by 16-year-old b-boy Willis of France (Red Bull, n.d.).

At the Paris 2024 Olympic Games at the Place de la Concorde (August 9–10, 2024), the b-girl gold medal went to Ami Yuasa of Japan, with Nicka (Dominika Banevič of Lithuania) taking silver and 671 (Liu Qingyi of China) bronze; in the b-boy event, Victor Montalvo of the United States won gold ahead of Shigekix (Shigeyuki Nakarai of Japan) (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.). In 2026 the WDSF World Breaking Championships (youth and 3v3) were held in Chengdu, China, on September 12–13, 2026, where B-Girl Royal made history with a sweep and B-Boy Lei landed eight consecutive one-hand airflares (WDSF, n.d.). Today's top female breakers include Olympic champion Ami Yuasa and the 2024 Olympic silver medalist Nicka, who remain the faces of the women's battle scene alongside stars such as B-Girl Stefani and B-Girl Alessandrina, who won Red Bull BC One cyphers in 2026 (IOC, n.d.) (Red Bull, n.d.).

Rules

Under the WDSF competition rules, breaking battles are decided by a panel of judges who score each round using five criteria: technique (execution of foundation moves — top rock, footwork, power moves and freezes), variety (range of moves), performance (stage presence, showmanship and crowd interaction), musicality (moving in time with the music) and creativity (originality and character) (WDSF, n.d.). At the Olympic Games, athletes first danced in a round-robin phase, then battled in knockout rounds; in each battle, each breaker performs one or more rounds of about 90 seconds, and the judges vote round by round — the majority of votes wins the battle (IOC, n.d.). A breaker who falls, touches the ground with hands during a power move incorrectly, or repeatedly goes out of bounds loses points; there is no contact between opponents — battles are decided purely by the judges' scores.

Formats and classifications: individual battles for b-boys and b-girls, crew battles (teams of three to five, as at the WDSF 3v3 worlds 2026), youth categories, and the Red Bull BC One circuit, which crowns world champions through national cyphers and regional finals (WDSF, n.d.) (Red Bull, n.d.). Men's and women's competitions use identical judging criteria and formats; mixed events are rare, but women's battles are a full medal event at the Olympics and at the WDSF world championships. The music is chosen by a DJ at the event, and breakers must adapt their sets to tracks they hear for the first time during the battle.

History

Breaking was born in the South Bronx, New York, in the early 1970s, when DJ Kool Herc's block parties — with the "break" of funk records that dancers used as their cue — gave rise to a new dance style pioneered by crews and figures such as Afrika Bambaataa and the Zulu Kings; the four elements of hip-hop culture — DJing, MCing, breaking and graffiti — were codified in the following years (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). From New York the dance spread worldwide through films such as "Flashdance" (1983) and "Wild Style" (1983) and through international competitions from the 1990s onward, including the Battle of the Year, founded in 1990 in Montpellier, France. Breaking entered the Youth Olympic Games at Buenos Aires 2018 and made its full Olympic debut at Paris 2024 at the Place de la Concorde — a milestone for a street culture that grew into an Olympic sport (IOC, n.d.) (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.).
