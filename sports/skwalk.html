Skwalk

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Skwalk is the FIS downhill ski-walking discipline: athletes ski downhill using two poles for propulsion, combining skating-style leg drive with double-poling, and are timed on a prepared course. The FIS Skwalk World Cup and World Championships crown the fastest ski-walkers; the discipline sits between cross-country skiing and alpine skiing and is popular in the Alps and Scandinavia (Wikipedia, n.d.; FIS, n.d.). Ski walking itself — walking on skis with poles over snow — was developed as a training method by the Finnish exercise physiologist Mauri Repo in the 1970s, whose academic material from 1979 and 1989 documented the technique (Mauri Repo materials, 1979).

Figure: The Finnish Workers` Sport Federation TUL. (1979). Historical ski-walking technique material by Mauri Repo, 1979 (the exercise form behind skwalk) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mauri-Repo-academic-nordic-walking-material_1979.jpg

Within the winter skiing family on this site its closest relatives are Cross-Country Skiing, Alpine Skiing Downhill, Speed Skiing, Roller Skiing and Nordic Combined; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

FIS skwalk competition: individual start or mass start on a groomed course with a downhill finish, timed electronically; poles must be used for propulsion (arms must move); skating technique is allowed; equipment follows cross-country rules (ski length and pole length limits per FIS); categories by age and gender, with men's and women's races (FIS, n.d.).

History

Ski walking as exercise was formalized by the Finnish physiologist Mauri Repo from the late 1970s, whose academic material documented the technique; competitive skwalk emerged in the 2000s in Austria, Switzerland and Italy, and the FIS has run a Skwalk World Cup since the 2010s, with mass-start and individual-start formats on groomed downhill courses (Wikipedia, n.d.; FIS, n.d.).
