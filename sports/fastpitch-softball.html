Fastpitch Softball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Fastpitch softball is the Olympic and World Cup form of softball: two teams of nine alternate batting and fielding over seven innings, with the pitcher delivering the 12-inch ball underhand from a flat circle 43 ft (13.1 m) from home plate in women's play, using the windmill delivery (World Baseball Softball Confederation [WBSC], n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the WBSC, formed in 2013 from the merger of the International Baseball Federation and the International Softball Federation (until 2013), which runs the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup — the XVIII edition's group stage in 2026 was played in Prague (June), Lima (14–18 July) and Oklahoma City (12–16 September), with the finals scheduled for Redcliffe, Australia, 5–10 April 2027 — and softball returns to the Olympic program at Los Angeles 2028 (WBSC, n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current teams are the traditional powers Japan and the United States, who renewed their rivalry in late 2026, with Canada, Australia and Chinese Taipei among the chasing nations (WBSC, n.d.-b).

Rules

Fastpitch is played under the WBSC Softball Rules: the field has 60 ft (18.3 m) base paths in women's softball and a pitching distance of 43 ft (13.1 m), and the ball is 12 in (30.5 cm) in circumference, optic yellow; the game lasts seven innings, with the home team batting last; the pitcher must deliver with a windmill motion, one foot on the pitcher's plate, and may not re-plant after release (WBSC, n.d.-a). Batters hit pitched balls and run the bases; a run scores when a runner touches all bases and home plate; three strikes (including a foul third strike) or three outs end the half-inning (WBSC, n.d.-a). Fielding positions are the standard nine; the catcher wears a mask and chest protector (WBSC, n.d.-a). The mercy rule ends games when one team leads by 15 runs after four innings, 10 after five or 7 after six; extra innings use the international tiebreaker with a runner on second base (WBSC, n.d.-a). Fouls include illegal pitches, batting out of turn, interference and obstruction, penalized by awards of bases or outs (WBSC, n.d.-a). Women's and men's softball are separate disciplines — the women's game uses the 43 ft / 60 ft dimensions, the men's 46 ft / 60 ft with a slightly different ball — and age-group World Cups (U-15, U-18) run alongside the senior events; men's and women's tournaments use the same rule structure (WBSC, n.d.-b).

History

Softball was invented in Chicago in 1887 as an indoor version of baseball, named "softball" officially in 1926; fastpitch pitching — the windmill — was popularized by the legendary Eddie Feigner and his King's All-Star teams in the 1930s–50s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's softball became the first official women's world championship in any sport in 1965 in Mexico, predating the men's by a year, and softball was an Olympic sport from Atlanta 1996 to Beijing 2008, returning at Los Angeles 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Softball Federation governed the women's game from 1965 until merging into the WBSC in 2013, which introduced the two-stage World Cup format in 2024 (WBSC, n.d.-b). Icons include the American pitcher Jennie Finch, Olympic gold medallist at Athens 2004 whose fastpitch dominance made the sport a television staple, and Japan's Monica Abbott; on the international stage, Japan and the USA have won every Olympic softball gold since 1996 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
