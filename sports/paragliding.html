Paragliding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Paragliding is a free-flying air sport in which a pilot launches on foot from a hill or mountain with a lightweight, foot-launched glider — a fabric wing that inflates with air and glides — and rides thermals and ridge lift to stay aloft for hours and cover long distances (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). The modern wing descends from the ram-air parafoil invented by Domina Jalbert in the 1960s, and the sport as we know it was born in June 1978 in Mieussy, France, when three friends — including Gérard Bosson — flew ram-air parachutes down a slope (Wikipedia, n.d.). Air sports are governed worldwide by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its paragliding commission CIVL, which sanctions the World Championships and the Paragliding World Cup; women and men compete in separate categories at the top events (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). Related air sports are skydiving, hang gliding and base jumping.

Rules

Competitive paragliding is divided into disciplines (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). In cross-country racing, pilots launch from a start and must pass a series of cylindrical turn points, with the goal cylinder scoring the most, and the fastest pilot to reach the goal wins; the premier events are the biennial FAI World Paragliding Championships and the Paragliding World Cup (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). In accuracy landing, pilots aim to land as close as possible to a marked target, with penalty points for the distance; in aerobatics (acrobatic paragliding), pilots are judged on maneuvers such as syncro spirals and helicopters (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). Races use GPS loggers to verify turn points, and pilots must carry reserve parachutes and helmets; men's and women's categories are scored separately at championships, with identical task rules (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). Age classes exist for juniors and masters in some national cups (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.).

History

The ram-air parafoil was invented by Canadian Domina Jalbert in the 1960s, and in June 1978 three friends — Gérard Bosson, Jean-Claude Bétemps and André Bohn — flew ram-air parachutes down the slope at Mieussy in Haute-Savoie, France, effectively inventing paragliding as a sport (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first paragliding world championships were held in Kössen, Austria, in 1979, and the FAI's CIVL commission now sanctions world championships in cross-country, accuracy and acrobatics (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). The sport boomed in the 1980s-1990s in the Alps, with Annecy, France, becoming a world center; today paragliding is flown in more than 100 countries, with top competition sites at Annecy, Saint-Hilaire, Ölüdeniz (Turkey) and Bir-Billing (India) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
