Paragliding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Paragliding is a free-flying air sport in which a pilot launches on foot from a hill or mountain with a lightweight, foot-launched glider, a fabric wing that inflates with air and glides, and rides thermals and ridge lift to stay aloft for hours and cover long distances. The modern wing descends from the ram-air parafoil invented by Domina Jalbert in the 1960s, and the sport as we know it was born in June 1978 in Mieussy, France, when three friends flew ram-air parachutes down a slope. Air sports are governed worldwide by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its paragliding commission CIVL, which sanctions the World Championships and the Paragliding World Cup. Related air sports are skydiving, hang gliding and base jumping.

Rules

Competitive paragliding is divided into disciplines. In cross-country racing, pilots launch from a start and must pass a series of cylindrical turn points, with the goal cylinder scoring the most, and the fastest pilot to reach the goal wins; the premier events are the biennial FAI World Paragliding Championships (first officially sanctioned edition in Kössen, Austria, 1989, after an unofficial 1987 event in Verbier) and the annual Paragliding World Cup. Acrobatic paragliding (acro) is judged on performed manoeuvres, individually or in pairs, at the FAI World Acro Championships. Hike-and-fly races such as the Red Bull X-Alps, first launched in 2003 and held every two years, combine hiking and flying over hundreds of kilometers. Pilots need a license issued through national federations under FAI rules, and competitions are scored from GPS flight tracks.

History

The technical foundation is the ram-air parafoil, patented by the Canadian-American inventor Domina Jalbert in 1963 (US patent 3131894) and first flown in 1964; he received the FAI Gold Air Medal posthumously in 2019 for the multi-cell ram-air wing. In 1965 the American David Barish developed the sail wing for NASA capsule recovery and pioneered "slope soaring", making the first ground launch on 15 October 1965 at Hunter Mountain, New York. In June 1978, inspired by an article on slope soaring in the Parachute Manual by Dan Poynter, Jean-Claude Bétemps, André Bohn and Gérard Bosson of Mieussy, Haute-Savoie, flew a square ram-air parachute down the Pointe du Pertuiset, and "parapente" (slope flying) was born. From the 1980s the sport grew rapidly, especially in France, which had over 25,000 licensed pilots by 2011, and today millions of pilots fly worldwide.
