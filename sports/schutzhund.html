Schutzhund

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Schutzhund (German for "protection dog"), known competitively today as IGP after earlier incarnations as IPO, is a dog sport that tests a working dog's tracking, obedience and protection ability in a three-phase trial. Developed in Germany in the early 1900s as a suitability test for German Shepherd Dogs, it became the model for evaluating all the German working breeds; any breed may now participate, though German Shepherds and Belgian Malinois dominate. In 2025 schutzhund and related working-dog activity was recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

Rules

A trial consists of three phases — tracking, obedience and protection — each scored out of 100 points by licensed judges, with a pass requiring at minimum 70 points in tracking and obedience and 80 in protection; dogs showing poor temperament (fear or unstable aggression) may be dismissed at any time. In tracking, the dog follows a track laid minutes earlier by a stranger (20 minutes for level 1, up to an hour at higher levels) and indicates articles; in obedience, dog-and-handler teams perform heelwork on and off leash, retrieves, send-outs and long downs under distraction, including gunfire steadiness; and in protection, the dog searches blinds, holds and barks at a "decoy" helper in a bite suit, performs escape-and-defence exercises, and guards a transported decoy, and on command must release cleanly and return to the handler. Titles progress through three levels — IGP 1, IGP 2 and IGP 3, with increasing difficulty at each stage — governed internationally by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) rules, and most clubs require a BH/ VT companion-behaviour test as a prerequisite. Training proceeds overwhelmingly in small local clubs, with certified decoys for protection work, and distinct national federations administer trials worldwide.

History

The sport was created in Germany in the early 1900s — the first trial was held in 1901 — as a breeding suitability test of the German Shepherd Dog's working character, as industrialisation pushed the old herding dogs toward police and military work; in 2012 the German Shepherd association (SV) renamed its traditional Schutzhund titles to IPO, and in 2019 the FCI renamed IPO to IGP (Internationale Gebrauchshunde-Prüfungsordnung), so that the names of a single, evolving trial system exist side by side in results and pedigree databases around the world. From the German Shepherd the test spread to the other protection breeds — Boxer, Dobermann, Rottweiler, and later the Belgian Malinois, which today dominates the highest levels alongside the GSD — and from Germany, clubs spread across Europe and North America, with national federations (the umbrella American Working Dog Federation, DVG in Germany, the United Schutzhund Clubs of America, and others) hosting national championships, FCI world championships and regional events. It was also in the DNA of the sport to serve breeds' welfare — only dogs of proven stable character are bred from — and modern IGP continues internationally on all continents as both a trial standard and a strong global club culture; in 2025 UNESCO inscribed schutzhund-style working-dog practices as intangible cultural heritage.
