Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) is a grappling-based martial art and combat sport that emphasizes ground fighting, positional control and submissions — joint locks and chokes — over striking, so that a smaller, weaker practitioner can defend against a larger opponent through leverage and technique. It is governed internationally by the International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation (IBJJF), which runs the World Championship and the Pan-American and European championships, while United World Wrestling (UWW) includes submission grappling in its world championships (International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation [IBJJF], n.d.) (United World Wrestling [UWW], n.d.). The premier no-gi professional event is the ADCC Submission Fighting World Championship, contested over 10-minute bouts (ADCC, n.d.). At the 30th IBJJF World Championship, held May 28–31, 2026 with the finals on June 1 at the Walter Pyramid in Long Beach, California — 335 adult black-belt matches (234 men, 101 women) and 115 submissions (34%) — Gabrieli Pessanha of Brazil won both the women's super-heavyweight and the absolute (open-weight) titles, bringing her career total to 12 world titles, the most ever by a female competitor; Mayssa Bastos won her seventh roosterweight title, Diego "Pato" Oliveira his fifth lightweight title (beating Khaled Alshehhi 9–0), Tainan Dalpra his fourth middleweight title, and Cole Abate won the men's absolute (IBJJF, n.d.).

On the women's side, the 2026 worlds confirmed BJJ's deep women's elite scene: besides Pessanha and Bastos, the tournament drew 101 female black-belt competitors, and stars such as Mackenzie Dern of the United States — a multiple-time world champion in both gi and no-gi and one of the most recognizable faces of the sport — continue to headline events worldwide (IBJJF, n.d.).

Rules

Under the IBJJF rule book, a BJJ match is won by submission (the opponent taps out), by points accumulated for dominant positions and techniques, or by referee decision; advantages are awarded for near-submissions and near-takedowns, and penalties are given for stalling and illegal grips (IBJJF, n.d.). In gi competition, points are awarded for a takedown (2), a sweep (2), a guard pass (3), mount (4) and back control with both hooks (4, plus 2 for the hooks); holding a position for three seconds is required to score. Matches at the World Championship last 5 to 10 minutes depending on age and belt division, with overtime in the absolute division. Weight classes run from rooster (up to 57.5 kg) through light feather, feather, light, middle, medium heavy, heavy and super heavy to ultra heavy, plus an open-weight absolute division; men and women compete in separate divisions under identical rules, and age divisions range from kids and juveniles to adults and masters (30+) (IBJJF, n.d.).

In no-gi grappling — as at the ADCC, where bouts last 10 minutes with points counted and a minus-one penalty for pulling guard — the same positional hierarchy applies, but grips on clothing are impossible, so control is achieved through underhooks, body locks and wrestling. Fouls include grabbing the inside of the opponent's sleeve or pant leg, twisting fingers, small joint manipulation (finger and toe locks), heel hooks in lower belts, and slamming a standing opponent; serious fouls bring immediate disqualification (IBJJF, n.d.). Competitors wear a gi (kimono) in gi events and rash guard and shorts in no-gi events, plus a mouthguard and cup.

History

BJJ traces its origins to Japanese jiu-jitsu and judo: Mitsuyo Maeda, known as Count Koma, arrived in Brazil in 1914 and taught the Gracie family — above all Carlos Gracie, who passed the art on to his younger brother Hélio Gracie (1913–2009). Hélio, frail in his youth, adapted the techniques to emphasize leverage and ground fighting, founding Gracie Jiu-Jitsu in Rio de Janeiro in the 1920s–30s (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). The art gained worldwide fame when Royce Gracie won the first, second and fourth Ultimate Fighting Championships (UFC 1, 2 and 4) in 1993–94, defeating much larger opponents with submissions. The IBJJF was founded in 1995 and has organized the World Championship since 1996, and BJJ became an Olympic-recognized sport through UWW grappling. On the women's side, pioneers such as Kyra Gracie — a two-time ADCC world champion — have driven the growth of women's competition since the 1990s (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.) (ADCC, n.d.).
