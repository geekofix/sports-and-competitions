Canyoning

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canyoning (canyon descente) is the sport of traveling downstream through a mountain canyon or gorge, combining hiking, swimming, climbing, rappelling (abseiling), jumping into pools and sliding down natural waterslides. In France it is governed by the FFME (Fédération Française de la Montagne et de l'Escalade) together with the French caving federation's canyon commission; France, Spain and the Alpine countries are the sport's heartland, and it is practiced worldwide in canyons from the Verdon Gorge to the gorges of Madeira.

Rules

There is no single international rulebook; in France the FFME publishes federal rules, safety advice and equipment standards, and routes are graded for difficulty (e.g., the French v/a/e scale for vertical, aquatic and commitment difficulty). Participants wear a wetsuit, helmet, harness and canyoning shoes, use ropes for rappels and a throw bag for rescue, and descend as a group with a qualified leader. Jumps are only made into deep, checked pools, and groups must respect the environment and other parties. Competitive canyon racing (descente chronométrée) ranks teams by total time on a marked course.

History

Canyoning developed as an organized activity in France in the 1980s, when cavers and climbers began systematically descending the limestone gorges of the Verdon, the Pyrenees and the Alps. The FFME integrated canyoning into its mountain sports in the 1990s and created federal instructor diplomas and a route directory (the canyon annuaire). The sport spread internationally in the 1990s and 2000s, with strong scenes in Spain, Italy, Switzerland, the United States and New Zealand.
