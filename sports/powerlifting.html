Powerlifting

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Powerlifting is a strength sport consisting of three attempts at a maximal weight on each of three barbell lifts — the squat, the bench press and the deadlift, in that fixed order; the sum of the best successful lift in each discipline is the total, and competitors are placed in bodyweight, age and sex classes (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport is governed internationally by the International Powerlifting Federation (IPF), founded in 1972, which sanctions world championships in equipped and unequipped ("classic"/raw) divisions; para powerlifting — bench press only — has been on the Paralympic programme since 1984 (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). The World Open Classic Powerlifting Championships 2026 were held 13–21 June 2026 in Druskininkai, Lithuania; among the current leading women are Tiffany Chapon of France, the 2025 and 2026 classic world champion at –47 kg, Amanda Lawrence of the USA in the middle classes, and Sonita Kyen Muluh of Belgium, the 2024 84+ kg champion, alongside the American Bonica Brown, a seven-time classic world champion and multiple world-record holder, while the men's open titles at Druskininkai 2026 went to the heaviest totals in each class (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Under the IPF Technical Rules, the three lifts are performed in the fixed sequence squat, bench press, deadlift at every IPF competition, each lifter receiving three attempts per lift; a bona fide attempt is required in all three to record a total (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). Each lift is controlled by the referee's audible commands — "squat" and "rack" in the squat, "start", "press" and "rack" in the bench press, "down" in the deadlift — with disqualification for faults such as double-bouncing the bar, uneven lockout, lifting the feet, or resting the bar on the thighs in the deadlift. Equipment differs by division: equipped lifters wear supportive suits and bench shirts, while classic (raw) lifters are limited to a belt, wrist wraps, knee sleeves and a singlet, with shin-length socks mandatory for the deadlift (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). Ties are decided by the lighter bodyweight or the earlier total, and Best Lifter is calculated by the IPF points formula (Wilks/GLP) at all championships. Competitions are divided into men's and women's classes across bodyweight categories from –43 kg to 84+ kg for women and –59 kg to 120+ kg for men, plus junior, sub-junior and masters age groups (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Powerlifting evolved from 19th- and early-20th-century "odd lift" contests — three-attempt formats with a wide range of lifts akin to strongman — standardized first in the United States into the three-lift format (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The IPF was founded in 1972 and staged the first men's world championships in 1973 and the first women's in 1980; classic (raw) world championships were added as a distinct event in 2012 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A parallel federation landscape developed around the IPF, including the drug-tested World Drug-Free Powerlifting Federation founded in 1988, and professional invitational meets such as the SBD Sheffield Powerlifting Championships, inaugurated in 2023, drew arena audiences (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
