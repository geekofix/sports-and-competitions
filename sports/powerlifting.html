Powerlifting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Powerlifting is a strength sport consisting of three attempts at a maximal weight on each of three barbell lifts: the squat, the bench press and the deadlift, in that fixed order. The sum of the best successful lift of each discipline is the lifter's total, and competitors are placed within bodyweight, age and sex categories. The sport is governed internationally by the International Powerlifting Federation (IPF), which sanctions world championships in both equipped and unequipped ("classic" or raw) lifting, and it has been a World Games sport and a Paralympic sport (bench press only, as para powerlifting) for decades.

Rules

Under the IPF Technical Rules, the three lifts must be performed in the same sequence at every IPF competition, each lifter receiving three attempts per lift. Runs of misses matter: a lifter who fails all attempts in a discipline may still continue with the others, but cannot record a total unless a bona fide attempt is made on all three. Each lift is controlled by a referee's audible commands — "squat" and "rack" in the squat, "start", "press" and "rack" in the bench press, and "down" in the deadlift — with strict cause-for-disqualification rules for faults such as double bouncing, lifting the feet, uneven lockout or supporting the bar on the thighs during the deadlift. Lifters wear a supportive suit ("equipped") or only approved basics such as a belt, wrist wraps, knee sleeves and a lifting suit ("classic"/raw); shin-length socks are mandatory for the deadlift, and a t-shirt under the lifting suit in all lifts. Ties are decided for the lighter lifter or the lifter who made the total first, and a Best Lifter award is calculated by IPF formula at all IPF championships.

History

Powerlifting evolved from 19th-20th-century "odd lift" contests — a three-attempt format with a wider range of events akin to strongman — which were standardized, first in the United States, to the current three lifts. The International Powerlifting Federation was founded in 1972 and staged its first world championships in 1973, growing into the sport's largest international body, while a parallel landscape of federations, drug-tested and open, equipped and raw, developed around it, from the World Drug-Free Powerlifting Federation (1988) onward. The IPF introduced the unequipped world championships as a distinct championship in 2012 and, in the same era, professional invitational meets such as the SBD Sheffield Powerlifting Championships (inaugurated 2023) drew large audiences. Powerlifting's bench-press-only para discipline has been on the Paralympic program since 1984, and the sport appeared at the World Games from 1981. Championship records in all classes continue to be broken by lifters such as the American Bonica Brown, a multiple-time world and World Games champion.
