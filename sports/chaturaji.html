Chaturaji

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chaturaji ('four armies') is the four-player chess of ancient India, played on 8x8 with four colored armies (red, yellow, green, black), each with king, minister (queen), chariot (rook), horse (knight), boat (bishop) and pawns; it is the ancestor of modern chess via shatranj. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Chess, Sittuyin, Shogi; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Alfonso X of Castile, 1283).

Figure: Chaturaji. (2021-05-15). Antique four-player chaturaji chess set arranged for play [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chaturaji_Chess_Arrangement.jpg

Rules

Four players at the four sides; pieces move as their modern counterparts (boat=bishop diagonal, etc.); captured pieces are removed; players may ally temporarily; the last army with a king standing wins. Dice were used in the original Indian form (as in the 1283 Libro de los juegos illustration) (Alfonso X of Castile, 1283).

History

Chaturaji is depicted in al-Beruni's 1030 account and in the 1283 Libro de los juegos of Alfonso X; it evolved into shatranj (two-player) and then modern chess. Antique four-player sets (e.g. the 2021 sandalwood set) are preserved (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
