Japanese Binocular Soccer

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Japanese binocular soccer (binocular football) is a novelty game that originated on Japanese television variety shows: players strap binoculars over their eyes and then play a normal game of football. Because binoculars magnify and distort the view, players cannot judge distances, miskick the ball and constantly collide, turning an ordinary match into slapstick comedy. It is played as a humorous challenge or party game rather than as an organized sport, though informal "world championships" of the gag have been staged.

Rules

There are no codified rules: the only twist is the equipment. Players wear binoculars (sometimes with one lens covered or the glasses modified so each eye sees a different magnification) and then play football under otherwise normal small-sided rules — two teams, goals, a referee and a match time. Goals scored count as usual; the entertainment comes from the chaos of misjudged passes, shots and tackles. Variations include playing with the binoculars upside down, which inverts the view, or wearing them only when receiving the ball.

History

The gag became famous through Japanese TV comedy and variety programs, where celebrities and amateur teams played binocular football for laughs, and clips of the matches spread worldwide as viral videos in the 2000s and 2010s, with similar stunts later appearing at festivals such as Bestival in the UK. It belongs to a family of handicap novelty sports — alongside blindfold football or three-legged races — designed to make an everyday game absurdly difficult. There is no governing body; anyone with a football pitch and a pair of binoculars can stage a match.
