Track Cycling Team Sprint

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The team sprint (Olympic sprint) is a short, explosive track cycling race between two national teams starting on opposite sides of the velodrome with a flying start: in the men's event three riders each lead one lap before peeling away, leaving the last rider to sprint the final lap alone; women race with two riders over two laps (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). It has been an Olympic event for men since Sydney 2000 and for women since London 2012, and a World Championship event for even longer (Olympics, n.d.). Among the current leading riders — women first — are New Zealand's women, Olympic champions at Paris 2024 with Ellesse Andrews, and the Netherlands, which took both the men's and women's team sprint rainbow jerseys at the 2025 World Championships in Santiago with Harrie Lavreysen's team (Olympics, n.d.; Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.).

Rules

Two teams race head-to-head against the clock from a flying start on opposite sides of the track (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). The men's team sprint is run by teams of three over three laps of the 250 m track: the lead rider takes the first lap, then swings up the banking and drops out, the second leads the second lap, and the third rider sprints the final lap alone (Wikipedia, n.d.). The women's event uses teams of two over two laps with the same drop-off mechanics — the key gender difference in the discipline (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Qualifying times seed the rounds — 16 riders at World Cups, 24 at World Championships — and the fastest eight advance to head-to-head rounds, with finals decided by a single race for gold and bronze (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). Riders must hold their lane until the changeover is complete; dangerous changes can be penalized (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate events with the different team sizes and distances described above (Olympics, n.d.).

History

The team sprint has been an Olympic event for men since Sydney 2000 and for women since London 2012, and it has been part of the UCI Track World Championships for decades (Olympics, n.d.). Britain's teams of Sir Chris Hoy, Jason Kenny and Matthew Crampton dominated the 2000s and early 2010s with Olympic golds in 2008 and 2012, while the German powerhouse teams of Robert Förstemann, Stefan Nimke, René Enders and Maximilian Levy — visible on the 2011 world podium — set the benchmark with huge leg strength (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Netherlands, with Harrie Lavreysen, Jeffrey Hoogland and Roy van den Berg, has been the dominant force of the 2020s, winning both Olympic and world team sprint titles, and New Zealand's women, led by Ellesse Andrews, took Olympic gold at Paris 2024 (Olympics, n.d.; Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). On the women's side, Germany's Kristina Vogel (retired after her 2018 crash) and Miriam Welte (retired) and Britain's Victoria Pendleton (retired) were the icons of the 2000s and 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.).
