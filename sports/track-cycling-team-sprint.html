Track Cycling Team Sprint

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The team sprint is track cycling's explosive short time trial: teams of three (men) or two to three (women) riders complete laps of the velodrome from a standing start, each rider leading one lap before peeling off, and the last rider's time decides the result (the team wins by time or by catching its opponent in the final). A pure speed event contested at world championships and the Olympic Games, it pairs kilo-power starts with precision riding.

Rules

From a standing start in the gate, the team races three laps (men) or two (women traditionally; formats set by category), with the lead rider pulling one maximum-turn lap and swinging up the track, the second doing the same, and the final rider coming home alone for the time; the team's clock stops on the last rider's front wheel crossing the line. Qualification is on time, finals on head-to-head pairings with the possibility of a catch, and rules govern gate starts, the order of pull-off riders (a rider must exit cleanly above the sprinter's line) and equipment, with 500-metre and 750-metre variants racing at junior level and trade-team world championships.

History

The team sprint was born from sprint squads' training exercises and became an international event during the late twentieth century (the sprint-sided counterpart of the pursuit), its two- and three-man turns a fixture of world championships and, from Beijing 2008 era formats onward, an Olympic event for men and women. The event rewards the kilo or match-sprint specialist: the world's top sprinters focus their preparation on the three-lap format where medal margins are measured in hundredths, and world records have plummeted with the arrival of bigger gears, aero equipment and start-gate technique refinements.
