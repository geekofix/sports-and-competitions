PUBG: Battlegrounds

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

PUBG: Battlegrounds is Krafton's realistic battle-royale shooter (100 players, shrinking blue zone, looting) played in squads of four (Krafton, n.d.); the PC esports circuit runs the PUBG Global Series (PGS), the PUBG Continental Series and the PUBG World Cup. At the Esports World Cup 2026 in Paris (21–26 July 2026), Virtus.pro won the PUBG: Battlegrounds tournament after 18 grand-final matches under the match-point (WWCD) rule (Gamelevate, n.d.; Liquipedia, n.d.). The PUBG Mobile World Cup 2026 (PMWC) at the same EWC was won by Turkey's S2G Esports with 154 points, ahead of Nongshim RedForce (149) (Games Press, n.d.).

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Fortnite, Apex Legends, Call of Duty, Overwatch, Halo and Valorant; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

PUBG esports rules: squads of four, third-person (TPP) or first-person (FPP) perspectives by event; lobbies of 16 teams (64 players); group stage plus grand finals of up to 18 matches; points for placement and kills; the first team to become 'match point eligible' and win a match (WWCD — winner winner chicken dinner) is champion, otherwise highest points after 18 games; open to all genders (Liquipedia, n.d.).

History

PUBG (PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds) was created by Brendan Greene (2017, Bluehole) and set the battle-royale template; the first PUBG Invitational ran at Gamescom 2017, and Krafton's esports structure (PCS/PGS/PWC/PMWC) has run since 2019, with the PUBG Global Championship as the season finale (Wikipedia, n.d.).
