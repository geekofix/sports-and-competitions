Watermelon Seed Spitting

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Watermelon seed spitting is a novelty festival competition in which contestants spit seeds from a cut watermelon as far as possible, measured from a marked spit-line to the point where the seed lands (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is the signature event of the annual Watermelon Thump festival in Luling, Texas — held every June since 1954 — where it is contested in age classes for children and adults and in separate men's and women's divisions, plus team events whose distances are added together (Luling Watermelon Thump, n.d.). The standing Guinness world record is 68 feet 9 1/8 inches (about 21 m), set by Luling local Lee Wheelis in 1989 — the same year John Wilkinson of Houston first set a Guinness mark of 65 feet 4 inches (Guinness World Records, n.d.).

Rules

Contestants receive a supply of fresh watermelon seeds and take turns spitting from behind a marked line on a measured lane called the spitway, typically with three attempts per round; the shot is measured from the line to the final resting point of the seed, and the longest valid spit counts (Wikipedia, n.d.). Feet must stay behind the line — stepping over it voids the attempt — and no foreign substance may be added to the seed. Age classes (children, juniors, adults) and men's and women's divisions compete separately, and team events total the distances of a squad. Typical winning distances at championship festivals are 20 to 30 feet, while the very best spitters reach beyond 60 feet (Luling Watermelon Thump, n.d.).

History

The famous contest grew out of Luling, Texas — a major watermelon-growing and shipping centre — whose four-day Watermelon Thump festival has featured seed-spitting championships since 1954, the idea credited to a local school principal, alongside a Watermelon Queen pageant and melon-eating contests (Luling Watermelon Thump, n.d.). The first Guinness world record was adjudicated there in 1989: John Wilkinson of Houston set 65 feet 4 inches, and Luling's Lee Wheelis re-established the record the same year with 68 feet 9 1/8 inches, a mark that still stands (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Texas Highways, n.d.). The pastime has since spread to other watermelon festivals — such as the Pride of the Patch festival in Indiana — where local seed-spitting contests remain a staple of summer fête calendars (Wikipedia, n.d.).
