Motorcycle Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Motorcycle racing (motorcycle sport) is the umbrella term for competitive riding of motorcycles, divided into paved-surface (road) disciplines and off-road disciplines. The world governing body is the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM), founded in 1904, which sanctions world championships in Grand Prix road racing (MotoGP, with Moto2 and Moto3), Superbike (superbike), motocross, supercross, enduro, trials and speedway, among others. The best-known single event is the Isle of Man TT, a road race on public roads first held in 1907.

Rules

Rules differ by discipline. In circuit racing (MotoGP, Superbike), riders race a set number of laps on a closed circuit, with qualifying setting the grid and points for the top finishers. In motocross, races are timed motos of about 30 minutes plus two laps on dirt circuits. In enduro, riders race against the clock over special stages with time checks. In trials, riders cross obstacle sections without putting a foot down, collecting fault points. In speedway, four riders race counter-clockwise on a flat oval of loose surface with one-gear, brake-less bikes over four-lap heats. All FIM disciplines require a valid license, medical checks and full protective equipment.

History

Organized motorcycle racing began in the early 20th century; the Isle of Man TT course was first used in 1907 and the first Tourist Trophy races drew international attention. The FIM was founded in Paris in 1904 and launched the Road Racing World Championship in 1949, making it the oldest motorsport world championship still running. Post-war racing was shaped by British, Italian and Japanese manufacturers, with legends such as Geoff Duke, John Surtees, Mike Hailwood and Giacomo Agostini in the 1950s-60s, and later Kenny Roberts, Valentino Rossi and Marc Marquez in Grand Prix racing. Off-road disciplines such as motocross (from 1924 in the UK), enduro (ISDT from 1913) and speedway (1920s) developed alongside, and the FIM today sanctions more than 50 national federations' worth of world-level competition across all disciplines.
