Pickleball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Pickleball is a paddle sport played with solid paddles and a perforated plastic ball on a court the size of a doubles badminton court, in singles or doubles. It combines elements of tennis, badminton and table tennis, and is related to paddle tennis, platform tennis and padel: serves are made underhand diagonally, the ball must bounce once on each side before volleys are allowed, and a seven-foot non-volley zone (the "kitchen") on each side of the net forbids volleying at the net (USA Pickleball, n.d.). The sport was invented in 1965 on Bainbridge Island, Washington, and has become one of the fastest-growing sports in the world, played in more than 100 countries, with USA Pickleball as the national governing body in the United States and the International Pickleball Federation coordinating internationally (International Pickleball Federation, n.d.). The game is open to all ages and both genders, with mixed doubles a staple of recreational and professional play; among today's stars are Anna Leigh Waters of the United States, the world's top-ranked women's singles and doubles player on the DUPR world rankings, and Ben Johns, long the leading men's doubles player — the professional field at a 2024 tournament is pictured below (DUPR, n.d.).

Rules

The rules are set out in the USA Pickleball Official Rulebook, first published in 1984 and updated annually (USA Pickleball, 2026). The court measures 20 by 44 feet; the net is 36 inches at the sidelines and 34 inches at the center. Serves are underhand, with contact below the waist, and must land in the diagonal service court; the serve and the first return must each bounce before being hit (the two-bounce rule). The non-volley zone extends seven feet from the net on each side, and a player may not volley — hit the ball out of the air — while touching the zone or carried into it by momentum, although standing in it is allowed at any other time. Only the serving side scores points in traditional play; games are normally played to 11 points and must be won by two, and in doubles the score is called as three numbers: serving team, receiving team and server number. The 2026 rulebook formalized a provisional rally-scoring option and added specific wheelchair rules, so the sport is fully inclusive across genders and abilities (USA Pickleball, 2026). Men's, women's and mixed events are played at every level from recreational to the professional PPA Tour and Major League Pickleball; age-group categories (19 and under, 35+, 50+, 60+, 70+) are standard in tournament play (USA Pickleball, n.d.).

History

The game was created in the summer of 1965 by Joel Pritchard, a congressman, and his friends Bill Bell and Barney McCallum at Pritchard's home on Bainbridge Island, Washington, improvising a game for their children with a wiffle ball, table tennis paddles and a lowered badminton net (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first permanent pickleball court was built in 1967, and the first known official tournament was held in 1976. USA Pickleball (originally the US Pickleball Association) was formed in 1984 and published the first rulebook in the same year, standardizing a game that had spread across the United States (USA Pickleball, n.d.). The sport's growth accelerated sharply in the 2010s and 2020s, aided by dedicated court construction, professional tours and celebrity players; the Sports & Fitness Industry Association counted about 24 million American players in 2025 (Sports & Fitness Industry Association, 2025). Internationally, the International Federation of Pickleball (IFP) was founded in 2010, reorganized as the International Pickleball Federation (IPF) in 2023, and in 2025 merged with the World Pickleball Federation under the World Pickleball Federation name; the rival Global Pickleball Federation, launched by USA Pickleball and 28 other nations in 2023, also operates today (International Pickleball Federation, n.d.). Pickleball is now played internationally, with member federations on several continents and international championships staged each year, and it is on the program for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.).
