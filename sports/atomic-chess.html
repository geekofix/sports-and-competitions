Atomic Chess

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Atomic chess is a chess variant in which every capture also 'explodes': the captured piece and all pieces on adjacent squares (except kings) are removed from the board. Checkmate still wins; a king can also be 'mated' by explosion; kings are immune to capture but can be checkmated. Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are Antichess, King of the Hill Chess, Chess; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Lichess, n.d.).

Figure: ChessVariantNut. (2017-11-13). Chess variant board (Castle Siege setup) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castle_Siege_Chess_using_the_Standard_Army.jpg

Rules

Standard moves; on a capture, the captured piece and every piece (own or enemy) on the eight surrounding squares are removed, except kings. Checkmate of the enemy king wins; stalemate counts as a win for the stalemating side in most rule sets (lichess). Pawns capture-explode like other pieces but do not promote by explosion (Lichess, n.d.).

History

Atomic chess appeared in the 1980s and became a standard online variant in the 2000s; lichess runs an annual atomic world championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
