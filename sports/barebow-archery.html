Barebow Archery

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Barebow archery is the discipline of shooting a bow without a sight, usually with a simple recurve or longbow, aiming instinctively ("instinctive shooting"). It is governed by World Archery alongside recurve and compound, and is contested at the World Archery Championships and the World Games, with a strong following in field archery.

Rules

Under World Archery rules, barebow archers shoot at the same targets as recurve archers — at the World Championships, 50 m on a 80 cm target in qualification and head-to-head matches to 5 sets of 3 arrows. The defining rule difference is equipment: no sight, no stabilizers (in most classes), and shooting off the shelf or fingers with a glove or tab. Aiming is done by instinct or "string walking". At World Archery field events, barebow classes use the same marked or unmarked 3D courses as other bow styles.

History

Shooting without a sight is the oldest form of archery; bows were used for hunting and war without sights for millennia. Modern barebow competition grew in the mid-20th century with field archery in the United States and Europe. World Archery added barebow as an official class at the World Championships in the 1990s, and the discipline is now contested worldwide, with strong scenes in Europe, Asia and the Americas.
