Individual Medley Swimming

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The individual medley (IM) is the four-stroke race in which one swimmer covers butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke and freestyle in that order, each quarter under that stroke's rules; it is contested over 200 m and 400 m in long course (the 100 m IM is short-course only) and is governed worldwide by World Aquatics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Aquatics, n.d.-c). The long-course world records at the end of the 2026 season belong to Léon Marchand of France — 1:52.69 over 200 m and 4:02.50 over 400 m (both 2025/2024) — and to Summer McIntosh of Canada on the women's side, who holds the 200 m (2:05.70) and 400 m (4:23.65) records, both lowered at the 2025 World Championships in Singapore where she also set the 400 m freestyle record of 3:54.18 — three individual world records in one week, a feat last achieved by a woman at Sydney 2000 (World Aquatics, n.d.-e; BBC Sport, 2025, July 27; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the athletes defining the 2026 season, the women lead: Summer McIntosh of Canada (pictured) holds four long-course world records (400 m freestyle, 200 m butterfly, 200 m and 400 m individual medley) and is the 2025 World Aquatics Female Swimmer of the Year, Kate Douglass of the USA holds eleven world records overall, and at the 2026 Asian Games in Nagoya the 13-year-old Yu Zidi of China swam the second-fastest 200 m individual medley in history (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the men's side Léon Marchand — 2024 Olympic champion over 400 m IM in 4:02.50 and holder of both IM world records after breaking Ryan Lochte's 1:54.00 over 200 m at the 2025 Worlds in Singapore — heads the lists, ahead of compatriot Max Litchfield and Japan's Tomoyuki Matsushita (BBC Sport, 2025, July 27; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Together with Swimming, this stroke belongs to the pool-stroke family — Freestyle Swimming, Backstroke Swimming, Breaststroke Swimming, Butterfly Swimming — and the full aquatics list is under Important Links.

Rules

In the individual medley the strokes are swum in the fixed order butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke, freestyle — one quarter of the race each over 200 m and 400 m (50 m each at 200 m, 100 m each at 400 m); each quarter must be swum according to that stroke's rules, and the freestyle quarter may be swum in any style other than backstroke, breaststroke or butterfly (World Aquatics, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Turns at the change of stroke must be executed according to the rules of the stroke being finished and the stroke being started; at the finish the swimmer must touch in the freestyle position (World Aquatics, n.d.-d).

The IM is swum in the standard certified LCM/SCM pools with block starts, the single-false-start disqualification rule, the 15 m underwater limit after each start and turn, and automatic timing to 1/100 s; races run as heats, semifinals and finals (200 m) or heats and finals (400 m), with records ratified under SW12 (World Aquatics, n.d.-d; World Aquatics, n.d.-e). Men and women contest identical events and distances; the 100 m IM exists in short course only, and para swimming offers the SM classes (World Aquatics, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The 400 m individual medley has been an Olympic event for men since Tokyo 1964 — won by the USA's Don Schollander — and the 200 m since Mexico City 1968; women have contested both since 1964/1968 respectively, with the 100 m IM added in short course (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Ryan Lochte of the USA, pictured, set the 200 m world record of 1:54.00 at the Shanghai 2011 World Championships — a mark that stood for 14 years until Léon Marchand broke it in Singapore 2025 — and won Olympic gold over 400 m IM at London 2012 and 200 m IM at Rio 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Katinka Hosszú of Hungary, pictured, the “Iron Lady”, set the 400 m world record of 4:26.36 at Rio 2016 and won three golds there including both medleys; Michael Phelps won a record eight Olympic individual medley titles between 2004 and 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
