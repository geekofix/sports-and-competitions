Gatka

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gatka (also spelled gadka or khutka) is the traditional Sikh martial art of stick-fighting from Punjab, India, using wooden sticks (soti, 91-107 cm long) that simulate swords, often paired with a small round shield (phari). Today it exists in two forms — the traditional ritual form (rasmi) and the standardized competitive sport form (khel) — governed internationally by the World Gatka Federation (WGF) and nationally in India by the National Gatka Association of India (NGAI), which publish official rulebooks.

Rules

Under the International Gatka Rules Book (WGF/NGAI), sport gatka bouts are fought between two competitors or teams in a defined arena, using the soti and phari; scoring is based on landing clean, controlled touches on valid target areas while demonstrating prescribed techniques, with penalties for prohibited weapons, stunts (bazigiri) and unsafe conduct. Traditional (rasmi) gatka is a non-competitive display of choreographed sequences (pharms) performed to the beat of drums, judged on authenticity, form and adherence to Sikh tradition. Competitions include individual bouts, team events and display categories at national and world championships. Protective equipment rules and referee/jury structures are defined in the rulebook.

History

Gatka originated in 15th-century Punjab as a branch of the wider Shastar Vidya martial system, taught by the Sikh Gurus (notably Guru Hargobind and Guru Gobind Singh) and practiced by the Nihangs; after the British banned swords in the mid-19th century, fighters switched to wooden sticks, turning gatka into a defense and display art. Kartar Singh Akali published a treatise with rules in 1936, and Panjab University institutionalized inter-college tournaments in the pre-partition era. The International Gatka Federation was founded in 1982 and formalized in 1987, and the WGF (with NGAI in India) now runs world championships. Gatka is performed at Sikh festivals worldwide and was demonstrated at events such as the 2010 Commonwealth Games in Delhi.
