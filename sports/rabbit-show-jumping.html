Rabbit Show Jumping

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rabbit show jumping (Swedish: kaninhoppning) is the competitive form of rabbit hopping, in which trained rabbits are led through standardized jumping courses — straight, crooked, high jump and long jump — in pursuit of fault-free rounds and titles; women and men compete equally as handlers, and bucks and does alike are entered (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport is administered by national federations such as Sveriges Kaninhoppares Riksförbund (SKHRF, founded 3 September 1994) in Sweden, the Rabbit Hopping Organization of America (RHOA, 2001) and the American Hopping Association for Rabbits and Cavies (AHARC, chartered with the ARBA in 2013), and is strongest in Scandinavia, though the 2026 European Rabbit Jumping Championships, held 9–11 October 2026 in Lysekil, Sweden, drew entries from across Europe (AFP, n.d.; Topend Sports, n.d.).

Rules

Official competitions consist of a straight course, a crooked course, high jump and long jump; straight and crooked courses are divided into five levels with set obstacle counts and dimensions — Mini 26/30 cm (6–8 obstacles), Easy 30/45 (8), Medium 38/65 (10), Advanced 45/75 (10) and Elite 50/80 (12) — where measurements differ slightly between countries (Wikipedia, n.d.; Topend Sports, n.d.). Rabbits are placed by faults — knocked rails and refusals — within a 60-minute course clock, with time used only to break ties, and earn promotion pins to climb levels (Topend Sports, n.d.). In high jump and long jump the bar rises or the distance lengthens round by round until one rabbit clears, like the puissance in equestrian show jumping, with three attempts per height and certificate thresholds of 70 cm and 180 cm in Elite (Wikipedia, n.d.). A rabbit that wins Elite classes with at least ten starters earns certificates, and three certificates in one discipline (from at least two clubs) make a Champion, two disciplines a Great Champion, three a Super Champion and all four Swedish championship disciplines a Grand Champion (Wikipedia, n.d.). The standing world records are 107 cm in the high jump, set in March 2023 by Holloway GtCh Tennessine of the Czech Republic, and 301 cm in the long jump, set in August 2017 by Miss Pinky's Grand Champion Harajuku "Dobby" of Sweden (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Rabbit show jumping began in Sweden in the 1970s, when the Varalöv Kaninklubb in the south started arranging competitions; the first Swedish championship followed in Helsingborg in 1986 and the first straight-course championship in Stockholm in 1987, and the SKHRF was established on 3 September 1994 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Norway joined in the early 1990s and Denmark's Kanin Hop clubs in 1993, whose judges Aase and Rasmus Bjerner later helped set up the American RHOA in 2001; the first official US national competition ran at the 2011 ARBA convention in Indianapolis and the AHARC was chartered in 2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia was founded in 2013 and Rabbit Hopping New Zealand in 2015 by Freya Pocock Johansson, and since the late 1990s the Nordic countries have harmonized their rules into a common framework, with European championships now rotating through Scandinavian venues (Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia, n.d.; AFP, n.d.).
