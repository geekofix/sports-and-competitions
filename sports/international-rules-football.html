International Rules Football

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

International rules football (Irish: Peil na rialacha idirnáisiúnta; called compromise rules in Ireland and international rules in Australia) is a hybrid of Gaelic football and Australian rules football, created so that the two nations could play representative matches — 18 players per side on the standard Gaelic/AFL field with round goalposts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Rules Series between Australia and Ireland has been played intermittently since 1984 and is governed jointly by the AFL and the GAA (AFL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The hybrid sits exactly between its two parent codes — Gaelic Football and Australian Rules Football — within the football-codes family listed under Important Links.

A match lasts 72 minutes — four 18-minute quarters — with goals worth 3 points and behinds 1 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The series was dormant after Australia's 2017 win at Domain Stadium in Melbourne, but talks in 2024–2026 produced a planned revival: AFL.com.au reported in June 2026 that both organizations were "working towards a return in 2027", with a series in Ireland in 2027 and a return tour to Australia in 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Women's international rules is now established as well: the first AFLW Australia v Ireland match was played on 1 August 2026, with Ireland's Orla O'Dwyer tackling the AFLW's Tyanna Smith (AFL, n.d.). Ireland last won the men's Cormac McAnallen Cup in 2015 with a 56–52 victory in a single Test at Croke Park, captained by Bernard Brogan (Irish Times, 2025).

Rules

International rules is a compromise between the two parent codes: 18-a-side teams play on the standard 130–145 m × 80–90 m pitch with round Gaelic-style goalposts; a goal is worth 3 points and a behind 1 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There is no offside rule, tackling is allowed but holding the ball carrier is restricted, and a player may run with the ball but must bounce or solo it every nine steps / 15 metres as in Australian rules; the mark rule from Gaelic football applies (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Matches last 72 minutes (4 × 18-minute quarters) with no sin-bin — a player sent off in Gaelic football simply leaves the field in the hybrid. Referees from both codes officiate, and a series is decided over one or two Tests, with aggregate score across Tests when two are played (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; AFL, n.d.). Men's, women's (AFLW v Ireland, first match 1 August 2026) and junior teams play only in tournaments or Tests — there are no dedicated club leagues (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The first tour, the Australian Football World Tour, took place in 1967 with matches in Ireland, the UK and the US; the following year games were played between Australia and a touring County Meath Gaelic football team (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The formal International Rules Series between the senior national teams began in 1984 and has generally been closely matched (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Croke Park has hosted record crowds for the fixture — the second Test drew 82,127, the largest attendance for an international sports fixture in Ireland at the time. Australia won the 2014 series in front of a sell-out Perth crowd and again in 2017 at Domain Stadium, Melbourne, with Joel Selwood and Zach Tuohy starring; Ireland's last series win was the 2015 Cormac McAnallen Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; AFL, n.d.). After a decade dormant, the AFL and GAA agreed in 2026 to work toward a 2027 return in Ireland and a 2028 tour of Australia (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
