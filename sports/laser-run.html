Laser-Run

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Laser run is the combined run-and-shoot discipline of modern pentathlon, contested stand-alone since 2015 — the first UIPM Laser Run World Championships were held that year — and governed by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM), founded in 1948 (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the senior format the athletes run five loops of 600 m and after each loop stop at a 10 m shooting range to score five hits on an electronic target, with a 50-second limit per shooting series; the first to finish the 3,000 m wins (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b). Women compete in their own individual events at every World Championship and in the mixed relay at the Youth Olympics — the women's individual laser-run final at the 2018 Summer Youth Olympics in Buenos Aires is pictured below — and men's and women's events run under identical rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). UIPM also runs the Global Laser Run City Tour and age-group categories from youth to masters (ages roughly 8 to 80), making it one of the most accessible shooting sports (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b).

Rules

In laser run the athlete starts at the start line, runs the first 600 m loop, then shoots five hits on the 10 m electronic target before continuing; the sequence of five 600 m loops with five shooting series is repeated, so the total is 3,000 m of running (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b). Each shooting series is limited to 50 seconds with unlimited shots in the senior format; a hit anywhere on the target counts, and the target — a 17 × 17 cm electronic unit with the center at 1.40 m height — registers the hits automatically (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b). The laser pistol is a Class 1 device (635–650 nm, eye-safe) weighing at least 800 g (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate individual events and in mixed relays under identical rules; age groups from U15 through seniors and masters categories apply the same format with adjusted distances and times (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). Rule source: UIPM Competition Rules, https://www.uipmworld.org/ (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). See also modern pentathlon, shooting, athletics and biathlon on this site.

History

Modern pentathlon has combined running and shooting since its inclusion in the Olympic programme at Stockholm 1912, an event designed by Pierre de Coubertin to simulate a soldier's dispatch; the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne was founded in 1948 in Sandhurst, England, to govern the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). For decades the shooting leg used .22 calibre pistols; UIPM moved the final combined event to eye-safe laser pistols in the 2010s and made laser run a stand-alone discipline in 2015, with the first UIPM Laser Run World Championships the same year (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). Laser run has been the final event of the Olympic modern pentathlon since Tokyo 2020, was contested at the 2018 Youth Olympics in Buenos Aires — where and it will again decide medals at Los Angeles 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
