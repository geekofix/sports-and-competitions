Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Association football (soccer) is the world's most popular sport, played by two teams of eleven who use any body part except hands and arms (goalkeepers excepted) to get a ball into the opponent's goal. It is governed by FIFA (Fédération Internationale de Football Association), founded 1904, which runs the FIFA World Cup — the most-watched sporting event on Earth — and by continental confederations such as UEFA (Europe) and CONMEBOL (South America).

Rules

Under the Laws of the Game (maintained by the International Football Association Board, IFAB), a match is two 45-minute halves on a pitch 100-110 m x 64-75 m (international) with goals 7.32 m wide and 2.44 m high. Each team has 11 players including a goalkeeper; a minimum of 7 players is required to start/continue. The ball is out when it fully crosses the touchline or goal line. Fouls are penalized with free kicks or penalty kicks (from 11 m) for fouls inside the penalty area; offside applies when a player is nearer to the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-last defender at the moment the ball is played, with exceptions. Yellow/red cards caution or send off players. Throw-ins, goal kicks and corner kicks restart play. The Laws are updated annually; recent changes include the 8-second goalkeeper rule (2025/26) and longer stoppage time. VAR (video assistant referee) is used at FIFA competitions since the 2018 World Cup.

History

Modern football was codified in England in 1863 when the Football Association (FA, founded 1863) split from rugby over the carrying rule, creating "association football." The first international was England-Scotland in 1872. FIFA was founded in Paris in 1904 by seven nations and organized the first World Cup in 1930 in Uruguay (won by Uruguay). The World Cup expanded from 16 to 32 teams (1998) and 48 (2026). Pelé, Maradona (1986 "Hand of God" and "Goal of the Century"), Cruyff, Beckenbauer, Zidane, Messi (8 Ballon d'Ors, 2022 World Cup winner) and Ronaldo are among the legends. Women's football grew after the first FIFA Women's World Cup in 1991 (China), won most often by the United States; the 2023 Women's World Cup in Australia/New Zealand drew record audiences.
