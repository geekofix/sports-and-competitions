Outrigger Canoeing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Outrigger canoeing (va'a paddling) is the racing of outrigger canoes, slender hulls stabilized by a float (ama) connected by booms (iako), crewed by one to twelve paddlers (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). The international classes are OC1, OC2, OC4 and OC6 — a six-person single-hull canoe with a rudder steered from seat 6 — and OC12, a double-hull twelve-person boat (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). The sport is governed worldwide by the International Va'a Federation (IVF), founded in 1986, and has been part of the Pacific Games since 1995 (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). Women's and men's crews race in separate V6/V12 events at the world championships under identical boat specifications (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). It is closely related to flatwater canoeing, kayaking and canoe sprint.

Rules

In OC6 sprint racing, six paddlers race 500 m, 1,000 m or 2,000 m courses with turns around buoys, while distance events run up to 42 km, the classic Hamilton Cup in Australia (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). The steerer in seat 6 controls the rudder and calls the strokes; in changeover races paddlers may be substituted (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). Para Va'a, a full medal discipline at the ICF World Championships and at the Paralympics since Rio 2016, follows the same course rules for athletes with impairments in VL1-VL3 classes (International Canoe Federation, n.d.). Since 1990 the IVF has mandated a standard OC6 hull based on the Tahitian Galaide II design, so all crews race identical boats (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). Races are run in heats and finals with lane discipline; false starts and equipment violations bring penalties, and men's and women's events use the same distances and hulls (International Va'a Federation, n.d.).

History

Outrigger canoe racing is rooted in the voyaging traditions of Polynesia, Micronesia and Melanesia, where va'a were the principal ocean-going vessels for more than 3,000 years (Wikipedia, n.d.). Modern competitive racing took shape in Hawaii in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with the founding of clubs such as the Outrigger Canoe Club in 1908, and in Tahiti, where the first organized races date to the 1890s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Va'a Federation was founded in 1986 and brought the sport to the Pacific Games from 1995; the first IVF Va'a World Championships were held in 1988 in Tahiti, and the World Sprints have been held since 1990 with a standardized OC6 hull (International Va'a Federation, n.d.). Today the sport's heartland is Tahiti, Hawaii, New Zealand and Australia, with strong clubs in France, Italy and the Cook Islands (Wikipedia, n.d.).
