Newcomb Ball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Newcomb ball (also called Newcomb, Mamanet or catchball) is a team ball game played on a court divided by a net or rope, in which the ball is caught and then thrown — never hit or batted — over to the opposing side. It was invented in 1895 by Clara Gregory Baer, a physical education instructor at Sophie Newcomb College of Tulane University in New Orleans, and is one of the few sports created by women; it was the second team sport played by women in the United States after basketball. It rivaled volleyball in popularity through the 1920s and may have influenced volleyball's development.

Rules

Two teams of six to nine players line up on opposite sides of a net or rope, with a neutral "base" area behind the centre line that players may not enter. The objective is to throw the ball into the opponents' area so that it hits the ground without being caught — originally called a "touch-down" and worth a point. The receiving team catches the ball and throws it back from the spot where it was caught, with each player throwing rather than hitting. Court size, net height and team size varied historically by gender, age and available space, and today Newcomb survives mainly as a teaching game for volleyball fundamentals, as a cooperative game, and as Mamanet, which has its own world federation and competitions.

History

Clara Gregory Baer devised Newcomb in 1895 while waiting for basketball baskets to arrive at Newcomb College, so that her students had a game that could be arranged quickly, played by any number of students in any space. She first published the game in the Posse Gymnasium Journal in January 1895 and included the first book of rules in her 1895 gymnasium manual "Newcomb and Basquette". It became a competitive tournament sport in schools, colleges and recreational leagues from 1895 into the 1920s, and sport historian Joan Paul argued in the Journal of Sport History (1996) that it likely preceded and influenced William G. Morgan's volleyball, also invented in 1895. Today the game is largely a "lost sport", kept alive in summer camps, adaptive recreation and as Mamanet in Israel and beyond.
