Cherry Pit Spitting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cherry pit spitting is the folk sport of propelling a cherry pit as far as possible from the mouth after eating the fruit: competitors place the pit on the tongue, tense the lips and blow or "spit" it over a measured line, and the longest of three attempts counts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The best-known contest was the International Cherry Pit-Spitting Championship at the Tree-Mendus Fruit Farm in Eau Claire, Michigan, founded in 1974 by farmer Herb Teichman, which ran for 45 years until 2019; its all-time Guinness world record is 28.51 m (93 ft 6.5 in), set in 2004 by Brian "Young Gun" Krause, who the same day reached 33.62 m in the freestyle category (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Rival contests continue elsewhere: the world championship in cherry pit spitting (Kirschkern-Weitspucken) is held at the Annakirmes in Düren, Germany — the 2005 world championship is pictured below — and pit-spitting contests are also part of the National Cherry Festival in Traverse City, Michigan (100th edition in 2026) and of local festivals in Witzenhausen, Germany; the International Cherry Pit Spitting Day is celebrated on the first Saturday of July, 4 July 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Both a men's and a women's division exist at every major contest, and women's record holders are as prominent as the men's — Marlene Krause won the women's title seven times — so the sport's roll of honor is genuinely mixed-gender (Guinness World Records, n.d.). The youth categories (under 5, 6–8, 9–12) make the event family-friendly.

Rules

At sanctioned contests each competitor is given three fresh cherries — organizers supply the fruit so that pits cannot be tampered with or pre-dried — and takes up to three attempts; both feet must stay behind the foul line, the pit must leave from the mouth (no aids, no compressed air), and the longest single spit is measured perpendicularly from the line and recorded (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitions are divided into classifications: men's and women's divisions, youth categories (under 5, ages 6–8, ages 9–12), and a freestyle category in which any technique is allowed as long as the pit leaves the mouth (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There is no world federation: the Michigan championship, which set the Guinness-recognized records, closed in 2019, and today the German Annakirmes world championship in Düren and other local festivals carry the tradition, with Guinness World Records acting as the record-keeping authority (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Fouls include stepping over the line, using a foreign object, or spitting anything other than the provided cherry pit; the measurement is taken by officials from the nearest mark (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The modern competitive era began in 1974 when Herb Teichman, owner of the Tree-Mendus Fruit Farm in Eau Claire, Michigan, organized the first International Cherry Pit-Spitting Championship to draw visitors to his farm stand; the contest grew into an international event and ran for 45 years, ending in 2019 when the farm was sold (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Rick "Pellet Gun" Krause won the men's title 19 times, and his sister Marlene Krause took the women's title seven times; in 2004 Brian "Young Gun" Krause set the Guinness world record of 28.51 m (93 ft 6.5 in) in the standard event and 33.62 m in freestyle on the same day (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In Germany, cherry pit spitting (Kirschkern-Weitspucken) has been contested as a world championship at the Annakirmes in Düren since the 1970s, and the National Cherry Festival in Traverse City, Michigan — whose 100th edition was celebrated in 2026 — has long included a pit-spit contest (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The tradition of spitting cherry pits after eating the fruit is far older than any contest and is found across cherry-growing regions of Europe and North America (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
