Cherry Pit Spitting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cherry pit spitting (cherry stone spitting) is a contest in which competitors spit a cherry pit for distance. The best-known event was the International Cherry Pit-Spitting Championship in Eau Claire, Michigan, USA, founded in 1974 by Herb Teichman at the Tree-Mendus Fruit Farm and held there until 2019, with men's, women's and youth categories. Each contestant gets three cherries and three attempts, and the farthest single spit counts. The Guinness World Record for the greatest distance is 28.51 m (93 ft 6.5 in), set by Brian "Young Gun" Krause in 2004.

Rules

At the championship, contestants stand behind a line and spit pits from fresh cherries; each competitor receives three cherries and up to three attempts, with the longest valid spit counting. Spits are measured from the line to where the pit lands; wind assistance and other aids are not permitted, and officials verify the pit. Categories include men, women and youth, and there is also a freestyle distance category. The contest combined athletic skill with showmanship, with competitors using varied techniques from pursed-lip spits to head-back power spits.

History

Cherry pit spitting contests are an American county-fair tradition, and Herb Teichman started the International Cherry Pit-Spitting Championship at his Tree-Mendus Fruit Farm in Eau Claire, Michigan, in 1974; it ran annually until 2019 and attracted international competitors and media. Brian "Young Gun" Krause set the Guinness world record of 28.51 m in 2004 and also holds the freestyle record (33.62 m). After the Eau Claire contest ended, smaller regional pit-spitting contests continue in the US.
