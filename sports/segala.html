Segala

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Segala is the Basque scything contest: competitors mow a marked strip of meadow with a scythe (sega); the fastest clean, even cut wins. It is a harvest herri kirolak that celebrates the scythe, the traditional mowing tool of the Basque mountains before the motor scythe. Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are Lasto Altxatzea, Zaku Lasterka, Trontza; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

Figure: TheCuttingEdgeScythe. (2026-07-07). Group scything at Southwell, Nottinghamshire [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scything_in_Southwell.jpg

Rules

The strip width and length are marked per competitor; the cut must be clean at ground level without missed patches; the scythe must be hand-sharpened beforehand (peening with a hammer is traditional). Timed heats; men's and women's heats at festivals (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

History

Scything was the core summer labour in the Basque highlands until the 1960s; competitive mowing appears in fête programmes from the late 19th century. Modern scything demonstrations (e.g. Southwell, England) and USDA technique photos show the same craft (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
