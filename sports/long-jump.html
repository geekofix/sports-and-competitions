Long Jump

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The long jump is the horizontal jump of athletics: after a full-speed approach on a 40 m runway the athlete takes off from a 1.22 m board and flies into a sand pit, the distance measured perpendicularly from the take-off line to the nearest mark. Together with the triple jump it forms the 'horizontal jumps'. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic programme since 1896 for men and since 1948 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world records are 8.95 m for men, set by Mike Powell of the USA at the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo, and 7.52 m for women, set by Galina Chistyakova of the Soviet Union in Leningrad on 11 June 1988 — both unbroken in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by Tara Davis-Woodhall of the USA (pictured at Paris 2024), the 2024 Olympic and world champion, whose 7.20 m in Los Angeles on 14 June 2026 was the world lead, with Malaika Mihambo of Germany and Larissa Iapichino of Italy; the men's event was led by Olympic champion Miltiadis Tentoglou of Greece (pictured), whose 8.61 m in Monaco on 10 July 2026 was the 2026 world best (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the jumps family — Triple Jump, High Jump and Pole Vault — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The take-off board is 1.22 m long, 20 cm wide and 10 cm deep, placed 1–3 m from the landing pit, with a plasticine indicator board to detect over-stepping; the jump is measured perpendicularly from the front edge of the take-off line to the nearest break in the landing material, to the nearest centimetre (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A foul is recorded for over-stepping the line, landing outside the pit or on its border, or taking off from outside the board's width (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Field events at major championships begin with a qualification round; in the final each athlete has one attempt at each height, and after three attempts the best eight receive three further attempts in reverse order of placings, with a one-minute time limit per attempt (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read for each jump and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates the jump for record purposes; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete separately under identical rules; junior and para competitions use adapted boards and classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

The long jump was part of the ancient Greek pentathlon; in the modern era Bob Beamon's 8.90 m at the high-altitude Mexico City Olympics of 1968 — the famous World Press Photo of the leap, pictured — stood for 23 years until the 1991 World Championships final in Tokyo, where Mike Powell jumped a world record 8.95 m and Carl Lewis's 8.91 m in the same final was wind-assisted and not ratified (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-e). Lewis had won four consecutive Olympic long jump titles from 1984 to 1996. On the women's side, Galina Chistyakova's 7.52 m in Leningrad 1988 and Heike Drechsler's 7.48 m of 1988 remain the two best marks ever (World Athletics, n.d.-e).
