Nine Men's Morris

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Nine Men's Morris (Mühle, Mills) is the ancient alignment game: two players with nine pieces each place them on a 24-point board and then move them along lines; making a 'mill' (three in a row) lets you capture an enemy piece. Boards are carved into ancient monuments (Egypt c. 1400 BC, Nevern Castle). Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Alquerque, Draughts, Latrunculi; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Alfonso X of Castile, 1283).

Figure: Santeri Viinamäki. (2015-02-12). A nine men's morris board in play [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Morris_game_board.JPG

Rules

Phase 1: place pieces on empty points; a mill (three in a row) captures one enemy piece. Phase 2: move pieces to adjacent empty points; mills still capture. Phase 3 (flying): with three pieces left, move anywhere. Player unable to move loses (Alfonso X of Castile, 1283).

History

One of the oldest known games: boards carved in the Temple of Kurna (Egypt, c. 1400 BC) and in the Novgorod Kremlin (12th c., museum photo); depicted with dice in Alfonso X's 1283 Libro de los juegos. Played worldwide; world championships exist (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
