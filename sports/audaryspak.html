Audaryspak

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Audaryspak is the Kazakh form of horseback wrestling, in which two mounted riders try to pull each other out of the saddle; the rider who stays seated while the opponent is dragged to the ground wins the bout (E-history.kz, n.d.-c). It was traditionally part of warrior training on horseback and is held today at Kazakh national holidays and festivals as well as an independent competitive sport, and it is a discipline of the World Nomad Games (E-history.kz, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The closely related Kyrgyz oodarysh and the Kazakh audaryspak are the same game under different names, as the photographs from Kyrgyzstan show.

The sport is open to men and women alike at festival level — women's bouts are increasingly staged at the World Nomad Games alongside the men's — and championships are organised under the auspices of the national federations and the World Nomad Games organisers, with the strongest riders coming from Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan (E-history.kz, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Each bout starts from a mounted face-off: the two riders grip each other (or each other's clothing and saddle) and each tries to unseat the other by strength and balance; the first rider pulled off the horse — or thrown to the ground — loses, and striking the opponent or the horse is forbidden, as is touching the ground while mounted except to lose (E-history.kz, n.d.-c).

Bouts are held on a flat, open arena or field, one-on-one, with weight and gender categories at organised championships (men's and women's divisions at the World Nomad Games) and no age limit beyond adulthood at festival events; the horse must be controlled throughout, and bouts are short, decided in seconds to minutes (E-history.kz, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Mounted wrestling was a training exercise of the steppe warriors of the Kazakh Khanate and other Turkic peoples, teaching the balance, grip and strength needed in real cavalry combat; it survived as a festival game through the Soviet period and was revived as a competitive sport in the 1990s (E-history.kz, n.d.-b; E-history.kz, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Since the first World Nomad Games in 2014 at Issyk-Kul, audaryspak/oodarysh has been a regular medal discipline, with Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan the dominant nations; the Kyrgyz name for the game is oodarysh, and the two are played under the same essential rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; E-history.kz, n.d.-c).
