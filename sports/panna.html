Panna

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Panna (also called Panna Knock Out) is a one-on-one street football game born in the squares of Amsterdam and Rotterdam in the late 1990s, in which two players face off in a small cage and a nutmeg, playing the ball through the opponent's legs, instantly wins the match. The word panna comes from Surinamese Sranan Tongo and means "gate" or "small gate"; in England the same trick is called a nutmeg. The platform Panna Knock Out was created with street players such as Edgar Davids and Nike's Edwin van Zanen, and the first tournament was held in 2001, leading to city, national and world finals. It is related to futsal, beach soccer and freestyle football.

Rules

Panna is played one-on-one in a "panna cage", a small enclosed area of asphalt with two small goals (the recommended cage is about 6 m across with goals of 90 by 50 cm). A match lasts about three minutes, and a goal scores one point. The decisive rule is the panna itself: if a player plays the ball through the opponent's legs and collects it on the other side, the match ends immediately regardless of the score. The official rulesets distinguish a Classic version and a World Finals version, and the community values are style, footwork and skills, with no gloating. Tournaments run locally, nationally and at the World Finals, where qualification events are held worldwide.

History

Panna grew out of the street football culture of Amsterdam and Rotterdam, particularly the Surinamese-Dutch community, where the word panna for a nutmeg has been used for generations. Inspired by technical professionals and AND1 mixtapes, street players in the late 1990s carved out the cage format, and the Panna Knock Out platform was founded around 2001 with Edgar Davids and Edwin van Zanen of Nike; the first tournament took place in 2001. The game spread worldwide through tournaments and social media, and in video games such as EA Sports' FIFA Street a panna game mode was included. Today panna is played across Europe, Africa and the Americas, with world championships held in the Netherlands.
