Panna

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Panna (also called Panna Knock Out) is a one-on-one street football game born in the squares of Amsterdam and Rotterdam in the late 1990s, in which two players face off in a small cage and a nutmeg — playing the ball through the opponent's legs — instantly wins the match (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The word panna comes from Surinamese Sranan Tongo and means "gate" or "small gate"; in England the same trick is called a nutmeg (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The platform Panna Knock Out was created with street players such as Edgar Davids and Nike's Edwin van Zanen, and the first tournament was held in 2001, leading to city, national and world finals with men's and women's divisions (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is related to futsal, beach soccer and freestyle football.

Rules

Panna is played one-on-one in a "panna cage", a small enclosed area of asphalt with two small goals — the recommended cage is about 6 m across with goals of 90 by 50 cm (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match lasts about three minutes, and a goal scores one point (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The decisive rule is the panna itself: if a player plays the ball through the opponent's legs and collects it on the other side, the match ends immediately regardless of the score — the "nutmeg" or panna is an instant win (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The official rulesets distinguish goalkeeper rules (the keeper may use hands in the goal area) and fair-play rules, with referees in organized Panna Knock Out events (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's tournaments use identical cage dimensions and rules, with separate divisions at world finals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Street football cage games have been played in the Netherlands since the 1970s-1980s, but panna as a codified one-on-one format emerged in Amsterdam and Rotterdam in the late 1990s, when players began playing for instant-win nutmegs in small cages (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Panna Knock Out brand was created in 2001 with former Ajax and Netherlands midfielder Edgar Davids and Nike's Edwin van Zanen, and the first official tournament was held the same year; city, national and world finals followed, with events sponsored by Tiger Beer in Thailand and elsewhere (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Davids, famous for his street skills and goggles, became the public face of the format (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today panna cages are installed in cities worldwide and the World Panna Championship crowns men's and women's champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
