Cable Wakeboarding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cable wakeboarding is wakeboarding behind an overhead cable system instead of a boat: the rider is pulled by a cable running around towers over a lake or cable park, hitting rails, kickers and other features. It is governed by the International Waterski & Wakeboard Federation (IWWF) through its Cable Wakeboard World Council, which publishes the IWWF Cable Wakeboard World Rule Book and runs the IWWF World Cable Wakeboard & Wakeskate Championships.

Rules

Under the IWWF Cable Wakeboard World Rule Book, riders get two passes over the course per round and are judged on their tricks on the features (rails, kickers, boxes) and on the water. Riders must wear a suitable well-fitted helmet and a life vest in the course; performing a routine without a helmet means disqualification. Landing an inverted aerial from the water onto a feature is not allowed. Divisions include men/women, open and junior categories, plus wakeskate; at World Championships the best results from heats count. The cable system runs at about 30-36 km/h (19-22 mph) around a loop of towers.

History

Cable wakeboarding began in the late 1990s in Europe and the US as an alternative to boat wakeboarding, with the first cable parks opening in Germany and Florida around 1999-2001. The IWWF took over governance through the Cable Wakeboard World Council, and the first IWWF World Cable Wakeboard Championships were held in the 2000s; the 2026 edition is scheduled for Beijing, China. Cable parks have spread worldwide, and cable wakeboard and cableski are included in the official program of The World Games 2029 in Karlsruhe.
