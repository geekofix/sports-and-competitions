Futnet

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Futnet — also known as football tennis, soccer tennis, or nohejbal in Czech and Slovak — is a sport played with a football-sized ball on a court divided by a low net (1.10 m high in the middle, 1.05 m at the sides), in which teams of one, two or three players try to make the ball bounce in the opponent's area using any body part except the hands and arms (Union Internationale de Futnet [UNIF], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game was invented in 1922 by footballers of Slavia Prague, who first played it over a suspended rope and later over a net ("football over the rope"); the first official rules were written in 1940, and futnet was recognized as an official sport by the Czechoslovak Sports Organisation (ČSTV) in 1961 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Today it is governed worldwide by the UNIF, founded in 2010 in Geneva by 15 countries from four continents (now 28 member associations), with the European Futnet Association (EFTA), founded in April 2010 in Marseille, as its continental body in Europe; the older FIFTA (International Footballtennis Association, founded 1987) still runs its own world championships (UNIF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). European championships have been held since 1991 and world championships since 1994, usually every two years (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Futnet World Championships Men 2026 were held in Prague, Czech Republic, from 2–6 December 2026 (competition 3–6 December), organized by UNIF with the Czech Futnet Association, in the three disciplines singles, doubles and triples; the FIFTA Football Tennis World Championships 2026 took place 19–24 October 2026 in Famagusta, Northern Cyprus (UNIF, n.d.-b; FIFTA, n.d.) (Union Internationale de Futnet, n.d.-a) (Union Internationale de Futnet, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading nations are the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Romania and Hungary, traditionally the strongest in all three disciplines, with Poland, France, Switzerland and Brazil among the growing challengers (UNIF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Under the UNIF Laws of the Game, futnet is played in three disciplines with identical scoring: singles (1 v 1, two touches and one bounce allowed per rally, court 9 × 12.8 m), doubles (2 v 2, three touches with no two consecutive by the same player, one bounce for men and two for women and juniors, court 9 × 12.8 m) and triples (3 v 3, three touches, one bounce for men and two for women and juniors, court 9 × 18 m) (UNIF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A set is won at 11 points with a two-point lead, capped at 15–14, and a match is the best of two sets (UNIF, n.d.-a). Players may use any part of the body except hands and arms — head, chest, thigh, foot — but may not touch the net during play, which awards a point to the opponents (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ball is a glued 32-panel synthetic-leather ball of football size that must bounce more than 0.5 m when properly inflated (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). If the ball hits the net and returns to the striker's own side, it may not be played again — unlike volleyball — and the point goes to the opponent (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play the same court and net, with the difference that women and juniors are allowed two bounces in doubles and triples; there are open and under-21 age categories at world championships (UNIF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Futnet began in 1922 in Prague, when players of the football club Slavia Prague started kicking a ball over a horizontal rope, later replaced by a net, calling the game "football over the rope" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first official rules were written in 1940 and the first futnet cup was played the same year; between 1953 and 1961 the first amateur league, the Trampská liga, was played, and in 1961 futnet was recognized as an official sport by the Czechoslovak Sports Organisation (ČSTV), with the Prague Futnet Commission established (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Czech Futnet Association followed in 1971 and the Slovak association in 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Internationally, the IFTA (later FIFTA) was founded in 1987, the EFTA in Marseille in April 2010 to revive the stagnant European scene, and the UNIF in 2010 in Geneva by former FIFTA members joined by new nations — by December 2012 it counted 17 member countries (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). World championships have been held every two years since the inaugural 1994 edition in Košice, Slovakia — won by Hungary in singles, the Czech Republic in doubles and Slovakia in triples — with later editions in Maceió 1996, Szolnok 1998, Prostějov 2000, Szombathely 2002, Oradea 2006, Nymburk 2008, Istanbul 2010, Nymburk 2012, North Nicosia 2014 and Prague 2022, dominated by Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Romania and Hungary (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
