Standing Volleyball

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Standing volleyball is volleyball played standing by athletes with a physical disability — typically amputees, with or without prostheses — under adapted rules on a standard volleyball court, and it was one of the original Paralympic disciplines when standing and sitting volleyball were both added at Arnhem 1980 (World ParaVolley, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Today the international movement World ParaVolley (WOVD) focuses on sitting volleyball and beach paravolley at world level, while standing volleyball continues at national and club level with world cup events such as the 2011 World Cup shown below (Wikipedia, n.d.). The rules follow the FIVB Laws of the Game with adaptations: six players per side on court, a standard 18 x 9 m court and 2.43 m (men) / 2.24 m (women) net, rotation, three touches per side and a point on every rally (World ParaVolley, n.d.). Both a men's and a women's division exist; women's sitting volleyball joined the Paralympics in 2004, and standing volleyball is open to women and men in their own divisions (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the net and court team ball games family on this site, its closest relatives are Volleyball, Sitting Volleyball, Beach Volleyball, Beach Volleyball 4x4, Snow Volleyball; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Standing volleyball uses the FIVB Laws of the Game with para-volley adaptations: teams of six on court plus liberos, a 2.43 m net for men and 2.24 m for women, rally-point scoring — sets to 25 points won by a two-point margin, with a deciding fifth set to 15 — best-of-five sets in international matches, and the same rotation, blocking and attacking rules as able-bodied volleyball (World ParaVolley, n.d.). The standing-specific classification system (VS1/VS2, shared with sitting volleyball) evaluates the functional impact of the impairment; in sitting volleyball at most one VS2-classified player may be on court at a time, and standing volleyball applies the same classification framework for eligibility. Fouls — more than three touches, a held or thrown ball, a net touch, a foot over the centre line — are penalized as in FIVB rules, awarding the rally to the opponent (Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches are played in pools followed by knockout rounds at world cups and Paralympic qualification events, with separate men's and women's tournaments (World ParaVolley, n.d.).

History

Volleyball for athletes with disabilities began in the Netherlands in the 1950s as rehabilitation therapy, and standing volleyball — for amputees and others who can play standing — was demonstrated at the 1976 Toronto Games and became an official Paralympic medal sport at Arnhem 1980 alongside sitting volleyball (Wikipedia, n.d.). Sitting volleyball grew faster internationally and women's sitting volleyball joined the Paralympics at Athens 2004, while standing volleyball remained strong in Europe and at club level, with world cup tournaments such as the 2011 edition continuing the tradition (World ParaVolley, n.d.). The Dutch and German federations were pioneers of the standing game in the 1960s and 1970s (Wikipedia, n.d.).
