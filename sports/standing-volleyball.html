Standing Volleyball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Standing volleyball is the Paralympic discipline of volleyball played standing up by athletes with physical disabilities "+E+" classically amputees, but also players with arm, leg and other impairments. It is played on an integrated court under FIVB rules, and it was among the first team sports for athletes with disabilities to reach the Paralympic Games; its beach offshoot, Beach ParaVolley, contests three-a-side standing volleyball on sand.

Rules

The game follows standard FIVB volleyball rules: six players a side, three touches to return the ball over the net, rally scoring and rotating positions. What makes it distinct is classification: athletes are grouped by the nature and degree of their impairment, and amputee players may choose to play on a prosthesis or to move on their sound leg, with some above-knee amputees preferring to hop without one; balanced team rules require a minimum number of players in higher impairment classes, keeping the court genuinely integrated. The same principles apply to Beach ParaVolley, a three-a-side version played on standard beach volleyball courts under the Paralympic classification system, which World ParaVolley has promoted at international events.

History

Standing volleyball has one of the longest Paralympic pedigrees in team sport: it debuted for men at the Toronto 1976 Paralympics "+E+" the first team sport for amputee athletes added to the programme "+E+" and appeared at every Summer Games through Athens 2004, when the men's tournament left the programme. The sport's international governance grew out of organizations such as the International Sports Organization for the Disabled and became today's World ParaVolley, which has continued world championships and development programs for both indoor standing volleyball and Beach ParaVolley, broadened eligibility across disability classes and nations, and promoted bids for the sport's return to major multi-sport events.
