Pesäpallo

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Pesäpallo (Finnish for "nest ball", Swedish boboll, also known as Finnish baseball or colloquially pesis) is a bat-and-ball sport that is the national game of Finland, invented in the 1920s by the athlete and physical educator Lauri "Tahko" Pihkala (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two teams take turns batting and fielding, superficially resembling baseball, softball and rounders, but with a different field, a vertical pitch and radically different tactics. The top league, Superpesis, runs separate men's and women's championships — the women's Superpesis crowns its own champion each season alongside the men's — and the Pesäpallo World Cup is played internationally about every three years (Superpesis, n.d.). Rules

The batting team scores runs (juoksut) by hitting the ball and advancing along the base path back to home base, while the fielding team tries to put the batter and the runners out (Suomen Pesäpalloliitto, n.d.). The most striking differences from baseball concern the field and the pitch. The three bases are not arranged in a diamond but lead away from home base in a spread pattern, and the pitcher (lukkari) stands directly opposite the batter at the home base, throwing the ball vertically so that it rises at least one meter and drops onto a circular pitching plate of 60 cm diameter placed in front of the batter, who strikes the ball as it drops. A hit that would be a home run in baseball counts as a foul in pesäpallo instead, so all runs are effectively of the inside-the-park variety and the game rewards placement and quick thinking over raw power. A men's field measures about 93.5 by 62 meters (women's about 82 by 56 meters), far larger than a baseball infield, and fielders position themselves across the wide outfield. The manager directs the offense with a multicoloured fan of signals, and the defense with hand signals — a unique tactical layer. Matches consist of two periods (jakso) of four innings each; a tied match goes to extra innings ( supervuoro), where the first team to score in an extra inning wins. Men's and women's matches use the same rules with the smaller women's field dimensions (Suomen Pesäpalloliitto, n.d.).

History

Pesäpallo, a combination of traditional Finnish ball-batting games and North American baseball, was developed by Lauri "Tahko" Pihkala in the 1920s after his travels in the United States; he coined the name from pesä, "nest", for the bases (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first match was played on 14 November 1920 at Kaisaniemi Park in Helsinki, and pesäpallo was a demonstration sport at the 1952 Summer Olympics in Helsinki (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The main leagues began to be called Superpesis in 1990, and Veikkaus introduced pesäpallo betting in 1993 — which led to the biggest crisis in the sport's history, the 1998 match-fixing scandal, after which betting was suspended for years and the eight involved teams were relegated (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Public interest recovered by 2009, and today Superpesis matches are broadcast nationally and streamed online; the record attendance of 7,640 was set by Hyvinkään Tahko vs Sotkamon Jymy in 1992 (Superpesis, n.d.). The Pesäpallo World Cup brings together national teams from Finland, Germany, Sweden, Switzerland, Australia and Canada (Suomen Pesäpalloliitto, n.d.).
