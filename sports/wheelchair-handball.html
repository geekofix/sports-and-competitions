Wheelchair Handball

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wheelchair handball is the adaptation of team handball for wheelchair athletes, played on a full-size handball court by two teams — in six-a-side form six players including a goalkeeper, and in the newer four-a-side form four players, with any teammate allowed to act in goal — throwing a handball into the opponent's goal while manoeuvring a sports wheelchair (International Handball Federation, n.d.). The discipline is explicitly mixed-gender: at least one woman must be on the court at all times and at least two women must be in each final roster, so the women's game is built into the format at the highest level (International Handball Federation, n.d.).

It is a developing rather than established Paralympic discipline — not yet on the Paralympic programme, with Brisbane 2032 a stated target — and is promoted worldwide by the International Handball Federation (IHF), which formed a Wheelchair Handball Working Group in 2018 after 40 national federations expressed interest at the 2019 IHF Congress (Wikipedia, n.d.). The IHF Four-a-Side Wheelchair Handball World Championship debuted in Egypt in 2022 with Brazil winning and was staged again in Egypt in 2024, while the six-a-side World and European Championship took place in Portugal in 2022 (International Handball Federation, n.d.).

Within the Para sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Wheelchair Basketball, Wheelchair Rugby, Goalball, Para Table Tennis, Para Badminton; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Six-a-side wheelchair handball follows indoor handball closely: two teams of six players including one goalkeeper, a 40 × 20 m court, goals of 3 × 1.7 m with the net secured so wheelchairs cannot entangle, and play governed by the Laws of the Game with specific amendments for chair use (International Handball Federation, n.d.). The four-a-side variant, influenced by beach handball, is played by two teams of four on the same court dimensions; any player may act as goalkeeper, but only one may be in the goal area at a time, and the format is mixed with at least one female player on court at all times (International Handball Federation, n.d.). In both variants the ball is advanced by passing and by dribbling — typically bouncing the ball on the lap between wheelchair pushes — goals are scored by throwing the ball into the opponent's goal, and only verified sports wheelchairs are permitted; the IHF has also published a classification manual developed with international classifiers to ensure fair competition across impairment types (Wikipedia, n.d.). Revised IHF Rules of the Game for both variants take effect on 1 July 2026 (International Handball Federation, n.d.).

History

Variants of wheelchair handball — including wheelball in Serbia and similar games — developed in several countries during the 1970s and 1980s as part of the broader adapted-sports movement for athletes with disabilities, played at national disability-sport events and demonstration tournaments without uniform rules (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Handball Federation began coordinating the discipline internationally in October 2018, when its Council in Doha unanimously agreed to form a Wheelchair Handball Working Group chaired by Dr. František Taborský, whose experience with the sport dated back to 1993; the first working-group meeting followed in Basel in October 2019, and the IHF Congress in Gothenburg in July 2019 saw 40 national federations register interest (International Handball Federation, n.d.). Standardised rules for the four-a-side and six-a-side variants were issued from 2020, the first IHF Four-a-Side World Championship was held in Egypt in September 2022, and the IHF now focuses on the four-a-side variant for future international events on the pathway toward Paralympic inclusion (Wikipedia, n.d.).
