Frontenis

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Frontenis is a racket-and-ball sport of the Basque pelota family played by pairs (doubles) — and casually in singles — on a 30 m frontón (fronton) of 30 × 10 m with 10 m high walls, using a tennis-like racket and a rubber ball (International Federation of Basque Pelota [FIPV], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ball must strike the front wall (frontis) before bouncing a second time, and the opponents must return it before that second bounce (FIPV, n.d.-a). Frontenis originated in Mexico around 1900 — tennis players hitting a ball against a wall in the house of Fernando Torreblanca, giving the name "frontontenis," later shortened to frontenis — and reached Spain via the Canary Islands in the 1940s; today it is played in around 18 countries, with Mexico the traditional powerhouse (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is one of the eight disciplines recognized by the FIPV (founded in Buenos Aires on 19 May 1929), alongside hand pelota, frontball, cesta punta, xare, paleta cuero, pala corta and paleta goma, and the 30 m frontón also hosts paleta goma (FIPV, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The XX Basque Pelota World Championship 2026 took place in Buenos Aires, Argentina, and Colonia del Sacramento, Uruguay, from 15 to 24 October 2026 — the first edition hosted by Argentina — with frontenis contested for men and women in the 30 m frontón, and the Liga de Naciones of October 2025 in Aguascalientes, Mexico (17 federations, 180 athletes) serving as the qualifier (FIPV, n.d.-b) (Federación Internacional de Pelota Vasca, n.d.-a) (Federación Internacional de Pelota Vasca, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the pairs that reached the women's final of the 2026 World Championship in the 30 m frontón, with Mexico and Spain the dominant nations in both categories (FIPV, n.d.-b). On the men's side, Mexico remains the most successful country in the history of the world championship, ahead of Spain, France and Argentina (FIPV, n.d.-b).

Rules

Frontenis is played on a 30 m frontón: 30 m long, 10 m wide, with a 10 m high front wall (frontis), a left wall marked every 3.5 m to help players position themselves, and a back wall; the lower 60 cm of the front wall is not playable and is topped by a metal sheet (chapa) that makes a ringing sound when struck (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; FIPV, n.d.-a). The two official balls are rubber balls: the frontenis preolímpico (Spanish origin, more popular as a leisure game) and the frontenis olímpico (Mexican origin), which is the official ball of the World Championships; the racket is similar to a tennis racket, of wood, fiber, metal or graphite, with no limit on weight, length or stringing, though double stringing is used in Olympic frontenis (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each point begins with a serve from the service area: the ball must bounce once on the court and then strike the frontis before the opponents hit it; during the rally, every stroke must send the ball to the front wall before it bounces twice on the floor, and a player may not hit the ball twice in a row (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match at senior international level is played as the best of two games to 15 points plus a deciding third game to 10 if necessary under the Spanish federation regulations (Real Federación Española de Pelota [FEP], n.d.). Men and women play identical rules and the same 30 m frontón, in separate men's and women's categories at world championships; the game can also be played in singles for recreation, though only doubles is contested internationally (FIPV, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Frontenis was created in Mexico around 1900 when tennis players — among them Buttlin, Sharp, Crowle, Pérez Verdia and Maldonado — began hitting a tennis ball against a wall with rackets at the pelota court built in Fernando Torreblanca's house; the name combined frontón (pelota court) and tenis (tennis) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game spread from Mexico to neighboring countries and reached the Iberian Peninsula via the Canary Islands in the 1940s; in the 1960s the pressurized rubber "Olympic ball" was introduced in Mexico by Francisco Beltrán — founder of the "201" ball company with Dr. Jesús Ledezma — replacing stripped tennis balls and oilcloth balls, and Mexican players then taught the technique in Spain, from where it spread across Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Frontenis entered international competition in 1952, when the first World Championship was held in Donostia-San Sebastián, Spain (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the legendary players is the Argentine Aarón Sehter (born 1934), winner of thirteen world gold medals — the most by any pelotari — including a frontenis silver medal at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, where Basque pelota was a demonstration sport, and a UNESCO Fair Play prize in 1989 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
