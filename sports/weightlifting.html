Weightlifting

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Weightlifting — Olympic weightlifting — is the strength sport in which athletes lift a loaded barbell from the platform to overhead in two judged lifts: the snatch, one continuous wide-grip pull into a deep squat, and the clean and jerk, a two-part movement to the shoulders and then overhead (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.). Each lifter takes three attempts at each lift, and the total score is the sum of the heaviest valid snatch and clean and jerk, contested in bodyweight categories that are separate for men and women (Olympics, n.d.). The sport appeared at the first modern Olympics in Athens in 1896 and has been on the programme at every Games since Antwerp 1920, and it is governed worldwide by the International Weightlifting Federation (IWF), headquartered in Lausanne with 193 affiliated national federations and Mohammed Hasan Jalood as president since June 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women's weightlifting entered the Olympic programme at Sydney 2000 after the first women's world championships in 1987; at Paris 2024 the field was trimmed to 120 lifters across ten events — men's 61, 73, 89, 102 and +102 kg and women's 49, 59, 71, 81 and +81 kg — and Los Angeles 2028 is confirmed for twelve events with 120 athletes (Olympics, n.d.). At the top of the women's sport today stands China's Li Wenwen, Olympic champion at +81 kg in Paris 2024 and a multiple world record holder (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Competition takes place on a 4 m × 4 m platform. Since 1973 the programme has consisted of exactly two lifts — the snatch and the clean and jerk (the clean and press was dropped after 1972) — and each lifter gets three attempts at each, taking the snatch first; the bar rises through the session, and the winner in each class is the athlete with the highest combined total, with ties broken by lower bodyweight and then by who reached the total first (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.). A lift is valid only if the bar is received with control — in the snatch the lifter drops under the bar into a squat and stands with arms locked; in the jerk the bar is driven overhead from a split or squat position — and the bar must remain motionless with arms, legs and feet in line until the referees' down signal; any downward movement of the bar, or losing control, voids the attempt. Three referees judge each lift by majority vote. The clock allows one minute between attempts, extended to two minutes when the same lifter must lift again. The men's barbell weighs 20 kg (28 mm shaft, 2,200 mm long) and the women's 15 kg (25 mm shaft, 2,010 mm long), with identical 1,310 mm sleeve spacing. Under current IWF rules there are eight bodyweight categories per gender at world and continental championships, and each Olympic Games selects a subset of classes; all competition is subject to WADA-compliant anti-doping control (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Lifting contests are among the oldest organised sports, and weightlifting appeared at the first modern Olympics in Athens in 1896 — Great Britain's Launceston Elliot won the one-hand lift and Denmark's Viggo Jensen the two-hand — but the sport was then absent in 1904, 1908 and 1912 before returning at Antwerp 1920, where it has remained ever since (Wikipedia, n.d.). The international federation's lineage runs from the Amateur-Athleten-Weltunion founded on 10 June 1905 in Duisburg, Germany, which split in 1920 into separate weightlifting and wrestling bodies, became the Fédération Haltérophile Internationale, and took its current name, the International Weightlifting Federation, in 1972 (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.). The women's era began with the first women's world championships in 1987 — won among others by the American Karyn Marshall and China's Cai Jun — and reached the Olympics at Sydney 2000, where China's Chen Yanqing began a run of two consecutive Olympic golds in 2004 and 2008 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Icons of the men's sport include the Turk Naim Süleymanoğlu (1967–2017), the three-time Olympic champion nicknamed the Pocket Hercules, alongside Halil Mutlu, Kakhi Kakhiashvili and Pyrros Dimas (Wikipedia, n.d.).
