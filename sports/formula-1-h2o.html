Formula 1 H2O

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

F1H2O is the sprint world championship of powerboat racing, sanctioned by the UIM: identical-spec tunnel-hull boats with 2.5 L outboards race short, spectator-friendly circuits in city harbours (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the powerboat family with offshore racing and the new E1 Series.

Figure: Ch. (2026-08-02 15:34:28). UIM F1H2O Grand Prix of Kyrgyzstan, Issyk-Kul, 2 August 2026 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/UIM_F1H2O_Grand_Prix_of_Kyrgyzstan_%E2%80%94_Issyk-Kul_%2802-08-2026%29_%2850%29.jpg

The UIM F1H2O World Championship has run since 1984; each Grand Prix is about 6 laps on a 1.5–2 km buoy course with a standing start, and the season champion is decided over 6–10 rounds worldwide (UIM, n.d.). Boats reach about 250 km/h on the straights (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Spec rules: hull and engine are controlled (currently 2.5 L Mercury-based or other homologated outboards depending on era), with strict safety cells, kill switches and rescue teams (UIM, n.d.). The 2026 calendar included a debut Grand Prix at Issyk-Kul, Kyrgyzstan (photo above) (UIM, 2026). Men and women compete — women have raced in F1H2O Grands Prix.

History

F1H2O was founded in 1984 by UIM president Bill Braden and promoter Jean-Maurice Bailly as "Formula 1 on water"; early rounds in Europe (Italy, France) and Asia made it a world tour, and Indonesian and Chinese rounds in the 2010s broadened it (Wikipedia, n.d.). Champions include Guido Cappellini (10 titles) and Shaun Torrente (multiple titles).
