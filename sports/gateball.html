Gateball

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gateball is a non-contact team sport played with a wooden mallet and plastic balls on a rectangular court of 20 × 15 m (internationally 20–25 m × 15–20 m), invented in 1947 by Kazunobu (Eiji) Suzuki in Memuro, Hokkaido, Japan, as a croquet variant playable with wood instead of rubber after the post-war rubber shortage (World Gateball Union [WGU], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two teams of up to five players — red balls with odd numbers 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and white balls with even numbers 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 — take turns hitting their own ball through three gates and finally at the goal pole; each gate passed in order scores 1 point and touching the goal pole (a "spark") scores 2 points, over a 30-minute game (WGU, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed worldwide by the WGU, established in Tokyo in 1985 after the Japan Gateball Union (founded 1984 under first president Ryōichi Sasakawa), and the Official International Gateball Rules were revised effective 1 April 2025 (WGU, n.d.-a). The first World Gateball Championship was held in Sapporo, Hokkaido, in 1986; the championships became quadrennial in 2018 — the 12th edition in São Paulo, Brazil, in September 2018 drew 90 teams from 20 countries and regions — and the 13th edition, postponed from 2022, was merged into the 2026 championship year (WGU, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Gateball was an exhibition sport at the 6th World Games in Akita in 2001, and more than 8 million people play it worldwide, 6 million of them in Japan, with the game popular among seniors but open to all ages and both sexes (WGU, n.d.-a) (World Gateball Union, n.d.-a) (World Gateball Union, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading teams are Japan, Brazil, Chinese Taipei, South Korea and Australia at world level, and the 2025 calendar included the 10th International Friendship Tournament in Memuro, the 2nd Pan American Championship and the Australian Championships (WGU, n.d.-b). Because of its low-impact, all-ages nature, women and men compete together in many club and veterans' tournaments (WGU, n.d.-a).

Rules

Under the WGU Official Rules (revised effective 1 April 2025), the court is 20 m × 15 m (up to 25 × 20 m) with a starting line, three gates and a goal pole; each team has five balls (red odd numbers, white even numbers), and every player plays the same ball throughout the game (WGU, n.d.-a). Play starts with the red team; turns alternate between the teams, and the white team always takes the final turn even if the 30-minute limit expires before its last player is called (WGU, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A player places their ball on the starting line and strikes it with the mallet through gate 1, gate 2 and gate 3 in order — each gate scored in order gives 1 point and grants an extra stroke — and finally at the goal pole for 2 points (a "spark"); a ball that passes a gate but ends out of bounds is an "out ball" that re-enters at the next turn (WGU, n.d.-a). When a ball touches another ball ("touch"), the striker places the touched ball against their own ball, steps on their ball and hits it, sending the other ball away ("croquet" or "croc"); touching a ball also grants an extra stroke (WGU, n.d.-a). Only the striker's ball may be struck with the mallet; the winner is the team with the higher score at the end of 30 minutes, with an "agile" finish if one team reaches 5 points first (WGU, n.d.-a). There are no gender or age divisions in the basic rules — mixed teams are common — and the game can be played indoors or outdoors (WGU, n.d.-a).

History

Gateball was invented in 1947 by Kazunobu Suzuki, a wood-industry worker in Memuro, Hokkaido, who adapted croquet rules using wooden balls and mallets because rubber was scarce after World War II; he designed the game for young people, and it spread when a physical-education instructor introduced gates into women's societies and elderly clubs in Kumamoto in the late 1950s, with the Kumamoto Gateball Association founded in 1962 and a national demonstration in 1976 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Japan Gateball Union was founded in 1984 under its first president, the shipping magnate Ryōichi Sasakawa, and the World Gateball Union followed in Tokyo in 1985 with founding members from Japan, China, South Korea, Chinese Taipei, Brazil and the United States (WGU, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first World Championship was held at Maruyama Stadium in Sapporo in 1986, with editions in Yokohama 1987, São Paulo 1988, Nagoya 1989, Seoul 1990, Wakayama 1994, Honolulu 1998, Toyama 2002, Jeju 2006, Shanghai 2010, Niigata 2014 and São Paulo 2018 (WGU, n.d.-b). Gateball was an exhibition sport at the 6th World Games in Akita in 2001, won by a young Japanese team (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
