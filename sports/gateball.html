Gateball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gateball is a team sport born in Japan in 1947 (originally called "croquet"), played by two teams of five on a 20 m x 15 m (or 25 m x 20 m) court with mallets, balls, gates and a goal pole. Each player hits their own colored ball through gates in sequence and finally at the goal pole to score; the team with the most points after 30 minutes wins. It is governed by the World Gateball Union (WGU), which runs the World Gateball Championships.

Rules

Under the WGU Official International Rules (applied at World and Asia Championships), a match lasts 30 minutes. Five players per team; each player uses a ball matching their team color (red odd numbers, white even numbers). Play starts from the start line; the batter hits the ball through Gate 1, Gate 2 and Gate 3 in order, and a point is scored only when the ball is hit onto the goal pole (agari). Touching another player's ball with your mallet or ball sends the touched ball (and possibly your own) to the touch line. The team with more points at the end wins; ties are broken by extra time. Helmets are required in designated WGU categories.

History

Gateball was invented in 1947 in Hokkaido, Japan, by Eiji Suzuki as "croquet" for post-war recreation, renamed gateball in 1970. It spread across Japan and Asia, especially among older adults, and worldwide through Japanese communities. The World Gateball Union (WGU) was established in Tokyo and governs national federations in over 30 countries; it runs the World Gateball Championships, held regularly since 1990, and the Asia Gateball Championships. Japan's Japan Gateball Union (JGU) is the national body.
