Ski Jumping

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ski jumping is a winter sport in which athletes launch from a ramp, fly as far and stylishly as possible, and land on a steep hillside, scored for distance and style by judges under rules of the International Ski Federation (FIS) (FIS, n.d.-a). The FIS Ski Jumping World Cup was founded by the Norwegian Torbjørn Yggeseth for the 1979–80 season and is today the sport's premier annual circuit; the 2025–26 season concluded on 29 March 2026 with the Planica 7 festival in Slovenia (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, ski jumping was staged at Val di Fiemme (Predazzo), Italy, from 7–16 February 2026, with men's and women's individual events on the normal and large hills plus a mixed team event (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the current leading women is Nika Prevc of Slovenia, the dominant force of the 2025–26 season, alongside Japan's Sara Takanashi, the record holder with 63 World Cup victories, and Slovenia's Ema Klinec; among the men, Domen Prevc of Slovenia won the 2025–26 World Cup overall (FIS, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the FIS International Competition Rules, ski jumping is contested on normal hills (construction point K 90–120 m) and large hills (K 120–200 m); ski flying hills (K 185 m and above) form a separate discipline (FIS, n.d.-a). Each jump is scored as measured distance points plus style points from five judges — the highest and lowest style marks are dropped — with automatic compensation for wind and for the starting gate, so that changing conditions are equalised; the sum of both rounds decides the result, the second round being jumped in reversed order of the intermediate standings (FIS, n.d.-a). World Cup and Olympic programmes include individual events for men and women on both hill sizes plus a mixed team event (two women and two men per nation); qualification rounds reduce the field before the final (FIS, n.d.-b). Women have competed in their own World Cup since the 2011–12 season and at the Olympics since Sochi 2014, on the same hills and under the same regulations as the men, and junior (under-20) categories exist alongside the elites (FIS, n.d.-b). Athletes wear a one-piece suit, a helmet and skis whose maximum length is tied to body-mass index, and landing without the telemark mark costs style points; falls beyond the K-point forfeit the distance of the round (FIS, n.d.-a).

History

The first recorded ski jumping competition took place in 1808 in Eidsberg, Norway, won by Olaf Rye, and Sondre Norheim of Telemark pioneered the modern jumping style in the 1860s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Holmenkollen ski festival, first held in 1892 in Oslo, became the sport's spiritual home, and the FIS was founded in Chamonix on 18 February 1924 during the first Winter Olympic Games, with ski jumping on the 1924 programme (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Birger Ruud of Norway became the first triple Olympic champion in the sport (1932, 1936 and 1948), and Matti Nykänen of Finland won four Olympic gold medals between Calgary 1984 and Lillehammer 1988, a record that still stands, alongside nine world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Other icons of the modern era are Finland's Janne Ahonen, Poland's Adam Małysz, and Austria's Gregor Schlierenzauer, all multiple world champions and Olympic medallists who are now retired from the elite circuit (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIS World Cup, launched by Torbjørn Yggeseth in 1979, transformed the sport into a year-round global circuit, and since the 1990s the Four Hills Tournament (Oberstdorf, Garmisch-Partenkirchen, Innsbruck, Bischofshofen) has decided its own overall title each New Year (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
