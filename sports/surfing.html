Surfing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Surfing is the sport of riding breaking ocean waves while standing on a board; at the elite level it is run as a professional Championship Tour by the World Surf League (WSL, founded in 1976 as the International Professional Surfers by Fred Hemmings and Randy Rarick), while the International Surfing Association (ISA, with 86+ member nations) governs Olympic surfing and the World Surfing Games (World Surf League, n.d.; International Surfing Association, n.d.). Surfing debuted at the Olympic Games at Tokyo 2020 — Italo Ferreira and Carissa Moore won gold — and at Paris 2024 at Teahupo'o, Tahiti, the women's gold went to the USA's Caroline Marks (silver Tatiana Weston-Webb of Brazil, bronze Johanne Defay of France) and the men's to France's Kauli Vaast (silver Jack Robinson of Australia, bronze Gabriel Medina of Brazil) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2025 WSL world champions are Molly Picklum of Australia (women) and Yago Dora of Brazil (men); the 2026 Championship Tour has 12 stops with an expanded 24-woman field and a Pipe Masters finale worth 1.5× points (World Surf League, n.d.). At the 2025 ISA World Surfing Games in El Salvador the women's podium was Janire González Etxabarri (Spain), Yolanda Hopkins (Portugal) and Sally Fitzgibbons (Australia) (International Surfing Association, n.d.).

Rules

In a heat, two to four surfers (up to five in early rounds) share the lineup for a set time — about 25–35 minutes on the CT — and each surfer's score is the sum of their two best waves, each wave judged 0.1–10.0 on the difficulty of maneuvers chosen and their speed, power and flow; the highest heat total advances (World Surf League, n.d.). Priority — the right of way on a wave — belongs to the surfer closest to the peak, and interference penalties can cost a wave's score (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment: shortboards typically 5'6"–6'6" (CT maximum 7'0"), leash mandatory, with separate divisions for longboard, bodyboard and stand-up paddle in ISA events; Olympic surfing uses shortboards only (International Surfing Association, n.d.). Men's and women's events are scored identically, and mixed team events (relay format) exist at the World Surfing Games (International Surfing Association, n.d.).

History

Surfing (heʻe nalu) originated in Polynesia and was codified in Hawaii, where Duke Kahanamoku — the Olympic swimming champion of 1912, 1920 and 1924 — popularized the sport worldwide with demonstrations in California (1914), Australia (1915) and elsewhere in the early 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitive surfing grew in California and Australia from the 1960s, and the IPS world tour launched in 1976 with Fred Hemmings and Randy Rarick; icons include Australia's Mark Richards (four titles, 1979–1982), Tom Curren (three), Andy Irons (three), Mick Fanning (three) and Kelly Slater, whose 11 world titles (1992–2011) span the youngest (age 20) and oldest (age 39) champion records (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the women's side, Layne Beachley won seven titles (1998–2006) and Stephanie Gilmore a record eight (2007–2024 era) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
