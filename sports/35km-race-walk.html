35km Race Walk

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 35 kilometres race walk was the long road race of athletics between 2022 and 2025: 35 km on a measured loop under full race walking judging, staged at the World Championships from Oregon 2022 in place of the 50 km. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The world records are 2:20:43 for men, set by Massimo Stano of Italy in Poděbrady on 18 May 2025, and 2:37:15 for women, set by María Pérez of Spain in Poděbrady on 21 May 2023 (World Athletics, n.d.-f).

María Pérez (pictured) was the last women's 35 km world champion, retaining her Tokyo 2025 title in 2:39:01 and completing the 'double double' with the 20 km — since 1 January 2026 the distance has been replaced by the 42.195 km marathon race walk, first staged at the 2026 World Race Walking Team Championships in Brasília, where Paula Torres of Ecuador won the women's marathon in 3:24:47 and Japan's Hayato Katsuki led the men (Xinhua, 2025; World Athletics, n.d.-e). Stano (pictured), the men's record holder, won the 2021 Olympic 35 km title in Tokyo (World Athletics, n.d.-f).

Rules

Race walking is defined by two visible infringements: loss of contact with the ground — the advancing leg must remain straightened from the moment of first contact with the ground until it passes under the body — and any visible loss of contact, each shown with a yellow warning paddle, a red paddle for loss of contact and a red card for a bent knee; three red cards from different judges disqualify the athlete, and the chief judge may award a fourth red card (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Road races are held on closed loops with refreshment stations; at World Athletics and Olympic competitions a panel of race walking judges including the chief judge officiates (World Athletics, n.d.-d). Since 1 January 2026 the 20 km and 35 km have been replaced by the half marathon (21.0975 km) and the marathon (42.195 km) race walk at World Athletics championships — first staged at the 2026 World Race Walking Team Championships in Brasília on 12 April 2026 — and Los Angeles 2028 will stage the half marathon as the only individual walk plus the marathon mixed relay (Xinhua, 2025; World Athletics, n.d.-d).

The 35 km race walk was never an Olympic event — the Olympic road walks were the 20 km and, from Paris 2024, the marathon mixed relay — but it was a championship discipline from 2022, with world records recognised from 1 January 2023; men and women raced the same distance under identical rules, junior events used 10 km, and para athletics offers the distance in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b; Xinhua, 2025).

History

The 35 km distance was introduced at the 2022 World Race Walking Team Championships in Muscat and added to the World Championships programme in Oregon 2022, replacing the 50 km, which had been an Olympic men's event from 1932 to 2020 and was dropped from the Olympic programme after Tokyo 2021 in favour of the marathon mixed relay (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-a). Kimberly García of Peru completed the 20 km/35 km double at the first edition in 2022, and Spain's María Pérez won both distances at the 2023 World Championships in Budapest and again — as the last champion in perpetuity — at Tokyo 2025 (World Athletics, n.d.-f).
