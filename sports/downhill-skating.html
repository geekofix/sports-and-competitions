Downhill Skating

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Downhill skating is gravity racing on inline skates down paved mountain roads at speeds above 100 km/h, with full leather suits, full-face helmets and slide gloves; the elite circuit is the IGSA (International Gravity Sports Association) World Cup and the World Skate downhill events (IGSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Courses are closed public roads with chicanes and hairpins; men's and women's categories, and junior classes, are contested.

Figure: Hagar66. (2008-04-18). Downhill inline skating world championship race on a wet road [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Inline-Skate_Downhill_WM.jpg

Within the skate/roller family on this site its closest relatives are Road Skating, Inline Speed Skating, Longboarding, Downhill Skateboarding, Street Skateboarding and Speed Skiing as the winter speed analogue; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

IGSA/World Skate downhill rules: timed single runs on a closed course, seeded by qualification; full-face helmet and leather suit mandatory, slide gloves required; skates with 4–5 wheels up to 110 mm; false start or course exit voids the run; categories men/women, junior, masters (IGSA, n.d.).

History

Downhill inline skating began in the 1990s in the Alps and the US (California canyon roads); the IGSA was founded in 2002 and has run the World Cup since, with classic venues in France, Italy, Switzerland, Spain and the USA (Wikipedia, n.d.).
