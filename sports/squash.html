Squash

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Squash is a racket sport played by two (singles) or four (doubles) players in a four-walled court, taking turns striking a small, hollow rubber ball onto the front wall above a metal tin so that the opponent cannot make a valid return. Fast, physically relentless and played in more than 150 countries, it is one of the world's most popular indoor games, with a professional tour organized by the Professional Squash Association (PSA) and Olympic inclusion secured for the Los Angeles 2028 Games.

Rules

A rally begins with a serve from the service box, striking the front wall between the service and out lines so the ball lands in the opposite back quarter; play then alternates, with each player returning the ball to the front wall above the tin and below the out line, either on the volley or after one bounce on the floor. Modern scoring is point-a-rally to 11 (win by two), with games best of five, and let, stroke and no-let decisions enforcing fair access to the ball "+E+" a central rule because both players share the same court. The ball is small, low-bouncing and extremely quick off the wall, so tactics revolve around controlling the T in the centre of the court: driving the ball deep into the corners, cutting it short with drops and lobs, wrong-footing the opponent, and winning the physical battle of long, punishing rallies.

History

Squash descends from the older game of rackets, played in London's schools and prisons, and was born at Harrow School in the 1830s, where boys found that a punctured rackets ball squashed on impact and produced a far more varied game on a smaller court. The first four-walled squash courts were built at Harrow and other English schools, and the game spread through the British Empire, with the first North American courts appearing at St. Paul's School in New Hampshire in 1884; national associations formed in the early twentieth century (the United States Squash Racquets Association in 1904, Britain's governing body in 1928) and standardized the court, ball and rules. The sport's amateur world and national championships matured through the mid-twentieth century, and the Professional Squash Association, founded in the 1970s, and its women's counterpart built today's global World Tour with the sport's greatest champions. Decades of campaigning for Olympic recognition finally succeeded when the International Olympic Committee voted in 2023 to add squash to the programme for Los Angeles 2028.
