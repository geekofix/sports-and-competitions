Bare-knuckle Boxing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bare-knuckle boxing is boxing without gloves: fighters contest with wrapped or bare fists under rules derived from the historical London Prize Ring tradition, in sanctioned rounds in a ring or an open-air circle. After more than a century of prohibition (the gloved Marquess of Queensberry rules became boxing's standard from the 1890s), the discipline re-emerged in the 2010s as a legally sanctioned professional sport in leading promotions.

Modern bare-knuckle features smaller gloves (hand wraps only), longer visible damage dynamics and its own world championships across weight divisions.

Rules

Modern promotions such as BKFC in the United States and BKB in Britain box in a circular or square ring over rounds (typically five of two minutes for non-title bouts, with championship fights longer), allowing punches only — no elbows, knees, kicks or grappling — with fighters wearing hand wraps but no gloves. Knockdowns and cuts are common, so ringside physicians and referees enforce conservative stoppages, and scoring follows the ten-point must system via judges.

History

Bare-knuckle prizefighting was boxing's original form: Broughton's Rules (1743) and the London Prize Ring Rules (1838,1853) governed the era of championship fights by Tom Johnson, Jem Belcher and John L. Sullivan's bare-fist title bouts that ended only with knockouts or police intervention. The Queensberry Rules (1867, with gloves) displaced bare-knuckle from legitimacy by the 1890s. The sport's revival came with Wyoming sanctioning professional bare-knuckle in 2018 and the growth of BKFC and BKB events drawing crossover stars from boxing and MMA, major television interest and their own world champions.
