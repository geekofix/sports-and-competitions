Bare-knuckle Boxing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bare-knuckle boxing is boxing without gloves, fought in a "squared circle" — a small ring with two chalk scratch lines about 3 ft apart where the fighters start each round, called to "toe the line" and "knuckle up." The modern professional scene is led by the Bare Knuckle Fighting Championship (BKFC), founded in Philadelphia in 2018 and in its ninth year in 2026, which broadcasts on DAZN under a three-year deal signed in October 2024. At KnuckleMania VI on February 7, 2026 in Philadelphia, former UFC heavyweight champion Andrei Arlovski stopped Ben Rothwell in round 3 to become the new BKFC Heavyweight World Champion; as of late September 2026 the BKFC champions included Arlovski (heavyweight, over 230 lbs after the July 2026 introduction of the new Ironweight division), Lorenzo Hunt (light heavyweight and ironweight), Esteban Rodriguez (cruiserweight), Jack Cullen (middleweight, crowned at BKFC 94 in Manchester on September 26, 2026), Kai Stewart (featherweight), Jamel Herring (bantamweight), John Dodson (flyweight), Ben Bonner (lightweight), Dustin Pague (welterweight, interim) and, among the women, Jessica Borga (featherweight), Christine Ferea (flyweight) and Britain Hart (strawweight, the only fighter to hold that title since 2022, defending it at BKFC Liberty Brawl in July 2026) (Bare Knuckle Fighting Championship [BKFC], n.d.-a; BKFC, n.d.-b) (Bare Knuckle Fighting Championship, n.d.-a) (Bare Knuckle Fighting Championship, n.d.-b).

BKFC UK has staged 17 events by September 2026, including its Scotland debut in November 2026; other promotions include BKB in the UK (Coventry) and Mahatch in Canada. Wyoming legalized bare-knuckle boxing in 2018 as the first US state (BKFC, n.d.-a).

Rules

Under BKFC rules, professional bouts are five rounds of two minutes each (title fights), with one-minute rests; amateur and women's bouts are three rounds of two minutes. Fighters may strike only with a closed fist above the waist; no elbows, knees, kicks, throws, grappling or holding; wrapping/bandaging of hands is limited to a small gauze pad held by tape. The referee enforces the scratch lines and the commands "toe the line" and "knuckle up" at the start of each round (BKFC, n.d.-b).

A bout ends by knockout, technical knockout, doctor's stoppage, decision of the judges (scorecards), or disqualification for fouls; draws are possible. Weight classes run from strawweight (women) up through flyweight, bantamweight, featherweight, lightweight, welterweight, middleweight, cruiserweight, light heavyweight and heavyweight, with the new ironweight division above heavyweight (over 230 lbs) added in July 2018's successor rules — introduced at BKFC in July 2026 (BKFC, n.d.-a). Men's and women's divisions use identical rules; women's bouts are shorter (three two-minute rounds). Medical requirements include pre- and post-fight checks, HIV/hepatitis testing and, for women, pregnancy tests (BKFC, n.d.-b).

History

Bare-knuckle boxing is the original form of the sport: the Marquess of Queensberry Rules of 1867 introduced gloves, three-minute rounds and a 10-second count, but before that the London Prize Ring Rules (1838) governed bare-knuckle fighting, and earlier still the Broughton Rules (1743). Champions of the bare-knuckle era include Jem Belcher, Tom Spring and John L. Sullivan — the last bare-knuckle heavyweight champion of the world, who won the title in 1882 and later toured with a gloved exhibition bout against Jake Kilrain in 1889, the famous 2-hour-16-minute fight in Mississippi (International Bare Knuckle Boxing Hall of Fame [IBHOF], n.d.).

Figure: Famous Fights Past and Present. (1901). The international fight between Tom King, of England, and John C. Heenan, of America, 1863 [Illustration]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain (PD-1923). https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Championship_fight_with_Tom_King.png

Tom King, who held the English title 1851–1853, fought the American John C. Heenan in 1863 in a famous international championship fight. After the Queensberry era, bare-knuckle fighting survived underground and in traveling shows until the modern revival began in the 2010s with BKFC (2018) and others; the International Bare Knuckle Boxing Hall of Fame (IBHOF) preserves the history of the bare-knuckle era (IBHOF, n.d.). On the women's side, modern bare-knuckle boxing has had a women's division from the start, and Britain Hart has been the strawweight champion since 2022.
