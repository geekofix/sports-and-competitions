Speedball 7-Man

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Speedball 7-man is the seven-a-side tournament format of competitive paintball, the classic style of the National Professional Paintball League (NPPL). Seven players per team take the airball field in a capture-the-flag game in which aggressive movement, synchronized shooting and strict timing decide who hangs the flag and wins the point.

Rules

The ground rules match speedball: two teams start from opposite stations on a mirrored field of inflatable bunkers, a player is eliminated when a paintball breaks on them or their equipment, and games run against the clock. In the seven-man configuration teams could roster up to nineteen players and substitute freely between games, a depth that rewarded specialist runners and shooters alike. The NPPL format awarded points within each game for staying alive to the end, for eliminating opponents, for pulling the opponent's flag and finally for hanging it at the team's own station, so a single match could produce rich intermediate scoring; league rulebooks also capped rates of fire and prescribed allowed firing modes to keep competition even and safe.

History

Seven-man grew directly out of the professionalization of tournament paintball in the 1990s: the NPPL built its national circuit on the larger roster format, which gave teams room for role specialists and made each game a long, tactical struggle. The format reached broadcast television in 2006, when the NPPL US Paintball Championship (the 2005 Commander's Cup in Miami) aired on ESPN2, the most prominent mainstream exposure the sport had yet received. As professional paintball consolidated around the five-player point-by-point formats of the 2000s and 2010s, the NPPL folded and 7-man faded from the top professional level, but the style lives on in regional and classic events, where many veterans consider its long capture-the-flag games the purest test of team paintball.
