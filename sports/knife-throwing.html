Knife Throwing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Knife throwing is both a circus and vaudeville art and a competitive sport in which participants throw knives at a target and are scored by where the blade sticks (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The modern commercial sport is governed by the World Knife Throwing League (WKTL), founded in 2021 as the sister organization of the World Axe Throwing League (WATL, est. 2017) by throwing-venue owners in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-b). WKTL sanctions official rules, tournaments and affiliated venues across multiple countries, and its disciplines are no-spin, rotational and distance throwing (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a). A standard match is ten knives thrown from a fault line at a 4 × 6 ft target backboard, and the thrower with the highest total wins — ties go to sudden death (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a). The 5th World Knife Throwing Championship (WKTC V) was held in 2026 in Tulsa, Oklahoma, together with the World Axe and Knife Throwing Championship, and the 2026 US Open took place 18–20 September 2026 at The Axe Trap in Winter Park, Florida; championship events are broadcast on ESPN (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate divisions at WKTL venues and at the World Knife Throwing Championship under identical rules (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the official WKTL rules, competition knives must be at least 13.5 inches (34 cm) long, with a total guard width of at most 4 inches and a maximum weight of 1.65 lb (0.75 kg); only knives produced by WKTL or Toro are legal at WKTL events, and throwing an illegal knife is a code-of-conduct violation (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a). Targets are 4 × 6 ft wooden backboards with the standard scoring rings, framed by 4 ft header and footer boards displaying "#WKTL" or "#WATL"; additional marks to distract the thrower are not permitted (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a). A match consists of ten knives per thrower from behind the fault line; each sticking knife scores according to the ring it hits, the highest ring scoring most, the highest total after ten knives wins the match, and a tie is broken by sudden-death throws (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Disciplines are no-spin (blade first with no rotation), rotational (one or more full rotations before sticking) and distance (greatest distance from the target), each with its own scoring (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a). Throwers with disabilities may use a wheelchair or cane, and league play is organized in seasons with regional, national and world ranking points (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate divisions under identical rules (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-b). Rule source: World Knife Throwing League, https://worldknifethrowingleague.com/knife-throwing-rules/ (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-a).

History

Knife throwing has roots in circus and vaudeville performance of the 19th and 20th centuries, when throwers such as David "The Yak" Adamovich made it a staple act (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The European competitive tradition organized itself around clubs such as EuroThrowers, whose October 2005 contest in Pullman City, Germany, is documented with arbiter-scored targets (www.knifethrowing.info via Wikimedia Commons, 2005). The modern commercial league era began with the World Axe Throwing League (WATL), established in 2017 as the governing body for axe throwing; venue owners then created the World Knife Throwing League in 2021 to bring knife throwing under the same international standard, with sanctioned venues across the US, Canada and the UK (World Knife Throwing League, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). World championships have been held annually — the 4th in Appleton, Wisconsin in 2025 and the 5th (WKTC V) in Tulsa, Oklahoma in 2026, broadcast on ESPN (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
