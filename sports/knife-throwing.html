Knife Throwing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Knife throwing is a precision sport in which competitors throw knives and axes at a target so that the blade sticks, scored by rings on the target face. It is practiced recreationally at throwing venues worldwide and competitively under bodies such as the World Knife Throwing League (WKTL) and the International Knife Throwers Hall of Fame (IKTHOF), which organizes world championships in no-spin, spin and multi-distance events. Competitions also include axe throwing and "duel" formats where two throwers face each other.

Rules

In standard competition, throwers make a set number of throws (often 10 per game in league formats, or 21 per distance in walk-back events) from marked fault lines at several distances, typically starting around 3 m (10 ft) and moving farther. Each knife must stick in the target; scoring uses concentric rings worth 5 (center) down to 1 point on the outer ring, and only the first stick of each throw counts. No-spin events require throwing from the handle with no rotation; spin events require at least a half or full rotation; axes must stick blade-first. In elimination events, a thrower who fails to stick enough knives at a distance is out. Safety rules require throwing only at designated targets, behind the fault line, with no one downrange.

History

Throwing knives and axes is an ancient martial and hunting skill, and competitive knife throwing has existed in Europe for centuries, with formal world championships organized since the early 2000s — the IKTHOF has held world championships in Herrischried, Germany, and Newark, England, since the 2000s. The sport gained mainstream attention through televised competitions and ESPN coverage in the 2000s-2010s, and today the WKTL sanctions leagues and venues across several countries, culminating in an annual World Knife Throwing Championship. Trick and entertainment throwing — knives thrown at boards, spinning targets or around assistants — is performed by professional stage entertainers, who popularized the spectacle safely using blunt props and protective equipment.
