Field Archery

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Field archery is archery shot at targets set out over natural terrain: instead of a single boss on a flat range, the archer walks a course of 24 targets of different sizes, distances and angles, shooting three arrows at each, with the distance known (marked round) or unknown (unmarked round) (World Archery, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport is governed by World Archery (WA, 156 member federations, headquartered in Lausanne, founded as FITA in 1931), which runs the biennial World Archery Field Championships — the 2026 edition was held in Yankton, South Dakota, in September 2026 — together with the World Archery 3D Championships, in which three-dimensional animal targets are shot at unmarked distances (World Archery, n.d.-a). The separate International Field Archery Association (IFAA) runs its own World Field Championships with 28-target field, hunter and animal rounds (IFAA, n.d.). See Archery and Target Archery on this site for the target disciplines.

At the 2026 World Field Archery Championships in Yankton, the leading women included Ella Gibson of Great Britain, who won the women's compound world title by defeating the American Paige Pearce in the final, and the Italian Roberta Di Francesco, the defending recurve champion and top qualifier; on the men's side the German Florian Unruh won the recurve title ahead of Brady Ellison of the United States on countback (World Archery, n.d.-a).

Rules

A World Archery field course consists of 24 targets — six faces in each of the four sizes (80, 60, 40 and 20 cm), arranged so that distances and angles vary; each archer shoots three arrows per target against a time limit of three minutes per end, and the highest ring scores 10 down to 1, with the inner-X worth 10 and used as a tie-breaker (World Archery, n.d.-b). The shooting position is marked by a colored peg — red, blue or yellow — set according to the division (bow class, gender and age): red-peg divisions shoot from the shortest distances, yellow-peg divisions from the longest, so a compound archer and a longbow archer on the same butt shoot from different pegs (World Archery, n.d.-b). Rounds are the 24-target marked round (distances known), the 24-target unmarked round (distances unknown, estimated by the archer) and the 24-target mixed round; major championships combine an unmarked round on day one with a marked round on day two, and the combined total decides the title (World Archery, n.d.-a; World Archery, n.d.-b). In 3D competition, life-size foam animal targets are set at unmarked distances and scored by kill zones (inner and outer kill, vitals), with two arrows per target at World Archery 3D events; the World Archery 3D World Championships have been held since 2003 (2024 edition at Mokrice Castle, Slovenia) (World Archery, n.d.-b). The IFAA field round uses 28 targets (two rounds of 14) in field, hunter and animal disciplines, scored 5–4–3 on concentric rings, shot from fixed pegs by class (IFAA, n.d.). Equipment is the archer's own bow — recurve, compound, barebow or longbow — with no sights in the traditional classes at field events and no mechanical release restrictions beyond the division rules; men and women compete in separate divisions with different peg assignments, and age classes (under 18, under 21, senior, veteran) are offered at championships (World Archery, n.d.-b). Fouls such as shooting before the signal or touching an arrow before it is scored cost points; safety rules require all archers to shoot from the peg line (World Archery, n.d.-b).

History

Hunting with the bow is one of the oldest shooting skills, and field archery as an organized sport grew out of bowhunting clubs in the mid-20th century: the National Field Archery Association was founded in the United States in 1956, and the International Field Archery Association (IFAA) followed, holding the first international field championships in the late 1960s (IFAA, n.d.). The international federation FITA (now World Archery) introduced its own field rounds and has staged World Field Archery Championships since the late 1960s, biennially in recent decades (World Archery, n.d.-a). The legendary American bowhunter and flight shooter Howard Hill (1899–1975) — who could hit a playing card at 50 yards and appeared in dozens of Hollywood films — made the bow a household image in the 1940s–50s and inspired generations of field archers (Wikipedia, n.d.). In Britain the National Field Archery Society (NFAS), founded in 1973, governs twelve traditional bow styles including longbow and barebow (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women have competed in field archery at world level since the early IFAA and FITA championships, and the women's compound and recurve titles are now among the most closely contested events on the course (World Archery, n.d.-a).
