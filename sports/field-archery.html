Field Archery

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Field archery is the discipline of archery shot at unmarked distances over natural terrain, with targets set at unknown ranges on hills, through woods and across varied ground, rather than on a flat range. It is governed by World Archery (formerly FITA), which runs the World Field Archery Championships (biennial) alongside the World Target Championships; in the US it is organized by the National Field Archery Association (NFAA).

Rules

At World Archery field championships, archers shoot two rounds over two days: a "field round" of 28 targets (14 on each day) at unmarked distances (red/blue/black pegs depending on class and bow type, from 5 m to 60 m), shot from a flat shooting line, one arrow per target (or three in some rounds), with a set time limit per end (usually 4 minutes for 3 arrows or 2 minutes for 1). Scoring uses the target face's five zones (10-6 points) plus a "X" inner ring in some rounds. Classes include compound, recurve, barebow and longbow, with separate men's, women's, junior and para divisions. Archers walk a marked course between targets; no range finders or electronic devices are allowed. The combined two-day score decides the ranking.

History

Field archery developed in the United States in the 1930s-50s, when bowhunters wanted realistic practice at unknown distances; the National Field Archery Association was founded in 1936 in the US, and the first NFAA national championship was held in 1938. The International Field Archery Association was founded in 1951 and merged into FITA (now World Archery) in 1971; the first World Field Archery Championships were held in 1969 (York, England). The sport spread to Europe in the 1970s, with strong participation in the UK, Sweden, Denmark and Italy. Field archery remains popular among bowhunters and traditional archers, with 3D archery (life-size animal targets) as a related discipline.
