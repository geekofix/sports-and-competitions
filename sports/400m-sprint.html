400m Sprint

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 400 metres is one full lap of the track — the longest sprint, run from staggered lane starts in separate lanes. It is governed by World Athletics and is one of the glamour events of every championship (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 43.03 s for men, set by Wayde van Niekerk of South Africa at the Rio 2016 Olympics from lane 8, and 47.60 s for women, set by Marita Koch of East Germany in 1985 — still the marks in 2026 (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the end of the 2026 season the women's 400 m was led by Femke Bol of the Netherlands (pictured) and Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone of the USA, while the men's event featured Rai Benjamin and Matthew Hudson-Smith among the leaders (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the sprint family — 100m Sprint, 200m Sprint and 4x400m Relay — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The 400 m is run entirely in lanes from a crouch start in blocks, each athlete covering exactly one lap. Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

The 400 m has been on the Olympic programme since Athens 1896 and is the event made famous by the film Chariots of Fire: Eric Liddell of Great Britain won gold at Paris 1924 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Michael Johnson of the USA set the 200 m and 400 m world records of 19.32 s and 43.18 s in 1999 and won the 200/400 m double at Atlanta 1996; Wayde van Niekerk broke Johnson's 400 m record with 43.03 in Rio 2016 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). On the women's side, Marie-José Pérec of France (pictured) won the 400 m at Atlanta 1996 in 48.25, and Marita Koch's 47.60 from 1985 remains the oldest standing women's sprint record (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
