Xare

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Xare (Basque for "net") is a discipline of Basque pelota played only in the trinquet, the enclosed four-walled court inherited from the jeu de paume, in which players use a wooden ring strung with a net — the xare or sare — to throw the ball against the front wall rather than strike it (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport is governed by the International Federation of Basque Pelota (FIPV), founded on 19 May 1929 in Buenos Aires with its seat in Pamplona, which oversees four playing modalities and fourteen disciplines across frontons of 30, 36 and 54 m and the trinquet (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.). One gender difference is explicitly documented in the federation's competition regulations: at the world championships only frontenis and rubber pelota in the trinquet are open to women, while xare remains a men-only discipline — women players instead compete in the other trinquet and fronton modalities, and ball speeds can exceed 200 km/h (Wikipedia, n.d.). At the end of this section, the current leading athletes are the reigning men's world champions in xare and the reigning women's world champions in the open trinquet events (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.). Xare is the netted-racket discipline of the Basque Pelota family, played in the trinquet inherited from jeu de paume — alongside Balle Pelote, Frontenis, Jai Alai, Valencian Pilota; the full list is under Important Links.

Rules

Xare is played in a trinquet about 28.5 m long, whose left wall and sloping roof (the "tambour") are inherited from the jeu de paume, with a glass front wall against which the ball is thrown (Wikipedia, n.d.). Instead of striking the ball with hand, glove or chistera, the player catches it with the netted ring and throws it back — quick, precise movements replace power, and play is continuous until the ball is dead (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.). Matches consist of games to a set target score, with points lost on aces into the net, balls hitting the roof and faults such as the ball bouncing twice; the serve alternates and players rotate positions according to the trinquet's conventions (Wikipedia, n.d.). Protective gear is minimal compared with other pelota modalities, and the men-only status of xare at world championships is a formal classification difference, not an equipment difference (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.).

History

Basque pelota consolidated in the 19th century in the Basque Country and spread to Argentina, Uruguay and the United States, where it was an Olympic demonstration sport at Paris 1924 and Barcelona 1992 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Federation of Basque Pelota was founded on 19 May 1929 in Buenos Aires by delegates from the Basque Country, Argentina, Uruguay and France, with its headquarters in Pamplona (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.). The Basque Pelota World Championships have been held since 1952 and World Cup editions ran from 1995 to 2021, with xare among the trinquet disciplines alongside frontenis and rubber pelota (Wikipedia, n.d.). The wooden ring of the xare is one of the most distinctive pieces of sporting equipment in the pelota family, and the discipline remains a living part of Basque sporting heritage.
