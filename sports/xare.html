Xare

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Xare is a Basque pelota variant (related to joko garbi) played with a curved basket (xare) strapped to the hand, in which players strike a small ball against a front wall at extremely high speed — one of the fastest ball sports in the world, with shots travelling at the highest speeds in the pelota family, played singles on a fronton court.

The name comes from the flat, basket-like bat used, derived from traditional uses of palm and wood, and the sport is contested in Basque country leagues and international pelota competitions, governed alongside pelota varieties by the Basque and international pelota federations.

Rules

Xare is played in a trinquet or fronton court with a front wall (and sometimes lateral walls in play), and points are scored when the opponent fails to return the ball legally to the fronton as in other frontenis-style games: the server strikes the ball with the basket, and the rally continues with players alternating until one side errs — returning out of turn, failing to reach the wall, or pitching the ball out. Baskets must be regulation size, and matches are scored to a set total (for example 12 or 14 points) with serves by rank between both sides in amateur variants; professional matches chiefly use singles, doubles existing at club level.

History

The xare tradition belongs to the Basque pelota family's long heritage in the Pyrenees, where hand-ball games against churchyard and village walls evolved from the seventeenth century into distinct specialities using the hand, leather gloves, wooden bats, baskets and paddle-like instruments. Specialities played with the xare basket emerged in the nineteenth century in the Basque Country and neighbouring regions of Spain, valued for explosive speed and lightning rallies, and the sport crossed the Atlantic through Basque communities to Argentina in particular, where pelota competitions including xare flourished. Pelota was an Olympic demonstration sport at Barcelona 1992, and international federations promote championship play across the family.
