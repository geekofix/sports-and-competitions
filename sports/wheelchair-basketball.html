Wheelchair Basketball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wheelchair basketball is the Paralympic team sport played from specially designed sports wheelchairs: five players per side pass, dribble and shoot a standard basketball on a standard 28 × 15 m court, with four 10-minute quarters and a 24-second shot clock (International Wheelchair Basketball Federation, n.d.). Every player is classified from 1.0 to 4.5 points according to functional ability — lower points mean greater impairment — and the combined points of the five players on court may never exceed 14.0 (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). The game grew out of rehabilitation programmes for paralysed World War II veterans — first played around 1945–46 at U.S. Veterans Administration hospitals and developed at Stoke Mandeville Hospital under Sir Ludwig Guttmann — and debuted at the Rome 1960 Paralympics for men, with the women's tournament starting at Tel Aviv 1968 (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed by the International Wheelchair Basketball Federation (IWBF), founded in 1973, which runs world championships (men since 1975, women since 1990) (International Wheelchair Basketball Federation, n.d.). At the Paris 2024 Paralympics at Bercy Arena, 29 August to 8 September 2024, the women's final saw the Netherlands defend their title with a 63–49 win over the United States — Bo Kramer scoring 23 points and Mariska Beijer 22 — the first women's team to win back-to-back Paralympic gold since 2008, while the U.S. men beat Great Britain in their final (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.).

Rules

Play follows FIBA basketball rules adapted to the chair: after at most two consecutive pushes on the wheels the player must dribble, pass or shoot; lifting the front casters off the floor while holding the ball is a travelling violation; and deliberately tipping or raising the chair is penalised — technical fouls for intentional tipping, unsportsmanlike fouls for dangerous play (International Wheelchair Basketball Federation, n.d.). The 14.0-point team rule shapes line-ups: a 1.0-point player can be balanced by four 4.5-point players, and substitutions are unlimited at stoppages. Games are four quarters of 10 minutes with a 24-second shot clock and 5-minute overtime periods as needed; ball and court are the standard FIBA size (Wikipedia, n.d.). Classification is medical-functional: points from 1.0 (minimal trunk control) to 4.5 (minimal impairment) are assigned by classifiers and may be appealed; men's and women's tournaments are run separately at the Paralympics and world championships (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.).

History

Wheelchair basketball began in the rehabilitation wards for paralysed veterans of World War II: around 1945–46, patients at U.S. Veterans Administration hospitals — including Van Nuys in California — began shooting baskets from their wheelchairs, and the game was organised as a sport at Stoke Mandeville Hospital in England under the neurologist Sir Ludwig Guttmann (1899–1980), who founded the National Spinal Injuries Centre in 1944 and staged the first Stoke Mandeville Games for wheelchair athletes in 1948 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Wheelchair basketball was on the programme of the first Paralympic Games in Rome in 1960 for men, and the women's tournament was added at Tel Aviv in 1968 — making Paris 2024 the fifteenth women's edition (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). The International Wheelchair Basketball Federation (IWBF) was founded in 1973 and has run men's world championships since 1975 and women's since 1990, growing the game to more than 100 member nations (International Wheelchair Basketball Federation, n.d.).
