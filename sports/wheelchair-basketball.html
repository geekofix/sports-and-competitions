Wheelchair Basketball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wheelchair basketball is a Paralympic team sport played by athletes with permanent physical disabilities affecting the lower limbs, in which two teams of five players each attempt to score in the opponent's basket while manoeuvring purpose-built sports wheelchairs — with rules closely mirroring running basketball but adapted for chairs, such as a shortened shot clock and rules against lifting or travelling with the ball while touching the wheels.

It is one of the largest and most visible Paralympic sports, played in dozens of countries with professional leagues in Europe, and internationally governed by the International Wheelchair Basketball Federation.

Rules

Games are played on a standard basketball court with 3.05-metre hoops and most running-basketball rules retained: 24-second shot clock, dribbling requirements (a player may push the wheel twice for every bounce), and fouls called for illegal chair contact. Each player is classified from 1.0 to 4.5 points based on functional ability, and a team's five players on court must not exceed 14.0 classification points in most competitions, ensuring mixed levels of disability play together. Technical rules specific to the chair include bans on touching the floor or rising out of the seat to gain advantage, and tilting the chair is judged under anti-tilt provisions requiring players to keep seat contact.

History

The sport originated in 1945-46 among American war veterans at rehabilitation hospitals, with early games played at the Birmingham VA hospital in California, while at Stoke Mandeville in England Sir Ludwig Guttmann used sport as rehabilitation for injured servicemen, growing into the first organised competitions. It appeared at the first Paralympic Games in Rome in 1960 and has featured at every Paralympics since, becoming the flagship team sport of the movement. The International Wheelchair Basketball Federation was founded in 1973 to govern the game internationally, with world championships since 1975, a women's world championship since 1990, and today professional club leagues such as those in Italy, Spain, Turkey and the IWBF Champions League.
