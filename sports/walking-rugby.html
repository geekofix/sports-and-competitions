Walking Rugby

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Walking Rugby is a low-impact, non-contact form of rugby union in which players walk rather than run and tackles are replaced by a two-handed touch on the shorts — a format designed so that mixed teams of any age, gender and experience can play a competitive passing game together. Matches are usually played by teams of up to seven players on a reduced pitch, and sessions emphasise fun, fitness and social contact as much as results.

By removing running, kicking and physical collisions, the sport opens rugby to ex-players, people returning from injury and complete beginners who want the running and handling skills of the game without full-contact tackles.

Rules

The core rules follow RFU guidance: no running or jogging — walking is judged by one foot remaining in contact with the ground when in motion — and no contact beyond a two-handed touch on the shorts that counts as a tackle. Passing must be backwards or lateral, and offside rules apply. There are no mauls, rucks or lineouts, and scrums are limited to a maximum of three players per team, standing upright and uncontested. A try is scored by walking over the try line with the ball in hand, with no bending or diving required.

History

The format began at Reading RFC in England in late October 2015, inspired by enquiries about a walking version of the game similar in spirit to Walking Football. From there it spread organically through English rugby clubs, and it is now played at more than 200 clubs across the United Kingdom, with county unions such as Surrey Rugby supporting standardised guidance and a national club directory.
