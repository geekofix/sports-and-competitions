Double Mini-Trampoline

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Double mini-trampoline (DMT) is a gymnastics apparatus event in which the athlete performs a series of acrobatic bounces on a small double-bed trampoline with handles, ending in a dismount. It is one of the disciplines at the FIG (International Gymnastics Federation) Trampoline Gymnastics World Championships and is contested individually and in synchronized pairs (men, women and mixed).

Rules

In an individual routine, the gymnast performs ten consecutive bounces on the DMT apparatus without a pause, counted by the judges from the first contact. Each bounce is judged on difficulty, execution, height and horizontal displacement, plus the final landing/dismount. Routines are not choreographed to music (unlike trampoline), and there is no time limit beyond the continuous sequence. In synchronized DMT, two athletes on adjacent apparatuses must perform the same skills at the same time, judged additionally on synchronization. At the World Championships, athletes qualify through preliminaries to finals, where the highest combined score wins. The apparatus consists of a rectangular bed (about 1.2 m x 0.6 m) on a slanted frame with handles, set above a landing mat.

History

The mini-trampoline was developed in the 1960s in the United States (the "Bedsport" style) and spread to Europe, where the double mini-trampoline with handles became standardized. The FIG recognized trampoline gymnastics as an official discipline and has included double mini-trampoline at the World Championships since the 1980s; the event is also part of the World Games. The sport demands explosive leg power, spatial awareness and precision, and leading nations include the United States, Canada, Great Britain, Germany, Portugal and China. Top athletes combine multiple somersaults and twists per bounce, with world-level routines reaching great height off the small bed.
