Kuresh

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kuresh (koresh, köräş, restraint of the Turkic peoples) is the traditional belt wrestling of the Tatars, Kazakhs, Bashkirs and other Central Asian peoples: two wrestlers in a standing bout grip a special belt (or towel thrown over the shoulder) and try to throw the opponent onto his back or shoulders. It is the centerpiece of the Tatar folk festival Sabantuy and a recognized UWW associated style (Kazakh "Qazaq Kuresi" rules since 2014; belt-wrestling world championships since 2002). The International Tatar Belt Wrestling Köräş Association (founded 9 September 2009, Kazan) runs world championships with athletes from 40+ countries. Men and women compete in weight classes. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Kurash, Wrestling, Bökh, Sumo; the related pages are listed under Important Links (International Belt Wrestling Koresh Federation, n.d.).

Rules

Standing bout only: grips on the belt (köräş) or towel, no grips below the belt, no ground wrestling. Win by throwing the opponent so both shoulder blades touch the ground simultaneously, or by forcing him out of the 9 m circle; points for takedowns decide if no fall. UWW Qazaq Kuresi rules (2023) govern international competition: weight classes, three periods, video review; fouls (grabbing below belt, leg grabs in some styles) are penalized. Women's and juniors' divisions exist (United World Wrestling, 2023).

History

Belt wrestling is among the oldest recorded sports — the Epic of Gilgamesh (c. 2000 BC) shows Gilgamesh and Enkidu belt-wrestling, and a 2600 BC bronze statuette from Khafaji shows two wrestlers. Köräş developed among the nomadic Turkic peoples; the first RSFSR championship was held in Kazan in 1960, repeated annually; the International Köräş Association was founded in 2009. It is contested at the World Nomad Games (koresh) and was recognized by UWW (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
