Blind Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Blind football (football 5-a-side) is football adapted for players who are blind or visually impaired: teams of five — four B1-class outfield players plus a sighted goalkeeper — play on a smaller pitch surrounded by boards, using a ball with a rattle or bell. It is governed by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA) and has been a Paralympic sport since Athens 2004.

Rules

Under the IBSA Blind Football Rulebook, the pitch is 40 x 20 m (smaller at youth events) and surrounded by kickboards so the ball stays in play. Each half lasts 20 minutes; there is no offside rule and no throw-ins (the ball is kicked or thrown in from the boards), but corner kicks exist. Outfield players wear eyeshades and are class B1 (totally or almost totally blind); the goalkeeper may be sighted but must not have been FIFA-registered for five years. Players call "voy" (Spanish for "I'm here" / "going") when moving toward the ball. If a match needs a winner, penalties decide — the first Paralympic final in 2004 was settled 3-2 on penalties.

History

Football for the blind began as a playground game in special schools; the first Spanish national championship was in 1986 and the first IBSA World Blind Football Championships in 1998. The sport joined the Paralympic program at Athens 2004. Brazil won every Paralympic gold medal until Paris 2024, where host France won gold. Stars include Ricardinho and Jefinho, two of the most skillful players in the game's history.
