Blind Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Blind football (football 5-a-side) is association football adapted for athletes with visual impairments: outfield players classified B1 wear eyeshades and play with a ball that contains bells, while the sighted goalkeeper defends the goal. It has been a Paralympic sport since Athens 2004 and is governed by the International Blind Sports Federation (International Blind Sports Federation [IBSA], n.d.-a). At the Paris 2024 Paralympics at the Eiffel Tower Stadium, host nation France won gold, Argentina silver and Brazil bronze — ending Brazil's streak of five consecutive Paralympic titles from 2008 to 2020 (IBSA, n.d.-b) (International Blind Sports Federation, n.d.-a) (International Blind Sports Federation, n.d.-b).

Brazil's Ricardinho remains the most decorated player in the sport's history: Paralympic champion in 2008, 2012, 2016 and 2020, bronze in 2024, IBSA world champion in 2010, 2014 and 2018, and named IBSA World Player of the Year in 2006, 2014 and 2018. On the women's side, IBSA runs women's blind football world championships alongside the men's events; the women's game uses the same rules and classification and is not (yet) on the Paralympic programme (IBSA, n.d.-a).

Rules

A team fields four B1 outfield players plus one sighted (or partially sighted) goalkeeper on a 40 m × 20 m pitch enclosed by kickboards, played in two 20-minute halves; there is no offside rule (IBSA, n.d.-a). The ball makes noise so players can track it, and outfield players call "voy" (French for "I'm going") when moving toward the ball to avoid collisions; the goalkeeper may leave the penalty area and is the only player allowed to handle the ball deliberately. Throw-ins are taken by rolling or throwing the ball, and matches are decided by goals; knockout ties go to penalties decided by sighted coaches calling directions. Classifications: B1 (totally blind, eyeshades mandatory) for outfielders; goalkeepers may be sighted or B2/B3. Men's and women's tournaments use identical rules, with the same pitch, ball and match length (IBSA, n.d.-a).

History

Blind football developed in the 1920s–1930s in Europe and Latin America as a rehabilitation and leisure activity for blinded war veterans, and was first included in the Paralympic programme at Athens 2004. Brazil dominated the early Paralympic era, winning gold in 2004 (demonstration), 2008, 2012, 2016 and 2020 with stars such as Ricardinho and goal machine Jardel; China and Argentina were the other powers of the 2010s. France, bronze medalists in 2016, broke Brazil's run at a home Paris 2024 to win their first Paralympic gold in the sport (IBSA, n.d.-b). IBSA world championships (held regularly since 2010) crown world champions outside the Paralympic cycle, and the women's world championship has been held since the 2010s, with teams from Europe, Asia and South America competing (IBSA, n.d.-a).
