200m Sprint

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 200 metres is the sprint around one curve and down the home straight of the 400 m track — half a lap from staggered lane starts. It is part of the Olympic and World Championship programme and is governed by World Athletics (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 19.19 s for men, set by Usain Bolt at the 2009 Berlin World Championships, and 21.34 s for women, set by Florence Griffith-Joyner in 1988 — both unbroken in 2026 (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the end of the 2026 season the women's 200 m was led by Melissa Jefferson-Wooden (pictured) and Julien Alfred, who beat her in the 2026 Diamond League final in Rome, while the men's event was led by Noah Lyles (pictured), the 2025 world champion, Letsile Tebogo of Botswana — the 2024 Olympic champion in 19.46 — and Kishane Thompson (World Athletics, n.d.-b; World Athletics, n.d.-c).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the sprint family — 100m Sprint, 400m Sprint and 4x100m Relay — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The 200 m starts from staggered positions so that every runner covers exactly 200 m in a lane; the race is run entirely in lanes with a crouch start in blocks. Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

The 200 m has been an Olympic event since 1900 for men and since 1948 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Jesse Owens won it at Berlin 1936, and Carl Lewis took the 200 m at Los Angeles 1984 on his way to nine Olympic golds. Michael Johnson of the USA set the 200 m world record of 19.32 at Atlanta 1996 before Bolt lowered it to 19.19 in 2009 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). On the women's side, Florence Griffith-Joyner's 21.34 from Seoul 1988 is one of the most enduring records in sport, and Elaine Thompson-Herah won Olympic gold in Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
