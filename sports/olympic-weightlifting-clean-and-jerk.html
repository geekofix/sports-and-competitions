Olympic Weightlifting Clean and Jerk

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The clean and jerk is one of the two lifts contested in Olympic weightlifting, alongside the snatch: the barbell is lifted from the ground to the shoulders in one continuous movement (the clean) and then driven overhead with a split or squat jerk, all in a single attempt (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-a). Together with the snatch it forms the two-lift total that decides Olympic and world titles. Weightlifting has been on the Olympic program for men since Athens 1896 and for women since Sydney 2000, and the women's competition uses exactly the same two lifts, weight classes and judging as the men's (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Among the current stars of the lift are China's Li Wenwen (women's +87 kg) and Georgia's Lasha Talakhadze (men's +102 kg), both reigning Olympic champions.

Within the strength and lifting family on this site, its closest relatives are Weightlifting, Olympic Weightlifting Snatch, Powerlifting, Powerlifting Squat, Powerlifting Bench Press; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In the clean and jerk the lifter must first pull the barbell from the platform to the shoulders in one motion, without pausing, catching it in a front rack position with the elbows forward; any downward movement of the bar between the pull and the catch, or a touch of the bar on the chest or thighs during the pull, invalidates the lift (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-a). The lifter then recovers to a standing position and, in the jerk, drives the bar from the shoulders to full arm extension overhead — either splitting one leg forward or squatting under the bar — and must hold it motionless with arms and legs extended until the referees' down signal (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-a). Each lifter gets three attempts at the clean and jerk in competition, performed after the snatch attempts, and the heaviest successful lift counts toward the total; ties are broken in favor of the lighter bodyweight lifter (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-b). Competitions are divided into bodyweight categories — ten per gender from 2025, and twelve per gender at Los Angeles 2028 — and fouls (press-outs, touching the bar to the head, losing control) result in a red light from each of the three referees (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Women's and men's events are judged identically, with the same platform, bar and rule set (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-b).

History

The clean and jerk has been part of weightlifting competitions since the late 19th century and appeared at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens 1896, when the two-hand lift was contested in a similar style (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). The press, clean and jerk and snatch were the three Olympic lifts until the press was abolished after the 1972 Munich Games, leaving the two-lift format from the 1973 World Championships and the 1976 Montreal Olympics onward (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The women's two-lift program was introduced at the Sydney 2000 Olympics (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Legends of the lift include the American Norbert Schemansky, Olympic champion 1952, the Soviet superheavyweight Vasily Alekseyev, double Olympic champion (1972, 1976), and China's Lü Xiaojun, who set world records in the 77/81 kg class; Thailand's Tairat Bunsuk, pictured below, won Olympic silver in the men's 69 kg at Rio 2016 in a memorable clean and jerk and took the 2017 world title (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
