Cyr Wheel

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Cyr wheel (also called the German wheel or Cyr wheel) is an acrobatic apparatus: a large hoop that the gymnast rolls, balances and spins in, performing straight-line rolls, spirals and vaults over the bar. It is governed together with the Rhönrad (gym wheel) by the Internationaler Rhönradturnverband (IRV), founded in January 1995 in Basel/Bern, which runs biennial World Championships (the 16th in 2026 in Göttingen, Germany). Scoring combines difficulty (max 10) and execution (max 10) minus deductions.

Rules

Under IRV rules, athletes compete in straight line, spirale (spiral) and vault disciplines, plus the Cyr wheel battle, a head-to-head knockout format. Routines are performed to music and judged on difficulty, execution and artistic impression, with deductions for falls or loss of control. The wheel is about 1.8-2.3 m in diameter and weighs around 40-60 kg, with handles and foot bars. At World Championships, all-around titles are decided across the disciplines.

History

The Rhönrad was invented in 1925 by Otto Feick in Rhön, Germany (patented 8 November 1925) and demonstrated at the 1936 Berlin Olympics. The Cyr wheel was developed in the 1990s by Daniel Cyr in Montreal, Canada; the first competition was in Chicago in 2011 and the first world championships for the Cyr wheel in 2013. The IRV was founded in 1995 to govern both apparatuses, and both were demonstrated at the World Games.
