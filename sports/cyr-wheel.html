Cyr Wheel

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Cyr wheel (roue Cyr) is a circus and gymnastics apparatus consisting of a single large metal ring in which the performer runs, balances and executes acrobatic skills while the wheel rolls across the floor (International Wheel Gymnastics Federation [IRV], n.d.-a). It is named after the Canadian circus artist Daniel Cyr, co-founder of Cirque Éloize, who created the first dedicated act, Excentricus, performed from 1997 to 2002 and awarded silver at the Cirque de Demain festival in 2003 (Cirque Éloize, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitive wheel gymnastics is governed by the International Wheel Gymnastics Federation (IRV), founded in January 1995 in Basel with headquarters in Bern, which has organized Wheel Gymnastics World Championships since the first edition at the 10th World Gymnaestrada-related wheel event in Chicago, 7–14 July 2013 (IRV, n.d.-b). The 16th Wheel Gymnastics World Championships were held 29 June to 5 July 2026 at the Sparkassen-Arena in Göttingen, Germany, with about 150 athletes from 17 nations, Germany taking the most medals (IRV, n.d.-b). The discipline is also staged at the World Games (World Games Association, n.d.). Wheel gymnastics is open to women and men, who compete in the same disciplines — straight line, spiral and vault — under the same code (IRV, n.d.-a) (International Wheel Gymnastics Federation, n.d.-a) (International Wheel Gymnastics Federation, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the IRV Code of Points, the wheel is a single ring whose diameter is 10–15 cm larger than the performer's height; the tube is 38 mm thick, the ring is assembled from 3 to 5 pieces and is PVC-coated, and optional handles may be fitted (IRV, n.d.-a). Competitors perform in three disciplines — straight line (running and balancing skills on a straight course), spiral (the wheel rolled on its rim in curves and circles), and vault (skills performed with the wheel used as a vaulting horse) — plus an all-around title combining them (IRV, n.d.-a). Routines are judged for difficulty, execution and artistic presentation under the Code of Points 2026 edition, with separate senior and junior categories and equal women's and men's competition (IRV, n.d.-a).

History

Daniel Cyr developed the Cyr wheel in Montreal in the 1990s while performing with Cirque Éloize, which he co-founded in 1993; his act Excentricus toured from 1997 to 2002 and won silver at Cirque de Demain in 2003, establishing the wheel as a stage apparatus (Cirque Éloize, n.d.). The first dedicated competition was held in Chicago in October 2011, and the first world championship followed at the 10th Wheel Gymnastics Worlds in Chicago, 7–14 July 2013 (IRV, n.d.-b). The IRV was founded in January 1995 in Basel and today governs the sport from Bern, organizing world championships every two years alongside the sport's presence at the World Games (IRV, n.d.-b; World Games Association, n.d.).
