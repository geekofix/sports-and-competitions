Para Athletics

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para athletics is track and field for athletes with physical, visual or intellectual impairments, governed by World Para Athletics within the International Paralympic Committee and run under World Athletics competition rules adapted per class (World Para Athletics, n.d.-c; World Para Athletics, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-e). Athletes are classified by their impairment: T classes for track (T11–T13 visually impaired, T20 intellectual, T35–T38 cerebral palsy, T40–T47 ambulant, T51–T54 wheelchair) and F classes for field events, with lower numbers meaning greater impairment; every event is offered per class and per gender (World Para Athletics, n.d.-c).

Para athletics has been part of every Paralympic Games since Rome 1960, wheelchair racing debuted at Tokyo 1964 and the marathon joined the programme in 1984; the biennial World Para Athletics Championships were last held in New Delhi in 2025 (12th edition, 186 events, about 2,200 athletes from 104 countries) and Paris 2024 staged the track programme at the Stade de France (World Para Athletics, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Olympics, n.d.).

Leading the women today: Catherine Debrunner of Switzerland (T53) won five gold medals at Paris 2024 — 100 m, 400 m, 800 m, 1500 m, 5000 m plus the T54 marathon in 1:41:50 — swept all five of her events at New Delhi 2025, and set the marathon world record of 1:33:16 at the 2026 Chicago Marathon, holding nine world records and 15 World Marathon Majors wins in 20 starts; Lamiya Valiyeva of Azerbaijan (pictured) won the F13 javelin gold at Paris 2024, and Raoua Tlili of Tunisia is a ten-time world champion in F57 shot put (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Para Athletics, n.d.-a). Among the men, Markus Rehm of Germany is the four-time Paralympic long jump champion (T64, 2012–2024) and Sumit Antil of India the F64 javelin Paralympic champion and world record holder from Paris 2024 (World Para Athletics, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the Para athletics family — Wheelchair Racing, Athletics and Marathon — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Within the Para sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Wheelchair Racing, Para Swimming, Para Table Tennis, Para Badminton, Para Equestrian Dressage; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Competitions follow the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules as adapted by World Para Athletics: track events on a 400 m track with staggered starts, crouch starts up to 400 m, false-start disqualification, photo-finish judging, and the same distances as able-bodied athletics per class (World Para Athletics, n.d.-b).

Classification assigns each athlete a sport class; visually impaired runners in T11–T12 run with a sighted guide tethered to them (both receive medals), wheelchair racers use racing wheelchairs on the track and road, and seated throwers in F31–F38 use throwing chairs; field events, relays (with classification points) and road races (10 km to marathon) complete the programme, and men and women compete separately in every event (World Para Athletics, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

History

The Paralympic movement grew out of the work of Sir Ludwig Guttmann, who organised the first Stoke Mandeville Games for wheelchair athletes in 1948 and brought the competition to Rome in 1960, where it was recognised as the first Paralympic Games; Italy's Antonio Maglio, with INAIL, co-founded the Italian Paralympic movement — both are pictured at Rome 1960 (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

Dominant icons include Australia's Louise Sauvage, who won four track gold medals at Sydney 2000 in T54, and her compatriot Kurt Fearnley, a three-time Paralympic marathon champion; in the swimming crossover, Trischa Zorn won 55 Paralympic medals between 1980 and 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).
