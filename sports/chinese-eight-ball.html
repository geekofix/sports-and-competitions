Chinese Eight-Ball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chinese eight-ball (recently renamed Heyball by the World Pool-Billiard Association, WPA) is a pool variant popular in China and governed internationally as an official WPA discipline. It is played with 15 balls and the black 8 ball on a 9 ft table whose rounded pockets are close to snooker dimensions and use 54 mm balls, which makes potting harder and rewards positional play. It is widely played in Chinese pool halls and has professional circuits, especially in China and the UK.

Rules

Under the WPA Chinese eight-ball rules, players break, then the table is open until a legal pot decides groups: one player takes the solid balls 1-7 and the other the stripes 9-15, and the 8 ball is potted last to win. Standard pool rules apply for fouls (ball in hand after a foul, loss of turn, and potting the 8 early loses the frame), but the pockets are 76-82 mm (about 3 inches) at the mouth and the jaws are tighter than US pool pockets, so banks and pots must be struck cleanly. Matches are usually best of frames to a set number of racks in tournaments.

History

Chinese eight-ball developed in Chinese pool halls in the late 20th century as a harder-pocketed variant of eight-ball pool, and it became hugely popular in China, with professional tournaments and TV coverage from the 2000s. The WPA adopted it as an official discipline alongside American pool, snooker and nine-ball. Top Chinese players such as Li Hewen and Wu Jiaqing have popularized the game internationally, and it is now played in pool halls and clubs across Asia, Europe and North America.
