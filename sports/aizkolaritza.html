Aizkolaritza

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Aizkolaritza is the competitive wood chopping of the Basque Country (herri kirolak, rural sports): the aizkolari stands on a vertical trunk (usually aspen or pine, 1–2 m in diameter, debarked) and hacks it through with an axe in a timed heat; the fastest clean cut through all the trunks of the round wins. It grew out of farm and forestry work and became a spectator sport at village festivals from the late 19th century, with organized championships since the 1980s; women's aizkolaritza is contested at modern events. Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are Harri Jasotzaile, Idi Probak, Trontza; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

Rules

Competitors work on upright logs of fixed species and diameter held in a frame; each blow must be a full axe swing and the trunk must be severed completely. Heats are timed individually, with knockout rounds pairing the fastest. Safety rules require helmet, gloves, steel-toe boots and a cleared spectator zone. Men's and women's divisions exist; the number of trunks and the species are set per championship (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

History

Basque farmers and charcoal burners chopped wood daily for centuries, and contests appear from the 19th century; the legendary axeman Luxia of Deba was photographed on a trunk-cutting bet in 1949. The Herri Kirolak associations codified the sport in the 20th century; today top events in Gipuzkoa and Bizkaia draw large crowds, and women's wood chopping has grown since the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
