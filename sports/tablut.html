Tablut

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tablut is the Swedish tafl variant (from the Fetlar/Hnefatafl family) reconstructed from Carl Linnaeus's 18th-century notes: the king with his 8 defenders faces 16 attackers on a 9x9 board; the king escapes to a corner to win, the attackers capture the king to win. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Hnefatafl, Brandubh, Latrunculi; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Figure: NTNU Vitenskapsmuseet. (2014-11-10). The 12th-century nefatafl board from Trondheim [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nefatafl_fra_Trondheim_(19896084560).jpg

Rules

9x9 board; attackers win by capturing the king (surrounded on four sides, or two if on the throne); defenders win by moving the king to any corner square. The throne and corner squares have special rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Tablut was played in Sweden until the 19th century; Linnaeus documented the rules in his 1732 diary (the Trondheim nefatafl board of the 1100s shows the family). Modern reconstructions follow the Fetlar rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
