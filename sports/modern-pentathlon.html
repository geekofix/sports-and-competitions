Modern Pentathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Modern pentathlon is the multi-discipline sport created for the modern Olympic Games, in which one athlete contests five disciplines — fencing (épée), swimming (200 m freestyle), obstacle course racing, and the combined laser run of running and laser pistol shooting (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). It is governed by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM), founded in 1948, and has been on the Olympic programme since Stockholm 1912; women first competed at the Olympics in Sydney 2000, and the mixed team relay has been contested since Tokyo 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Since 2025 the fifth discipline at senior level is obstacle course racing, which replaced show jumping after a decision of the UIPM Congress on 27 November 2021, confirmed for the Los Angeles 2028 Games (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). At the 2026 World Modern Pentathlon Championships in Guiyang, China (24–30 August 2026), Hungary's Blanka Guzi won the women's individual title with 1,485 points ahead of Egypt's Farida Khalil and Great Britain's Kerenza Bryson, while the Czech Republic's Matej Lukes won the men's title; Egypt's 15-year-old Jana Attia set a new obstacle course world record of 25.17 seconds (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women in the sport are reigning world champion Blanka Guzi of Hungary, two-time world champion Farida Khalil of Egypt, and Tokyo 2020 Olympic champion Michelle Gulyás of Hungary (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the UIPM Competition Rules, each athlete contests five disciplines and collects ranking points in each (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). Fencing is épée with one-touch bouts of one minute in a round-robin format plus a bonus round; swimming is 200 m freestyle against the clock; obstacle course racing is a short, fast course of obstacles — walls, bars, ropes and balance elements — on which Egypt's Jana Attia set the world record of 25.17 s at the 2026 World Championships (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-b). The final discipline, laser run, combines cross-country running with laser pistol shooting at targets — in the 2026 format five 600 m loops interrupted by shooting stages; the athlete with the most points after the first disciplines starts with a time handicap, and the first athlete across the finish line wins. Show jumping, formerly the riding discipline, was replaced by obstacle course racing for senior athletes from 2025 after the UIPM Congress decision of 27 November 2021 (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). Women and men compete in separate individual events at the same venue plus the mixed team relay; age groups from youth to juniors and seniors have their own classifications with adjusted distances and equipment (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Modern pentathlon was created by Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympic Games, as a test of the ideal soldier — riding, fencing, swimming, running and shooting — and debuted at the Stockholm 1912 Olympics, where the American officer George S. Patton finished fifth (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The UIPM was founded in 1948 at Sandhurst, England, and the sport has been at every Olympics since; women joined at Sydney 2000, with Britain's Stephanie Cook the first Olympic women's champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After the riding incident at Tokyo 2020, in which a horse refused the course, the UIPM Congress voted on 27 November 2021 to replace show jumping with obstacle course racing from 2025, a change confirmed for Los Angeles 2028 (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.-a). The laser run, combining running and shooting, has decided the Olympic title since London 2012 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
