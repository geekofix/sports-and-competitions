Lancashire Wrestling

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lancashire wrestling is the historic "catch-as-catch-can" style of Lancashire — no holds barred, any grip allowed above or below, ground wrestling included once down — which became the root of American catch wrestling and professional wrestling's early contests. It was the style of the great northern wrestling booths at fairs and music halls in the 18th–19th centuries; champions like Edwin Bibby toured internationally. Today it survives in heritage demonstrations and as the historical base of catch wrestling (submission) competitions. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Cornish Wrestling, Cumberland & Westmorland Wrestling, Dorset Wrestling, Wrestling; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Scientific Wrestling, n.d.).

Rules

Catch-as-catch-can: any hold is legal (half nelsons, hammerlocks, leg rides, ground pins); the bout starts standing and continues on the ground; win by fall (both shoulders), submission (the opponent cries "uncle"/taps) or referee decision. Modern catch wrestling (FILA/UWW grappling, and pro catch-style bouts) codifies this with time limits, a 9 m circle and activity points — pinfall or submission wins. No strikes; headbutts historically tolerated in the Lancashire style but barred in modern rules (Scientific Wrestling, n.d.).

History

Lancashire wrestling was the dominant professional style in northern England from the 1700s; the traveling-circus era in 19th-century America turned it into catch wrestling as a spectator sport. Edwin Bibby ("the Napoleon of wrestling") was world champion in the 1880s; the 1889 Pollock/Badminton Library manual illustrated the holds. The style directly influenced catch wrestling, shoot wrestling and the early UFC/Vale tudo rules (Scientific Wrestling, n.d.).
