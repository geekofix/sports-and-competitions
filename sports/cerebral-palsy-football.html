Cerebral Palsy Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cerebral palsy (CP) football, also known as 7-a-side football, is football adapted for players with cerebral palsy or other neurological conditions affecting coordination and balance. It is governed worldwide by the International Federation of Cerebral Palsy Football (IFCPF), and matches are played 7 versus 7 on a smaller pitch with adapted rules based on FIFA's Laws of the Game. CP football was a Paralympic sport from 1984 until 2016 and is now contested at IFCPF world championships.

Rules

CP football follows FIFA 7-a-side rules with modifications: teams have seven players including a goalkeeper, the pitch is smaller (about 75 by 56 m), there is no offside, and throw-ins may be taken one-handed. Players are classified by functional ability into classes FT5 to FT8 (ambulatory players); each team must field at least one FT5-classified player and may not field more than a set number of higher-functioning players, so the classification rules shape team selection. Matches are two halves of 30 minutes, and the Laws of the Game apply otherwise, including the offside-free rule and reduced-size goals.

History

Adapted football for players with cerebral palsy developed in Europe in the 1970s, with the first international matches in the late 1970s, and CP football joined the Paralympic program at Arnhem 1984. The IFCPF (originally CP-ISRA, the International Sports and Recreation Association for people with cerebral palsy) became the world governing body. After classification challenges, CP football was not on the Paralympic program from Tokyo 2020 onward, but the IFCPF World Championships continue the international competitive scene.
