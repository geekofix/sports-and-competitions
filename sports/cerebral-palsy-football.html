Cerebral Palsy Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cerebral palsy (CP) football — also called Football 7-a-side — is the Paralympic team sport for athletes with cerebral palsy or other acquired neurological impairments such as stroke or traumatic brain injury. It is governed worldwide by the International Federation of Cerebral Palsy Football (IFCPF), which has run the sport since January 2015 under the Para Football umbrella and took over from CPISRA, the Cerebral Palsy International Sports and Recreation Association founded in 1978 (IFCPF, n.d.; International Paralympic Committee [IPC], n.d.). The top events in 2026 were the Men's World Championships in Italy in August 2026, where hosts Italy defeated CP Soccer USA 3–1 in the final, and the IFCPF World Cup in Fayetteville, Arkansas, USA, from 14 to 24 October 2026, where England beat the USA 2–1; the maiden Women's World Cup was held in 2024, and Denmark will host the 2027 European Championships (IFCPF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Football 7-a-side was on the Paralympic program from Arnhem 1984 through Rio 2016 and was dropped for Tokyo 2020, so today the IFCPF World Cup is the pinnacle event (IPC, n.d.). Both a men's and a women's game exist at the top level with the same Laws of the Game and dedicated development provisions for women's CP football in the IFCPF rule modifications (IFCPF, 2025); the current leading women's national teams contest the Women's World Cup first held in 2024, while the 2026 men's world champion is England of the Fayetteville World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rules

CP football is played under the IFAB Laws of the Game with the IFCPF's published Modifications to the IFAB Laws of the Game (2025 edition, updated 2024/2025), which are the official rule source and are linked from the IFCPF (IFCPF, 2025; IFCPF, n.d.). The modifications are: teams of seven players (including the goalkeeper); a smaller pitch — maximum 75 m × 56 m, minimum 55 m × 40 m; smaller goals of 5 m × 2 m; the offside rule is eliminated; one-handed throw-ins are permitted; and matches consist of two 30-minute halves with a 15-minute half-time (IFCPF, 2025; Wikipedia, n.d.). Athletes are classified into sport classes C5 to C8 — C5 being the class with the greatest impact of impairment, C8 the least — under the classification code implemented in January 2018, which replaced the old FT5–FT8 labels; two team-composition rules apply at all times: at least one C5- or C6-classified player must be on the field of play, and no more than one C8-classified player may be on the field at the same time (IFCPF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's competitions use the same Laws and pitch dimensions, with additional development modifications for women's CP football set out in the same IFCPF document; youth and development tournaments apply age-group squad limits. The IFCPF is the world governing body and names the rule source on its tournaments page (ifcpf.com/tournaments/rules) (IFCPF, n.d.).

History

The first international CP football competition was held in Scotland in 1978 at the Cerebral Palsy International Games, and CPISRA — founded in 1978 — organized the first CPISRA World Championships in Denmark in 1982, followed by world championships roughly every four years (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Football 7-a-side made its Paralympic debut at Arnhem 1984 and remained on the program through Rio 2016, with national teams such as Portugal, silver medallists at Barcelona 1992, among the leading sides; it was dropped from the program for Tokyo 2020 (IPC, n.d.). In January 2015 the IFCPF succeeded CPISRA as the world governing body and rebranded the world championship as the IFCPF World Cup from 2019 onward (IFCPF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The women's game developed later — international women's CP football fixtures date from the 1990s onward — and reached a milestone with the maiden Women's World Cup in 2024, with Denmark hosting the 2027 European Championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). No single iconic player dominates the sport's history; its defining figures are the administrators and classifiers who built the C5–C8 classification system in 2018, which remains the model for ambulant football classes across Para sport.
