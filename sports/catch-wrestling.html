Catch Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Catch wrestling (catch-as-catch-can) is an English wrestling style with fewer restrictions than other styles: it allows holds on the legs, allows joint locks (submissions), and requires no prescribed starting grip. Its defining rule is "wrestlers shall take hold how and where they please". It is the ancestor of freestyle wrestling, American folkstyle wrestling, Brazilian luta livre and modern submission grappling, and it has seen a competitive revival since the 1980s through events such as the Snake Pit Catch Wrestling World Championships and the ACWA US Open.

Rules

In its classic form, a catch bout is won by a pinfall (both shoulders down) or by forcing a submission, using holds called hooks (joint locks) and stretches (punishing holds). The Olympic version, contested from St. Louis 1904 through 1936 (not 1912), banned dangerous submission holds, and the international bodies' modified ruleset eventually became freestyle wrestling. Modern competitive catch, as run by the Snake Pit in Wigan, England, and similar promotions, restores submissions alongside pins under referee supervision, with weight classes and time limits.

History

Catch-as-catch-can grew out of Lancashire wrestling, the most permissive English folk style, and became the dominant professional style in Britain and the US in the 19th century, where the Amateur Athletic Union adopted it in the 1880s. It entered the Olympic Games in 1904. As amateur federations standardized rules and removed submissions, the style declined as a distinct competitive form, but its techniques survived in professional wrestling and in Japanese puroresu (shoot and strong style). The revival since the 1980s has re-established catch wrestling championships in the UK and the US.
