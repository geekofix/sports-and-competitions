Vovinam

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Vovinam is the Vietnamese martial art founded in 1938 by Nguyen Loc: a comprehensive system combining hard techniques (punches, kicks, throws, sweeps and grappling) with soft principles (yielding, redirection and breathing cultivation), practiced through forms (quyen), self-defence sequences, traditional weapons and combat applications. Vietnam's most internationally spread martial art, it is organized on every continent with continental federations and world championships under the Vovinam Viet Vo Dao federation family.

Rules

Training follows the art's curriculum of hard and soft: striking, kicking and blocking fundamentals, the trademark jumping and scissor kicks, throws and joint locks, self-defence sets against weapon attacks, weaponry (staff, sword, machete and more) and forms practice for grading and competition. Competition formats include sanda-style fighting bouts (points scored for controlled strikes, throws and takedowns under protective rules including bans on excessive contact) and judged forms divisions, while belt progression runs from beginner belts through the blue belt of the art's unified tradition (distinguishing leadership levels with yellow-and-red belt grades), and the federation's rulebook governs weight categories, timing, legal techniques and safety equipment for championships.

History

Nguyen Loc founded Vovinam (Vo Viet Nam, the martial art of Vietnam) in Hanoi in 1938 seeking a martial method that fused Vietnam's traditional wrestling and folk fighting arts with modern training and a philosophy of national character; the art survived colonial persecution and war-era hardship, spread through the Vietnamese diaspora after 1975, and underwent a global organizing wave from the 1990s onward (world congresses, continental federations and world championships staged across Europe, Asia and the Americas). Today Vovinam is taught worldwide as both cultural heritage and competition sport, with its largest federation communities in Vietnam, France, Germany and across Southeast Asia and a recognized role in Vietnam's national identity.
