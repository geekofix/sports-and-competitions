Sambo

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sambo is a martial art and combat sport developed in the Soviet Union in the early 20th century — its name is the Russian acronym for "self-defence without weapons" (SAMozashchita Bez Oruzhiya) — from a synthesis of judo and jujutsu with the many wrestling styles of the former Russian Empire and further abroad. Practised in sport (jacket-wrestling) and combat (with strikes) forms, it is governed internationally by the International SAMBO Federation (FIAS), which held the first world championships in 1977 in Oviedo, Spain; sambo is recognised by the International Olympic Committee and is a style of amateur wrestling included by United World Wrestling alongside the Olympic wrestling styles.

Rules

Sport sambo is a jacket wrestling format: competitors wear a kurtka (a short jacket tied with a belt, similar to a judogi top) with matching shorts and wrestling shoes, and no visible ranks or belts — national federations award competitive titles such as "Master of Sport" instead. Victory may be achieved by total victory (an immediate win), by technical points for throws, hold-downs and submissions, or by point advantage, and two forms of competition are recognised by FIAS: sport sambo, which permits leg locks and armlocks but bans chokes and strangles, and combat sambo, which adds punches, kicks, knees and elbows to the grappling. Competition is organised in weight divisions — nine senior categories per gender, from 52 kg up to over 100 kg for men and 48 kg to over 80 kg for women — contested for five-minute bouts on mats, docked with points, warning penalties and disqualifications for illegal holds (such as twisting necklocks) set out in the FIAS rules; the ranking of results at a tournament decides medal placement by direct comparison of results.

History

Sambo's development began in the early 1920s in the Soviet Red Army's hand-to-hand combat programme, built by two pioneers working largely independently: Viktor Spiridonov, a World War I veteran whose pre-war exposure to regional wrestling styles shaped a "softer", less strength-dependent approach, and Vasili Oshchepkov, who had studied judo at the Kodokan under Jigoro Kano and taught the art to Red Army men; Oshchepkov died in the Great Purge in 1937 after being accused of espionage for Japan, and their styles cross-pollinated into one system. Anatoly Kharlampiev, a student of Oshchepkov, is credited with systematising the art and lobbying for its official recognition, which arrived on 16 November 1938 with USSR sports committee order no. 633 — the recognised birth date of sambo; his book "Sambo Wrestling" (1940) became the sport's main technical reference. FILA (now UWW) adopted sambo as an international wrestling style, sambo was demonstrated alongside the 1980 Moscow Games, and FIAS was founded on 13 June 1984, meeting for the first time with delegates from 56 nations; after a 1990s organisational split between "West" and "East" FIAS was healed, the federation unified and now counts over a hundred member nations. Sambo has surged in global MMA and combat-sport visibility through champions such as Fedor Emelianenko and Khabib Nurmagomedov, FIAS stages world and continental championships annually, and the sport has appeared at multi-sport events such as the World Games while being repeatedly nominated for future Olympic inclusion.
