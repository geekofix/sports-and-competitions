Track Cycling Elimination Race

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The elimination race (also known as the miss-and-out) is the track cycling bunch race in which the last rider across the line each sprint lap is eliminated, one by one, until a handful of survivors sprint for the win. A pure pressure test of positioning and nerve, it forms part of the omnium programme at world championships and Olympic Games and appears on national track programmes worldwide.

Rules

Riders start as a field and race laps with an elimination bell every second lap: at each sprint, the last rider's front wheel to cross the line is eliminated (must leave the track promptly), and racing proceeds until the field has been whittled to the final two or three, who sprint for the win. Positioning is everything: riders fight for the front in the closing metres of each sprint lap, and tactics range from steady front-running to last-corner dives; rules penalize improper track conduct (holding wheels, forcing elimination) and govern how eliminated riders exit. The race is judged under UCI track conduct rules, with commissaires stationed at the line on every sprint lap.

History

The elimination format grew out of novelty miss-and-out races run on American and European tracks in the twentieth century, where promoters loved the built-in drama of shrinking fields; it was standardized into the international omnium programme and stands today as one of track cycling's most spectator-friendly races. Its modern championship status came with the omnium's adoption (the discipline was fully integrated when the omnium format was redesigned in the 2010s), and the elimination remains a staple of six-day racing where crowd favourites ride their survival instincts to the podium.
