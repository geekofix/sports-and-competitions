Road Cycling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Road cycling is the discipline of bicycle racing held on paved roads, and it is the most popular professional form of cycle sport in terms of competitors, events and spectators. It is governed worldwide by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), founded in 1900 in Paris, whose calendar spans one-day classics, stage races and the annual UCI Road World Championships. Its most famous event is the Tour de France, a three-week stage race that can draw more than 500,000 roadside spectators in a single day.

Rules

The two fundamental formats are mass-start road races, where all riders start together and the first across the finish line wins, and individual time trials, where riders start separately and race alone against the clock against wind, tactics and equipment limits. Professional racing is organised in trade teams under the UCI's team-code system (WorldTeams, ProTeams and Continental teams), with the UCI WorldTour the top men's series of roughly 35 to 40 races per season, including the three Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — and the five Monument classics. At the UCI Road World Championships, riders compete for national teams rather than trade teams in road races and time trials, and since 2019 a mixed team relay; the winner of the elite road race wears the rainbow jersey for a full year. UCI technical regulations standardise the bicycle itself (type, minimum weight and dimensional limits), riders must wear helmets and follow team support and drafting rules, and anti-doping controls are enforced under the UCI's regulations with the Biological Passport introduced in 2008.

History

Road racing grew out of the early organised bicycle races of the late 19th century — the first recognised world championships were track events held by the International Cycling Association in Chicago in 1893 — before the founding national federations created the Union Cycliste Internationale in Paris in April 1900 to unify the sport's rules. Stage racing began with the Paris daily newspaper L'Auto, whose editor Henri Desgrange created the Tour de France in 1903, quickly followed by the Giro d'Italia (1909) and later the Vuelta a España (1935), while one-day classics such as Paris–Roubaix (first run 1896) and the Tour of Flanders (1913) predate them. The first professional world road championship was held at the Nürburgring in Germany in 1927 and won by Alfredo Binda of Italy; a women's road race was added to the world championships in Reims in 1958. Olympic road racing was on the programme of the first modern Games in 1896 and permanently since 1936, with women's Olympic road racing added in 1984. The modern era saw the UCI ProTour, rebranded as the UCI WorldTour in 2011, replace the one-day World Cup series in 2005; the biological passport followed in 2008 and, since 2022, equal world championship prize money for men and women and the reborn Tour de France Femmes.
