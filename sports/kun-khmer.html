Kun Khmer

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kun Khmer (Khmer: kbach kun pradal khmer), also called Pradal Serey ("free boxing"), is Cambodia's national kickboxing art: stand-up striking with punches, kicks, knees and elbows, plus an active clinch with sweeps and trips, over five three-minute rounds in a ring of about six metres, judged on a 10-point must system by three judges. It was codified by the French colonial administration in the early 20th century from the ancient Khmer close-combat system Bokator, depicted in the Angkor-era bas-reliefs of Bayon, Ta Prohm and Angkor Wat. Kun Khmer debuted at the SEA Games 2023 in Phnom Penh (19 medals, Cambodia topped the table) and was contested at the first Asian Kun Khmer Championship in Vietnam in September 2026; the World Kun Khmer Championship in February 2026 drew 125 athletes from 20 countries. Men and women compete in 18 weight classes from 47.6 kg up. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Muay Thai, Lethwei, Kickboxing, Boxing; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Combat Culture, 2026).

Rules

Professional bouts: five rounds of three minutes with 90-second to two-minute breaks (international friendship matches may be three rounds). Legal: punches, all elbow strikes, knees, kicks, clinch with sweeps and short trips; victory by KO, TKO or on points, draw if equal. Fighters wear leather gloves and nylon shorts; the pre-fight Wai Kru ritual is accompanied by the traditional Pinpeat orchestra. Weight classes run from mini-flyweight (47.6 kg) to super heavyweight (95.3+ kg); weigh-ins and medical checks apply. Elbows are the signature weapon and produce more victories than any other strike (Khmer Boxing Phnom Penh, 2026).

History

Angkor-period bas-reliefs (9th–12th centuries) show clinch fighting, knee and elbow strikes, evidence that Cambodians practiced the art for centuries before the French codified Pradal Serey in the early 1900s; early bouts in dirt pits with rope hand-wraps were so lethal (deaths in Battambang in the 1920s–30s) that gloves were introduced. The Khmer Boxing Federation governs the sport today; Cambodia insists on the name Kun Khmer, which caused a diplomatic dispute at the 2023 SEA Games when IFMA boycotted over the term. Icons include SEA Games champions such as Tit Sorphorn and Prom Samnang (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
