Braemar Gathering

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Braemar Gathering is the annual one-day Highland games held on the first Saturday of September at the Princess Royal and Duke of Fife Memorial Park in Braemar, Aberdeenshire, in the Cairngorms. It has been run in its present form since 1832 and is the world's most famous Highland gathering: the programme combines the heavy athletics events (tossing the caber, stone put, Scottish hammer throw, weight over the bar), foot races over the Braemar brae — the world's oldest regularly organised foot races, held since 1832 — Highland dancing (including the Walker's Shortbread League presentation) and pipe-band displays. Since Queen Victoria's visit in 1848 the reigning monarch has attended regularly and is styled Chieftain of the Gathering (King Charles III today). Women compete in the foot races and dominate the Highland dancing; the heavy events are traditionally men's, with women's heavy events now held at many gatherings. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are Cowal Games, World Nomad Games, World Indigenous Games, Cheese Rolling; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Braemar Gathering, n.d.-a).

Rules

The Gathering is a festival of many sports, not a single sport; each discipline follows its own rule set — the heavy events under the rules of the Scottish Highland Games Association (SHGA), e.g. the caber must be tossed to land upright on its end for a valid throw and is measured for distance and straightness; the foot races (e.g. the main race over the brae) are timed mass-start or handicap races; Highland dancing is judged against set dances (Highland Fling, Sword Dance, Seann Triubhas, Reel of Tulloch) on a points system. Competitors register in advance in age and gender classes; kilts/traditional dress are worn (Braemar Gathering, n.d.-a).

History

Gatherings at Braemar are traced to King Malcolm Canmore (Malcolm III) in the 11th century, who reportedly held clan games there to test strength and pick soldiers. The Braemar Wright Society was founded in July 1815 (registered as a Friendly Society in 1817); on 20 July 1832 its General Meeting at Braemar Castle voted £5 of honorary funds as prizes for competitions on 23 August 1832 — stone throwing, hammer throwing and tossing the bar (caber) — with £4 19s 4d distributed. Queen Victoria attended on 14 September 1848 at Invercauld and the Society received Royal status in 1866; the Braemar Royal Highland Charity (2002) now organises the event and runs the Duke of Rothesay Highland Games Pavilion and Visitor Centre (opened March 2019) (Braemar Gathering, n.d.-c).
