Longboarding

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Longboarding is skateboarding on longer, wider boards (typically 33–60 inches), practiced as cruising, downhill racing, freeride sliding, dancing, slalom and long-distance pushing (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The competitive core is downhill: timed runs on closed mountain roads at speeds above 90 mph (145 km/h), where riders adopt an aerodynamic tuck or stand up and control speed with standup slides (International Gravity Sports Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Gravity Sports Association (IGSA) sanctions the world championship tour, and Guinness World Records tracks the official speed categories (Guinness World Records, n.d.). The current landmark is Spanish racer Diego Poncelet, who set a new Guinness world record for the fastest downhill skateboard speed — 94.34 mph (151.83 km/h) — at Les Éboulements, Quebec, in September 2026; among the fastest women are Emily Pross of the United States and Cassandra Duchesne of Canada, and Brianne Davies of Canada was the women's downhill champion in the late 2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Seismic Skate Systems, n.d.).

Rules

Downhill races are timed runs on a closed course of 1–4 km with a mandatory start gate; depending on the event format the winner is decided by the single best time or the aggregate of two to four runs (International Gravity Sports Association, n.d.). Riders may start in a standing or tucked position, and in sanctioned pro events full-face helmets, leather or Kevlar suits and slide gloves are compulsory (International Gravity Sports Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment is regulated by class: wheels are urethane of roughly 65–80 mm diameter, trucks are precision downhill trucks, and boards for racing are stiff, low and directional (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Slalom is timed through a course of cones with penalties of 0.1–0.3 s per cone hit or missed, and long-distance push races (LDP) are mass-start marathons where the first rider across the line wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's downhill use the same courses, safety rules and timing; women race in dedicated women's categories at IGSA events (International Gravity Sports Association, n.d.). Guinness World Records recognizes separate speed categories — standing downhill skateboard, street luge and electric skateboard — each verified by radar on a closed road (Guinness World Records, n.d.).

History

Longboarding grew out of 1960s California surf culture: surfers mounted roller-skate wheels under wooden planks ("sidewalk surfing"), and the invention of urethane wheels in the early 1970s — pioneered by Cadillac Wheels — transformed grip and made downhill riding and sliding possible (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first organized downhill races were held on paved roads in the United States in the mid-1970s, and the sport spread to Europe, South America and Australia; Maryhill Festival of Speed in Washington State, first held in 2006, became the iconic closed-road venue (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The IGSA formalized the world tour in the 2000s. Among the icons is Mischo Erban of Canada, who set the standing downhill record of 130.08 km/h (80.83 mph) on a Colorado road on 30 September 2010, verified by Guinness at 80.74 mph on 18 June 2012 at Les Éboulements, and who won the IGSA world title in 2009 and 2011; Sweden's Erik Lundberg broke Erban's mark on 31 May 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). On the women's side, Brianne Davies won the IGSA women's downhill titles from 2008 to 2010, paving the way for today's fastest women such as Emily Pross and Cassandra Duchesne (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
