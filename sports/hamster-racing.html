Hamster Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hamster racing is a novelty sport in which hamsters run in hamster balls or miniature racing vehicles ("dragsters") down a straight track — the standard professional course is 9 m (30 ft) — and the fastest hamster to cross the finish line wins. It gained brief professional form in the UK in 2001 when bookmaker Blue Square organized a betting-backed series, and remains a popular amateur attraction at pet stores and rodent shows (e.g., Petco's Hamster Ball Derby in the US).

Rules

In professional-style racing, hamsters run in individual lanes of a straight 9 m track inside hamster balls or fitted dragsters; the first to cross the finish line wins, with photo-finish timing (the reported world record for the 9 m setup is 38 seconds, set in 2001). Race hamsters are divided into classes by species (dwarf vs Syrian/Golden hamsters) and coat type (short-hair, long-hair, patterned, novice). Amateur events use shorter sprint tracks (e.g., Petco's 8 ft track) with heats and finals; owners may not push the hamster, only release it at the start. Welfare rules at shows cover clean balls, rest between races and veterinary checks.

History

The concept of racing hamsters professionally began in 2001 in the UK, when the foot-and-mouth outbreak cancelled horse racing and online bookmaker Blue Square promoted a hamster racing series with qualifying rounds and a webcast final. MTV launched the "HamTrak '06" world tour of professional hamster teams in 2006. Amateur hamster racing had already been popular at pet shows and church outings since the late 20th century; Petco ran biannual Hamster Ball Derbies in its US stores from 2007, with over 14,000 hamsters racing in 2006-07 events. Today it survives mainly as a family-friendly novelty at pet events.
