Teqvoly

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Teqvoly is the volleyball-style team sport played on and around the curved teq table: two teams of three (or a paired doubles format) hit a football over the table's net with hands-free techniques borrowed from teqball and volleyball, with serves, blocks and touches governed by FITEQ rules. Part of the teq family of sports that grew out of Hungary's curved-table invention, it brings organized team play to the rebound-table world.

Rules

The game is played three-a-side (with doubles variants) over the curved table rigged with a net in the middle: play begins with a serve kicked or headed from behind the baseline, teams may take up to three touches (and a block) to return the ball over the net, and no player may touch the ball with hands or arms at any time, in keeping with the teq family's football-only rule. Points are scored as in volleyball when the opponents fail to return legally (missed table, double bounce on one side, net or touching violations), sets are played to set point totals with two-set match formats, and FITEQ rules govern rotation, service order, the no-hands rule and court marking for both recreational and ranked competition.

History

Teqvoly grew from the teq table's broader family after the parent sport's 2014 invention and 2016 launch: players worldwide discovered that the curved surface plus a net made a natural volleyball-style team game, and the international federation standardized the format as its team counterpart to teqball's singles-and-doubles play. It spread through the same clubs, schools and national federations that adopted the teq table, and the sport is now contested internationally at FITEQ events alongside teqball, with a growing base in Europe, the Americas and Asia and development programs at the grassroots level.
