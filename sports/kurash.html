Kurash

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kurash is the traditional jacket-wrestling style of Uzbekistan and Central Asia, in which two competitors grip each other's quilted jacket (yakhtak or chapan) and soft belt and try to throw the opponent cleanly onto his back (International Kurash Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Kurash Association (IKA), founded on 6 September 1998 in Tashkent, which now counts more than 80 member countries and organizes more than 50 international events a year, including two World Championships in 2026 (International Kurash Association, n.d.-a). Kurash debuted at the Asian Games in 2018 in Jakarta and is again on the programme of the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, as well as a core sport of the World Nomad Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete in their own weight classes at every World Championship — the Uzbek women's team, led by 2018 Asian Games medalists such as Kumush Yuldashova, is among the strongest in the world — and men's and women's tournaments run in parallel at all IKA events (International Kurash Association, n.d.-a).

Rules

A kurash bout is fought standing only: ground wrestling, strikes, joint locks and grips below the belt are forbidden, and the contest ends immediately with a halal — a full, clean throw in which the opponent lands flat on his back with both shoulder blades touching the mat (International Kurash Association, n.d.-b). Two yambosh scores (partial throws) equal one halal, and a chala is a partial score; if no halal is scored, the winner is the athlete with the higher score after the bout time (four minutes for senior men, shorter for women and juniors) or by golden score (International Kurash Association, n.d.-b). Competitors wear the yakhtak jacket with a soft belt, in green for one athlete and blue for the other, on a circular contest area of 7–9 m diameter (International Kurash Association, n.d.-b). Weight classes follow the judo model — men −66, −73, −81, −90, −100 and +100 kg; women −52, −57, −63, −70, −78 and +78 kg — with junior (U21) and youth (U18) divisions, and women and men compete in separate draws under identical rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rule source: International Kurash Association, Rules of Kurash, https://kush-ika.org/ (International Kurash Association, n.d.-b). See also judo and wrestling on this site.

History

Kurash descends from the folk wrestling styles of the Turkic peoples of Central Asia (kuresh), which were practiced across the steppe for centuries and formalized as a sport in Uzbekistan in the 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Kurash Association was founded on 6 September 1998 in Tashkent to govern the sport internationally, and kurash was included in the Asian Games programme from 2018 (Jakarta), with Uzbekistan's athletes — among them Olympic-level wrestlers such as Yakhyo Imamov and Ruslan Buriev, and on the women's side Kumush Yuldashova — becoming the reference point of the sport (International Kurash Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The IKA's 2026 calendar lists more than 50 events worldwide, two World Championships and continued inclusion at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya and the World Nomad Games (International Kurash Association, n.d.-a).
