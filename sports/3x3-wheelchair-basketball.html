3x3 Wheelchair Basketball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

3x3 wheelchair basketball is the 3x3 format adapted for wheelchair athletes. It is governed by the International Wheelchair Basketball Federation (IWBF) on the basis of the FIBA 3x3 rules and made its Paralympic debut at Tokyo 2020.

Rules

The game is played on a half court with one basket, a 12-second shot clock, and games to 21 points or 10 minutes — the FIBA 3x3 framework with IWBF modifications. Players are classified from 1.0 to 4.5 points according to their functional ability, and the combined classification points of the players on court are capped. Dribbling rules are adapted to wheel pushes: a limited number of pushes on the wheels counts as a dribble, and lifting both hands off the wheels and the ball is travelling. Contact and foul rules are wheelchair-specific. Variants: some national leagues play 4x4 formats or shorter games, and the classification-point cap differs between 3x3 and 5x5 wheelchair basketball.

History

Wheelchair basketball was invented around 1945–1948 at Stoke Mandeville Hospital by Sir Ludwig Guttmann, who used the game in the rehabilitation of patients with spinal cord injuries. It has been part of every Paralympic Games since Rome 1960, and the 3x3 format was added to the Paralympic program for Tokyo 2020. The US men's team and the Australian women's "Gliders" are among the most successful national programs.
