Sporthocking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sporthocking, also called extreme sitting, is a street sport invented in Germany in which athletes perform tricks "+E+" spins, kicks, jumps, slides and juggling combinations "+E+" with a specially designed stool (the sporthocker) and finish each move by landing seated on it. Mixing skateboarding-style trick culture with juggling and freestyle movement, the sport is one of the distinctive action-sports inventions of the 2000s; the term has also been used loosely for freestyle kicking games in other countries, but the stool-based German discipline is the sport's core form.

Rules

The apparatus is central: the sporthocker is a lightweight, brightly coloured, rubber-lined stool balanced for spinning, flipping and sliding, sturdy enough to withstand kicks and jump landings but light enough to be hurled vertically in mid-trick. Routines are built from a growing library of named tricks "+E+" the stool is kicked or flipped into the air, spun on the ground, slid along rails or pavements, and juggled or exchanged between athletes "+E+" and each trick ends with the athlete sitting down on the stool, which marks the move as complete and shows the stool stayed under control throughout. Freestyle jams and exhibition contests judge on style, difficulty and creativity, with the landing-seated rule acting as the sport's signature constraint; demo teams from the founding scene perform trick shows, teach workshops and release video parts that keep expanding the trick catalogue.

History

Sporthocking was invented in Germany around 2007 by the Landschutz brothers, who developed the stool with friends and took the new sport to juggling conventions, skate scenes and street festivals. Official demo teams performed across Europe, turning heads by mixing skateboard-style aerials with synchronized stool acrobatics, and the sport gained industry attention at trade shows where the sporthocker was showcased as a new kind of action-sports product, earning recognition at the ISPO Awards. Online videos of trick stalls and freestyle battles spread the discipline worldwide in the 2010s, and while it remains a niche pursuit, sporthocking keeps a devoted community of practitioners, demo teams and hobby athletes who keep inventing new ways to finish a trick by sitting down.
