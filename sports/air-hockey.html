Air Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Air Hockey is a tabletop sport for two players (or two teams in doubles) on a low-friction table: a lightweight plastic puck floats on a cushion of air blown through tiny holes, and players use hand-held mallets (pushers) to shoot it into the opponent's goal. Competitive play is organized in the US by the United States Air Hockey Association (USAA, founded 1978), which runs the USAA World Championships, and by leagues such as the American Air Hockey League (AAHL); in Europe, the World Table Hockey Association (WTHA) organizes air hockey competitions.

Rules

Under the USAA rules, play starts with the puck placed on the center line and both players malleting "5-4-3-2-1-go". A goal is scored when the puck fully enters the opponent's goal slot; the first player to reach 7 goals wins the game (win by 2 in some formats). A puck that stops on the center line is replayed. Touching the puck with anything but the mallet, crossing the center line with the mallet, or distracting the opponent is a foul and costs a goal or a timeout. Variants: table brands (e.g. Dynamo vs. Brunswick-style goals), best-of formats and doubles rules differ between USAA, AHPA and WTHA events.

History

Air hockey was invented in the late 1950s by engineers at Brunswick and popularized in the 1970s when the first coin-operated and home tables appeared. The USAA was founded in 1978 and has run USAA World Championships since then; the AHPA was founded in 2015, the AAHL in 2024, and the WTHA has organized European air hockey competition since 2008.
