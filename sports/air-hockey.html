Air Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Air hockey is a tabletop sport for two players — or two teams of two in doubles — on a low-friction table: a lightweight plastic puck floats on a cushion of air blown through thousands of tiny holes, and each player uses a hand-held mallet (pusher) to shoot the puck into the opponent's goal slot (United States Air Hockey Association [USAA], n.d.-a). Competitive play in the United States is organized by the USAA (founded 1978), which runs the USAA World Championships, together with the Air Hockey Players Association (AHPA, 2015), the North Carolina Air Hockey Player Association (NCAHP, 2016) and the American Air Hockey League (AAHL, 2024); in Europe, the World Table Hockey Association (WTHA, based in Prague) has organized air hockey competitions since 2008 (USAA, n.d.-a; American Air Hockey League [AAHL], n.d.). Women compete in dedicated divisions at USAA and AAHL events, and the world championships have crowned women's singles and doubles champions since the 1980s (United States Air Hockey Association, n.d.-a) (United States Air Hockey Association, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the USAA rules, only 8-foot tables are approved for sanctioned tournament play, and games are played to 7 goals. Each point begins with a face-off: the puck is placed on the center line and both players count "5-4-3-2-1-go" while striking with their mallets. A goal is scored when the puck fully enters the opponent's goal slot; a puck that comes to rest on the center line is replayed. The puck may be struck only with the mallet — touching it with a hand or arm is a foul — and a player may not cross the center line with the mallet or reach over it; "goal tending" (blocking the goal mouth with the mallet) is also a foul. Fouls cost a goal or a timeout depending on the rule set, and doubles is played 2 vs 2 on the same table (USAA, n.d.-a; AAHL, n.d.). Tournament tables measure about 244 cm (8 ft) in length, and approved pucks are Lexan polycarbonate discs, usually yellow, red or green, with a strip of tape on the top face.

History

The technical basis of air hockey dates to the 1940s, when physics demonstrations used "air tracks" to show frictionless motion — John Stull at Alfred University built one of the first. Engineers at Brunswick developed the first air tables in the late 1950s, the first commercial table appeared around 1969, and Sega's MotoPolo (1968) plus coin-operated home tables popularized the game in the 1970s; the game as a standardized contest is generally dated to 1972. The USAA was founded in 1978 and has run USAA World Championships ever since; the AHPA followed in 2015, the NCAHP in 2016, the AAHL in 2024, and the WTHA has organized European air hockey competition from Prague since 2008, after the European Air Hockey Association was active in 2006–2007 (USAA, n.d.-b; AAHL, n.d.).
