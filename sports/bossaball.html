Bossaball

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bossaball is a hybrid team sport invented in 2004 by Belgian entrepreneur Filip Eyckmans in Spain: it combines elements of football, volleyball, gymnastics and trampolining on an inflatable court with a trampoline on each side of a net, a "bossawall" behind each goal, and a samba-style referee plus a DJ. The first World Cup was held in Turkey in 2009, European Cups have run since 2010, and the top nations are Brazil, Belgium and the Netherlands (Bossaball, n.d.-a).

The women's game is fully established: women's and mixed teams compete at the Bossaball World Cup and European championships, with the same rules as the men's game (Bossaball, n.d.-b).

Rules

A team fields three to five players on an inflatable court about 18 m × 9 m (doubles: smaller) with a 3 m-high net and a trampoline on each side; play starts with a serve thrown by hand or foot onto the opponent's trampoline (Bossaball, n.d.-a). A team may contact the ball up to eight times, with a maximum of two contacts with head or feet and one with hands — after the serve, the ball must touch the own side or trampoline at least once before crossing. Scoring: a ball landing on the opponent's trampoline is worth three points, on the opponent's court one point; sets are played to 25 points with a two-point lead, the deciding set to 15, best of three sets. Fouls include more than eight contacts, double hits and touching the net. Men's and women's tournaments use identical rules, court and scoring; youth and amateur events use lower nets and smaller courts (Bossaball, n.d.-b).

History

Filip Eyckmans, a former tennis player, conceived bossaball in 2004 in Spain and patented the inflatable court; the first official World Cup followed in 2009 in Antalya, Turkey, and the sport was showcased at festivals worldwide. The 2013 World Cup on Bonaire (Dutch Caribbean) featured the Netherlands' attacking play and marked the sport's Caribbean expansion; European Cups since 2010 have built a club scene in Belgium, the Netherlands, Spain and Germany. Brazil, with its beach-sport culture, has been the dominant nation, ahead of Belgium and the Netherlands (Bossaball, n.d.-b).
