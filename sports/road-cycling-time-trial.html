Road Cycling Time Trial

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The road cycling time trial (individual time trial, ITT) is the race against the clock in which each rider starts alone at staggered intervals and the fastest time over the course wins; it is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale under Part 2 of its regulations and is a core event of the UCI Road World Championships and the Olympic Games (UCI, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 99th UCI Road World Championships in Montreal, Canada, 20–27 September 2026 — 52 years after the 1974 edition in the same city — the elite time trials were both contested over 39.2 km, the longest course ever used for the elite women (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Among the 2026 world champions, the women lead: Marlen Reusser of Switzerland (pictured) defended her women's time trial title, 40 seconds ahead of Britain's Zoe Bäckstedt, with Germany's Franziska Koch third; on the men's side Remco Evenepoel of Belgium won a fourth consecutive title, 57 seconds ahead of Italy's Filippo Ganna, with Paul Seixas of France third (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). At the same championships the women's road race went to Demi Vollering of the Netherlands, who attacked from more than 30 km out (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

This is part of the road cycling disciplines — Road Cycling, Gravel Cycling, Cyclo-cross — within the cycling family (parent Cycling); the full list is under Important Links.

Rules

Under UCI road regulations, time trial riders start at intervals of one to two minutes in reverse order of ranking; drafting or slipstreaming behind another rider is forbidden — a rider being overtaken must move aside and may not follow the overtaking rider (UCI, n.d.-c). Equipment is regulated: the bicycle must weigh at least 6.8 kg, aerodynamic handlebar extensions (triathlon/aero bars) and disc or full-disc wheels are permitted, and a helmet is mandatory (UCI, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Distances at the World Championships and Olympics are around 40–50 km for elite men and women (39.2 km at Montreal 2026 for both), shorter for under-23 and junior categories; timing is electronic to the thousandth of a second, and the start order and course are fixed by the organizer; men and women race on the same or parallel courses in separate classifications, and team time trials also exist at the World Championships (UCI, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

History

Races against the clock date back to the late 19th century; the hour record — one hour as far as possible on a track — became cycling's most famous time trial, and road time trials have been part of the UCI Road World Championships since the first edition in 1927 and of the Olympic programme since 1912 for men and 1996 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

Spain's Miguel Induráin, pictured during the 1993 Tour de France, is the discipline's great icon — five consecutive Tour de France titles (1991–1995), two world time trial titles (1995, 1996), Olympic gold in Atlanta 1996 and the hour record in 1994; on the women's side, France's Jeannie Longo (b. 1958) won 13 world titles including the 1996 Olympic time trial and set the hour record in 1996 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
