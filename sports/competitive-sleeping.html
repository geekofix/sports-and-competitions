Competitive Sleeping

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive sleeping is a novelty contest in which participants try to sleep the longest or the deepest within a set time, monitored by sleep-tracking devices. The best-known event was the "Matt Race by Koala" in Tokyo on 5 October 2026, where contestants slept for 90 minutes and the winner combining speed to fall asleep and depth of sleep took a 2 million yen prize; around 47,000 people applied. Spain hosts the "Dormilón" napping championship in Madrid, and China has held "lie-flat" endurance contests in Baotou, where the 2024 winner stayed flat for 33 hours 35 minutes.

Rules

In timed napping contests, competitors lie on mattresses for a fixed period (for example 90 minutes) while wearables record sleep stages; winners are ranked by a combination of sleep onset time, total sleep time and deep-sleep share, with penalties for getting up. Endurance variants (lying flat continuously) are ranked by total duration, with medical supervision. Rules are set by each organizer; unlike sport, there is no international federation, and the Guinness record for the longest time voluntarily without sleep (Randy Gardner, 264.4 hours in 1964) is the inverse benchmark studied for health risks.

History

Napping competitions have been held in Spain since the 2010s as part of the Madrid siesta culture, and Chinese "lie-flat" endurance contests became popular in the 2010s-2020s. The Koala "Matt Race" in Tokyo in 2026 brought the concept to mainstream international media with its large prize and huge applicant numbers. Sleep scientists note that such contests are entertainment, and that chronic sleep deprivation (the opposite extreme, studied in the Randy Gardner case) is dangerous.
