Wine Slinging

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wine slinging is a comic festival contest in which participants throw, hurl or spit wine the greatest distance — a quirk of European harvest and wine-festival culture alongside grape stomping and similar grape-stomping festival games — usually played with a mouthful of wine spat from a marked line, in the manner of the better-known cherry pit and watermelon seed spitting contests.

Like other harvest novelty events, it is entirely local in organisation: rules, distances and prize lists are set by each festival committee, and results are a matter of village pride rather than a governed international sport.

Rules

Contests take place on a marked course: participants take wine assigned by the organisers, step behind the spit-line, and project it as far as possible along a measured lane, with the distance from the line to where the wine lands deciding the winner in a set number of attempts. Other formats replace spitting with slinging a measured quantity of wine from a container or cup in an underarm throw at a target — closest or longest wins, with penalties for stepping over the line. Local rules fix the number of tries, age limits and wine allowances, since no international federation governs the pastime.

History

The contest is one of numerous harvest-time games European wine villages have celebrated for rural festivals over the centuries, tied to the vineyard cycle and September vintage time. Modern wine festivals in Germany, Italy, France and Spain include comic events between tastings, from grape stomping to wine slinging and barrel rolling, with village festivals maintaining their own records. Memorable comic contests of this type have been run at events from the Bad Durkheim wine festival in Germany's Palatinate to local fairs from Austria to Australia, kept alive by festival committees.
