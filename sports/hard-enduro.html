Hard Enduro

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hard enduro is the extreme form of enduro motorcycle racing on very difficult natural terrain — steep rocky hills, log obstacles, waterfalls and riverbeds — where finishing the course at all is the challenge. The premier event is the FIM Hard Enduro World Championship (formerly the World Enduro SuperEnduro series), alongside classics such as the Red Bull Romaniacs, Erzbergrodeo (Austria), Red Bull Romaniacs and the Roof of Africa.

Rules

At FIM Hard Enduro World Championship events, riders race against the clock over extreme terrain with a mix of timed special tests and untimed extreme sections; the rider with the lowest total time wins, with time penalties for missing checkpoints. Famous races use a mass-start "hurdles"/extreme format: at the Erzbergrodeo (since 1995 near Eisenerz, Austria), only a small fraction of the ~500 starters finish within the four-hour time limit, and the winner is the first to complete the course (Carl's Diner/Red Bull Romaniacs use checkpoint time-attack scoring). Riders must wear full protective gear (helmet, goggles, chest protector, boots); bikes must pass technical inspection, and assistance outside designated zones is forbidden. Finishing rates are often under 1-5% at the hardest events.

History

Hard enduro grew out of classic enduro racing in the 1990s as riders tackled ever more extreme terrain; the Erzbergrodeo was founded in 1995 by Karl Katoch and became the sport's most famous event, and the Red Bull Romaniacs (founded in Romania in 2003) and the Roof of Africa in Lesotho followed in the mid-2000s. The FIM (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme) took over the World Enduro SuperEnduro series in 2016 and rebranded it as the FIM Hard Enduro World Championship, with events at Romaniacs, Erzbergrodeo, Roof of Africa and others counting toward the title. Stars include Manuel Lettenbichler (Austria, multiple champion), Jonny Walker, Graham Jarvis and Alfredo Gómez.
