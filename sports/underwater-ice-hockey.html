Underwater Ice Hockey

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Underwater ice hockey is the upside-down form of hockey played on the underside of a frozen lake: breath-hold divers in wetsuits — with no oxygen tanks — chase a buoyant puck that floats up against the ice ceiling and flick it with short sticks into goals hung from the ice (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport was invented in 2005 by the Austrian freediver Christian Redl, after his under-ice apnea world records of 2003: the first match was played on 5 March 2005 at the Weissensee in Carinthia (930 m, Europe's highest-altitude swimming lake), where Austria and Germany drew 8–8 — Redl and Jaromir Foukal against Philipp von Heydebreck and Knut Stender — followed in 2006 by a 9–8 Austrian win over Slovakia (Wikipedia, n.d.; Red Bull, n.d.). The first world championship took place at the Weissensee in 2007 with eight teams — Finland won, Austria was runner-up — and a second World Cup followed in February 2013, won by Austria (Wikipedia, n.d.). The rink is a roughly 6 × 8 m area beneath ice at least 12 inches thick, with water at about 2 °C; women and men play under identical breath-hold rules, and mixed teams compete at the Weissensee events (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

The playing surface is the underside of a thick natural ice sheet: a rink of about 6 × 8 m is marked beneath ice at least 12 inches (30 cm) thick, and a goal is hung from the ice at each end, each with an escape hole cut beside it (Wikipedia, n.d.). Players wear wetsuits or drysuits and breathe-hold — no tanks are allowed — surfacing roughly every 30 seconds through the safety holes, while four safety divers plus a photographer and cameraman stand by under the ice (Wikipedia, n.d.). The puck is buoyant and floats up against the ice ceiling, so players, inverted, flick it with short sticks toward the suspended goals; matches run as short 10-minute games with 10-minute warm-up breaks between them (Wikipedia, n.d.). Teams are small — typically three field players and a goalkeeper per side — and men's and women's teams follow identical rules; there is no world federation, with events organized by Redl's team and allied extreme-sports promoters under strict ice-safety protocols (Red Bull, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Underwater ice hockey was invented in 2005 by the Austrian freediver and extreme athlete Christian Redl (born 1976), who had set under-ice apnea world records in 2003 and wanted a spectacular team game for frozen Alpine lakes (Red Bull, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The world's first match was played on 5 March 2005 at the Weissensee in Carinthia, Austria — Europe's highest swimming lake at 930 m — between Austria (Redl and Jaromir Foukal) and Germany (Philipp von Heydebreck and Knut Stender), ending 8–8; in 2006 Austria beat Slovakia 9–8 at the same venue (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first world championship followed at the Weissensee in 2007 with eight nations — Finland won the title and Austria finished runner-up — and a second World Cup was held there in February 2013, won by Austria (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport has remained on the demonstration and extreme-sports circuit at the Weissensee, never seeking mass participation; its fame rests as one of the most physically extreme conversions of a mainstream sport ever staged, played breath-hold at 2 °C under the ice (Wikipedia, n.d.; Red Bull, n.d.).
