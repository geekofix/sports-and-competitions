Offshore Powerboat Racing

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Offshore powerboat racing is long-distance circuit racing of high-performance catamarans and monohulls on open water — the UIM Class 1 World Championship is the pinnacle, with boats exceeding 160 km/h (100 mph) in rough seas (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the powerboat racing family with F1H2O and E1.

Figure: Opac1cat. (2019-11-09). Class 1 offshore powerboat MAXEDRUSH at full speed (Jimmy Cazzani) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Cazzani_MAXEDRUSH_ThaLia_Ann_Angela.jpg

Class 1 boats are 10–14 m catamarans with twin engines of 2,000+ hp total, running on race fuel; the UIM (Union Internationale Motonautique) governs the World Championship, which has raced since the 1980s in Europe, the Middle East, Asia and the US (UIM, n.d.). The Italian Cazzani boats in the photo are long-time front-runners of the class (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Races run on closed circuits of buoys for a set number of laps or time; crews are driver/throttle with optional second crew; safety requires helmets, life jackets, kill switches and rescue boats (UIM, n.d.). Men and women compete — women have raced and won in Class 1 and S1 classes.

History

Offshore racing grew out of 1960s US ocean racing (Miami–Nassau etc.); the UIM created the Class 1 World Championship in 1981, and European and Middle Eastern rounds (Italy, Malta, Dubai, China) made it a world series (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 1980s–90s rivalry of Cazzani, Victory and others defined the era.
