Gymnastics Pommel Horse

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The pommel horse is the men's artistic gymnastics apparatus of pure swing: a 1.15 m high, 1.60 m long padded body with two pommels, on which the gymnast performs continuous circular leg swings (circles), scissors, travels along the horse and finishes with a dismount — with no stops allowed (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is the second men's apparatus in the rotation; at Paris 2024 the title went to Rhys McClenaghan of Ireland — his nation's first Olympic gymnastics medal (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.).

Among the leading pommel workers of the 2026 season: Donghua Li of China (pictured), Rhys McClenaghan, and the 2026 U.S. championships medallists Danila Leykin and Fuzzy Benas of the USA (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). This is a men's apparatus at elite level — the women's counterpart is the uneven bars (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

This belongs to the eight artistic-gymnastics apparatus — Floor Exercise, Rings, Vault, Parallel Bars, Horizontal Bar, Uneven Bars, Balance Beam — within the gymnastics family — the parent Artistic Gymnastics page covers all eight apparatus; full list under Important Links.

Rules

The pommel horse is 1.15 m high and 1.60 m long, with two pommels mounted crossways on top of the body; the gymnast must keep both legs swinging in circles without stops, and the routine has no time limit (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c). Eight elements count toward the D-score, with bonuses for difficulty and connections; the E-panel starts at 10.0 and deducts heavily for stops, falls (0.5), bent legs and amplitude faults (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a).

The top eight from qualification advance to the apparatus final; the highest D + E wins. Men compete on the pommel horse; women have no equivalent apparatus at elite level (the uneven bars are the closest counterpart), junior categories use a lower horse (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

History

The pommel horse descends from 19th-century vaulting and swinging apparatus and has been an Olympic apparatus since 1896; the modern pommel with removable pommels dates to the mid-20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Zoltán Magyar of Hungary, pictured, known as the “Lord of the Rings”, won the pommel horse gold at Montreal 1976 and Munich 1980 and was renowned for his graceful circles; Great Britain's Max Whitlock, pictured, became the first man to win three Olympic pommel titles (London 2012, Rio 2016, Tokyo 2020), and Nikolai Andrianov and Vitaly Scherbo of the USSR dominated the 1970s–80s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
