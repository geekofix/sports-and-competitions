Soft Tennis

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Soft tennis is the Japanese variant of tennis played with a soft, inflatable rubber ball about half the weight of a standard tennis ball, using lighter rackets, mostly played as doubles on an ordinary tennis court. Developed in Japan around 1884, during the Meiji-era introduction of Western tennis, it became Japan's own racquet discipline and then an East Asian competitive sport — an Asian Games event since 1994 — governed internationally by the International Soft Tennis Federation (ISTF), with world championships since 1975.

Rules

Soft tennis follows tennis's basic architecture — court dimensions, scoring, serve-and-volley logic — but is mostly contested as doubles, with the ball, a rubber inflatable sphere of about 30 grams, giving longer rallies built around spin and flight; the net is also slightly different (the soft tennis net hung level along its whole length). The serve is a decisive weapon — rules revised in 1992 formalised singles as an event and let the forward (net) player in doubles serve — and matches are commonly best-of-seven-game sets (a set to four points) with match formats defined by the ISTF rule book; only one serve is permitted per point, placing high value on serve consistency. As in tennis, balls must land in the diagonally opposite service box, and lets, faults and the standard stroke repertoire (groundstrokes, volleys, lobs, smashes) apply, with the soft ball rewarding drop-shot touch and team play at the net.

History

The sport began in Japan in 1884, a few years after lawn tennis arrived from the West: Japan's humid climate spoiled the imported balls and equipment was scarce, so players developed a lightweight rubber ball (and lighter rackets) that suited both climate and cost — and the new game spread through Japanese schools and universities as a discipline of its own. Japan, Korea and Taiwan jointly established the Asian Soft Tennis Federation in 1955 (Asian championships ran 1956\u20131973); the International Soft Tennis Federation followed in 1973, staging the first World Soft Tennis Championships in Hawaii in 1975, with subsequent editions in Taiwan (1977 venue Taichung), Korea (1979) and beyond, at first biennial and since 1987 quadrennial. Soft tennis was adopted as an official Asian Games sport in 1994 — Japan, South Korea and Chinese Taipei dominating the medal tables ever since — while the sport spread from the 1970s through Africa (notably DR Congo), Europe (federations in Hungary, Czechia, Poland), the Americas (Brazil, Venezuela) and the United States (a Hawaii federation since 1972 and mainland demonstrations from 1978), with school-level competition in Japan remaining the sport's deepest base and the All-Japan championships its traditional summits.
