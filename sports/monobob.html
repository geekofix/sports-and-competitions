Monobob

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Monobob is the single-athlete event of bobsleigh: one pilot rides a homologated two-man sled alone, supine and feet-first, steering with the shoulders and calves at speeds above 130 km/h on the same ice tracks as the other bobsleigh events (International Bobsleigh & Skeleton Federation, n.d.-b). The discipline debuted at the Youth Olympic Games in Lillehammer 2016 and became an Olympic medal event at Beijing 2022 with the women's monobob; men's and women's monobob are both contested on the IBSF World Cup circuit (International Bobsleigh & Skeleton Federation, n.d.-a). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics (15–16 February, Cortina Sliding Centre "Eugenio Monti"), the United States' Elana Meyers Taylor won the women's gold medal in a combined time of 3:57.93, including a track record of 59.08 in the third run, ahead of Germany's Laura Nolte (3:57.97) and her compatriot Kaillie Humphries (3:58.05) (International Bobsleigh & Skeleton Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women in the discipline are Olympic champion Elana Meyers Taylor, 2025–26 World Cup winner Laura Nolte of Germany and 2025 world champion Kaysha Love of the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

A monobob race consists of four runs over two days (two runs per day) on the same 1,000–1,400 m refrigerated ice track used for the other bobsleigh events, and the lowest combined time wins; a dead heat is broken by the fastest individual run (International Bobsleigh & Skeleton Federation, n.d.-b). The sled must be IBSF-homologated, and minimum and maximum weights for sled plus athlete are enforced at scrutineering — violations bring penalties or disqualification, as do false starts and equipment irregularities. Athletes wear aerodynamic one-piece suits and full-face helmets, start seated on two handles, and steer by flexing the runners with the calves and shifting shoulder pressure. The women's event has been on the Olympic programme since Beijing 2022, while men's and women's monobob are both run at World Cup and World Championship level; the event uses identical sleds, tracks and timing for women and men (International Bobsleigh & Skeleton Federation, n.d.-a).

History

Bobsleigh originated in the Swiss resort of St. Moritz in the late 19th century, where the first sled races were held on the Cresta Run from 1884, and the International Bobsleigh and Tobogganing Federation (FIBT, now IBSF) was founded in 1923; bobsleigh debuted at the first Winter Olympics in Chamonix 1924 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The single-athlete monobob was introduced as a new discipline at the 2016 Youth Olympic Games in Lillehammer — where both a women's and a men's event were staged — and the IOC added the women's monobob to the Olympic programme for Beijing 2022, making monobob the first new bobsleigh event at the Games in decades (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
