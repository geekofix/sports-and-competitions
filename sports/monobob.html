Monobob

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Monobob is the single-athlete version of bobsleigh: one pilot pushes, drives and brakes the sled alone on an artificial ice track. It was introduced at the Winter Youth Olympic Games in Lillehammer 2016 for both men and women, and the IOC added a women's monobob event to the Olympic program for Beijing 2022 to give female pilots a second Olympic discipline alongside two-woman bobsleigh. At the Olympics all athletes use identical sleds supplied by the organizers, so the result depends purely on driving. The sport is governed by the International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation (IBSF).

Rules

The monobob athlete starts alone, pushing the sled by hand on the flat start section, then loads it and steers with subtle body movements, using the runners and handles; braking is done with the feet after the finish line. Races are run over several heats (four at the Olympic Games), and the lowest combined time over all heats wins. The sled must meet IBSF dimensional and minimum-weight regulations, and athletes wear aerodynamic suits and helmets. At IBSF events, monobob is also used for Para bobsleigh and the youth monobob series, where athletes share sleds in a set order.

History

Single-person sleds were introduced into international competition for adaptive bobsleigh and as a youth discipline, because younger athletes cannot push a heavy two- or four-person sled. Monobob made its official debut at the 2016 Winter Youth Olympic Games in Lillehammer, and on 18 July 2018 the IOC Executive Board decided to add women's monobob to the Beijing 2022 program. The Olympic debut at the Yanqing Sliding Centre was won by Kaillie Humphries of the United States, ahead of her teammate Elana Meyers Taylor and Christine de Bruin of Canada; Laura Nolte of Germany is the only female athlete to win both Youth Olympic and Olympic bobsleigh gold. At Milano Cortina 2026 the field grew to 25 starters.
