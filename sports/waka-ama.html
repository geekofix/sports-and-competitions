Waka Ama

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Waka ama is the Māori term for outrigger canoe racing in New Zealand (Aotearoa): crews of one to twelve paddlers (W1–W12) sprint over 250 m, 500 m or 1,000 m, with the W6 1,000 m the classic world-championship distance (Waka Ama New Zealand, n.d.). The sport is governed internationally by the International Va'a Federation (IVF) and nationally by Waka Ama Aotearoa New Zealand; the IVF World Sprint Championships 2026 in Singapore crowned Aotearoa New Zealand elite men's V12 500 m champions in 1:50.12 (NZ Herald, 2026; Waka Ama NZ, 2026). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are Va'a, Outrigger Canoeing, Para Va'a, Canoe Sprint, Surf Ski, Canoe Marathon; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Figure: Tijuana Brass. (2007). Racing outrigger canoes (waka) side by side [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Racing_outriggers.jpg

Rules

Sprint races run over 250 m (juniors), 500 m and 1,000 m in W1, W6 and W12 hulls; crews start aligned and change seats at marked zones in W6/W12. Distance racing (up to 30 km) is also sanctioned, with W6 and W12 the premier classes. Hulls are classed by the IVF technical regulations; paddles are single-bladed. Men's, women's and mixed crews compete in the same classes, and para va'a (VL classes) is integrated at IVF world events (IVF, n.d.; Waka Ama NZ, n.d.).

History

Waka ama blends Māori waka (canoe) tradition with modern sprint racing; competitive waka ama grew from the 1980s in New Zealand, and the IVF World Sprint Championships — first held in 1988 — are the highlight of the calendar. New Zealand clubs such as Horouta Waka Hoe have been world champions at club level (Waka Ama NZ, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
