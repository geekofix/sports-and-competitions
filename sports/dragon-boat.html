Dragon Boat

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dragon boat racing is a team paddling sport in which crews of 10 or 20 paddlers (plus a drummer and a steerer) race decorated boats with dragon heads over 200 m, 500 m and 2,000 m distances. It is governed by the International Dragon Boat Federation (IDBF), which runs the IDBF Club Crew World Championships, and by the International Canoe Federation (ICF), which includes dragon boat as a discipline in the Canoe Sprint World Championships program.

Rules

At IDBF World Championship events, standard boats carry 20 paddlers, a drummer at the front who sets the stroke rate and a steerer (helm) at the rear; small boats (10 paddlers) are used at some national events. Races are held over 200 m, 500 m and 2,000 m in lanes, with heats, semifinals and finals. The start is given by the starter's commands synchronized with the drummer; false starts disqualify. Boats must stay in their lane (lane violations judged by video), and the first boat whose dragon head (bow) crosses the finish line wins. Paddles and boats follow IDBF standard specifications (boat length about 12.5 m for 20-paddler boats, paddle blade size regulated), and crews are mixed-gender at many events with minimum female paddler quotas. At ICF events, dragon boat races follow the canoe sprint rules over 200 m and 500 m.

History

Dragon boat racing originates from the Duanwu (Dragon Boat) Festival in China over 2,000 years ago, traditionally held on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month to commemorate the poet Qu Yuan (c. 340-278 BC). The sport spread across East and Southeast Asia and, from the 1980s, worldwide through the IDBF, founded in 1991 in Hong Kong, which standardized international competition with the first World Championships in 1995. The ICF added dragon boat to its program in the 2000s, and it was a demonstration sport at the 2008 Beijing Olympics. Today the IDBF has members in over 80 countries, and the Club Crew World Championships and World Championships attract thousands of paddlers; the drummer's call and the synchronized stroke remain the heart of the sport.
