Quadball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Quadball (called quidditch until its 2022 renaming) is a mixed-gender contact team sport inspired by the fictional sport from the Harry Potter novels, played with a broom or PVC stick held between all players' legs. Two teams of seven — three chasers, two beaters, a keeper and a seeker — score points by throwing a volleyball (the quaffle) through the opponent's hoops, while dodging dodgeballs (bludgers) thrown by beaters; the game ends when a neutral flag runner, standing in for the "snitch", is caught. The sport's international governing body is the International Quadball Association (IQA), with national bodies such as US Quadball running leagues and championships.

Rules

Under IQA and US Quadball rules, played on a field roughly like a handball pitch with three hoops of different heights at each end, chasers advance the quaffle and score 10 points per goal through any hoop, with physical contact regulated much like in rugby — tackles below the shoulders and above the knees are not allowed, and dangerous contact removes players from play. Beaters throw the bludgers to hit opponents; any player hit by a bludger is "knocked out", must drop the quaffle and return to their hoops before re-entering play. All players must hold a broom between their legs at all times. The snitch in fiction is replaced by a neutral snitch runner dressed in yellow, released at the 18th minute, whom each team's seeker tries to catch — worth 30 points, and ending the game; a seeker must remove the tail or flag attached to the snitch runner. Games therefore have no fixed end time but finish only when the snitch is caught. The IQA's famous gender rule caps each team at a maximum of four players of the same gender among its seven on pitch.

History

Quadball began in October 2005 at Middlebury College in Vermont, where freshmen Xander Manshel and Alex Benepe adapted J.K. Rowling's fictional game into a working outdoor sport with elements of lacrosse, dodgeball and rugby; the first match at Battell Beach drew about 30 players, and the college soon had ten intramural teams. The "World Cup" era followed: Middlebury played Vassar in the first intercollegiate match in 2007, the third World Cup in 2009 drew 21 teams and 2,000 spectators, and the sport spread to hundreds of universities. Governance matured with the Intercollegiate Quidditch Association (founded 2009, renamed International Quidditch Association in 2010), US Quidditch (incorporated 2010, renamed US Quadball in July 2022) and leagues across Europe and beyond; an IQA World Cup for national teams has been held since 2012. In 2022 the sport's leaders renamed both governance bodies and the game itself "quadball" — originating in trademark disputes with the Harry Potter rights-holders and distancing the community from the author's public statements — with the IQA rebranding its championship structure under the new name.
