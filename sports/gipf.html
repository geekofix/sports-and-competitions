GIPF

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

GIPF is the first game of Kris Burm's GIPF project (1997, Belgium): two players move marbles on a hexagonal board of 37 intersections, aiming to make lines of four of their color, which are then removed (capturing opponent pieces in them); a player who cannot move loses. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are DVONN, TZAAR, ZERTZ; the related pages are listed under Important Links (GIPF project, n.d.).

Figure: Aldaron , a.k.a. Aldaron .. (2007-07-21). GIPF in play [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.5. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:GIPF_in_Play.jpg

Rules

Hexagonal board; on each turn a player introduces a piece (single or double) at an edge point and pushes it inward; any line of exactly four same-color pieces is removed (captured if opponent's); if you make three in a row nothing happens. You lose if you cannot move or have no pieces left. A world GIPF championship exists (GIPF project, n.d.).

History

Designed by Kris Burm (Belgium) in 1997 and self-published; it founded the GIPF project (DVONN, TZAAR, ZERTZ, YINSH, etc.) and won the Mensa Select award. Tournaments photographed at the Deskohraní game fair (Prague 2008) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
