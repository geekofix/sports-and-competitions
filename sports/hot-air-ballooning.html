Hot Air Ballooning

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hot-air ballooning as a sport covers competitive ballooning: precision flying tasks, long-distance racing and record attempts with hot-air balloons. It is governed by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its Ballooning Commission (CIA), which sets world records and runs the FAI World Hot Air Ballooning Championships, held biennially since the 1970s.

Rules

At FAI Category 1 events (World Championships and record attempts), competitors fly tasks set by the task-setter: a "pilot declared goal" (the pilot declares a goal before flight and the marker drop closest to it scores), "fox hunt" (follow a lead balloon and drop a marker on its basket), "hesitation waltz" (fly to one of several goals revealed at the last moment), and maximum/minimum distance tasks. Scoring is based on the distance of the marker drop from the goal in meters, with 1,000 points for a drop inside the goal. Pilots must hold an FAI balloon pilot license, and balloons must be airworthy and registered. Records cover distance, altitude and duration in the AX size classes.

History

Hot-air ballooning began with the Montgolfier brothers' first public flight in Paris in 1783; Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes flew over Paris on 21 November 1783, and Blanchard and Jeffries made the first cross-Channel flight in 1785. Competitive ballooning grew in the 19th century with long-distance flights. The FAI, founded in 1905, has recognized ballooning records since the early 20th century and runs the World Hot Air Ballooning Championships since 1973 (first in Albuquerque, New Mexico). The Gordon Bennett Cup (gas balloons, since 1906) is the oldest ballooning trophy, and the America's Challenge is the modern gas-balloon race.
