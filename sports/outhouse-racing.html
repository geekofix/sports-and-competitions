Outhouse Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Outhouse racing is a novelty team race in which decorated outhouses (privies) mounted on wheels are pushed or pulled through the streets of a town while one team member sits inside on the "throne" at all times (Dawson City, n.d.). The best-known event is the Great Klondike International Outhouse Race in Dawson City, Yukon, Canada, held every Labour Day weekend since 1977; similar races are held in Cochrane and Almonte in Canada, Bristol, Connecticut, and many other towns in North America (Wikipedia, n.d.). The races combine speed, engineering and humor, and are usually part of local festivals; women's and men's teams compete in mixed heats under the same rules.

Rules

A team typically consists of three to five members: one rider who must remain seated inside the outhouse for the whole race (teams may trade off who sits) and two to four runners who push or pull it along a set course through town — about 2 km in Dawson City (Dawson City, n.d.). The outhouse rides on four caster wheels or, in winter variants, on skis across a frozen lake (Wikipedia, n.d.). The team with the fastest time wins; many events run head-to-head heats leading to a final (Dawson City, n.d.). Decoration and costume categories are judged separately, and the race committee can disqualify any outhouse deemed unsafe or unsound (Dawson City, n.d.). Obstacles and scavenger stops may be part of the route; all teams — men's, women's and mixed — follow the same course and rules (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

The Great Klondike International Outhouse Race began in 1977 as part of Dawson City's Labour Day celebrations during the Klondike Gold Rush anniversary, when local teams raced wheeled outhouses — a nod to the outdoor toilets of the 1898 Gold Rush era — down Front Street (Dawson City, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The event became an international draw, with teams arriving from across Canada, the United States and overseas, and has been described as one of the most hilarious traditions in the Yukon (Klondike Visitors Association, n.d.). Copycat outhouse races spread to Cochrane and Almonte, Ontario, Bristol, Connecticut, and many other towns, each with its own festival framing (Wikipedia, n.d.).
