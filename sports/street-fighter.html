Street Fighter

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive Street Fighter is the esports scene of Capcom's 2D fighting-game series — currently Street Fighter 6 (2023) — played 1v1 in double-elimination tournaments with best-of-3 sets (best-of-5 in grand finals), 99-second rounds and health bars; a round ends when a player's health reaches zero or time expires (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The premier open tournament is the Evolution Championship Series (EVO), held in Las Vegas: EVO 2026 ran 26–28 June 2026 at the Las Vegas Convention Center with 5,774 competitors across twelve main games including Street Fighter 6 and Tekken 8 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

MenaRD (Saul Leonardo Mena II, Dominican Republic) won the Street Fighter 6 tournament at EVO 2026, defeating Shigematsu in the grand final — the first back-to-back EVO SF6 champion since Daigo Umehara in 2009–2010 (EVO, n.d.). Earlier in 2026, Yamaguchi won EVO Japan (1–3 May, Tokyo) with 7,683 registered players — a Guinness World Record as the biggest single-game fighting tournament ever — and Craime won the SF6 title at the 2026 Esports World Cup (Capcom, n.d.; esports.gg, n.d.). The Capcom Pro Tour culminates in Capcom Cup 13 with a $1 million prize (Capcom, n.d.).

Women compete in the same open brackets as men at every major fighting-game event — there are no gender divisions at EVO or the Capcom Pro Tour (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Rules

Tournament sets are best-of-3 (best-of-5 at grand finals of majors), double elimination, on the current patch with a standard ruleset: 99-second rounds, no items, stage select by mutual agreement or random in later rounds. SF6 adds the Drive Gauge system — Drive Impact, Drive Parry, Drive Rush and Super Arts — which shapes neutral play (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Ranking circuits: the Capcom Pro Tour (Premier, Super Premier and Pro Tour events feed Capcom Cup, $1M prize for the 2026 Cup 13), the Esports World Cup club-points series, and EVO's open brackets; anti-collusion rules (adopted after the 2004 EVO Soulcalibur II incident) forbid intentionally losing matches (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's players compete in the same open brackets — there are no gender divisions (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Competitive fighting games coalesced in the late 1990s (Battle by the Bay, 1996) and the Evolution Championship Series has run since 2002, becoming the world's largest fighting-game tournament. EVO 2004 produced "Evo Moment #37": Daigo Umehara parried 15 consecutive hits from Justin Wong's Chun-Li with Ken, then won the match — still the most famous moment in fighting-game history; the same event's Soulcalibur II final led to EVO's collusion rule (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Daigo Umehara ("The Beast"), six-time EVO champion across multiple games and inducted into the EVO Hall of Fame in 2024, is widely considered the greatest Street Fighter player ever; Justin Wong, Tokido and MenaRD are other icons (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). EVO 2026's SF6 bracket was again won by MenaRD, with Shigematsu second and Kilzyou third (EVO, n.d.).
