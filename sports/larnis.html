Larnis

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Larnis is a Filipino blade art within the arnis/eskrima/kali family, emphasizing the sword (kalis), kampilan and dagger ranges with flowing footwork and blade control; it is taught as a subset of arnis in the Philippines and by FMA schools worldwide. Together with arnis de mano (empty hand) and latigo y cadena (flexible weapons) it forms one of the three classical divisions of Filipino martial arts. Men and women train; blade-sparring with protective gear is contested at FMA tournaments. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Eskrima, Arnis de Mano, Pekiti-Tirsia, Latigo y Cadena; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Sport larnis follows the modern arnis competition rules (World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation / WEKAF, RA 9850 in the Philippines): padded sticks for stick events, live blade only in choreographed or cutting events with full protective gear; point sparring over three two-minute rounds; disarms, locks and throws count. Traditional larnis has no ring rules — it is a combat system assessed through forms and controlled sparring (World Eskrima Kali Arnis Federation, n.d.).

History

When the Spanish arrived in 1521, Filipinos fought with kalis and kampilan and defeated Spanish fencers (the battle of Mactan, 1521, against Magellan's men, led by Lapu-Lapu). During 330 years of Spanish rule the arts were driven underground and developed into the regional systems known as arnis (Luzon), eskrima (Visayas) and kali (Mindanao); "larnis" names the blade component. In the 20th century the Philippine government promoted arnis (Palarong Pambansa since 2006; RA 9850 declaring arnis the national martial art, 2009) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
