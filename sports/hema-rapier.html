HEMA Rapier

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rapier (and smallsword) is a major HEMA discipline fought with blunted steel thrusting swords modeled on 16th-18th century European fencing, especially the Italian and Spanish schools (Capo Ferro, Fabris, Destreza) and later French smallsword. See HEMA for general rules and organizations; rapier is the second-largest division at events like the HEMA Alliance Tournament and Longpoint.

Rules

Rapier bouts follow the HEMA Alliance Rulebook or event rulesets: the valid target is the torso (and often the head) in modern HEMA rapier — full-body scoring is used in some events — with points for clean thrusts with control; afterblow rules apply for doubles; matches are to a point cap or time limit (typically 3-5 minutes), best of three. The weapon is a blunted steel rapier (approx. 1.1 m, 0.9-1.2 kg) with a complex hilt (cup, knuckle bow, rings); off-hand weapons (dagger, cloak) are allowed in "rapier & dagger" divisions. Mandatory protection: fencing mask, gorget, gloves, and forearm/arm protection; thrusts to the face require adequate mask ratings (e.g., CEN Level 2 or overlay for some events). Footwork, distance management (finding the blade, parry-ripostes) are central.

History

Rapier fencing is documented from the 16th century — Italian masters Camillo Agrippa (1553) and Ridolfo Capo Ferro (1610), the Spanish Destreza masters (Thibault, Godinho, Pacheco de Narváez), and later French masters such as La Boëssière and Besnard and English smallsword masters (Silver, Hutton) — before sport fencing standardized the épée/foil in the 19th-20th centuries. The modern HEMA rapier scene revived these sources from the 2000s, with tournaments growing rapidly; the rapier community is particularly strong in the US, Sweden and across Europe.
