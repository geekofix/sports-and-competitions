Apopudobalia

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Apopudobalia (Greek: jumping off) refers to the ancient equestrian feat in which a rider jumps on and off a galloping horse — or leaps from one galloping horse to another, a practice known as amphippodromia and related to the apobates chariot event of the ancient Greek games. Today it is revived as a demonstration sport in historical and equestrian shows.

Rules

There is no single modern rulebook: in historical reenactment and equestrian show competitions, riders are judged on mounting and dismounting a cantering or galloping horse safely and cleanly, and on multiple horse-to-horse leaps, with penalties for falls or loss of control. Safety equipment and horse welfare rules apply at organized events.

History

Horse-to-horse leaping (amphippodromia) was performed at festivals in ancient Thessaly and is described in classical sources; the apobates event at the Panhellenic games involved an armed rider jumping off a moving chariot. The practice was revived in 19th-century cavalry shows and survives today in trick-riding and historical reenactment performances.
