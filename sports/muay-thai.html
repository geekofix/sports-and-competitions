Muay Thai

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Muay Thai (muaythai), the Thai national martial art also called "the art of eight limbs", is a full-contact striking sport that uses fists, elbows, knees and feet, plus sweeps and the clinch. Professional bouts are held in famous Bangkok stadiums such as Lumpinee (opened 1956) and Rajadamnern (opened 1945), and globally through promotions such as ONE Championship. Amateur muaythai is governed by the International Federation of Muaythai Associations (IFMA), which the IOC fully recognized on 20 July 2021; the ancient precursor is Muay Boran, and related arts include lethwei and kickboxing.

Rules

Under IFMA rules, elite amateur bouts consist of three rounds of three minutes with one-minute rests and protective equipment (gloves, shin guards, head guard), while professional stadium and world title fights are five rounds of three minutes with one-minute rests, scored round by round by judges. Fighters wear shorts and the mongkhon headband and perform the wai kru ram muay pre-fight ritual. Legal techniques are punches, kicks, knees, elbows, sweeps and the clinch; fouls include biting, eye gouging, groin strikes, headbutts and attacking a downed opponent, which bring warnings, point deductions or disqualification. A fighter wins by knockout, technical knockout or on points; weight classes and weigh-ins apply at all sanctioned events.

History

Muay thai's documented history runs from the warrior training of the Ayutthaya period, through the regional schools of Korat, Lopburi, Chaiya and Thasao, to the 1929 codification that introduced gloves, weight classes and timed rounds. Rajadamnern Stadium opened in Bangkok in 1945 and Lumpinee in 1956, making stadium muay a national institution. The International Federation of Muaythai Amateur (later IFMA) was inaugurated in 1993 with now more than 128 member countries, the World Muaythai Council (WMC) was established in 1995 for the professional side, muaythai became a full SEA Games medal sport in 2005 and has been on the World Games program, and the IOC granted full recognition to IFMA on 20 July 2021 at the 138th IOC Session in Tokyo. Legends of the sport include Dieselnoi Chor Thanasukarn, Samart Payakaroon and Buakaw Banchamek.
