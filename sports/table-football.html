Table Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Table football, also known as foosball, is the tabletop version of association football: figures mounted on rods are twisted, pushed and pulled to strike a small ball into the opponent's goal on a boxed table with eight rods and rows of players. Played casually by millions in bars and recreation rooms, it is also a serious tournament sport governed internationally by the International Table Soccer Federation (ITSF).

Rules

Two players or pairs each control four rods "+E+" goalkeeper, defence, midfield and attack "+E+" turning and sliding them to trap, pass and shoot a ball down the field and into the opponent's goal, with a serve through the side hole or onto the feet of a centre figure restarting play after each goal. Formal rules prohibit spinning the rods a full rotation before or after striking the ball, enforce possession-time limits, and govern dead balls and timeouts; matches are typically races to a fixed number of goals in best-of series, and tournament tables differ by brand, with several makes sanctioned for ITSF world championship play.

History

Foosball's ancestor tables first appeared in the 1890s in Germany and France, with British, Spanish and Galician inventors all patenting versions in the 1920s and 1930s (Alejandro Finisterre, the Galician poet, patented his futbolin in Madrid in 1937, and that Spanish form survives worldwide). The game reached the United States in the 1950s through Lawrence Patterson's imports and peaked there in the 1970s bar era, when players staged the first professional tournaments and prize money; the name foosball arrived from the German tischfussball on imported coin-operated tables. The International Table Soccer Federation was founded in 2002 to unify the rules and grow competition, and today ITSF world championships and national circuits contest singles, doubles and team titles on multiple sanctioned table makes.
