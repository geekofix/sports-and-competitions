Softball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Softball is the bat-and-ball sport played between two teams on a diamond-shaped field, a close sibling of baseball pitched underhand, that exists in two principal forms — fastpitch (the Olympic, world-championship and intercollegiate format) and slowpitch (the recreational favourite). Invented in Chicago in 1887 as an indoor "mush ball" game, softball became one of the world's most-played team sports; it is governed globally by the World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC), with women's fastpitch contested at the Olympic Games from 1996 to 2008 and again from 2028.

Rules

The fastpitch game is played nine-a-side over seven innings on a diamond with 60-foot base paths and a pitching rubber 43 feet from home plate (women's); the pitcher must deliver underhand — the windmill motion, with speeds recorded to 79.4 mph (127.8 km/h) in elite women's play — while batters face balls of 12-inch circumference in fastpitch (11-inch for younger players). The Windmill-style speed of fastpitch contrasts with slowpitch's high arc and makes stealing, bunting and hit-and-run tactics live, with the game otherwise following baseball's architecture: three outs per half-inning, strikeouts and walks, catches for outs. Slowpitch plays ten a side, forbids bunting and base stealing, and enforces 6\u201310-foot pitch arcs under USA Softball rules. Both games mandate batting helmets, enforce bat performance standards for safety, and are played worldwide under WBSC and national-body rulebooks.

History

The game was invented on Thanksgiving Day 1887 at the Farragut Boat Club in Chicago, where Harvard and Yale alumni awaiting a football score turned a wrapped boxing glove (hit with a broom handle) into a new indoor game — with reporter George Hancock codifying rules; the 16-inch "mush ball" remains Chicago's beloved bare-handed variant. The sport spread quickly through the Midwest as an indoor winter game and then outdoors; the Amateur Softball Association (ASA) codified national rules in 1933 — national championships debuted at that year's Chicago World's Fair — the International Softball Federation followed in 1952, and the first Women's Softball World Championship was staged in Melbourne in 1965 (won by Australia). Women's fastpitch debuted at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics and stayed on the programme through 2008 — the United States winning three of the first four golds and Japan taking Beijing 2008 and Tokyo 2020 back-to-back — before the WBSC's 2013 formation (merging softball and baseball's federations) and softball's confirmed return to the programme at Los Angeles 2028. Today the WBSC governs the sport in over 110 countries, with strong men's fastpitch traditions in New Zealand, Argentina, Canada, the Czech Republic and Japan (the Men's Softball World Cup's roll of honour), while in the United States alone tens of millions play recreational softball every summer — from corporate slowpitch to the college fastpitch game whose Women's College World Series is a television fixture.
