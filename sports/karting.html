Karting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Karting is motor sport with small, open-wheel, four-wheeled vehicles (go-karts or racing karts) without suspension, raced on dedicated kart circuits. It is both a mass-participation recreational activity and the traditional entry point into professional motor racing. Internationally the sport is governed by FIA Karting (the Commission Internationale de Karting, CIK-FIA, founded by the FIA in 1962), which organizes the FIA Karting World Championship, held annually since 1964 in classes such as OK (direct-drive 125 cc) and KZ (six-speed gearbox 125 cc).

Rules

CIK-FIA events run in sprint format: free practice, qualifying, qualification heats and a final of typically 15-30 minutes over several kilometres, with points awarded for the final classification. Chassis, engines, tires and fuel are homologated under FIA technical regulations, and drivers compete in age and engine classes from OK-Junior up to KZ and Superkart. Karts have no suspension and no differential on the rear axle (or a rigid axle), so drivers manage grip by chassis flex and driving technique; minimum weights and driver equipment (helmet, suit, rib protector) are strictly regulated. The FIA Karting Endurance Championship, held at Le Mans, is a 24-hour team relay race.

History

The first go-kart, the Caretta-West Bend, was built in August 1956 in Glendale, California, by race-car builder Art Ingels with Lou Borelli, using a surplus West Bend two-stroke engine, and was tested in the Rose Bowl parking lot, where it attracted hundreds of enthusiasts. The Go Kart Club of America was founded in 1957, and karting spread to Europe by the end of the decade, prompting the FIA to create the International Karting Commission in 1962. The first CIK-FIA World Championship followed in 1964. Karting became the proving ground for future stars: Alain Prost was the first karting champion to win the Formula 1 World Championship, followed by Ayrton Senna, Michael Schumacher, Lewis Hamilton, Nico Rosberg, Sebastian Vettel, Max Verstappen (KZ world champion 2013) and Lando Norris (2014). The only female FIA karting world champion is Italy's Susanna Raganelli (1966).
