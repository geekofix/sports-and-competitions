Oil Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Oil wrestling (yağlı güreş) is the national sport of Turkey, a form of wrestling in which two pehlivan (wrestlers) wearing kispet, thick leather trousers soaked in olive oil, grapple until one brings the other's back or belly to the ground. The most famous event is the annual Kırkpınar tournament in Edirne, held since 1362 and recognized by Guinness as the oldest continuously sanctioned sporting event in the world, and the sport is inscribed on the UNESCO list of intangible cultural heritage. Matches are sanctioned by the Turkish Wrestling Federation.

Rules

Before a bout the wrestlers are anointed with olive oil, which makes gripping difficult, and they wear the kispet, leather trousers weighing about 13 kg when dry. A match is won when one wrestler pins the opponent so that the back or belly touches the ground, or by lifting the opponent and controlling him with a grip on the kispet, classically carrying him three steps or spinning him around, a winning hold known as paça kazık. Since 1975 bouts have time limits: up to 40 minutes in the başpehlivan (head-wrestler) category and 30 minutes in the pehlivan category, with a score overtime of 15 and 10 minutes if no winner emerges; in overtime the first point or successful hold wins. Tournaments run over several days with hundreds of wrestlers in ranked categories, and a wrestler who wins the Kırkpınar title three years in a row is awarded the Golden Belt (kemer) and becomes başpehlivan for life.

History

Oil wrestling has been practiced in Anatolia and Thrace for centuries, with organized festivals documented from the Ottoman period; the Kırkpınar tournament near Edirne has been held since 1362, traditionally in early summer, and is listed by Guinness World Records as the world's oldest continuously licensed sporting event. The sport was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2010. Famous pehlivan include Kızılcıklı Mahmut, and today around a thousand wrestlers compete at Kırkpınar over three days. Similar oil-wrestling festivals are also held in Bulgaria and Greece among Turkic and Balkan communities.
