Mölkky

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mölkky is a Finnish outdoor throwing game in which players toss a wooden pin at a group of 12 numbered wooden skittles (1 to 12), trying to reach exactly 50 points (International Mölkky Organisation, n.d.-a; Mölkky Oy, n.d.). It was invented in 1996 by the Lahti-based company Lahden Paikka (formerly Tuoterengas) as a gentler, more accessible relative of the centuries-old Karelian skittles game kyykkä, and the name Mölkky is a protected trademark of Mölkky Oy (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today it is played worldwide by women and men alike in mixed teams, with national associations in more than 20 countries and an annual Mölkky World Championship organized by the International Mölkky Organisation (IMO) (International Mölkky Organisation, n.d.-b).

Within the lawn and throwing games family on this site, its closest relatives are Kubb, Horseshoes, Cornhole, Washer Toss, Ladder Toss; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

At the start of a game the 12 skittles stand upright in a tight formation 3.5 m (about 11.5 ft) from the throwing line, arranged in four rows (1-2; 3-10-4; 5-11-12-6; 7-9-8) (Mölkky Oy, n.d.). Players or teams take turns throwing the throwing pin; knocking over exactly one skittle scores the number printed on it, while knocking over two or more skittles scores only the number of skittles felled (for example three fallen pins score 3 points) (Mölkky Oy, n.d.). A pin counts only if it lies flat on the ground, not leaning on another pin, and after each throw the fallen skittles are stood up where they landed, not in their original positions (Mölkky Oy, n.d.). The first player to reach exactly 50 points wins; scoring more than 50 sends the player back to 25 points, and a player who misses all skittles three turns in a row is eliminated (Mölkky Oy, n.d.). Matches at championship level are played best of three games, with a mölkkout shootout deciding a tied match (International Mölkky Organisation, n.d.-a). The rules are identical for women and men; the World Championship is open to all genders and played in mixed teams (International Mölkky Organisation, n.d.-b).

History

Mölkky was created in 1996 by Lahden Paikka in Lahti, Finland, and sold nearly 200,000 sets in Finland alone within a few years (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Finnish Mölkky Championship was first held in 2001, and in 2004 FIMA (the Finnish International Mölkky Association, predecessor of the IMO) organized the first Mölkky World Championship together with the national championship (International Mölkky Organisation, n.d.-b). The International Mölkky Organisation was founded in 2016 in Le Rheu, France, as FIMA's successor, and the World Championship has since been held outside Finland for the first time (France 2016); more than 20 nations are now members (International Mölkky Organisation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). The game spread from Finland through Europe and to Australia, Japan and the United States, becoming a staple of summer gardens, parks and corporate events worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.).
