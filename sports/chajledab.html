Chajledab

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chajledab is a javelin-like throwing game of indigenous Mexico, contested at the World Indigenous Games (2015 Palmas) among the throwing disciplines (spear toss, chajledab): competitors throw a light wooden javelin (chajledab) for accuracy and distance at a marked target or zone. It is part of the ancestral-games programme promoted by AJDATY (Yucatán) and the Mesoamerican ballgame/rubber-ballgame community (chaaj, pok-ta-pok, chajchaay). Men and women compete in separate categories. Within the target and precision sports on this site, its closest relatives are Chaza, Javelin Throw, Axe Throwing, Boomerang Throwing; the related pages are listed under Important Links (eturbonews, 2017).

Rules

At indigenous-games tournaments the javelin throw is run like a target/distance dual event: a throwing line, a marked target zone, a set number of throws (usually 3–5), scoring by ring/zone for accuracy plus distance bonus in some rule sets; safety line and retrieval on signal. Local variants (Oaxaca, Yucatán) differ in target shape and javelin weight (eturbonews, 2017).

History

Throwing games (dardo/jabalina) are documented among Mesoamerican peoples from pre-Columbian times (the atlatl tradition) and were kept alive in indigenous communities; the AJDATY association of Yucatán has promoted chajledab alongside pok-ta-pok exhibitions (e.g. at the Tocati festival in Italy, 2011) since the 2010s; it was on the 2015 World Indigenous Games programme in Palmas (Wikipedia, n.d.).
