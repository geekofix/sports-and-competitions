Kite Foil

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kite foil (Formula Kite) is a hydrofoil racing class in which the rider stands on a board with a wing-like foil beneath the water and is pulled by a large controllable kite, lifting the board clear of the water at speed. It is governed by World Sailing under the Formula Kite class and became an Olympic medal event at the Paris 2024 Games, replacing the mixed multihull and making kiteboarding part of the Olympic sailing program. Riders reach speeds well above 40 knots and can foil in very light wind.

Rules

In Formula Kite racing, competitors use standardized equipment: a registered foil board and a kite of limited size (the class allows a set of kites for different wind ranges). Races are short upwind-downwind courses around buoys, usually 10-15 minutes, in a fleet of up to 20 riders, with heats progressing to semifinals and a final; the first across the line wins, with Olympic-style medal races for the top ten. Foiling tacks and gybes, where the board briefly touches down, are allowed, but a rider who falls must restart. The class is mixed-gender at the Olympics, with men's and women's events.

History

Hydrofoil boards date to experiments in the 1950s, but the modern kite foil took off in the late 2000s, when production foil boards and the French pioneers around the Lafforgue family made the discipline raceable; it was added to the Olympic sailing program as Formula Kite for the Paris 2024 Games in Marseille, where Austria's Valentin Bockstaller won the men's gold. The International Kiteboarding Association (IKA) developed the racing class before it moved under World Sailing, and the Formula Kite World Championships now decide world titles annually.
