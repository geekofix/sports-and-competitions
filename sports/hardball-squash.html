Hardball Squash

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hardball squash (hardball singles and doubles) is the fast, hard-hitting variant of squash played mainly in the United States with a small, hard rubber ball that rebounds much faster and hotter than the softball used internationally. It is governed in the US by US Squash, which sanctions the US Hardball Singles and Doubles Championships (historic events include the North American Open and the Tournament of Champions).

Rules

Hardball singles is played on the standard 18.5 ft x 32 ft court with a hard rubber ball (about 1.875 in / 4.76 cm diameter, much harder than the softball). Scoring is to 15 points, games best of five, under hand-out or point-a-rally scoring depending on the event. Doubles is played on the wider 25 ft court with a slightly different ball and rules (points to 15, side-out scoring). The ball must be struck before the second bounce; volleys are allowed. Let rules and out-of-bounds lines follow standard squash court markings. The US Hardball Championships are held annually, and the sport is also played at club level across the US and in pockets internationally (e.g., Philadelphia, New York, Boston).

History

Hardball squash developed in the United States in the early 20th century as squash moved from the softer "softball" to a harder, faster ball, especially at clubs like the Racquet Club of Philadelphia, founded in the 1890s and the University Club of New York. US national hardball titles have been contested since the early 20th century, with the North American Open and Tournament of Champions as the premier pro events. Legends include Sharif Khan and Qamar Zaman, who dominated hardball in the 1990s. Internationally, softball squash (World Squash Federation) is the Olympic variant, played since 1922 World Championships.
