Beach Rugby

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Beach rugby is the sand variant of rugby union played under World Rugby's beach rugby laws, most commonly as Beach Five Rugby with five players per side; there is no single world championship, but the Rugby Europe Beach Rugby Championship and the European Beach Rugby Association (EBRA) Series crown continental champions, and the SVNS series crowns the world champions of rugby sevens, the sport's closest relative (World Rugby, n.d.-b). The Rugby Europe Beach Rugby Championship 2026 was held in Chișinău, Moldova, on August 22–23, 2026, with eight men's teams — Moldova beat Greece 7–5 in the final, with Bulgaria fourth — and a women's tournament alongside (Rugby Europe, n.d.). The EBRA Series 2026 included events at Costa Blanca, Spain, and The Hague, Netherlands (the North Sea event was cancelled because of extreme heat). The inaugural European Beach Five Rugby Championships were held in Moscow in 2017, the Rugby World Cup Sevens was discontinued after the 2022 Cape Town edition (won by Fiji in men's and Australia in women's), and the 2025–26 SVNS season concluded at Cape Town on December 5–6, 2026, where South Africa beat Argentina 21–19 (European Beach Rugby Association, n.d.). Beach rugby is the sand variant of Rugby Union, within the rugby-codes family — Rugby Sevens, Tag Rugby, Touch Rugby, Snow Rugby and others listed under Important Links.

Rules

Under the World Rugby beach rugby laws, each team fields five players on a sand pitch 50 m × 35 m, and matches last two halves of five to seven minutes (World Rugby, n.d.-b). A try is worth one point and there are no conversions; restarts are taken with tap kicks, kick-ins and free kicks, and there are no scrums or lineouts. The ball may be passed backwards or sideways, and a player is tackled by pulling one of two flags or, in flag variants, by tagging; after six tackles or a knock-on the other team gains possession. Men's and women's matches use the same laws and pitch, and youth tournaments shorten the halves; mixed-gender beach rugby is also played socially in some countries (World Rugby, n.d.-a).

History

Beach rugby grew out of rugby sevens in the late 20th century as a faster, sand-based variant suited to summer festivals and beach sports events; the European Beach Rugby Association (EBRA) was formed to run a European series, and the inaugural European Beach Five Rugby Championships were held in Moscow in 2017, with the women's tournament alongside the men's from the early editions (European Beach Rugby Association, n.d.). On the world stage, the Rugby World Cup Sevens ran from 1993 to 2022 — the 2022 Cape Town edition was won by Fiji (men) and Australia (women) before the tournament was discontinued — and since 2024 the SVNS series has crowned the world champions of sevens, with South Africa winning the 2025 Cape Town final 21–19 over Argentina and the 2025–26 season concluding in Cape Town in December 2026 (World Rugby, n.d.-b). Estonian players such as Karl-Henry Korjus, photographed at the Eesti Rannaragbi Festival, represent the strong beach rugby scene in the Baltic states, and South American beach rugby was showcased at the 2019 South American Beach Games in Rosario (Rugby Europe, n.d.).
