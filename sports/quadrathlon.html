Quadrathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Quadrathlon (also quadriathlon or tetrathlon) is an endurance multisport consisting of four disciplines completed in succession — swimming, kayaking, cycling and running — with the lowest overall time deciding the winner. It adds kayaking to triathlon for a balanced test of upper and lower body. The sport is governed internationally by the World Quadrathlon Federation (WQF), which stages world championships and a World Cup series, and in Britain by the British Quadrathlon Association.

Rules

WQF events are held at several standard distances, from sprint (roughly 750 m swim, 4 km kayak, 20 km bike, 5 km run) to middle and long (up to 4 km swim, 20 km kayak, 100 km bike and 21-25 km run), always starting with the swim and ending with the run, with the kayak and bike order variable. Competitors race without outside assistance — no drafting on the bike beyond permitted gaps, no flippers or snorkels in the swim — and the kayaks/canoes must be non-mechanical and weigh over 8 kg. Race numbers must be visible in the run, upper body covered, and gear is deposited in the designated transition area; penalties range from warnings to disqualification. At world championship level, athletes race as individuals or as national-team relays, and winter variants replace the swim and run with cross-country skiing and snowshoeing.

History

After failed attempts to launch a four-sport formula in the early 1980s, the Italian Sergio Ferrero staged the first quadrathlon in 1987 on Ibiza under the name "Diamond Man" — 5 km open-water swim, 20 km kayak, 100 km bike and 21.1 km run, won in just under seven hours by Australia's Philip Gabel — and founded the World Quadrathlon Federation in 1990. The European Quadrathlon Federation followed in 1997 under the Czech official Vaclav Marek. The WQF World Cup has run since 2001 as a multi-country series in which each athlete's best four races count, and world championships are staged at sprint and middle distances; Germany, Great Britain and the Czech Republic have produced many of the champions, with Stefan Teichert and Lisa Maria Hirschfelder of Germany among the most decorated athletes of recent decades.
