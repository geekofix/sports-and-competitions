Air Racing

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Air racing is the motorsport of the skies: aircraft race at low altitude around pylons or marked circuits at speeds from about 250 km/h in sport classes to well over 500 km/h in unlimited warbirds. Internationally the discipline is sanctioned by the FAI (World Air Sports Federation), which defines the racing classes and ratifies records; the best-known standalone meetings are the National Championship Air Races in the United States and the former Red Bull Air Race series (FAI, n.d.). At the 2026 National Championship Air Races in Roswell, New Mexico (September 16–20, 2026 — the first full seven-class field since 2022), Steven Hinton of Chino, California, won Unlimited Gold in the P-51D "Bardahl Special" at 432.897 mph, and Jason Googe won Jet Gold in the L-29 "Arcangel" at 495.908 mph, the fastest speed of the week (Reno Air Racing Association, n.d.). Women pilots race in all classes, and Reno's fields regularly include female Unlimited, Sport and Formula One pilots.

Rules

Race formats divide into pylon racing — timed laps around inflatable air gates, with penalties for cutting a pylon or making an incorrect pass — and cross-country or circuit time trials against the clock. Classes keep the racing close by limiting aircraft type and engine power: Formula One (small single-engine racers), Sport, Biplane, T-6 (military trainers), Jet (turbine-powered jets), STOL Drag and Unlimited (largely modified World War II fighters). At the National Championship Air Races, pilots first qualify, then race in heat brackets (Bronze, Silver, Gold), and the Gold heat decides the class championship. Pilots must hold FAI racing licences, fly the prescribed course profile and pass every gate within limits; dangerous flying or gate infringements bring time penalties or disqualification (FAI, n.d.; Reno Air Racing Association, n.d.). The standing closed-course speed records are 507.105 mph, set by Skip Holm in "Dago Red" in 2003, and 543.568 mph, set by Curt Brown in the L-29 "Viper" in 2009.

History

Air racing predates circuit motorsport: the Gordon Bennett Cup races from 1906 and the great interwar air tours made aviators national heroes. Pylon racing took root after World War II with surplus military aircraft, and the National Championship Air Races began in Reno, Nevada, in 1964, founded by Bill Stead, who was killed in a crash there on April 28, 1966; Reno hosted the event until 2023, when a T-6 collision killed pilots Nick Macy and Chris Rushing, and the races moved to Roswell, New Mexico, from 2025. The Red Bull Air Race brought a standardized pylon-slalom world championship from 2003 to 2019, touring world cities; after a 2022 revival attempt was abandoned, Yoshihide Muroya's AIR RACE X has run the series since 2023 in digital and live formats. A tragic turning point for spectator safety came in 2011, when the "Galloping Ghost" crashed at Reno, killing pilot Jimmy Leeward and ten spectators (FAI, n.d.; Reno Air Racing Association, n.d.).
