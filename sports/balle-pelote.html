Balle Pelote

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Balle pelote (Basque pelota) is a family of wall-and-court games in which players hit a ball — with the bare hand (hand pelota, the premier form), a wooden bat (pala), a leather glove (cesta / jai alai) or a racket (frontenis) — against a wall called the fronton. It is governed by the International Federation of Basque Pelota (FIPV), founded on May 19, 1929 in Buenos Aires, which sanctions the quadrennial Basque Pelota World Championships — the 20th edition was held in October 2026 in Buenos Aires, Argentina, and Colonia del Sacramento, Uruguay (October 15–24, 2026), with the 2022 Biarritz edition drawing a record 32 nations; women's events have been included since 1990, and the 2026 edition added frontball and adapted pelota (International Federation of Basque Pelota [FIPV], n.d.-a). Hand pelota is played on a 36 m fronton (trinquet) or 54 m fronton, to 22 or 40 points, with serves that must land in the service box and a volley or after one bounce; professional serves regularly exceed 100 km/h (FIPV, n.d.-b) (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.-a) (International Federation of Basque Pelota, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under FIPV rules, hand pelota (pelota mano) on a 36 m trinquet is played to 22 points by two players or two pairs; the serve must be struck from the service square and land in the service box, and after the serve the ball may be played on the volley or after one bounce. A point is won when the ball bounces twice, is hit out of bounds, or the opponent fails to return it. The front wall (frontis) and left wall are in play; the right wall and back wall are out. Matches at the World Championships are to 22 points in trinquet and 40 in fronton (FIPV, n.d.-b).

Other modalities have their own rules: pala (wooden bat) on a 36 m court, cesta punta (the long curved glove of jai alai) on a 54 m fronton to 40 points, chistera (shorter glove), frontenis (rubber ball with a racket on a 30 m court, one wall) and paleta goma (rubber ball). In all forms, men and women compete under the same rules and court dimensions — the women's world championships have run since 1990, and the 2026 edition in Buenos Aires included women's hand pelota, pala and cesta events (FIPV, n.d.-a).

Age classes include youth (under 18) categories at world championships, and adapted pelota for athletes with disabilities was added to the 2026 program. Equipment rules specify ball weights by modality — the hand pelota ball is a leather-covered core of about 100 g — and protective gear such as hand taping is mandatory in hand pelota.

History

Basque pelota descends from the jeu de paume, the medieval European court game played in walled courts, which was brought to the Basque Country and evolved into the direct-wall game. Hand pelota was codified in the 19th century in the Basque Country and Navarre; the first frontons were built in the mid-1800s, and the professional game spread to France (Paris's Chiquito de Cambo trinquet dates to 1893) and to the Americas (FIPV, n.d.-b).

Pelota was an Olympic sport at Paris 1900 (hand pelota, men's) and a demonstration sport in 1968 (Mexico City) and 1992 (Barcelona). The FIPV was founded in Buenos Aires in 1929 by Basque and Argentine delegates, and the first World Championships were held in 1952 in San Sebastián; they have been quadrennial since. Jai alai, the cesta-punta variant, became hugely popular in Florida and Connecticut in the early 20th century, with frontons in Miami (Jai-Alai Fronton, 1926) and Dania Beach. Women's world championships began in 1990, and legendary players include the Spanish hand-pelota champions of the 1980s–2000s and the current stars of the 2026 Buenos Aires worlds (FIPV, n.d.-a).
