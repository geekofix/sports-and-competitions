Balle Pelote

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Balle pelote is the French name for hand pelota (pelota mano), the bare-hand variant of Basque pelota played against a wall (fronton). The ball can also be struck with a leather glove (gant) or a curved basket (chistera) in other modalities. Internationally the sport is governed by the Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque (FIPV), which organizes the Basque Pelota World Championships.

Rules

Under FIPV regulations, hand pelota is played on a 36 m fronton (or in a trinquet), in singles or pairs, to 22 or 40 points depending on the venue. A point ("tanto") is scored when the opposing side fails to return the ball before its second bounce, hits it out of bounds, or lets it hit the wall without reaching the required distance. The serve must be struck so the ball lands beyond the service line and bounces first in the service zone. Players may let the ball bounce once or hit it on the volley ("à la volée"), and in the bare-hand version the ball may reach speeds over 100 km/h.

History

Hand pelota descends from jeu de paume, the medieval ancestor of tennis, and was played for centuries in the Basque Country, Navarre and Béarn, where nearly every village had a fronton. The FIPV was founded on 19 May 1929 in Buenos Aires by the French, Spanish and Argentine federations, and the first Basque Pelota World Championships were held in 1952. Pelota was contested at the 1900 Paris Olympics and appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1968 and 1992 Games.
