Vert Skateboarding

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Vert (vertical) skateboarding is performed on a large U-shaped halfpipe ramp — typically about 12–14 ft (3.7–4.3 m) tall with vertical walls and platforms — where riders pump for speed and launch aerials above the coping (X Games, n.d.). Unlike street and park, vert is not an Olympic discipline; its biggest stages are the X Games and the World Skate vert world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The 2026 Vert Skateboarding World Championships in Asunción saw the USA's JD Sanchez and Brazil's Gui Khury pull away in the run section, Khury sealing it with a final-run heater that included 900 variations (World Skate, n.d.). At the X Games, Tony Hawk's 900 at X Games V on 27 June 1999 remains the most famous vert moment ever, and Khury became the first to land a 1080 on a vert ramp in competition at the 2021 X Games at age 12 — the youngest X Games gold medalist in history (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Women are at the forefront of vert's modern era: Arisa Trew became the first female skateboarder to land a 900 (29 May 2024), Ema Kawakami was the youngest ever to land a 900 (age 7, August 2022) and the first woman to land a 1080 in competition (X Games 2025), and Sky Brown was the youngest X Games medalist ever with park bronze at age 11 in 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Rules

A vert ramp is a halfpipe with ~12 ft (3.7 m) walls, 2 ft (0.6 m) vertical extensions (vert) and 8 ft (2.4 m) platforms; riders drop in from the platform, pump through the flat bottom and gain height for aerials above the coping (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In competition, each skater gets three 45-second runs (best counts) plus a best-trick contest; five judges score runs 0–100 on difficulty, height, variety, execution and use of the ramp (X Games, n.d.; World Skate, n.d.).

Tricks are aerial rotations and grabs — 540s, 720s, 900s, 1080s — plus lip tricks on the coping. The 900 (two-and-a-half mid-air revolutions) was first landed in competition by Tony Hawk in 1999; the 1080 (three full revolutions) was first landed on a mega ramp by Tom Schaar (2012) and on a vert ramp by Gui Khury (2020) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's events are separate but scored identically (World Skate, n.d.).

History

Vert skating began in the empty swimming pools of 1970s drought-hit Southern California; the first commercial halfpipes appeared in 1977–78 and the X Games (ESPN, since 1995) made vert its centerpiece (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tony Hawk landed the first 900 at X Games V on 27 June 1999 after ten failed attempts — "the best day of my life" — a trick that stood as the benchmark for 21 years (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

The next generation broke it: Tom Schaar landed the first 1080 on a mega ramp in 2012 at age 12, and Brazil's Gui Khury landed the first 1080 on a standard vert ramp in May 2020 (age 11) and in competition at the 2021 X Games (age 12), becoming the youngest X Games gold medalist ever; Arisa Trew landed the first female 900 in May 2024 and Ema Kawakami the first female 1080 in competition at the 2025 X Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d). Brighton Zeuner (2017) and Sky Brown (2019, age 11) were earlier record-young X Games medalists in park (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
