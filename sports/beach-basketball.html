Beach Basketball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Beach basketball is basketball played on sand courts, usually in the 3x3 or 4x4 formats derived from FIBA 3x3 basketball, and is governed internationally by International Basketball Federation (FIBA), which also runs dedicated beach basketball competitions (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; International Basketball Federation, n.d.-b). The underlying 3x3 format has been an Olympic sport since Tokyo 2020 — added to the programme on 9 June 2017 — with the USA women and Latvia men winning gold in Tokyo and Germany's women (including Marie Reichert) and the Netherlands' men taking gold at Paris 2024; the field expands to 12 teams per gender at Los Angeles 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

At the top of the current international calendar, the women's side is led by Olympic champions Germany and world-number-one nations in the FIBA 3x3 ranking, while the 10th FIBA 3x3 World Cup was held in Warsaw, Poland, from 1 to 7 June 2026 with 40 national teams (20 men's, 20 women's) at Parade Square, double as LA28 qualification via the FIBA ranking (Olympics.com, 2026; International Basketball Federation, n.d.-b).

Rules

Beach basketball follows the FIBA 3x3 rules on a sand court: the playing area is 15 × 11 m around a single basket with a backstop, each team fields three players plus one substitute, and the official ball is the 28.5-inch outdoor Wilson 3x3 ball (International Basketball Federation, n.d.-a; International Basketball Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

A game lasts one period of 10 minutes or ends when a team reaches 21 points (overtime: first to lead by 2); the shot clock is 12 seconds, baskets inside the arc count 1 point and beyond the 6.75 m arc 2 points, free throws 1 point; after every score the defending team must clear the ball behind the arc and the offense checks the ball with the referee; team fouls 7–9 give two free throws and from the 10th also possession, but no player is fouled out; the game starts with a coin toss (International Basketball Federation, n.d.-a; International Basketball Federation, n.d.-c). Men's and women's tournaments are separate at every level, from grassroots beach events to the Olympic Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

3x3 basketball grew out of streetball and was formally launched by FIBA in 2010, with the first FIBA 3x3 World Cup in Athens in 2012 and annual editions since; the IOC added it to the Olympic programme on 9 June 2017, and it debuted at Tokyo 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Beach basketball as such developed in coastal communities — the Philippines' beach courts are among the most photographed — and FIBA organises beach basketball world-level events within the 3x3 family, while independent bodies such as FIMBA run beach-specific circuits; the 2026 FIBA 3x3 World Cup in Warsaw marked the 10th edition and fed the LA28 qualification race (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; International Basketball Federation, n.d.-b; Olympics.com, 2026).
