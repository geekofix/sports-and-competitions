Mountaineering

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mountaineering (alpinism) is the set of outdoor activities involved in ascending mountains: rock and ice climbing, glacier travel, ski mountaineering, high-altitude trekking and via ferratas. It combines athletic performance with exploration and demands self-reliance, technical skill and mountain safety. The international governing body is the Union internationale des associations d'alpinisme (UIAA), also known as the International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation, which is recognized by the International Olympic Committee. Related competitive disciplines include sport climbing, rock climbing and ice climbing.

Rules

Pure mountaineering is non-competitive: there are no fixed rules, and success is measured by reaching a summit safely under one's own power. What is regulated internationally are the safety standards: the UIAA Safety Label, created in 1960 and internationally approved in 1965, certifies equipment such as ropes, harnesses, helmets, ice screws and carabiners against UIAA safety standards. The UIAA also defines the international climbing grade scale (UIAA grades), runs the Ice Climbing World Cup and World Championships, oversees skyrunning and publishes the Mountain Ethics Declaration (2009), a code of sportsmanship and environmental respect. Olympic recognition of climbing (1995 for the UIAA) covers the competitive disciplines rather than expeditions.

History

The first ascent of Mont Blanc on 8 August 1786 by Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard, encouraged by the reward offered by scientist Horace-Bénédict de Saussure, is widely considered the birth of modern mountaineering. The ascent of the Wetterhorn in 1854 by Sir Alfred Wills inaugurated the Golden Age of Alpinism (about 1854-1865), when most major Alpine peaks were first climbed, largely by British climbers with local guides. The Alpine Club, founded in London in 1857, was the world's first mountaineering club. The UIAA was founded in August 1932 in Chamonix by 20 national mountaineering associations and became IOC-recognized in 1995. On 29 May 1953 Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay made the first confirmed ascent of Mount Everest during a British expedition led by John Hunt.
