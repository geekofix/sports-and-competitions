Novuss

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Novuss is a Baltic tabletop billiards game often described as "sea billiards", played on a square wooden board about 100 cm to a side with a pocket in each of the four corners (International Novuss Federation, n.d.). Two players or teams compete with 16 small discs in two colors, aiming to sink all eight of their own discs in the corner pockets while blocking the opponent, using small pucks as strikers and cue sticks to shoot them (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game originated among seamen in the Baltic region in the 1920s and is today the national game of Latvia, governed internationally by the International Novuss Federation (FINSO); women and men compete in separate categories at the International Novuss Championship (International Novuss Federation, n.d.). Related regional variants include the Estonian koroona and the Finnish korona, and the game is related to carrom and billiards.

Rules

The board is a square wooden table, roughly 100 by 100 cm, with a circular pocket in each corner (International Novuss Federation, n.d.). Each player starts with eight discs of one color placed anywhere on the board; the striker pucks and cue sticks are placed at the edges (Wikipedia, n.d.). Players take turns using a cue stick to flick a striker puck across the board, attempting to knock their own discs into a corner pocket while sending the opponent's discs away from the pockets (International Novuss Federation, n.d.). A disc that lands in a pocket is removed from the board, and a striker puck that ends in a pocket is also out of play for that turn and must be replaced (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first player to sink all eight of their own discs wins the game; matches at championship level are played as a set number of games, and the player with the most game wins takes the match (International Novuss Federation, n.d.). The official rules are published by FINSO and apply at the International Novuss Championship, held annually since 1993, with men's and women's singles and team events under identical rules (International Novuss Federation, n.d.).

History

Novuss spread in the Baltic Sea region in the 1920s, when seamen from England introduced the game to ports in Latvia, Estonia and Finland, where it developed into the Latvian novuss, the Estonian koroona and the Finnish korona (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first professional competition was held in Riga, Latvia, in 1932, organized by Albert Raminsch, and the Latvian Novuss Federation was founded on 6 December 1963, making novuss an organized competitive sport with national championships (Latvian Novuss Federation, n.d.). The International Novuss Federation (FINSO) coordinates the international development of the game, and the International Novuss Championship has been held annually since 1993 (International Novuss Federation, n.d.). Today novuss is played recreationally and competitively across the Baltic states and beyond, with clubs in several European countries and North America, and it remains a symbol of Latvian national identity (Wikipedia, n.d.).
