Cow Chip Throwing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cow chip throwing is an American novelty contest in which competitors throw dried cow pats ("cow chips") for distance. The World Championship Cow Chip Throwing Contest has been held in Beaver, Oklahoma, since 1969, on the third Saturday of April at the Beaver County Fairgrounds as part of the Cimarron Territory Celebration. Each contestant throws two chips, and only chips more than 6 inches (15 cm) in diameter are allowed; divisions exist for women, men, VIPs and teams.

Rules

Contestants throw their chips from behind a foul line, and the farthest point reached counts; if a chip breaks in mid-air, the farthest piece is measured. Each competitor gets two chips and the better of the two distances is recorded. The event is judged by officials on the fairgrounds, and the winners of each division receive trophies. Throwing is from a standstill with both feet behind the line, similar to other throwing contests at county fairs.

History

The Beaver contest began in 1969 and became the recognized world championship of cow chip throwing, drawing competitors and spectators to the small Oklahoma town every April. It is part of the wider Cimarron Territory Celebration, which also features a parade, chili cook-off and other festivities. Similar chip- or pat-throwing contests exist at other US fairs, but Beaver remains the best-known, covered regularly by regional newspapers and magazines.
