Biathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Biathlon combines cross-country skiing with rifle shooting: athletes ski a set distance, then shoot at targets from prone and standing positions, with penalty loops added for missed targets. It is governed by the International Biathlon Union (IBU) and has been an Olympic sport since Squaw Valley 1960. The flagship events are the IBU World Championships and the World Cup circuit.

Rules

Under IBU rules, the individual race is 20 km for men and 15 km for women, with four shooting bouts (two prone, two standing) at a 50 m range; each missed target adds a 150 m penalty loop. The sprint is 10 km (7.5 km women) with two shootings. The relay runs 4 x 7.5 km with two shootings per leg and up to eight spare rounds. At the World Championships, the mass start (15 km / 12.5 km, five shootings) and mixed relays are also contested. Targets are 4.5 cm (prone) and 11.5 cm (standing) in diameter at 50 m.

History

Biathlon descends from military ski-and-shoot competitions in Scandinavia; the first known contest was held in Norway in 1767. It was a demonstration sport at the first Winter Olympics in 1924 and became a full medal sport in 1960. The IBU was founded in 1993 in London when biathlon separated from the UIPM. Dominant nations include Norway, Germany, France and Russia; legends include Ole Einar Bjørndalen (NOR), the most decorated Winter Olympian of all time, and Magdalena Neuner (GER).
