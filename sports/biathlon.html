Biathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Biathlon combines cross-country skiing with rifle shooting and is governed by the International Biathlon Union (International Biathlon Union [IBU], n.d.-b), founded in 1993; it has been on the Winter Olympic programme since Squaw Valley 1960 for men and Albertville 1992 for women (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, held February 8–21, 2026 at the Anterselva Biathlon Arena (Südtirol Arena Alto Adige) in Rasen-Antholz, South Tyrol, Italy, 11 events were staged: Norway's Maren Kirkeeide won the women's sprint and completed a full medal set at the Games, Italy's Lisa Vittozzi won the women's 10 km pursuit — Italy's first Olympic biathlon gold — Sweden's Martin Ponsiluoma won the men's pursuit, Sweden's first biathlon Olympic medal, France's Quentin Fillon Maillet won the men's sprint in 22:53.1 for his fourth career Olympic gold (five golds and nine medals in total make him the most decorated French Olympian), and Norway's Johan-Olav Botn won the men's individual ahead of France's Éric Perrot and Norway's Sturla Holm Lægreid; France won the mixed relay on February 8, 2026 with Julia Simon collecting her third gold of the Games, ahead of Italy and Germany (IBU, n.d.-a). Also at the 2026 Games, France's Lou Jeanmonnot won four medals including two golds and Océane Michelon won two golds (IBU, n.d.-a) (International Biathlon Union, n.d.-a) (International Biathlon Union, n.d.-b).

Rules

In biathlon, athletes ski a set distance with a .22-calibre rifle on their back and stop at the shooting range to fire at five targets 50 m away; each missed target costs a 150 m penalty loop. The shooting positions alternate between prone and standing: the prone target is 4.5 cm in diameter and the standing target 11.5 cm (IBU, n.d.-b). The Olympic and World Cup programme includes five disciplines for each gender: individual (20 km men, 15 km women, four shootings), sprint (10 km men, 7.5 km women, two shootings), pursuit (12.5 km men, 10 km women, four shootings), mass start (15 km men, 12.5 km women, four shootings) and relay (4 × 7.5 km men, 4 × 6 km women), plus a mixed relay of two women and two men over 4 × 6 km. Skiing is done in the skating (freestyle) technique on groomed courses, and the rifle must weigh at least 3.5 kg with a five-shot magazine. Men's and women's events differ only in distance, not in format, and junior and youth categories use shorter courses and lighter rifles (IBU, n.d.-b).

History

Biathlon descends from Scandinavian military patrol skiing races of the 18th and 19th centuries, in which armed soldiers raced over snow and fired at targets; it was a demonstration sport at early Winter Olympics and entered the Olympic programme at Squaw Valley 1960, where Sweden's Adolf Wiklund won the first individual gold (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.). The International Biathlon Union was founded in 1993, taking the sport out of the UIPM, and the World Cup series has run since the early 1980s. The most iconic athlete is Norway's Ole Einar Bjørndalen, "the King of Biathlon", with eight Olympic gold medals and the record for the most Winter Olympic medals ever; on the women's side, Germany's Magdalena Neuner won two Olympic golds at Vancouver 2010, and the women's Olympic programme began at Albertville 1992 (IBU, n.d.-a).
