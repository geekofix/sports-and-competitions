Billiards

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Billiards is the umbrella term for cue sports played on a cloth-covered table: pocket billiards (pool) such as 8-ball, 9-ball and 10-ball, carom billiards such as three-cushion, and snooker. Worldwide governance is shared by the World Confederation of Billiard Sports (World Confederation of Billiard Sports [WCBS], n.d.-a), which groups the three world federations: the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA) for pool, the Union Mondiale de Billard (UMB) for carom, and World Snooker (WST) for snooker (World Confederation of Billiard Sports, n.d.-a) (World Confederation of Billiard Sports, n.d.-b) (World Snooker, n.d.-a) (World Snooker, n.d.-b).

The 2026 World Snooker Championship at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield (April 18–May 4, 2026) was the 50th consecutive edition at the venue; Wu Yize of China beat Shaun Murphy 18–17 in the final to become the second Chinese world champion after Zhao Xintong in 2025 and the second-youngest winner in Crucible history, with a record 258 centuries and a £500,000 first prize from a £2,395,000 purse (World Snooker [WST], n.d.-a). In carom, Eddy Merckx of Belgium won his third UMB three-cushion world title at the 78th World Championship in Blois, France (September 23–27, 2026), beating Phuong Vinh Bao of Vietnam 50–39 (WCBS, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the Women's World Snooker Championship crowns its own champion annually — England's Reanne Evans holds a record seven titles and Thailand's Mink Nutcharut won in 2022 (WST, n.d.-b).

Rules

Snooker is played on a 12 ft × 6 ft (3.57 × 1.78 m) table with 22 balls: 15 reds (1 point each) and six colours (yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7). Players alternate potting a red and a colour until the reds are gone, then the colours must be potted in order; the highest score wins the frame, and matches are best-of-frames (WST, n.d.-a). Pool (8-ball, 9-ball, 10-ball) is played on a 9 ft × 4.5 ft table with numbered balls pocketed in order or, in 8-ball, in solids/stripes groups before the 8; a scratch or foul gives ball-in-hand to the opponent (WCBS, n.d.-a). Three-cushion carom is played without pockets on a 10 ft × 5 ft table: the cue ball must contact both object balls, with at least one cushion hit before the second object ball; matches are played to a set number of points (usually 40 or 50) or within a time limit (WCBS, n.d.-b). Gender and age differences are mainly organizational: men's and women's events are held separately at world championships, and junior and senior categories differ in match length and, in carom, target scores.

History

Cue sports descend from 15th-century European ground-billiards games; the modern indoor table with cushions emerged in the 1820s–1830s, and snooker was codified in 1884 by British Army officers in India under Sir Neville Chamberlain. Pool's 8-ball and 9-ball formats were standardized in the United States in the 19th and early 20th centuries, and carom billiards (including three-cushion) spread from France and Belgium in the 1800s. Iconic figures include snooker's Joe Davis (15 world titles, 1927–1946) and Steve Davis, pool's Willie Mosconi and Earl Strickland, and carom's Raymond Ceulemans and Torbjörn Blomdahl. The women's game grew from the 1970s onward, with Reanne Evans becoming the most successful women's snooker player with seven world titles between 1976 and 2012 (WST, n.d.-b).
