Mas-Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mas-wrestling (Yakut: mas tard'yhyy, "stick pulling") is the traditional ethnosport of the Sakha Republic (Yakutia) in Siberia, in which two athletes sit on a wooden board facing each other, brace their feet against a support and grip a wooden stick (the mas); the first to pull the opponent so that his buttocks leave the board — or to make him touch the ground — loses the bout (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Bouts are best-of-three, contested in weight classes and an absolute category, and the sport is governed internationally by the International Mas-Wrestling Federation (IMWF), which organizes the World Mas-Wrestling Championship — the VIII World Championship among men and women, plus the absolute-weight event, is scheduled for 26–30 November 2026 in Kazan, Russia (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.). Women compete on equal terms: the world championships have included women's divisions, and the 2026 North American Open in Calgary featured women's events (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.).

Rules

The bout takes place on a board about 80 cm long set on the ground with a support block between the athletes' feet; both sit with legs extended, soles of the feet pressed against the support, and both hands gripping the mas, a smooth wooden stick roughly 30–40 cm long (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). When the referee gives the signal, both pull; the bout is lost by the athlete whose buttocks lift off the board or who touches the ground with any part of the body, and the winner is the one who completes the pull (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.). Matches are best of three bouts, with a time limit per bout (traditionally up to 2–3 minutes; a draw can be called), and competitors are grouped by body weight — typically up to 60, 70, 80, 90 kg and over, plus an absolute category — at world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The competition area is a flat surface with a marked center line, and fouls such as slipping the grip to one hand or moving the feet off the support are penalized (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.). Men's and women's tournaments use identical equipment, bout format and weight classes, and women have held their own world championship since the divisions were formalized (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.).

History

Mas-wrestling is a traditional Yakut sport with roots in the Ysyakh summer festival, where it was long practiced as a test of strength at gatherings; it was codified into a competitive sport in the mid-20th century with weight classes, a board and the mas stick, and it became one of the republic's signature ethnosports alongside khapsagai and Yakut jumping (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Mas-Wrestling Federation was established to govern the sport internationally, holding its congress in Moscow in November 2015 and organizing the World Championship series — the VIII edition in Kazan in November 2026 marks the sport's continued globalization, with federations now active in Europe, North America and Asia (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.). Women's divisions were added to the world championships, making mas-wrestling one of the rare traditional strength sports with full international women's competition (International Mas-Wrestling Federation, n.d.).
