Clout Archery

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Clout archery is a traditional long-distance form of archery in which archers shoot at a flag — "the clout" — planted in the ground at a set distance and score by how close each arrow lands to the flag pole; an arrow embedded in the pole scores the maximum (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is one of the oldest competitive archery disciplines in Britain, run under Part 7 of the Archery GB Rules of Shooting, and it belongs to the archery family alongside target, field and flight archery (Archery GB, n.d.).

Figure: Mr Barndoor (talk). (2007-10-20 11:29:27). Archers collecting arrows in the scoring zones at a clout shoot [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Clout_Archery_Collecting_Arrows.jpg

In the Archery GB version, a single clout round is three dozen arrows in ends of six, and major shoots are double clout rounds of six dozen; flags are set at defined distances by age and gender — for example 180 yd (160 m) for senior men and 140 yd (130 m) for senior women in the imperial round, with corresponding metric distances (Archery GB, n.d.). Scoring zones are measured from the flag pole: 18 in (45 cm) scores 5 points, 3 ft (0.9 m) scores 4, 6 ft 3, 9 ft 2 and 12 ft (3.5 m) 1 point, anything wider scores zero (Wikipedia, n.d.). The National Clout Championships are the highlight of the British clout calendar and carry UK record and tassel status (Archery GB, n.d.).

Rules

Shooting may be "one way" (single line and single set of flags) or "two way" (two lines and two sets of flags at opposite ends, archers turning around after each end); a minimum overshoot zone is required behind the flags for safety (Wikipedia, n.d.). The flag is a 12-inch (30 cm) square of coloured fabric on a short softwood pole, set as close to the ground as practical (Wikipedia, n.d.). Arrows are collected in each zone, sorted into sets, and each archer calls their own scores in descending order — an honour-based system that has defined the sport for centuries (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women shoot the same round format, only at different flag distances; junior and age-group distances step down in defined increments (Archery GB, n.d.).

History

Clout shooting is first documented in England in the 14th century and was a favourite of the yeoman archers; by the 17th century it was a formal part of archery society meetings (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Grand National Archery Society (today Archery GB, founded 1861 as the governing body; the Royal Toxophilites date to 1781 and the Woodmen of Arden to 1785) has run national clout championships since the 19th century, and the tassel — a small silk emblem — is still awarded for a perfect double clout (Archery GB, n.d.). Irish and American clout traditions grew from the same English root, and today clout is shot competitively across the UK, Ireland, the United States, Canada and Australia (Wikipedia, n.d.).
