BMX Freestyle

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

BMX freestyle is the acrobatic branch of BMX cycling, performed on 20-inch BMX bikes in skateparks (park discipline) or on flat ground (flatland). It is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) and has been an Olympic discipline since Beijing 2020; the Paris 2024 park competition at Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines was won by Argentina's José "Kato" Torres Gil in the men's event — Spain's first individual Olympic cycling medal — and by China's Deng Yawen in the women's event (Union Cycliste Internationale [UCI], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). The 2026 season was marked by the cancellation of the UCI Urban Cycling World Championships planned for Saudi Arabia in October 2026, after which the UCI BMX Freestyle World Cup series remained the top international circuit (UCI, n.d.-b) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-a) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-b).

Among the riders who shape the sport today, the women come first: China's Deng Yawen, the 2024 Olympic park champion, and the USA's Hannah Roberts, the 2023 world champion and long-time world No. 1 who was the favorite in Paris but finished eighth after crashes. On the men's side, José Torres Gil, Britain's Kieran Reilly (2023 world champion) and France's Anthony Jeanjean lead the rankings (UCI, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the UCI BMX Freestyle Park rules, each rider gets two 60-second runs in major finals, and judges award a score from 0 to 100 based on difficulty, execution, height, variety and flow of the tricks; the better of the two runs counts. The park course consists of ramps, quarter pipes, bowls, spines and rails, and riders must use the whole course — hiding in one corner scores poorly (UCI, n.d.-a). Qualification rounds use two runs as well, with the top eight or so advancing to the final.

Flatland is judged similarly — two 60-second runs on a flat paved area, with tricks such as scuffs, steamrollers, hang-5s and boomerangs scored on difficulty and execution. At the Olympic Games and UCI World Championships, men and women compete under identical rules and scoring; there are no equipment differences beyond the bike, which must be a 20-inch BMX with a functioning rear brake (the front brake is optional and often removed for park).

Age and classification differences: UCI runs Junior (17–18) and elite categories, and the Youth Olympic Games feature park events; para-cycling does not include BMX freestyle, but adaptive flatland-style riding exists in community programs. Riders must wear a full-face helmet (mandatory in park at all UCI events), and knee/elbow pads are required at the Olympics and World Championships (UCI, n.d.-a).

History

BMX itself was born in Southern California in 1971, when kids began racing Schwinn Sting-Ray bicycles on dirt tracks, inspired by the motocross film "On Any Sunday" (1971). Freestyle emerged in the late 1970s in empty swimming pools and skateparks, pioneered by riders such as Ron Wilkerson and the KENS crew; flatland evolved separately in Japan and Europe in the 1980s, with flatland specialists like York Uno and Martti Kuoppa becoming legends (UCI, n.d.-b).

The UCI integrated BMX freestyle into its calendar in the 2010s, launching the UCI BMX Freestyle World Cup in 2016 and the first UCI BMX Freestyle World Championships in 2017 (winners: Logan Martin and Hannah Roberts). BMX freestyle made its Olympic debut at Tokyo 2020, won by Australia's Logan Martin (men) and Britain's Charlotte Worthington (women), and was repeated in Paris 2024 (UCI, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). Women have been part of the competitive scene since the 1980s; ahead of her 2024 Olympic gold, China's Deng Yawen had already won silver at Tokyo 2020 as an 18-year-old, and the USA's Hannah Roberts became the first woman to land a 360 tailwhip in competition.
