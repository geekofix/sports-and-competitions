Kumis Alu

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kumis alu — "picking up silver" (Kazakh kümis alu), also known in its Kazakh form as tenge ilu or tyiyn enmei — is a traditional Central Asian equestrian game in which a rider gallops along a track and, leaning down from the saddle at full speed, picks up coins or small silver objects laid out on the ground (World Ethnosport, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It has been part of the World Nomad Games programme since the games were founded in 2014 and was again contested at the 2026 World Nomad Games in Kyrgyzstan (31 August – 6 September 2026), where the hippodrome events in Cholpon-Ata included the equestrian picking-up games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In modern competition the course is a 120 m platform with five objects placed at 10 m intervals in small rags or shallow holes, and the winner is the rider who collects the most objects in the fastest time (World Ethnosport, n.d.-a). Traditionally a male contest rooted in warrior training, kumis alu is now increasingly open to women riders, and mixed fields compete at national festivals and the World Nomad Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In June 2024 the International Tenge Ilu Federation was established in Kazakhstan to promote the game internationally, and the national championship was held in Kokshetau (The Astana Times, 2024).

Rules

The rider starts behind the start line and gallops along the 120 m course; at each of the five stations — spaced every 10 m — a coin or small sack (traditionally silver pieces wrapped in cloth) lies in a shallow hole or rag on the ground, and the rider must lean from the galloping horse and pick it up without slowing down or touching the ground with a hand (World Ethnosport, n.d.-a). Points are awarded for each object collected, and the final result combines the number of objects with the time taken; dropping an object or leaving the marked lane costs points under the World Nomad Games regulations (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Equipment is the horse with standard rider's gear and the five competition objects; there are no weight classes, and men and women compete in the same events, with separate junior categories at the World Nomad Games (World Ethnosport, n.d.-a). Rule sources: World Nomad Games regulations (https://worldnomadgames.org/) and World Ethnosport, https://worldethnosport.org/tenge-ilu (World Ethnosport, n.d.-a). See also kyz kuu on this site.

History

Picking up objects from the ground at a gallop was a training exercise of the nomadic warriors of the Kazakh and Kyrgyz steppe, who used it to develop the balance, speed and one-handed skill needed in battle and in hunting, where a fallen enemy's belongings or spoils were picked up on the ride (World Ethnosport, n.d.-a). The game was also staged at weddings and festivals, where a rider who picked up jewelry could gift it to a chosen person as a gesture of goodwill (The Astana Times, 2024). Kumis alu became an international competition discipline when it was included in the programme of the first World Nomad Games in Cholpon-Ata, Kyrgyzstan, in 2014, and it has been contested at every edition since, including Astana 2024 and the 2026 games in Kyrgyzstan (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Tenge Ilu Federation of Kazakhstan has promoted the sport since the 2010s, and the International Tenge Ilu Federation was founded in June 2024 (The Astana Times, 2024).
