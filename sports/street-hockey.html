Street Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Street hockey (also called ball hockey or road hockey) is the off-ice form of ice hockey played on pavement, asphalt or sport courts with a ball or purpose-made street-hockey puck, on foot or on inline skates. From driveway shootouts to world championships, it brings the fastest team game in the world to anyone with sticks, a net or two and a stretch of pavement, and it is played in organized leagues from North America to Europe and Asia.

Rules

Games follow ice hockey's positional logic "+E+" forwards, defensemen and a goaltender, with body contact and puck-handling rules adapted for the surface "+E+" but running is forbidden on foot (players stride or jog), checking is limited or banned in most leagues, and the ball or dedicated street puck replaces the vulcanized rubber puck. Rink sizes are flexible: official ball-hockey leagues play on sport court or asphalt bowls with boards, while informal games use shoes, cans or chalk for goals. League play typically runs two or three periods of running time with penalty shot calls for fouls; equipment rules require gloves and shin protection and encourage fullface protection for goaltenders, and international play is governed by federations such as the International Street and Ball Hockey Federation, which stages world championships in men's, women's and junior divisions.

History

Street hockey grew from the pickup tradition of Canadian kids playing ice hockey's skills on driveways and rinks all summer "+E+" the game immortalized in street, road and pond hockey scenes across Canada's cities "+E+" as the natural off-season form of a winter sport. The automobile-era game formalized during the 1970s with painted lines, rules adapted for dry pavement, and organized leagues in Montreal and Toronto that pioneered the sport's equipment (the first plastic orange ball sticks and blade designs). From there it spread through Czech, Slovak and other European leagues, producing elite international competition: the ISBHF has run world championships since 1996, and the sport's heartlands remain Canada, the Czech Republic and Slovakia, alongside a thriving inline-hockey cousin played on skates in rinks worldwide.
