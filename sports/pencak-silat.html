Pencak Silat

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Pencak silat is an umbrella term for the traditional martial arts of the Indonesian archipelago and the Malay world, encompassing hundreds of regional styles (aliran) with empty-hand techniques, grappling, throws and traditional weapons such as the stick (toya), machete (golok) and dagger (keris). Since 1980 the sport has been organized internationally by the International Pencak Silat Federation (PERSILAT), founded in Jakarta on 11 March 1980, which has more than five million members across dozens of national federations. Pencak silat has been a regular sport at the Southeast Asian (SEA) Games and was included in the Asian Games for the first time in 2018. PERSILAT distinguishes four aspects of the art: self-defense (bela diri), sport (olah raga), artistic forms (seni) and mental-spiritual development.

Rules

In international competition, PERSILAT sanctions four categories: tanding (full-contact sparring) and three artistic categories—tunggal (solo forms), ganda (doubles) and regu (group). Competition is governed by the International Pencak Silat Competition Rules and Regulations issued by PERSILAT. Tanding bouts are fought by athletes in protective gear, weighted in classes of roughly 5 kg increments, with points awarded for clean strikes, sweeps and takedowns; attacks to the spine, throat and groin are prohibited. Artistic competitors perform jurus (formal movement sequences) before a jury that scores precision, power, flow and fidelity to tradition. International events such as the SEA Games apply the same PERSILAT rulebook, with one athlete per weight category and per seni event.

History

Structured pencak silat traces its earliest organized roots to the Minangkabau region of West Sumatra in the 6th century, and over the centuries regional schools developed distinctive styles tied to local traditions. In 1948 the Indonesian Pencak Silat Association (IPSI) was founded in Solo and the unified term "pencak silat" was adopted for the national art. The international federation PERSILAT followed in Jakarta on 11 March 1980, founded jointly with Malaysian, Singaporean and Bruneian federations. Pencak silat entered the SEA Games in 1987 and the Asian Games in 2018, and In 2019 the traditions of pencak silat were inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Indonesia has dominated the sport's international championships, which PERSILAT stages every two years for juniors and seniors.
