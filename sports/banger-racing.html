Banger Racing

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Banger racing is a British oval-track stock-car formula in which drivers deliberately ram each other — the last car running wins — on short asphalt or shale ovals (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the contact stock-car family with figure-8 racing and demolition derby.

Figure: Mattbull1. (2019-10-05 20:02:50). Unlimited National Banger World Final at Foxhall Stadium, Ipswich [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Unlimited_National_Banger_World_Final_-_Foxhall_Stadium_-_Ipswich.jpg

The premier UK series is BriSCA F1 Stock Cars, whose highlight is the Unlimited National Banger World Final at Foxhall Stadium, Ipswich (photo above); other classes include F2 and Ministox (Wikipedia, n.d.). Races are points-scored over heats, semi-finals and a final, with points for finishing position and for "contact made" in some formulas (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Cars are aging production saloons (Ford Granada, Volvo 240/740 etc.) stripped to a roll cage, with armoured windows and radiators moved inside; drivers wear helmets and neck braces, and fireproof suits in top classes (Wikipedia, n.d.). Rules: no deliberate T-boning or reversing into cars, and a car that stops is out after a count-down (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — ladies’ classes exist in several series.

History

Banger racing grew out of the 1960s British "folkstock" and contact racing scene, with BriSCA founded in 1954 to standardize the formula; the World Final at Foxhall became the biggest event (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport also spread to the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany and even Russia (Tolyatti, photo) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
