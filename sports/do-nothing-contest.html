Do-nothing Contest

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The do-nothing contest, internationally known as the Space-Out Competition, is a contest in which participants compete to do absolutely nothing for 90 minutes without falling asleep, talking, laughing, checking a phone or getting up (BBC Newsround, 2020). It was created in Seoul in 2014 by the South Korean visual artist Woopsyang (Kim Sunae) as an ironic protest against the country's hyper-competitive work culture (Wikipedia, n.d.). Contestants sit in a public space while wrist heart-rate monitors record their pulse every 15 minutes; the participant whose heart rate stays the most stable — combined with audience votes — wins a trophy modeled on Auguste Rodin's sculpture The Thinker (The Independent, 2024; Times of India, 2024). Editions have been held in Seoul, Beijing, Taipei, Tokyo, Hong Kong and Rotterdam. The 2025 Seoul edition was won by 36-year-old Byung-jin Park, who outlasted roughly 100 competitors, and the 2026 Seoul edition was won by 35-year-old freelance announcer Kwon So-a (Times of India, 2024; The Independent, 2024) — the sections below cover rules, history and more.

Women and men compete on equal terms in the do-nothing contest.

Rules

Contestants sit on chairs in a public space and must do nothing for 90 minutes: no sleeping, no talking, no laughing out loud, no phone use and no standing up or wandering around (BBC Newsround, 2020). Heart rate is measured every 15 minutes, and at the end the competitor with the most stable heart rate — the smallest drop from the starting measurement — is ranked first, with audience applause votes breaking close ties (The Independent, 2024). Anyone who falls asleep, laughs, talks or disturbs other participants is disqualified, so the winning strategy is radical calm. There are no age or nationality restrictions, spectators watch in silence, and the winner receives the Thinker-shaped trophy (Times of India, 2024). Organizers in each host city apply the same 90-minute format with local adaptations approved by the original Seoul organizers (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Woopsyang organized the first Space-Out Competition in Seoul in 2014 with 36 participants, and it quickly became an international media phenomenon because it inverted the logic of achievement culture: the prize was doing nothing at all (BBC Newsround, 2020). The format spread within a few years to other cities — editions were held in Tokyo in 2023 and in Hong Kong in 2024, each organized with local artists — and the contest has been covered by outlets such as the BBC, The Independent and the Times of India, which describe it both as performance art and as a meditation on modern stress (The Independent, 2024; Times of India, 2024). Recent Seoul editions drew more than 100 contestants each; the 2025 winner was Byung-jin Park and the 2026 winner was Kwon So-a (The Independent, 2024).
