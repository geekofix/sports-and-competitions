Do-nothing Contest

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The do-nothing contest, internationally known as the Space-Out Competition, is a competition in which participants compete to do absolutely nothing for the longest time without falling asleep. It was created in 2014 by the South Korean visual artist Woopsyang (Kim Sunae) as an ironic protest against South Korea's hyper-competitive society. The winner is the contestant with the most stable heart rate over the 90-minute event.

Rules

Contestants sit in a public space and do nothing for 90 minutes while their heart rate is measured every 15 minutes. The person whose heart rate drops the least and remains the most stable wins. Anyone who falls asleep, laughs out loud or disturbs others is disqualified, so staying calm and relaxed is the winning strategy. There are no age or nationality restrictions, and spectators watch silently. Branches of the competition have been held in Seoul, Beijing, Rotterdam, Taipei, Hong Kong and Tokyo.

History

Woopsyang organized the first Space-Out Competition in Seoul in 2014 with 36 participants, and it quickly became an international media phenomenon because it inverted the logic of achievement culture: the "prize" was doing nothing at all. The format spread to other cities within a few years, including a 2023 edition in Tokyo and a 2024 edition in Hong Kong, each organized with local artists. The competition has been covered by outlets such as the BBC, The Independent and the Times of India, and it is often described as both a performance art piece and a meditation on modern stress.
