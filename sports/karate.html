Karate

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Karate is a striking martial art that originated on the island of Okinawa, where indigenous fighting methods (te) merged with Chinese kung fu, and was brought to mainland Japan in 1922 by Gichin Funakoshi, who founded the Shotokan school in Tokyo. Today it is practiced worldwide as a sport, a self-defense system and a character-building discipline, with two competition disciplines: kata (pre-arranged forms) and kumite (sparring). The World Karate Federation (WKF), founded in 1970, governs the sport internationally, and karate made its Olympic debut at Tokyo 2020.

Rules

Under WKF competition rules, kumite is a timed sparring match (three minutes at senior level) in which competitors score with punches and kicks to legal target areas: a punch to the trunk is yuko (1 point), a kick to the trunk is waza-ari (2 points), and a kick to the head or a throw is ippon (3 points). Matches are decided by the first competitor to lead by eight points or by the higher score at the buzzer; electronic scoring with sensors supports the four corner judges, and repeated or dangerous contact brings penalties up to hansoku (disqualification). Kata competitors perform a form from the official WKF kata list before a panel of judges who award scores, with the highest and lowest dropped. Competitions are divided into age classes and weight categories. See karate kata and karate kumite for the individual disciplines.

History

Karate's roots lie in Okinawa's te, influenced for centuries by Chinese martial arts brought by traders and envoys. In 1901 Anko Itosu introduced karate into Okinawan schools, and in 1922 Gichin Funakoshi demonstrated it in Tokyo and opened the Shotokan dojo; major styles followed — Goju-ryu (Chojun Miyagi), Shito-ryu (Kenwa Mabuni) and Wado-ryu (Hironori Otsuka). The World Union of Karate-do Organizations was founded in 1970 with Jacques Delcourt as its first president and became the World Karate Federation. Karate was included in the Olympic program for Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024. Legendary figures include Funakoshi, Masutatsu Oyama (founder of Kyokushin) and Olympic medalists such as Sandra Sánchez of Spain.
