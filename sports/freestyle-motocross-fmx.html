Freestyle Motocross (FMX)

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freestyle motocross (FMX) is the acrobatic discipline of motocross: riders perform aerial stunts — backflips, whip variations, nac-nacs — off dirt ramps in front of judges or crowds (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the motocross family with supercross and endurocross.

Figure: Carlos Delgado. (2013-10-06 09:29:23). Maikel Melero mid-trick at the Freestyle GP, Spain 2013 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Maikel_Melero_-_Exhibici%C3%B3n_Freestyle_GP_Cami%C3%B3n_de_Espa%C3%B1a_2013_-_01.jpg

Competitions (X Games, Nitro Circus, Red Bull X-Fighters, FIM FMX World Championship) run qualification plus final rounds; each rider gets 1–2 runs of 60–90 seconds with 5–8 tricks, judged on difficulty, execution, variety and style (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Safety: full-face helmet, neck brace, chest protector, boots; ramps are built to FIM standards with landing ramps matched to take-off (Wikipedia, n.d.). Bikes are 250/450 cc motocross machines with strengthened frames and modified suspension (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — the X Games added a women’s FMX event, and the FIM FMX World Championship has women’s categories.

History

FMX began in the 1990s when motocross riders at shows like the X Games (started 1995) began throwing acrobatic tricks off double ramps; Carey Hart popularized backflips on a dirt bike in the late 1990s–2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Stars like Travis Pastrana, Mat Rebeaud, Taka Nakagami and Australian star Josh Brookes — and on the women’s side, Vicki Golden and others — pushed the sport to double-corks and full-send tricks (Wikipedia, n.d.). Maikel Melero of Spain (photo) is a multiple X Games medalist.
