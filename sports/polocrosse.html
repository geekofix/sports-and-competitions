Polocrosse

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Polocrosse is a mounted team sport that combines elements of polo and lacrosse, played outdoors with a racquet — a stick with a loose, thread net — in which a soft rubber ball is caught, carried and thrown. It is often called "the one horse sport" because each rider may use only one horse for a match. Teams have six players divided into two sections of three, and the game was developed in Australia in the late 1930s. The world body is the International Polocrosse Council, and the Polocrosse World Cup has been held every four years since 2003.

Rules

The pitch is a level field 160 yards long and 60 yards wide, divided into three zones: a goal-scoring area 30 yards deep at each end and a center area. Only the designated attacker (No. 1) may enter the goal-scoring area to score, the center (No. 2) must stay in the middle zone, and the defender (No. 3) plays in their team's defensive area; goals are scored only from within the scoring area, between posts eight feet apart. Matches consist of chukkas of six to eight minutes, usually four per team per match at club level and up to eight in championship play, with the two sections of each team playing alternate chukkas. The ball, of sponge rubber about 100 mm in diameter, is picked up, carried, bounced and passed with the racquet, and the game starts with the referee throwing the ball up. Players may not take the ball into another zone while carrying it, must not carry the racquet across the horse, may not ride through a goal melee, and dangerous play, such as striking down on an opponent's racquet, is penalized.

History

The sport was created in Australia in 1938-39 by Edward and Marjory Hirst of Sydney, after they read a British magazine article about a "polo crosse" riding-school exercise and enlisted the help of the Sydney riding instructor Alf Pitty to design a new game that would hone the riding-school pupils' skills over the winter. The first organized demonstration took place at Ingleburn near Sydney in 1939, and the game spread through rural New South Wales and then across Australia, with state associations forming in the 1940s and 1950s. Overseas, polocrosse took root in South Africa and Rhodesia in the late 1940s, then in New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and beyond; the International Polocrosse Council was formed in Queensland on 21 June 1976 by the five founding countries, and it now groups full members including Australia, Ireland, New Zealand, South Africa, the UK, the USA, Zambia and Zimbabwe, with affiliate members in Europe, North America and Asia. The Polocrosse World Cup ran first in 2003 and is held every four years, with Australia and South Africa the leading nations and the United States Pony Clubs among the strongest youth programs.
