Tang Soo Do

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tang soo do (the way of the Tang hand) is the Korean martial art preserving the pre-unification Korean karate tradition: striking techniques from Okinawan and Japanese roots blended with Chinese influences, practiced through hyeong forms, one-step sparring and free sparring rather than the sport orientation of Olympic taekwondo. Founded in the Moo Duk Kwan tradition of Hwang Kee, it flourishes through independent federations worldwide.

Rules

Training follows the classical martial arts structure: fundamental strikes, blocks and the art's signature kicks; hyeong (forms, including the pyung ahn series shared with karate's pinan lineage plus the Bassai and other traditional sets); one-step and self-defence sparring; free sparring in controlled contact; and breaking. Ranks advance through geup (colored belt) grades to dan (black belt, with the Moo Duk Kwan tradition's midnight blue belts), and tournaments contest forms, sparring, breaking and weapons divisions under federation rules styled on traditional semi-contact. Compared with WT-style taekwondo, tang soo do retains hand-technique emphasis, deeper stances and a self-defence-oriented curriculum, and practitioners typically wear a plain white dobok unlike the WT v-neck jacket.

History

Tang soo do emerged in Korea after the Second World War, when the original kwans taught a self-defence art under the old name (the Korean reading of the characters historically read karate-do), and Hwang Kee's Moo Duk Kwan became its most famous vehicle at the height of its extent, when a large share of Korean martial artists practiced under the banner. When most kwans unified into taekwondo in the 1950s and 1960s, Hwang Kee declined to join the sportification process: he registered his art as soo bahk do in 1960 with a claim to ancient Korean lineage, and the worldwide family of schools keeping the tang soo do name preserved the older system. Through diaspora masters and founders like Chuck Norris (a famous practitioner who spread Korean karate in the West), tang soo do built a global network of federations, and its schools continue to uphold the traditional Korean karate that predates Olympic-style taekwondo.
