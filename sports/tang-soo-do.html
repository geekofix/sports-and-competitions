Tang Soo Do

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tang Soo Do ("Way of the Tang Hand") is a Korean martial art rooted in karate and older Chinese arts; its school, Moo Duk Kwan ("school of martial virtue"), was opened by Hwang Kee on 9 November 1945 in Seoul and renamed Soo Bahk Do on 30 June 1960 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Internationally the style is organized by the World Tang Soo Do Association (WTSDA), founded by Grandmaster Jae C. Shin at a charter convention on 13–14 November 1982 in Philadelphia, with headquarters in Burlington, North Carolina, and more than 100,000 members in 36 countries; the US Tang Soo Do Association dates to 1968 (World Tang Soo Do Association, n.d.). Women and men train and compete side by side — forms (hyung), one-step sparring, free sparring and breaking are open to both genders in the same age and rank categories at WTSDA championships (World Tang Soo Do Association, n.d.).

Rules

WTSDA competition follows the association's official rules: individual and team hyung (forms) from the Pyong Ahn (1–5) and Naihanchi series, one-step sparring, point-style free sparring and board breaking, with competitors grouped by age (children, juniors, adults, seniors) and rank (gup color belts and dan black belts) (World Tang Soo Do Association, n.d.). Men's and women's divisions compete in the same events under identical scoring, and team events mix genders at club level (World Tang Soo Do Association, n.d.). The official rule source is the WTSDA rulebook (World Tang Soo Do Association, n.d.).

History

Hwang Kee (1914–2002) studied the "Tang method" under Chinese masters in Manchuria from 1936, opened Moo Duk Kwan in Seoul on 9 November 1945 after Korea's liberation, withdrew from the kwan unification that created taekwondo in 1958, and renamed his art Soo Bahk Do on 30 June 1960; after his death in 2002 the Moo Duk Kwan is led by his son Hwang Hyun-chul (b. 1947) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Grandmaster Jae C. Shin (1936–2012) founded the WTSDA in Philadelphia in 1982, and the art's most famous practitioner is Chuck Norris, who earned his black belt under Shin (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Tang Soo Do Association, n.d.). The foundational hyung date from the Giecho Hyung (1947) to the Pyong Ahn series (1982) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
