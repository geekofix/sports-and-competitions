Guts Frisbee

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Guts (Guts Frisbee) is the oldest organized disc sport: two five-player teams stand about 15 yards (14 m) apart and hurl a flying disc at each other, scoring when the defending team fails to catch a legal throw with one clean hand (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is the paramount event of the International Frisbee Tournament (IFT), the world's oldest frisbee organization, founded at Eagle Harbor, Michigan, in 1958 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Throws in elite guts regularly travel 60–70 mph (97–113 km/h) and have been clocked faster — around 88 mph — and the fastest recorded throw with a one-handed catch was 83.3 mph (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The receiving line may "bobble" or tip the disc to keep it alive, which frequently involves several defenders in a scramble (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Women have competed at the IFT since the 1960s, when the tournament already documented women's participation alongside uniformed teams, and guts is played competitively by mixed and women's teams today; the WFDF runs a World Ultimate and Guts Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Within the disc sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Freestyle Frisbee, Ultimate, Disc Golf, Ultimate Tazer Ball, Disc Dog; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Teams of five line up arm's length apart along their goal line; a disc flip decides who throws first. The thrower raises an arm and the defenders freeze; a legal throw must cross the defenders' line top-side-up, within their extended arm's reach, not vertical or past vertical, and not into the ground first (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The defending team must catch the disc cleanly with one hand — trapping it against the body or using both hands does not count — but players may bobble (tip) the disc repeatedly to slow it before completing the catch (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring: the throwing team scores a point if a legal throw is not caught; the defending team scores a point if the throw is illegal (out of the scoring area, over their heads, vertical, or into the dirt); a clean catch means no point and the teams switch roles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Games are played to 21 points and must be won by at least two (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's, women's and mixed teams compete at the IFT and in WFDF-sanctioned events.

History

Guts was created by the Healy family — brothers James (Tim), John (Jake), Robert (Boots) and Peter (Beka) — after Rich Warden brought a Pluto Platter disc home from Dartmouth and spun discs on Eagle Harbor beach; the first International Frisbee Tournament was held in Eagle Harbor, Michigan, in 1958, with guts as the main event (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Official rules were written for the 12th IFT in 1969, the same year Michigan proclaimed 6 July "Frisbee Day". In the 1970s the sport was televised on ABC's Wide World of Sports and the 1975 World Frisbee Championships at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena drew some 40,000 spectators; Wham-O introduced guts to Japan and Taiwan in the 1970s, where it became more popular than in the US (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The IFT continues at Eagle Harbor — the 61st edition was played in 2018 — while the WFDF sanctions the modern World Ultimate and Guts Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
