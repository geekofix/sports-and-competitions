Kin-Ball

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kin-Ball is a team sport invented in 1986 in Quebec, Canada, by physical education professor Mario Demers; the Omnikin company was founded in 1985 and published the official regulations in August 1987 (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Three teams of four players compete simultaneously on a 20 × 20 m court with a giant ball 1.22 m (48 in) in diameter weighing about 1 kg (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The attacking team designates one of the other two teams by shouting "Omnikin!" followed by its colour — black, grey or blue — and hits the ball with a body part above the hips while all four teammates touch it; if the designated team fails to control the ball before it touches the ground, it loses a point and the other two teams each gain one (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match is played over three periods of about 7–15 minutes (typically around 10); when the first team reaches 9 points in a period, the lowest-scoring team leaves the court and the remaining two play on until one reaches 11 points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Kin-Ball Federation (IKBF), created in 2000 and headquartered in Lévis, Quebec, coordinates more than 30 member countries and claims 3.8 million participants, and runs World Championships with separate men's and women's tournaments — Canada, Japan and France have dominated both (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the official IKBF rulebook, the court measures 20 × 20 m (lines included) and the ball is 1.2 m in diameter and about 1 kg; team colours are black, grey and blue (pink is sometimes used instead of blue) (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). To attack, three players hold the ball while the fourth — the server — strikes it with any body part above the hips, and all four attackers must be touching the ball at the moment of impact; before the hit the server must announce "Omnikin!" plus the colour of the team being attacked, within a referee-controlled window (a 10-second team discussion followed by 5 seconds to strike) (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-a). If the designated defending team controls the ball before it touches the ground, it becomes the attacking team; if it fails, it loses one point and each of the other two teams gains one point (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Fouls — including failing to catch the ball before it hits the ground, hitting it out of bounds, walking with the ball after the third touch, hitting downward, hitting from under 1.8 m, not all players touching the ball, a wrong colour call, more than one defender within 1.8 m of the hit, and intentional interference — each award one point to the other two teams (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A period ends when the leading team reaches 9 points, the trailing team is eliminated, and the remaining two teams play until 11 points; the match is the best of three periods (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is played by mixed, men's and all-women teams — all-women teams are common in schools and at the World Championships — and age classes exist for youth, junior and senior players (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rule source: International Kin-Ball Federation, https://kin-ball.com/ (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-a).

History

Kin-Ball was created in 1986 by Mario Demers, a physical education professor in Quebec, who sought a game in which several teams could play at once and every player would stay involved; the Omnikin company was founded in 1985 and the official regulations were published in August 1987 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game spread through schools in Quebec and then across Canada and the United States, reaching Japan and Europe — Belgium, France, Switzerland, Spain, Germany, Denmark, the Czech Republic, Malaysia, China and Hong Kong — and the International Kin-Ball Federation (IKBF) was created in 2000 to coordinate the national federations, with its headquarters in Lévis, Quebec (International Kin-Ball Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Regular World Championships have been held since the 1990s with separate men's and women's tournaments, dominated by teams from Quebec/Canada, Japan, France and Belgium (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the IKBF counts 3.8 million participants, primarily in the countries listed above, with Kin-Ball UK the newest national federation, formed in 2018 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
