Langdi

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Langdi is a traditional South Asian field sport of Maharashtra that combines tag and hopscotch: the chasing team's players hop on one leg and try to tag the defenders, who are out if they step over the line. Two sides of 12 players (plus three substitutes) play a 36-minute match of four 9-minute innings, alternating chasing and defending; the National Langdi Federation received national recognition in 2010, national men's and women's championships are held (the fourth in Chandigarh, May 2013), the first International Langadi Championship took place in Bhutan in May 2014, and Mumbai University introduced langdi at college level for female students in 2014.

Within the tag and chase games on this site, its closest relatives are kabaddi, circle-kabaddi and kho-kho; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The field is at most 18 × 18 m with a main playing square of about 10 m per side. The team winning the coin toss defends. Chasers must hop on one foot — right foot in their first chasing inning, left foot in the next — and may step outside the ground as long as they keep hopping; if a chaser touches the ground with the other foot, they leave the field and are replaced. Defenders are out if they step out of the ground or commit a line fault; when all defenders in the field are tagged, a new batch enters from the designated entry zone and the chaser must cross to the other half before tagging them. The team tagging the most defenders wins. Rules are standardized by the Akhil Maharashtra Sharirik Shikshan Mandal.

History

Langdi was originally played during the Pandiyan dynasty under the name nondiyaattam and is described by Marathis as a sport with a Marathi ethos; it is considered the foundation of many sports and useful training for kho kho, volleyball and gymnastics. The National Langdi Federation was recognized in 2010, Mumbai University introduced it at college level in 2014 (first for female students), and expatriate Indians have promoted it in countries such as Thailand.
