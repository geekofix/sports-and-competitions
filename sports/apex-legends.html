Apex Legends

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Apex Legends is Respawn Entertainment's free-to-play battle-royale hero shooter (trios, 60 players, ring-closing zones) published by EA; its global circuit is the Apex Legends Global Series (ALGS), which uses a match-point format: the first team to reach 50 points and then win a game takes the title (Wikipedia, n.d.) (Electronic Arts / Respawn, n.d.). The ALGS 2026 Championship (Year 5) was played 15–18 January 2026 at the Daiwa House PREMIST DOME in Sapporo, Japan, and was won by Oblivion — a clubless squad that qualified through the Last Chance Qualifier — with 86 points, clinching in game 9 of the finals and denying Team Falcons; Blinkzr was finals MVP (esports.gg, n.d.).

Figure: Respawn Entertainment. (February 4º, 2019). Apex Legends logo [Logo]. Wikimedia Commons. CC0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Apex_Legends_logo.png

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Fortnite, PUBG: Battlegrounds, Overwatch, Call of Duty, Halo and Valorant; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

ALGS rules: five regional Pro Leagues (NA, SA, EMEA, APAC N, APAC S) feed split playoffs and the championship; championship finals use the match-point format (50-point threshold plus a win); prize pools in the millions (the 2022 championship paid $500,000 to the winner of a $2M pool); rosters of three, with substitute rules; open to men and women alike — there are no gender divisions (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Apex Legends launched in February 2019 and won Best Multiplayer Game at The Game Awards 2019; the ALGS was announced the same year with regional championships in year 1 (2020: Kungarna NA, Scarz EMEA) and has run season-long splits since 2021–22; DarkZero won the 2022 championship in Raleigh, and Oblivion's 2026 Sapporo run made them the first LCQ team to lift the trophy (Wikipedia, n.d.; esports.gg, n.d.).
