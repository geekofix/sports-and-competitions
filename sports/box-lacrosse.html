Box Lacrosse

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Box lacrosse is the indoor, walled variant of lacrosse: two teams of six — five runners and a goaltender — play on a rectangular field surrounded by boards, using netted sticks (crosses) to catch, carry and throw a hard rubber ball into the opponent's small goal, with on-the-fly substitutions and a running clock. It is governed worldwide by World Lacrosse, which publishes the official box rule book and runs the World Lacrosse Box Championships (World Lacrosse [WL], n.d.-a). The premier professional league is the National Lacrosse League (NLL), whose 2025–26 season began on November 28, 2025 with 14 teams and ended on May 17, 2026, when the Toronto Rock defeated the Halifax Thunderbirds 2 games to 0 in the NLL Finals (12–7 in Halifax after 13–11 in Hamilton) to claim the franchise's seventh title, tying the Buffalo Bandits' record; rookie Sam English of the Rock was named Finals MVP (National Lacrosse League [NLL], n.d.). For the 2026–27 season, which starts on November 28, 2026, the league will play with 12 teams after the Ottawa and Philadelphia franchises folded (NLL, n.d.). The 2026 World Lacrosse Box Championships took place August 16–22, 2026, on the Onondaga Nation near Syracuse, New York — the first world box championship held on Indigenous lands — where the Akwesasne Thunder defeated the Seneca WarChiefs 11–4 in the final (WL, n.d.-b) (World Lacrosse, n.d.-a) (World Lacrosse, n.d.-b).

On the women's side, box lacrosse has a growing international and media profile: women's field lacrosse is a full World Lacrosse championship discipline, and in February 2026 Maki Jenner became the first woman to call play-by-play for an NLL game (February 14, 2026), a milestone for women in the sport's broadcast booth (NLL, n.d.).

Rules

Under the World Lacrosse box rule book (2026–2028 edition), box lacrosse is played 6 against 6 on a rectangular field of roughly 200 × 85 ft enclosed by boards 42 to 48 in high; goals are much smaller than in field lacrosse — 4 ft 9 in × 4 ft (1.45 × 1.20 m) in the NLL and 4 × 4 ft in the Canadian Lacrosse Association (CLA) — with a crease around each net (WL, n.d.-a). A game consists of four 15-minute quarters (the CLA plays three 20-minute periods); teams must advance the ball into the offensive half within 10 seconds (8 seconds in the NLL) and attempt a shot within the 30-second shot clock. Substitutions are on the fly at the bench, and play restarts quickly after goals and penalties.

Equipment: players wear a helmet with face mask, shoulder pads, arm pads and gloves; the stick consists of a shaft and a head with a strung pocket, used to catch, carry, pass, shoot and cradle the ball; only the goaltender may touch the ball with their hand inside the crease, and goalies use a larger head and extra padding. Scoring: one goal per ball fully crossing the goal line. Penalties are served in a penalty box — minor penalties last two minutes, major penalties five minutes; fighting is a five-minute major, not an automatic ejection, and a team may not enter the offensive half while short-handed. Overtime in the NLL is sudden-death; in international play, timed overtime periods decide ties (WL, n.d.-a).

Formats and classifications: professional box (NLL; CLA Senior A), senior international (World Lacrosse Box Championships for men's and women's national teams), junior (Minto Cup in Canada), youth and women's box variants, plus adaptive formats. Men's and women's international box lacrosse use the same rule book, with women's box played on a smaller field with smaller goals in some competitions; age-group events use modified contact rules (WL, n.d.-a) (NLL, n.d.).

History

Box lacrosse grew out of the Indigenous stick games of North America and was codified in Canada: the Mann Cup, the senior amateur box championship, adopted box rules in 1932, and the Minto Cup for juniors followed in 1937. The first professional indoor league, the original NLL, played in 1974–75; on March 13, 1986, Russ Cline and Chris Fritz launched the Eagle Pro Box Lacrosse League in the United States, which merged with the Major Indoor Lacrosse League (MILL, founded 1988) to become today's NLL under that name in the 1997–98 season (WL, n.d.-b). Icons of the modern game include Brodie Merrill, Jim Huelskamp and Jimmy Quinlan, whose scoring and playmaking defined the league's golden era, and the box game remains central to Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) culture — the 2026 world championship on Onondaga lands was a homecoming for the sport's Indigenous heartland (WL, n.d.-b).
