Box Lacrosse

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Box lacrosse (indoor lacrosse) is the enclosed, fast-paced form of lacrosse played on a hockey rink covered with artificial turf, surrounded by boards. It is governed internationally by World Lacrosse, which publishes the official Box Lacrosse Rule Book and sanctions the World Lacrosse Box Championships (held every four years since 2003). The top professional league is the National Lacrosse League (NLL) in North America.

Rules

Under the World Lacrosse Box Lacrosse Official Playing Rules (2026-2028 edition), teams field six players — five runners and one goalkeeper. A game has four 15-minute quarters with a stopped clock, face-offs at center to start each quarter and after each goal. There is a 30-second shot clock and a 10-second rule to advance the ball from the defensive half. The boards (minimum 3 ft high) keep the ball in play; live substitutions ("on the fly") are allowed. Body contact is regulated: illegal checks and crease violations cost possession or bring penalties. At the World Lacrosse Box Championships, national teams compete in a group stage and knockout rounds.

History

Box lacrosse was invented in Canada in the early 1920s-30s, attributed to Montreal field-lacrosse referee Paddy Brennan, and became the main form of the game on Native American reservations, where the Haudenosaunee compete internationally as a sovereign nation. The first World Indoor Lacrosse Championship (now World Lacrosse Box Championships) was held in 2003 in Ontario; Canada has won every edition. The Major Indoor Lacrosse League (MILL, founded 1986) became the NLL in 1997, now with 14 teams in the US and Canada.
