Gurning

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gurning is the British tradition of pulling grotesque faces — originally performed while wearing a horse collar (a "braffin") — as a competitive event. The World Gurning Championship is held annually at the Egremont Crab Fair in Egremont, Cumbria, England, a fair dating to 1267 when King Henry III granted it a royal charter.

Rules

At the Egremont World Gurning Championship, contestants place a horse collar around their head (gurning through the braffin), and judges score the most grotesque or entertaining facial distortion; the winner is decided by the judges (in some years by audience applause), and there are men's, women's and children's categories. No hands or tools may be used to distort the face — many contestants remove dentures to enhance the effect. The competition is the highlight of the Crab Fair, held each September, alongside the wheelbarrow race, pipe-smoking contest and ferret show.

History

The origins of gurning contests are unclear, but local tradition at Egremont claims great antiquity, and the Cumberland Paquet newspaper described it as an ancient custom in 1852. Anne Woods won the women's championship a record 28 times (her record was ratified by Guinness World Records in 2010), and Tommy Mattinson holds the men's record with 19 titles (ratified by Guinness in September 2024). Peter Jackman won four times from 1998 with his "Bela Lugosi" face. The tradition also survives in a similar "Mr Ugly" contest in Zimbabwe, and the word "gurn" is defined in the Oxford English Dictionary as to snarl or distort the countenance.
