Alpine Skiing Downhill

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Downhill (DH) is the fastest and most spectacular alpine skiing discipline: athletes make a single timed run down a steep, icy course marked with wide gates, reaching speeds above 130 km/h (81 mph) — and up to around 160 km/h on the legendary Stelvio course in Bormio, Italy. It is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS) and has been an Olympic event since the first Winter Games in Chamonix 1924 (International Ski and Snowboard Federation [FIS], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c).

Among the athletes who shape the discipline today, the leading women come first: the United States' Breezy Johnson won Olympic downhill gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 in 1:36.10 on the Olimpia delle Tofane in Cortina, and her compatriot Mikaela Shiffrin took the 2025/26 FIS World Cup overall title. On the men's side, Switzerland's Franjo von Allmen won Olympic gold on the Stelvio in Bormio in 1:51.61 at Milano-Cortina 2026, and his compatriot Marco Odermatt is the reigning World Cup overall champion; the 2026/27 World Cup season opened in Sölden on October 25, 2026, with Lucas Pinheiro Braathen (BRA) and Emma Aicher (GER) leading the early standings (FIS, n.d.-a; FIS, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the FIS International Competition Rules, each athlete gets one timed run in the downhill — after mandatory official training runs on the preceding days — and the fastest time wins; ties are possible (FIS, n.d.-c). The course must meet fixed vertical-drop ranges per category — at World Cup level, for example, 800–1100 m for men and 700–800 m for women — with wide-set gates and safety netting along the course. A skier must pass through every gate with both ski tips; missing a gate means immediate disqualification. Equipment must meet FIS specifications: minimum ski lengths and sidecut radii are prescribed, a FIS-approved helmet is mandatory, and speed suits must comply with air-permeability rules (FIS, n.d.-c). Men and women race under the identical rule set — the only differences are the vertical-drop ranges and the equipment minima. Related speed disciplines are Alpine Skiing Super-G and Alpine Skiing Giant Slalom on this site.

History

Downhill has been on the Olympic program since Chamonix 1924 and on the FIS Alpine World Ski Championships program since the first championships in 1931. The Stelvio course at Bormio — venue of the men's Olympic downhill at Milano-Cortina 2026 — hosted World Championships in 1985 and 2005. Legendary downhillers include Austria's Franz Klammer, Olympic champion at Innsbruck 1976, and Hermann Maier; on the women's side, the United States' Lindsey Vonn, with 82 World Cup victories, is the most successful downhiller of all time, and Mikaela Shiffrin has added Olympic and World Cup titles in the speed disciplines (FIS, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
