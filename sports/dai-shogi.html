Dai Shogi

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dai shogi (great shogi) is the 15x15 shogi variant of the Muromachi period with 96 piece types — the largest historical shogi, played from the 14th century until it was replaced by modern 9x9 shogi in the 16th century. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Shogi, Chu Shogi, Tenjiku Shogi; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Kobayashi Kiyochika, 1904).

Figure: 301fieriw. (2023-06-15). Dai dai shogi initial setup diagram [Diagram]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:DaiDaiShogi.png

Rules

15x15 board; 96 piece types with multi-step promotions; drops as in shogi; mate wins. A 1904 woodblock print shows a dai shogi game in the Russo-Japanese War context (Kobayashi Kiyochika, 1904).

History

Dai shogi was the prestige variant of the Muromachi and early Edo periods; the 1904 Kobayashi Kiyochika print satirizes the Russo-Japanese War with a dai shogi game. Modern display sets (e.g. the 2022 Austin set) reconstruct it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
