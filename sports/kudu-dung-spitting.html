Kudu Dung-spitting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kudu dung-spitting is a novelty competition in which contestants spit dried dung pellets of the kudu — a large antelope native to southern Africa — for maximum distance. It originated as a joke among farmers and hunters in South Africa and has become an organized championship event held in the Eastern Cape, where participants compete in categories for distance, and the sport attracts crowds and media attention as an eccentric but beloved part of South African rural festival culture (News24, n.d.).

Women and men compete on equal terms in kudu dung spitting.

Rules

There are no universal rules: at the organized championships, competitors take a dried kudu pellet, place it in the mouth, and spit it as far as possible from a marked throwing line, with the distance measured where the pellet first lands. Contestants usually get several attempts, and the longest spit counts; categories may be divided by age or sex, and some events also award prizes for style or accuracy onto a target. Because the pellets are dry, hard and lightweight, distances of more than ten meters are achievable with good technique.

History

The contest began as a humorous pastime at farms and hunting gatherings in South Africa, where kudu dung was always at hand and the idea of a spitting competition arose as a joke. It grew into a formal event with an annual world championship in the Eastern Cape, drawing local and international competitors and coverage from South African and international media. The event is a lighthearted celebration of rural life and has been featured by broadcasters and newspapers as an example of South Africa's quirky sporting traditions.
