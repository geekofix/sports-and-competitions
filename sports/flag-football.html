Flag Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Flag football is a non-contact variant of American football in which the ball carrier is stopped by pulling one of two flags attached to a belt, instead of tackling. It is governed internationally by the International Federation of American Football (IFAF), which runs the IFAF Flag Football World Championship (biennial, since 2012 for 5v5), and in the US organized by USA Football and NFL FLAG.

Rules

At IFAF World Championship level, teams field five players on a field about 70 yards x 25-30 yards (roughly 64 x 23-27 m) with end zones. Games are two 20-minute halves (running clock except final two minutes) or 12-minute quarters in some formats. The offense has four downs to cross midfield and four more to score a touchdown (6 points); a touchdown is scored by carrying or catching the ball in the end zone. After a touchdown, the try is from the 3-, 10- or 20-yard line for 1, 2 or 3 points. The quarterback cannot run with the ball past the line of scrimmage (in most rule sets). Defenders must wait out a set rush count (typically five seconds) before rushing the quarterback, and must pull one flag to stop the play; flag guarding (blocking with hands or lowering the hips to protect flags) and diving are penalties. Turnovers on downs, interceptions and fumbles (ball dead when it hits the ground) apply. Overtime uses a mini-game format.

History

Flag football originated in the 1940s on US military bases as a safer alternative to tackle football, and recreational leagues grew from the 1960s-90s (NFL FLAG launched in the 1990s, now the largest youth flag program in the US). The IFAF, founded in 1964, formalized international competition and held the first IFAF Flag Football World Championship in 2012 in Sweden (USA won the men's and women's titles); the 2024 edition was in Lahti, Finland. Flag football will make its Olympic debut at Los Angeles 2028 in a 5v5 format, which has accelerated global growth, with strong programs in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan, Denmark, Austria and Israel.
