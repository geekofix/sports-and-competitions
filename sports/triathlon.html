Triathlon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Triathlon is the multi-sport of swim, bike and run raced continuously: athletes complete an open-water (or lake/sea) swim, transition to a bike leg, then finish with a run, with transition zones counting time toward the total. From the Olympic distance (1.5-kilometre swim, 40-kilometre bike, 10-kilometre run) to sprint and long-course races including the Ironman distance, it is one of the world's fastest-growing participation and professional endurance sports, governed internationally by World Triathlon.

Rules

Race distances run the range: super-sprint, sprint (about 750 m/20 km/5 km), standard or Olympic distance, half and full Ironman (3.8 km swim, 180 km ride, marathon run) and longer ultra distances. Drafting rules split the elite racing into draft-legal (Olympic-style, bikes racing in packs) and non-drafting (age-group Ironman-style, athletes spaced and penalized for riding in packs) formats, and rules govern wetsuit legality (water temperature thresholds), transition conduct (no nudity or assistance, racked bikes, helmet rules), equipment specifications (triathlon bars, disc wheels in non-drafting) and penalties (time or stop-and-go) for cycling infringements, with race marshals and referees enforcing the federation rulebook at world cups, world championship series and age-group worlds.

History

Triathlon traces its modern birth to San Diego in 1974 (the first Mission Bay event founded by the Jack Johnstone-Don Shanahan era runners and swimmers of the local track club), with the sport's founding myth extending to France's 1920s multi-sport events; the Hawaii Ironman (born 1978 as a debate about which athletes were fittest) created the sport's legendary long-course distance, and the sprint-standard race formats internationalized through the 1980s. The International Triathlon Union (today World Triathlon) formed in 1989, the world championships began that decade, and triathlon debuted at the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games with the draft-legal format; the sport's modern era features a world championship series, mixed relay (an Olympic event since Tokyo 2020), the booming age-group Ironman 70.3 and long-course scenes, and stars whose all-around endurance fuels one of multisport's biggest communities.
