Pétanque

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Pétanque is a French boules sport in which players throw hollow metal balls from a stationary circle with both feet on the ground, aiming to place them closer to a small wooden target ball (the jack, or cochonnet) than their opponents; the name comes from the Provençal pès tancats, "feet planted" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIPJP — Fédération Internationale de Pétanque et Jeu Provençal, founded 8 March 1958 in Marseille — governs the game internationally with more than 600,000 licensed players in over 100 federations (FIPJP, n.d.). The 2026 FIPJP World Championships were held 20–24 November 2026 at Khon Kaen University in Thailand, with 48 countries contesting the 52nd men's triples and the 17th precision shooting title, while the 65th Mondial la Marseillaise à Pétanque in July 2026 set a participation record with 4,832 triplettes — the women's trophy (GP Paprec) went to the French team of Camille Picard, Nadège Baussian-Protat and Aurore Boriès (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women and men compete in separate world championship categories — triples féminines, doublettes féminines and mixed events — as well as side by side in open club play (FIPJP, n.d.).

Rules

Under the FIPJP Official Rules, play is from a throwing circle 50 cm in diameter (larger circles in some events) with both feet on the ground until the boule lands; the jack is thrown 6 to 10 metres from the circle (FIPJP, n.d.). Teams are singles (3 boules each), doubles (3 each) or triples (2 each); after all boules are thrown, the team holding the closest boule scores one point for each of its boules closer to the jack than the opponent's best, and the end continues from a new circle drawn where the jack finished; the first team to 13 points wins the game. Close situations are decided by measure; rules cover dead jacks, out-of-bounds boules and replaced circles. World championships are staged in men's, women's, youth and mixed categories plus precision shooting (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Pétanque was born in 1907 in La Ciotat near Marseille, when the former jeu provençal champion Jules Lenoir, crippled by rheumatism and unable to run, was accommodated with a shortened game thrown from a circle; Ernest and Joseph Pitiot formalized it and organized the first tournament in 1910, and the original ground survives as the Jules Lenoir Boulodrome (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport spread through Provence and all of France, helped by industrially cast metal boules — Obut was established in 1955 and became the global standard — and the FIPJP was founded in 1958 with the first Men's Triplet World Championship in 1959 at Spa, Belgium; pétanque has been on the World Games programme since 1985 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
