Wushu Sanda

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sanda (also called sanshou) is the full-contact fighting discipline of competitive wushu, in which two athletes exchange punches, kicks and throws on a raised platform until one is knocked down or outscored (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed worldwide by the International Wushu Federation (IWUF), founded on 3 October 1990 in Beijing during the 11th Asian Games and recognised by the International Olympic Committee in 2002 (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Sanda has been part of the Asian Games since 1990, and the IOC permitted the 2008 Beijing Wushu Tournament to run alongside the Olympic Games, with three men's and two women's sanshou events — women have contested world-championship sanda since 2003, and at the end of this section the current leading athletes are the reigning women's and men's world champions across the weight classes, crowned at the 17th World Wushu Championships in Brasília in 2025 (International Wushu Federation, n.d.).

Rules

A senior bout is fought on a leitai — an 8 × 8 m platform raised about 80 cm — over three rounds of two minutes with one-minute rests (Wikipedia, n.d.). Valid targets are the head, trunk and legs; punches and kicks score points, throws and takedowns score highest, and knockdowns or forcing an opponent off the platform earn extra points, so the fighter with the most points after three rounds wins — or the bout ends immediately on a knockout (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Illegal targets are the back of the head, the neck and the groin; elbow strikes, knee strikes to the head and attacking a downed opponent are fouls that bring warnings or disqualification (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitors wear headgear, a gumshield, chest and shin protectors and gloves, and are divided into weight classes with separate men's and women's divisions — the Beijing 2008 tournament used men's 56, 70 and 80 kg and women's 52 and 60 kg — while junior and children's categories apply modified rules (International Wushu Federation, n.d.).

History

Sanda's roots lie in centuries of Chinese bare-handed fighting, but modern competitive rules were codified in China in the mid-20th century, with protective gear and weight classes introduced as the sport spread internationally (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Wushu Federation was founded on 3 October 1990 in Beijing, and the first World Wushu Championships followed in 1991 in Beijing, held biennially since; the 16th edition took place in Fort Worth, Texas, in 2023 and the 17th in Brasília in 2025 (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Wushu debuted at the 1990 Asian Games in Beijing, and the IOC granted special permission for the Beijing 2008 Wushu Tournament, held 21–24 August 2008 at the Olympic Sports Center Gymnasium with 128 athletes from 43 countries — ten taolu and five sanshou events (Olympedia, n.d.). Wushu was a demonstration sport at the Nanjing 2014 Youth Olympics and becomes an official Youth Olympic sport at Dakar 2026 (International Wushu Federation, n.d.).
