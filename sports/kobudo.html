Kobudo

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kobudo (古武道, "old martial way" or "way of ancient weapons") is the weapons art of Okinawa, practiced alongside karate and preserved today chiefly as Ryukyu Kobudo. Its weapons grew out of farming and fishing tools after weapons were restricted on the islands: the bo (six-foot staff, kon), sai (trident), tonfa (rice-mill handle), nunchaku, kama (sickle), eku (boat oar), tekko (knuckle-dusters), tinbe-rochin (shield and spear) and surujin (weighted chain). The art was systematized in the 20th century by Taira Shinken, who compiled 42 kata and founded the Ryukyu Kobudo Hozon Shinko Kai in 1955.

Rules

Kobudo is practiced through kata — fixed forms with each weapon — and through paired applications; there are no points-based matches as in karate kumite, though some schools include light sparring with protective gear. Gradings (kyu/dan) are awarded by the school or federation, and international bodies such as the Okinawa Ken Kobudo Renmei and the World Budokan coordinate events. Practitioners train striking, blocking, trapping and joint-lock techniques with each weapon, and the bo is regarded as the "king" of the Okinawan weapons. Safety rules for training with live-edged or metal weapons (iaito-style blunt steel) are strict.

History

Weapon arts developed in the Ryukyu Islands from the 14th century, when the kingdom traded with China and Southeast Asia; after the Satsuma invasion of 1609 and repeated weapons bans, peasants and fishermen disguised their arms as everyday tools — the origin of the kobudo weapons. In the early 20th century, masters such as Yabiku Moden (who founded the Ryukyu Kobujutsu Research Association in 1911) and his student Taira Shinken (1897-1970) traveled the islands collecting and preserving weapon kata, founding preservation societies; after World War II, Taira established the Ryukyu Kobudo Hozon Shinko Kai in 1955. Matayoshi Shinpo developed the Matayoshi kobudo line. Today kobudo is practiced worldwide, with the first US demonstrations in Chicago in 1963.
