Bocce Raffa

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bocce raffa is the precision-throwing discipline of the bocce family in which the ball must be thrown so that it rolls along the ground (tiro di raffa) toward the pallino, or to strike an opponent's ball; it is the flagship discipline of the Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale (Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale [CBI], n.d.-a), founded in Chiasso, Switzerland, on September 3, 1983 by 13 nations. Recent 2026 highlights include the raffa events at the XX Mediterranean Games in Taranto (August 22–25, 2026, at Montemesola Sport Hall), the African Raffa Championship in Chlef, Algeria (July 11–18, 2026), and the European Juniors in Predazzo, Italy (September 21–27, 2026); the 16th Kemer Open in Antalya (November 6–10, 2025) was the last major international before the Brazil worlds (CBI, n.d.-a) (Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale, n.d.-a) (Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale, n.d.-b).

The women's side competes in senior and youth raffa at CBI and continental level — the African Raffa Championship 2026 included a women's section, and the European Juniors field girls' teams alongside boys' — so women play the identical game at every level (CBI, n.d.-b).

Rules

Raffa is played on a court about 27.5 m long and 4 m wide, surrounded by boards; each player or team has a set number of balls (typically four per player in team events), synthetic resin balls 106–107 mm in diameter and 900–920 g in weight, and a much smaller wooden pallino (CBI, n.d.-a). The ball is thrown one-handed from behind the throwing circle with a running approach; a legal raffa throw must land and then roll on the ground at least once toward the pallino, and the player may apply spin ("spock"). Scoring follows bocce convention: the closest ball to the pallino scores one point per ball closer than the opponent's best, and sets are played to 15 points (or a time limit) in international team matches. Fouls include crossing the throwing line, touching the pallino, and unsportsmanlike conduct; men's and women's events use identical rules, equipment and scoring, while youth (U18/U19) categories use shorter sets and slightly lighter balls (CBI, n.d.-b).

History

Raffa developed in northern Italy in the 19th century as a variant of bocce with a running, rolling throw; the CBI was founded in Chiasso in 1983 and has organized world championships since, with the 2022 joint raffa/volo worlds in Mersin, Turkey, gathering 52 teams from 37 countries — the largest bocce world championship to date (CBI, n.d.-a). The 2009 Bevagna world championship reunited raffa and volo at a single event after years of separate editions. The women's game has grown through continental championships and the youth pipeline, with girls' teams now standard at European juniors (CBI, n.d.-b).
