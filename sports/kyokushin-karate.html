Kyokushin Karate

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kyokushin karate is the full-contact karate style founded by Masutatsu Oyama (1923–1994), characterized by knockdown fighting: powerful kicks and punches to the body, kicks to the head, and no punches to the head (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed internationally by the International Karate Organization (IKO) Kyokushinkaikan, established in April 1964 in Ikebukuro, Tokyo, which runs the World Open Karate Championships every four years — the first in Tokyo in 1975, the 13th on 17–19 November 2023 with 166 competitors from 35 countries (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete in all weight classes at every level under the same knockdown rules, with a mandatory chest protector added to the standard groin and shin protection, and women's bouts have been part of the World Open since the 1990s; at divisional championships, female fighters such as the multiple world medalists from Japan and Brazil headline the draws (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a). Kyokushin's knockdown rules also influenced kickboxing: Oyama's students founded the Seidokaikan school in 1993, which led to K-1 kickboxing (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Kyokushin competition is knockdown karate: a senior bout lasts three minutes (two in some finals formats) on an 8 × 8 m mat, and is won by ippon — a clean knockdown, meaning the opponent is down and cannot resume within the count, or any technique that incapacitates — or by waza-ari advantage, with two waza-ari equal to one ippon (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a). Full-contact strikes are allowed to the trunk and legs and kicks to the head, but punches and strikes to the head are forbidden; there is no clinching beyond a brief grab, and the referee (shushin) stops the bout for fouls or knockdowns (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The World Open Championships are openweight for men and women; divisional and national championships use weight classes, and junior (U18) and cadet divisions apply modified contact and time rules (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a). Women wear a chest protector in addition to the mandatory groin protector and shin guards, and otherwise compete under identical rules to men (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a). Rule source: International Karate Organization (IKO) competition rules, https://www.kyokushinkaikan.org/ (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a). See also karate and kickboxing on this site.

History

Masutatsu Oyama, born Choi Young-eui on 27 July 1923 in Gimje, Korea, moved to Japan in 1938, trained under Gichin Funakoshi and later under Go Kenki (Neichu) in China, and opened his Oyama Dojo in Mejiro, Tokyo, in 1953 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). He founded the International Karate Organization (IKO) Kyokushinkaikan in April 1964 in Ikebukuro and organized the first World Open Karate Championship in Tokyo in 1975, held every four years since (International Karate Organization, n.d.-a). Oyama completed the legendary 100-man kumite (100 consecutive full-contact bouts) in 1953; later completers included Steve Arneil, Shokei Matsui and Francisco Filho (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Oyama died on 26 April 1994; the IKO later split into several organizations, including the Shinkyokushinkai (WKO) founded in the 1990s, while the original IKO continues the World Open (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 1993 Oyama's students established Seidokaikan, the school that with Kazuyoshi Ishii produced K-1 kickboxing (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
