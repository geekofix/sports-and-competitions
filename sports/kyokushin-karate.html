Kyokushin Karate

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Kyokushin ("ultimate truth") is a style of full-contact karate founded by Masutatsu Oyama (Choi Young-eui, 1923-1994), who opened his first dojo in Tokyo in 1953 and named the style Kyokushin in the 1950s. It is known for bare-knuckle fighting: punches to the head are forbidden, but kicks to the head and body are allowed at full power, and bouts end by knockout, technical knockout or referee decision. The International Karate Organization (IKO), headquartered in Tokyo (Honbu), governs the style and runs the quadrennial World Open Tournament.

Rules

Under IKO rules, a senior bout lasts three minutes (two minutes in some divisions) with no protective gear except a groin protector and mouthguard; competitors wear white gi and fight barefoot on a mat or in a ring. A punch to the face is prohibited, but kicks to the head and body, knee strikes and throws are allowed at full contact. A knockdown is counted: the fighter has ten seconds to rise; three knockdowns in a bout mean defeat. The fight can also be ended by ippon (a decisive technique), by TKO when the referee judges the fighter unable to continue, or by decision of the judges. Competitions use weight divisions, with open-weight categories at the World Open, and kata are also contested.

History

Mas Oyama, born Choi Young-eui in Korea, moved to Japan, trained in Shotokan karate under Gichin Funakoshi and in goju-ryu, and traveled to Thailand to fight in muay Thai before opening his Oyama Dojo in Tokyo in 1953. After a famous retreat on Mount Minobu, he developed the full-contact system and named it Kyokushin. The organization grew worldwide, and after Oyama's death in 1994 it split into several groups, with the IKO Honbu continuing as the largest. The first Kyokushin World Open Tournament was held in Tokyo and the event has been staged every four years since. Famous Kyokushin fighters include Andy Hug, Francisco Filho and Peter Aerts, and many mixed martial artists began in Kyokushin.
