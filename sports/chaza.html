Chaza

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chaza (chazas) is the spear-throwing game of the Guambiano (Misak) people of Cauca, Colombia: throwers hurl a blunted wooden spear (chaza) at a target, scoring by accuracy and distance. It is contested at Colombian national indigenous games and was on the programme of the 2015 World Indigenous Games in Palmas, Brazil, alongside archery and the bow-hunting-fish event. Men and women compete; regional variations exist across the Colombian Andes. Within the target and precision sports on this site, its closest relatives are Javelin Throw, Chajledab, Axe Throwing, Archery; the related pages are listed under Important Links (eturbonews, 2017).

Rules

Chaza follows the indigenous-games format: each competitor throws a set number of chaza spears at a marked target from a fixed line; points for hits, with closer rings worth more (local rule sets vary); the highest score wins. Safety rules: throwing line, retrieval only on signal, blunted spear points. At the World Indigenous Games the spear toss is scored like javelin accuracy at a target (eturbonews, 2017).

History

Spear throwing is one of the oldest indigenous disciplines of the Colombian Andes; the Guambiano people of Silvia, Cauca, hold chaza at their festivals. The Colombian Federation of Indigenous and Traditional Games and Sports (FENnjtrad) organizes national championships; chaza was contested at the 2015 World Indigenous Games in Palmas (spear toss) and remains on the indigenous-games programme (Wikipedia, n.d.).
