Floorball

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Floorball is a fast indoor team sport played with a lightweight plastic stick and a perforated plastic ball on a rink of 40 × 20 m surrounded by boards, with goals of 1.60 × 1.15 m; six players per team — five outfield players and a goalkeeper — try to score, and body checking is prohibited (IFF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Floorball Federation (IFF), founded in 1986 in Helsinki with 80 member associations, which organizes the Floorball World Championships — men's since 1996, women's since 1997 — and the Champions Cup; the IFF received full IOC recognition in 2021, and floorball made its Olympic debut at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics with six men's and six women's teams (IFF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The men's World Championship 2026 (16th edition) is held in Tampere, Finland, from 5 to 13 December 2026, with Finland defending the title they won in Malmö 2024 by beating Sweden 5–4 in overtime; Sweden hold the record with ten men's world titles (IFF, n.d.-a) (International Floorball Federation, n.d.-a) (International Floorball Federation, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the Switzerland national team, winners of the 2025 Women's World Championship (2–0 in the final) and number one in the IFF women's world ranking, ahead of Sweden and Finland; on the men's side Sweden, Finland, the Czech Republic and Switzerland remain the dominant nations, and the Swiss club Storvreta and the Swedish club Thorengruppen won the 2026 Champions Cup (IFF, n.d.-a). In the 3v3 format — played 3 against 3 plus a goalkeeper on a 22 × 11 m rink in two 10-minute periods — the 2026 World Championship was held in San Lorenzo de El Escorial, Spain, the format's second edition after Lahti 2024 (IFF, n.d.-b).

Within the hockey family on this site, its closest relatives are Ice Hockey, Field Hockey, Indoor Field Hockey, Roller Hockey, Street Hockey; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

A floorball rink is 40 × 20 m (minimum 36 × 18 m) enclosed by 50 cm high boards with rounded corners, with goals of 1.60 m × 1.15 m placed 4 m from each end line; matches at World Championship level are three 20-minute periods of effective time, stopping for goals, fouls and the ball out of play (IFF, n.d.-b). Each team fields six players — five outfield players and one goalkeeper; the goalkeeper wears a mask and may use hands and body inside the goal crease but may not cover the ball for more than 3 seconds and may not receive a pass from a teammate with hands or arms; there is no offside, and a goalkeeper leaving the crease becomes an outfield player without a stick (IFF, n.d.-b). Players use a plastic stick with a basket-like blade and play a 23 g perforated ball; only the blade and the lower part of the stick may touch the ball, and the stick may not be raised above waist level in a shot or pass (high-stick foul) (IFF, n.d.-b). Contact is limited: no checking, holding, pushing or tripping; fouls are punished with 2-minute minor penalties, 5-minute major penalties, 10-minute misconduct penalties, match penalties and penalty shots taken from a spot 5 m in front of the goal (IFF, n.d.-b). Play restarts with face-offs at the center spot and at the fixed face-off dots after goals and fouls; substitutions are unlimited and on the fly (IFF, n.d.-b). Tied matches at World Championships go to a 10-minute overtime (golden goal) and then penalty shots; at the Olympics the format is three 20-minute periods with a 5-minute golden-goal overtime and then penalty shots (IFF, n.d.-b). The IFF Rules of the Game valid from 1 July 2026 introduced changes including stricter rules on coincidental penalties and improved goalkeeper safety (IFF, n.d.-b). Men and women play identical rules; youth classifications U19, U17 and U15 exist, and the 3v3 format uses a 22 × 11 m rink, three players plus a goalkeeper and two 10-minute periods (IFF, n.d.-b). Floorball is also a World Games sport since 2017 — the women's tournament debuted at the Chengdu 2025 World Games, where Switzerland won bronze (IFF, n.d.-a).

History

Floorball developed in Sweden in the 1960s–70s as innebandy, an indoor form of Ice Hockey and bandy played with a ball and plastic sticks in gymnasiums and corridors; the rules were codified around 1968–1970 and the game spread quickly to Finland, Switzerland and the Czech Republic (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Floorball Federation was founded in 1986 in Helsinki by Finland, Sweden and Switzerland, and the first men's World Championship was held in Sweden in 1996 — won by Sweden — with the women's championship following in 1997 (IFF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Sweden and Finland have won most of the world titles — Sweden a record ten on the men's side — with Switzerland and the Czech Republic the other leading nations; among the best-known players is the Finn Mika Kohonen, multiple world champion and long-time captain of the Finnish national team (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, Sweden dominated the early editions, and Switzerland's rise culminated in the 2025 world title; the IFF received provisional IOC recognition in 2011 and full recognition in 2021, paving the way for the Olympic debut at Milano Cortina 2026 (IFF, n.d.-a).
