Juggling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Juggling is the art and sport of keeping multiple objects in continuous motion in the air by throwing and catching them — typically balls, clubs or rings. As a performance art it accompanies circus and street theatre, and as a competitive sport it includes endurance "numbers" events (most catches of three, five or more objects without a drop) and judged freestyle routines. The International Jugglers' Association (IJA), founded in 1947, is the world's largest juggling organization and runs conventions and competitions.

Rules

In numbers juggling, competitors get three attempts at a record, e.g. the most consecutive catches of three balls (cascade), five balls or seven clubs, with a fall ending the attempt. In freestyle competitions, individuals, pairs or teams perform choreographed routines to music before a panel of judges, who score difficulty, execution, variety and presentation. Props may be balls, clubs, rings, torches or almost anything throwable; contact juggling and manipulation are separate disciplines. There is no contact sport element — the contest is purely about control, creativity and endurance.

History

Juggling is one of the oldest performing arts: a mural in the Beni Hasan tomb in Egypt (around 1900 BC) shows women juggling balls, and jugglers entertained at courts in China, India, Greece and Rome; in the Middle Ages they performed at fairs and markets, and in the 19th century they became stars of the circus and music hall. Modern competitive juggling took shape in the 20th century, and the International Jugglers' Association, founded in 1947, began holding annual conventions that evolved into formal competitions. Records have climbed from three balls to ever higher numbers — five balls, seven balls, nine balls and beyond — with pioneers such as Enrico Rastelli in the early 1900s and Anthony Gatto in the modern era, who popularized records of nine and more balls.
