Lawn Bowls

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lawn bowls (bowls) is a precision ball sport played on a flat grass green: players deliver large biased bowls (woods) so they curve and finish as close as possible to a small white target ball called the jack. Games are played in singles, pairs, triples or fours. The sport was codified in 19th-century Britain — bowls equipment has been found in ancient Egyptian tombs — and is governed internationally by World Bowls; the premier event is the World Bowls Championship, and the indoor variant is played as short-mat bowls.

Rules

The green is a square of up to about 40 m per side, divided into parallel rinks 5-6 m wide. At the start of each end, the jack is rolled from a mat at one end, and players alternate delivering their bowls, aiming to finish nearer to the jack than the opponents. Because the bowl is biased (heavier on one side), it curves as it slows; players may also hit ("drive") the jack or other bowls. After all bowls are played, the player whose bowl is closest to the jack scores one point for every bowl closer than the opponent's best. A match consists of a set number of ends, and the highest total wins. Bowls that leave the rink are dead.

History

Bowling games are ancient — bowls have been found in Egyptian tombs over 5,000 years old — and the modern game of lawn bowls was codified in 19th-century Britain, with the first written rules drawn up in Scotland in 1829 and clubs spreading across England. The sport became hugely popular in the British Empire — bowling greens are a fixture of parks in Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and Canada — and World Bowls was founded to govern it internationally, running World Championships since 1966. The World Bowls Championship in singles and pairs is the pinnacle event, and the sport also features at the Commonwealth Games, where it is one of the core sports.
