Alpine Skiing Super-G

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Super-G (super giant slalom) is the alpine skiing speed discipline that sits between downhill and giant slalom: one timed run on a long course with fewer, wider turns than giant slalom, producing high speeds. It is governed by the FIS, joined the World Ski Championships in 1987 and the Olympic program at Calgary 1988 (International Ski and Snowboard Federation [FIS], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c).

Among the athletes who shape the discipline today, the leading women come first: Italy's Federica Brignone won Olympic super-G gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 in 1:23.41. On the men's side, Switzerland's Franjo von Allmen took Olympic gold in 1:25.32, and compatriot Marco Odermatt is the reigning World Cup overall champion (FIS, n.d.-a; FIS, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under FIS rules, each athlete gets one timed run in the super-G — plus official training runs — and the fastest time wins (FIS, n.d.-c). The course must meet fixed vertical-drop ranges per category, lying between the downhill and giant slalom ranges, with a smaller number of direction changes than giant slalom but more than downhill; missing a gate — both ski tips must cross the gate line — means disqualification. Equipment follows FIS specifications, including the mandatory helmet and prescribed minimum ski lengths (FIS, n.d.-c). Men and women race under the identical rule set, differing only in the vertical-drop ranges and equipment minima.

History

Super-G was added to the FIS World Ski Championships at Crans-Montana 1987 and to the Olympic program at Calgary 1988. It rewards the ability to carry speed through terrain while still making clean turns. Notable champions include Austria's Hermann Maier, the United States' Lindsey Vonn and Norway's Aleksander Aamodt Kilde among the men; on the women's side, Vonn's super-G world titles and Federica Brignone's Olympic gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 stand out (FIS, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
