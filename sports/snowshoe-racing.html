Snowshoe Racing

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snowshoe racing (snowshoe running) is the winter sport of running over snow on snowshoes — lightweight frames with decking strapped to the feet — governed internationally by the World Snowshoe Federation (WSSF), founded in 2010 and recognised by the International Olympic Committee, which organises the annual World Snowshoe Championships (WSSF, n.d.-b). Races range from 100-metre sprints to 100-kilometre ultras such as the "Iditashoe", and the sport is part of the Arctic Winter Games and Special Olympics programmes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2018 World Championships in the Picos de Europa, Spain, were won by Michelle Hummel of the United States in the women's race and Stéphane Ricard of France in the men's, and the 2025 edition was held 19–21 December 2025 in Brillion, Wisconsin (WSSF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Among the current leading women are Michelle Hummel, Ragna Debats of the Netherlands and Rebecca Harman of the United States, a former US national champion; among the men, Stéphane Ricard and Joseph Gray of the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the specialist winter running events — Skyscraper Running, Cross Country Running and Trail Running — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

Under WSSF competition rules, races run on marked, packed snow courses — point-to-point, loop or out-and-back — with mass-start or interval starts over distances from 100-metre sprints through 5 km and 10 km classics to marathons and 100 km ultras (WSSF, n.d.-a). Snowshoes must meet the size and flotation rules set by the organiser; bindings and boots are the runner's choice, and traction devices are required on icy courses (WSSF, n.d.-a). Leaving the marked course, receiving outside assistance or impeding other competitors brings penalties up to disqualification, and lapped riders may be pulled in long events (WSSF, n.d.-a). World Championships award individual titles plus team and relay classifications; categories are split by gender — women's and men's — and by age groups such as juniors, masters and grandmasters, with identical courses for both sexes at elite level (WSSF, n.d.-b).

History

Snowshoes are among humanity's oldest tools, invented in Central Asia roughly 4,000–6,000 years ago; Ötzi the Iceman, found in the Alps and dated to about 3300 BCE, carried a snowshoe-like frame, and Indigenous peoples of North America — documented by Samuel de Champlain in 1609 — developed many frame shapes for winter hunting (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitive snowshoe running organised in twentieth-century North America: the United States Snowshoe Sports Association (USSSA) was founded in 1977 in Corinth, New York, the "Snowshoe Capital of the World", and has staged US national championships since 2001 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first World Snowshoe Championships were held in 2006 at Schladming/Dachstein, Austria — won by Emanuele Manzi and Maria Grazia Roberti of Italy — followed by editions at Vancouver/Dachstein 2010 (Antonio Santi, Roberti), Myoko, Japan, 2011 (David Le Porho; sprint: Joseph Gray and Djamila Bengueche), Quebec City 2015, Vezza d'Oglio 2016 and Saranac Lake 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The WSSF was founded in 2010 as the International Snowshoe Federation (ISSF) and renamed in 2015 (WSSF, n.d.-b).
