Zhorgo Salysh

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Zhorgo salysh (also jorgo salysh, “the zhorgo race”) is the traditional Kyrgyz pacing race in which the horse must maintain the zhorgo gait — a fast lateral ambling gait in which the legs on one side move together — while racing; judges score both speed and purity of gait. It is contested at the World Nomad Games for older horses (the 2026 edition ran 11,000 m and was won by Akzholtoi of Kyrgyzstan, ahead of Imperatritsa of Kyrgyzstan and Enesai of Russia) and is a staple of Kyrgyz hippodrome racing alongside byshty zhorgo (a shorter pacing sprint, 6,000 m in 2026, won by Erke) and the galloping races at chabysh. Within the Central Asian nomad games on this site, its closest relatives are Kunan Chabysh, Mongolian Horse Racing, Baiga; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The zhorgo salysh is a timed race over a set course (11 km at the 2026 World Nomad Games) in which the horse must keep the zhorgo pace throughout; a horse that breaks into a trot or canter is warned and, on repeated infringements, disqualified or placed behind. Jockeys ride light, whip use is restricted, and horses are classed by age (the zhorgo classes are for older horses, while kunan chabysh is for the young). Officials at the hippodrome judge gait purity while electronic timing records the finish order; prizes go to horse and jockey.

History

Pacing races are a celebrated part of Kyrgyz and Kazakh equestrian culture — the zhorgo gait is prized for its comfort and speed over long distances, and pacers are trained from foalhood. Zhorgo salysh (listed as jorgo salysh) has been a medal event at every World Nomad Games since 2014 in Cholpon-Ata, where the equestrian program also included kunan chabysh, alman baige and the flat races at chabysh. The 2016 Games grouped it with the other horse races as “horse racing (at chabysh / jorgo salysh / kunan chabysh)”, and the 2026 edition in Cholpon-Ata again crowned its champions at the Issyk-Kul hippodrome.
