Speedball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Speedball is the tournament form of paintball: a fast, arena-based game in which two small teams race to eliminate each other and capture the opponent's flag or press their buzzer on a compact field of inflatable bunkers. It is the de facto format of competitive paintball worldwide \u2014 played professionally in leagues such as the National Xball League (NXL) and its predecessors, with matches decided in short, high-intensity points decided by seconds of speed, communication and shooting accuracy.

Rules

Games are played on a standardized, symmetrically mirrored field (roughly 150 by 120 feet at the elite level) covered with inflatable bunkers \u2014 the "airball" layout that lets players snap shoot, slide and dive between cover. Teams of five (or seven, depending on the league) start from opposite stations; a player is eliminated when a paintball breaks on them or anything they wear or hold, including their marker, and must leave the field. The classic capture-the-flag format awards the point to the team that grabs the opponent's flag and returns it to their own station; the modern NXL standard instead scores a point when a surviving player presses the buzzer at the opposing start station. Match structures vary by league, from single timed games to the point-by-point Xball format with a running game clock, and rulebooks cap rates of fire and specify allowed firing modes to keep play fair and safe.

History

Speedball emerged as paintball moved from its recreational woodsball roots toward a genuine spectator sport in the 1990s: short games on small, open fields with artificial cover proved far easier to watch, officiate and broadcast than long woodland skirmishes. The National Professional Paintball League (NPPL) ran a 7-man capture-the-flag circuit and reached television, while Paintball Sports Promotions (PSP) developed the point-by-point Xball format; in 2007 a single PSP World Cup event drew over 35,000 spectators and 373 teams. After the PSP collapsed following the 2014 season, the National Xball League (NXL) \u2014 revived in 2015 and now spanning the United States, Latin America and Europe \u2014 became the sport's premier circuit, hosting divisional play from beginner to professional and the annual World Cup in Florida. Speedball's professionalization brought coaches, corporate sponsorships and franchise-style teams such as San Diego Dynasty, and it remains the gateway format through which nearly all tournament paintball is played.
