Roller Figure Skating

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller figure skating — artistic roller skating — is the competitive sport of figure skating performed on roller skates instead of ice skates, usually quad skates, with inline skates contested in separate events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by World Skate, the successor of the Fédération Internationale de Roller Sports (FIRS) founded on 21 April 1924 in Paris, which merged with the International Skateboarding Federation in 2017 and was rebranded World Skate (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). World Skate publishes the Artistic Technical Rules 2026 and stages the World Championships, where women and men compete in singles, pairs, solo dance and precision (groups of 12–24 skaters) (World Skate, n.d.). The first Artistic Skating World Championship was held in 1947 in Washington, D.C. (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Famous Olympic ice champions who began on rollers include Brian Boitano, Tara Lipinski and Marina Kielmann (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Within the skate and roller sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Skateboarding, Street Skateboarding, Vert Skateboarding, Downhill Skateboarding, Freestyle Skateboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Under the World Skate Artistic Technical Rules 2026, quad and inline skaters compete in separate events; the disciplines are figures (skating prescribed loops on circles), free skating (a choreographed programme of jumps, spins and step sequences judged on technical elements and programme components), solo dance and pairs, plus precision skating where 12–24 athletes move as one unit at high speed (World Skate, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Jumps and spins are graded for difficulty and for quality of execution (QOE), with deductions for falls and programme faults; free skating programmes have minimum required elements and time limits by category (World Skate, n.d.). Skates consist of a boot, plate, four wheels and bearings; free skaters have a toe stop for jumps, and jump bars may be added for stability (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitions run in junior and senior categories for women and men, with separate women's and men's singles, and pairs and dance combine women and men (World Skate, n.d.). The sport was on the World Games programme and appeared at the World Skate Games, most recently in the 2024 edition in Italy (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Organized roller skating dates to the 19th century, and the international federation — founded in 1924 by Fred Renkewitz and Otto Myer as the FIPR with France, Germany, Great Britain and Switzerland — staged the first Rink Hockey World Championship in Stuttgart in 1936 and the first artistic world title in Washington, D.C., in 1947 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The federation was renamed FIRS in the 1960s, organized artistic, rink hockey and speed skating world championships for decades, and became World Skate in 2017 after merging with the ISF (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Roller skating has repeatedly fed Olympic ice skating: Brian Boitano, Tara Lipinski and Marina Kielmann all trained as artistic roller skaters before winning Olympic ice gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
