Roller Figure Skating

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller figure skating — today usually called artistic roller skating — is the roller-sport equivalent of ice figure skating, in which skaters perform judged programmes of jumps, spins, footwork and dance on quad or inline skates. It is governed worldwide by World Skate, the IOC-recognised international federation for roller sports, whose World Skate Artistic committee publishes the official technical regulations and stages world championship events; in Europe the discipline is overseen by World Skate Europe — Artistic Skating. Inline figure skating has been included in the world championships since 2002.

Rules

Competitions are held in the principal disciplines of figures, free skating (solo), dance (compulsory dance, style dance and free dance) and pairs, with skaters placed by panels of judges using a grading system defined in the World Skate Artistic regulations. Figures require skaters to trace circles painted on the floor — unlike compulsory figures on ice, where skaters draw their own circles — and judges evaluate the quality of the tracing, edges and turns; the White System of scoring is used for figures and show events. Free skating programmes combine technical elements — jumps, spins and footwork sequences, with features such as combination spins and change-foot spins defined in the rules — scored alongside artistic impression, with timing rules capped on the music and deductions applied for violations such as inappropriate lyrics. Dance events include compulsory dances such as the Imperial Tango, the Fourteen Step and the Flirtation Waltz, performed to standardised music with fixed tempo; since the 2015–16 season a Style Dance (similar to ice dancing's short dance) has accompanied the Free Dance in international competition, and the two form the junior and senior international dance segments. Quad and inline skaters generally compete in separate events, and music, costume and equipment rules are set out in the general World Skate regulations.

History

Artistic roller skating developed alongside the quad roller skate in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, riding the same wave of rink culture that produced roller hockey and speed skating, and it remained the prestige discipline of roller sport through the 20th century under the International Roller Sports Federation (FIRS), whose artistic technical committee organised world championships from the interwar period onward. The sport was long dominated by Italy, whose clubs and coaches shaped its technical development, with strong international competition from nations such as the United States, Spain, France, Germany, Portugal and Brazil. Inline figure skating emerged in the 1990s partly as an off-ice training tool for ice figure skaters, was recognised by World Skate as artistic roller skating on inline skates, and entered the world championships in 2002 in Wuppertal, Germany; the World Inline Figure Skating Association was founded in Paris on 30 January 2011 to develop the inline branch independently. In 2017 the FIRS was reorganised as World Skate, which since then has staged the artistic world championships as part of its multi-sport World Skate Games, and the modern discipline continues to crown world champions in figures, free skating, dance, pairs, show and inline events on a biennial world-championship cycle.
