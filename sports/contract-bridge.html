Contract Bridge

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Contract bridge is the competitive form of the card game bridge, played by four players in two partnerships. After the auction (bidding), one partnership becomes the declaring side and must make a stated number of tricks with a named trump suit or notrump. It is governed worldwide by the World Bridge Federation (WBF), which publishes the Laws of Contract Bridge and runs the World Championships, the Bridge Olympiad and the World Cup series; in Europe the European Bridge League (EBL) organizes the European Championships.

Rules

Under the Laws of Contract Bridge, the game proceeds in four phases: dealing, bidding, play of the hand and scoring. The bidding determines the contract (level plus strain); the declaring side must win the stated number of tricks (six plus the contract level) to make the contract, while the defenders try to prevent it. During the play, the declarer plays both their own hand and the dummy's cards. Scoring includes trick points, overtricks, honors, game/vulnerability bonuses and slam bonuses; rubber bridge and duplicate (tournament) formats are used, with boards compared across tables in duplicate play.

History

Bridge evolved from earlier trick-taking games such as whist (which it replaced in the late 19th century); auction bridge was developed by Harold Stirling Vanderbilt around 1925, and contract bridge's modern laws were codified in the 1920s by the Portland Club and later the American Contract Bridge League. The WBF was founded in 1958 and the first Bridge Olympiad was held in 1960 in Turin. Legendary players include Italy's Giorgio Belladonna and Benito Garozzo of the dominant Italian "Blue Team", and the game is played by millions in clubs and online platforms worldwide.
