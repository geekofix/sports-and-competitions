Canoe Ocean Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canoe ocean racing is downwind paddling in the open sea, raced in surfskis and outrigger canoes (OC1, OC2, va'a) on courses that follow the swell and wind. It is a discipline of the International Canoe Federation (ICF), which runs an Ocean Racing World Cup and the ICF Canoe Ocean Racing World Championships (the 2027 edition was awarded to Tahiti). Races are typically 10 to 30 km long, with shorter technical sprint courses in the big surf.

Rules

Under the ICF ocean racing rules, races are started from the beach in heats or as a mass start, and the first paddler to cross the finish line wins; safety craft escort the fleet. Competitors must wear life jackets and leashes, and craft must meet ICF specifications for each class. Downwind legs let paddlers surf the waves, while flat sections demand steady paddling; re-starts and buoy infringements carry penalties. Classes include men's and women's surfskis and OC1, plus sprint events on a technical course.

History

Ocean racing grew out of the surf lifesaving and surfski cultures of Australia, South Africa and Hawaii, where paddlers raced downwind in the 20th century. The ICF formally added ocean racing as a discipline with its own world cup and world championships, and the sport has spread to Europe and Asia. The 2026 ICF Canoe Ocean Racing World Cup opened in Madeira, Portugal, and Tahiti will host the 2027 world championships.
