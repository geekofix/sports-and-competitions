Speed Skiing

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Speed skiing (KL — 'kilomètre lancé') is the FIS discipline in which a skier is timed over a measured 100 m section of an extremely steep, specially prepared course, reaching well over 200 km/h: the men's world record is 254.958 km/h by Italy's Ivan Origone at Vars, France, on 26 March 2016 (the all-time absolute mark is 255.500 km/h by France's Simon Billy, 22 March 2023), and the women's record is 248.270 km/h by Italy's Valentina Greggio at Vars on 3 April 2026 — Greggio has also won six world championship titles and 55 World Cup races (Wikipedia, n.d.; FIS, n.d.). FIS competition rules cap race speeds at 200 km/h, so record attempts happen at dedicated venues such as Vars 'La Chabrière'; equipment classes S1 (production gear) to S7 (aerodynamic suit and helmet) are defined by FIS. The discipline is not on the Olympic program but holds its own World Cup and World Championships (FIS, n.d.).

Within the winter skiing family on this site its closest relatives are Alpine Skiing Downhill, Alpine Skiing Super-G, Skwalk, Alpine Touring and Heliskiing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

FIS speed skiing rules: the course has a steep inrun (first 300–400 m) for gaining speed, a measured 100 m timing section where the top speed is recorded, and a 500 m run-out for braking; each athlete gets several runs and the best counts. FIS races are designed so that 200 km/h is not exceeded; equipment classes S1–S7 regulate skis (max 2.40 m long, 10 cm wide, 15 kg per pair), bent poles (min 1 m) and the aerodynamic suits and helmets of the top classes; false starts void the run. Men's and women's competitions are separate, with junior categories as well (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Competitive speed skiing began in the 1930s; after a fatal accident at an unsanctioned event in 1992, the FIS introduced the 200 km/h speed limit for its races. The classic venue Vars 'La Chabrière' in the French Hautes-Alpes has hosted the Speed Ski World Cup and all records beyond 240 km/h since 2016; the Origone brothers — Simone, a multiple world champion, and Ivan, the 254.958 km/h record holder — and Valentina Greggio are the discipline's icons (Wikipedia, n.d.).
