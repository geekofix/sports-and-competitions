Orienteering

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Orienteering is a group of navigation sports in which participants use a detailed map and a compass to find their way through unfamiliar terrain from one control point to the next as quickly as possible. The International Orienteering Federation (IOF), founded on 21 May 1961 in Copenhagen and recognized by the IOC since 1977, governs four disciplines: foot orienteering (FootO), mountain bike orienteering, ski orienteering and trail orienteering. Orienteering has been part of the World Games since 2001, and the sprint format is a candidate for future Olympic inclusion.

Rules

Each competitor receives a specially prepared orienteering map only at the start, showing the exact location of the control points marked with orange-and-white flags. The athlete must visit all controls in the prescribed order and punch an electronic timing chip at each one before crossing the finish line; the fastest total time wins. Any assistance from other people, using a GPS device or deviating from the order of controls leads to disqualification. At the World Orienteering Championships the main formats are sprint, middle distance, long distance and relay, with sprint relays and mixed sprint relays run with a mass start; a knockout sprint format has been added in recent years. Maps are drawn to strict IOF specifications, typically at scales of 1:4,000 to 1:15,000, and control descriptions are given in international symbols.

History

Orienteering grew out of military land-navigation exercises in Scandinavia in the late 19th century; the first public competition is usually dated to 31 October 1897 near Oslo, Norway. The sport spread through Sweden and Finland, and the IOF was founded in Copenhagen in 1961 by ten European federations. The first World Orienteering Championships were held in Fiskars, Finland, in 1966, biennially until 2003 and annually since, alternating sprint and forest formats. The annual Orienteering World Cup has run since 1986. Mass-participation relays define the culture of the sport: Tiomila in Sweden since 1945, the night relay Jukola in Finland since 1949 and O-Ringen in Sweden since 1965 are among the largest sporting events in the world by number of participants. Stars of the modern era include Sweden's Thierry Gueorgiou and Tove Alexandersson.
