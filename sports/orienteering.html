Orienteering

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Orienteering is a group of navigation sports in which participants use a detailed map and a compass to find their way through unfamiliar terrain from one control point to the next as quickly as possible (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). The International Orienteering Federation (IOF), founded on 21 May 1961 in Copenhagen and recognized by the IOC since 1977, governs four disciplines: foot orienteering (FootO), mountain bike orienteering, ski orienteering and trail orienteering (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate elite classes at the World Orienteering Championships under identical course standards (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Orienteering has been part of the World Games since 2001, and the sprint format is a candidate for future Olympic inclusion (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the orienteering and adventure family on this site, its closest relatives are Mountain Bike Orienteering, Ski Orienteering, Trail Orienteering, Foot Orienteering, Rogaining; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Each competitor receives a specially prepared orienteering map only at the start, showing the exact location of the control points marked with orange-and-white flags (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). The athlete must visit all controls in the prescribed order and punch an electronic timing chip at each one before crossing the finish line; the fastest total time wins (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Any assistance from other people, using a GPS device or deviating from the order of controls leads to disqualification (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). At the World Orienteering Championships the main formats are the sprint (urban, about 15 minutes), the middle distance (about 35-40 minutes), the long distance (about 80-90 minutes) and the relay, in which three team members each run one leg (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Men's and women's elites run separate courses of similar difficulty; junior and veteran age classes (from under-16 to over-35 in five-year steps) follow the same rules on adapted courses (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.).

History

Modern orienteering was born in Sweden and Norway in the late 19th century, when military exercises using map and compass evolved into civilian competitions; the first public orienteering race was held in Norway in 1897, and Sweden's "25-manna" relay began in 1919 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Orienteering Federation was founded on 21 May 1961 in Copenhagen with ten member nations, and the first World Orienteering Championships were held in Norway in 1966 (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Scandinavian pioneers such as the Swedish champion Ulrik Salén and Norway's first great female orienteer, Ingrid Hadler, made the sport truly international; Hadler won three relay world titles in the 1970s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today the IOF counts more than 70 member federations, and the sport is strongest in the Nordic countries, Switzerland, France and Central Europe (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.).
