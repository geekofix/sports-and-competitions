Superkart

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Superkart racing is the top class of karting, contested with gearbox-equipped racing karts that reach speeds of 250 km/h on long circuits "+E+" the 250cc class being the fastest category of kart racing and quicker than many racing cars over a lap. Racing alongside motorcycle and car meetings on full-size road circuits, superkarts are a small but spectacular branch of motorsport governed worldwide by the FIA's karting commission.

Rules

A superkart is a full-size racing kart with bodywork, wingless aerodynamics defined by the driver's exposed position, and a 250cc two-stroke engine coupled to a six-speed gearbox driving the rear axle; the flagship E class uses production engines, while divisional classes specify intake restrictors and minimum weights to balance performance. Racing takes place primarily on long circuit road courses, where the karts' power-to-weight ratio (rough 250-plus kilometres per hour on straights) and high cornering grip produce lap times comparable to small formula cars, with competitions run as timed qualifying, sprint races and endurance events under CIK-FIA technical rules; kart and driver safety requirements include full protective equipment and racing suits, and the machinery demands precise setup of chassis, gears and brakes.

History

Gearbox karting grew naturally out of karting's 1950s roots in American and British parking-lot racing: as the sport organized and engines grew more powerful, purpose-built long-circuit kart classes developed in Britain (the birthplace of the 250-class rules under the sport's national body) and Europe. Superkarts became a fixture of road-race meetings from the 1960s and 1970s, running as a crowd-pleasing support class at venues such as British and continental circuits, and FIA-affiliated championships formalized the category with the CIK-FIA European and world-level championships. The class remains a specialist pursuit with a devoted European heartland (Britain, France and the Netherlands supplying most of the field and champions), and its alumni graduate regularly to formula racing, with karting as a whole the recognized first step on the single-seater ladder.
