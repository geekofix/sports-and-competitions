Rink Hockey

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rink hockey — quad hockey, hardball hockey or, in British English, roller hockey — is the traditional quad-skate form of roller hockey: two teams of five (four skaters plus a goalkeeper) drive a hard ball with curved cane sticks into the opponent’s goal on a smooth rink of about 40 × 20 m, playing two 25-minute halves with a 15-minute halftime, up to two 5-minute golden-goal overtime periods and, if still tied, a penalty shootout (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is a limited-contact sport: excessive contact is forbidden, unlike inline hockey (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world governing body is World Skate (successor of FIRS, founded 21 April 1924); the Rink Hockey World Championship has been held since 1936 in Stuttgart and, since 1989, every two years on odd years, and since 2017 the discipline has been part of the biennial World Skate Games (World Skate, n.d.). More than 44 editions have been played, dominated by Spain (18 titles), Portugal (16), Argentina (6) and Italy (4), and the sport was a demonstration sport at the 1992 Barcelona Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rink hockey is played in around 60 countries and is especially popular in Latin countries and Southern Europe, where the club scene is professionalized — SL Benfica, the oldest roller hockey club in the world (first game 1917), FC Porto, FC Barcelona and Liceo La Coruña are the most successful clubs, Barcelona alone with 21 European Cups (Wikipedia, n.d.). In Europe the elite competition is the CERH European Championship run by World Skate Europe, while England has the NRHA Roller Hockey Premier League (Wikipedia, n.d.). In the United States the sport was organized by the RSROA from 1959 with national championships from 1961, and it appeared at the Pan American Games in 1979, 1987, 1991 and 1995 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Both men’s and women’s world championships are held, and modern rinks are often paved blue or white so the ball is visible on television (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Matches are governed by the World Skate Rink Hockey rulebook (a consolidated edition with the 2027 changes is published on worldskate.org): two 25-minute halves, five players per side including the goalkeeper, all on quad skates, with curved sticks similar to bandy and shinty and a hard spherical ball (World Skate, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Body checking is not allowed — play is limited-contact — and fouls are punished with free hits, penalties and penalty shootouts; ties after regulation go to up to two 5-minute golden-goal periods with the clock stopped when the ball is dead, then a shootout (Wikipedia, n.d.). The rink measures roughly 40 × 20 m with rounded corners and 1 m boards, with goals at each end (World Skate, n.d.). Goalkeepers use a flat batting glove for rebounds (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate world championships under the same rules, and age-group (U-19/U-20) world cups exist alongside the senior events (World Skate, n.d.).

History

Organized rink hockey was played in Europe as early as 1901, and the first world championship was held in 1936 in Stuttgart, Germany, under the FIPR/FIRS federation founded in 1924; the silent-film stars Stan Laurel and Charlie Chaplin were photographed playing it in the early 1900s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport debuted as an Olympic demonstration sport at Barcelona 1992 and joined the Pan American Games programme from 1979 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Since 2017 the World Championship has been staged within the World Skate Games, after FIRS became World Skate in 2017 (World Skate, n.d.). Latin countries have dominated since the 1940s, with Spain and Portugal the leading nations (Wikipedia, n.d.).
