Combat Ju-Jitsu

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Combat ju-jitsu is a full-contact form of ju-jitsu in which controlled strikes are combined with throws, takedowns and ground fighting. It is governed internationally by the Combat Ju-Jitsu International Federation (CJJIF), which runs world championships, while the Ju-Jitsu International Federation (JJIF) includes a similar full-contact "fighting system" in its sport ju-jitsu program at the World Games. Combat ju-jitsu is practiced for self-defense and competition in many countries.

Rules

Under CJJIF competition rules, bouts are fought in rounds with weight categories; athletes score points for controlled strikes and throws, and the bout can be won by submission, knockout or by points when time expires. The JJIF fighting system divides a bout into three parts: Part I is distance combat with controlled attacks using arms and legs; once a grab is made, Part II allows throws and takedowns but no more hits; Part III is the ground fight with locks, strangles and control, where the first to score ippon wins. Protective gear (gloves, shin guards, groin protection) is required at championships.

History

Modern competitive ju-jitsu emerged in Europe in the mid-20th century, with the European Ju-Jitsu Federation founded in 1977 by Germany, Italy and Sweden, becoming the International Ju-Jitsu Federation in 1987 and the JJIF in 1998. The fighting system was introduced in 1990 and has been part of the World Games since 1997. Combat ju-jitsu as a distinct full-contact style with strikes developed alongside self-defense systems in the late 20th century, and the CJJIF now organizes its own world championships with weight classes.
