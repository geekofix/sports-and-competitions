Mermaid Diving

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mermaid diving is the competitive and performance form of swimming with a monofin — the single large fin shaped like a mermaid’s tail — in which swimmers glide through water with a dolphin kick; it sits at the intersection of finswimming, artistic swimming and performance art (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the aquatics family and is related to finswimming and freediving.

Figure: Helmardine. (2019-02-06). All-rubber monofin used in mermaid diving and finswimming [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/All-Rubber_Monofin.jpg

Competitive mermaid diving (sometimes "mermaid finswimming") is judged on speed over set distances (25–100 m with monofin) and on choreography in artistic categories; CMAS and national finswimming federations include monofin events, and there are dedicated mermaid competitions with solo/duo categories (CMAS, n.d.). The monofin itself (photo above) is made of rubber or fiberglass with a full foot pocket (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Rules follow finswimming: races are in pools with surface swimming between turns or underwater starts allowed in finswimming events; monofin competitions use the same pool safety rules — no underwater swimming beyond the permitted start/turn distances in pool races, buddy rules in open water (CMAS, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate categories.

History

The monofin was invented in the 1970s — Soviet/Russian finswimmers pioneered it — and finswimming became a CMAS world championship discipline in the 1950s–60s (Wikipedia, n.d.). "Mermaiding" as a performance art (Weeki Wachee Springs, Florida, since 1947 — photo) popularized the tail; competitive mermaid swimming with monofins has grown since the 2010s with international mermaid conventions and races (Wikipedia, n.d.).
