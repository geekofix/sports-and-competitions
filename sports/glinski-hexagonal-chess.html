Glinski Hexagonal Chess

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Glinski's hexagonal chess is played on a board of 97 hexagonal cells (three hexagon orientations) invented by Władysław Gliński in 1936; pieces move along the six cell directions, giving bishops six diagonals and queens twelve. Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are Capablanca Chess, Chess; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Chessvariants.com, n.d.).

Figure: Michael!. (2016-08-21). A regular hexagonal chess board (r=6) [Diagram]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:R=6_hexagonal_board_(grey).png

Rules

Hexagonal board with 97 cells; pawns move forward and capture diagonally-forward; rooks along straight cell lines; bishops along the six diagonal directions; queen = rook+bishop; knights jump in the L-shape adapted to hex cells; king as in chess. Castling on the back rank (Chessvariants.com, n.d.).

History

Władysław Gliński, a Polish draughts champion, published the variant in 1936; modern enthusiasts (e.g. the Hexagonal Chess community and 'Glinski's hexagonal chess' on chessvariants) play it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
