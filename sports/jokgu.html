Jokgu

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Jokgu is the Korean foot volleyball: four players per side play a ball over a 1.1 m net on a 16 × 7 m court using only the feet (below the knee) and the head (above the chin) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game was invented in 1960 by members of the Republic of Korea Air Force and spread through the military and schools; the Korea Jokgu Association governs the domestic game, the first World Jokgu Championship was held in 2022 together with the founding of the International Jokgu Federation (IJF), and the World Foot Volleyball Championships 2023 in Korea marked its international breakthrough — over 1,000 teams now play in Korea (Korea Jokgu Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ball is about 20 cm in diameter and weighs roughly 360 g (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In 2026 the leading teams are the Korean club champions of the professional jokgu league, and the women's game — played on the same court and net height — is led by the Korean women's national team, the reigning world champions (International Jokgu Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The court is 16 × 7 m with a net 1.1 m high (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each team fields four players on court plus up to three substitutes from a registered roster, with rolling substitutions (Korea Jokgu Association, n.d.). A side may contact the ball a maximum of three times, with at most one bounce of the ball on the floor between contacts, and must return it over the net (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Only the feet below the knee and the head above the chin may play the ball — hands and arms are forbidden, which distinguishes jokgu from sepak takraw and footvolley (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match is best of three sets to 15 points, rally point scoring, with a two-point lead required (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Serving is underhand or with the foot from behind the end line into the diagonal service court; the serve rotates among players (Korea Jokgu Association, n.d.). A ball touching the net on the way over is still in play; a ball landing on a line is in; touching the net with the body is a fault (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's competitions use identical courts, nets and rules, with separate tournaments (International Jokgu Federation, n.d.).

History

Jokgu was invented in 1960 by Republic of Korea Air Force personnel, who adapted foot volleyball into a game playable in barracks and on small courts; it spread through the military and schools in the 1960s and 1970s, and organized play with formal rules began in the following decades (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Korea Jokgu Association was founded to run national championships and the professional league, in which more than 1,000 teams now compete (Korea Jokgu Association, n.d.). Internationally, the first World Jokgu Championship in 2022 coincided with the founding of the International Jokgu Federation, and the World Foot Volleyball Championships 2023 in Korea brought the game to a global audience alongside sepak takraw (International Jokgu Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women's jokgu developed alongside the men's game from the 1980s, and women's world championships are now part of the IJF calendar (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Because jokgu is a young team sport, its icons are the founding national team captains of the 1960s and the stars of the current Korean professional league (Korea Jokgu Association, n.d.).
