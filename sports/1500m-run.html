1500m Run

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 1500 metres — three and three-quarter laps from a standing start, known as the metric mile — is the tactical middle-distance classic, governed by World Athletics (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 3:26.00 for men, set by Hicham El Guerrouj of Morocco in Rome 1998, and 3:48.68 for women, set by Faith Kipyegon of Kenya in 2025 as the first woman under 3:49 (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was dominated by Kipyegon (pictured), the three-time Olympic champion, while the men's race was led by Olympic champion Jakob Ingebrigtsen of Norway, pictured at the 2019 World Championships (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

Figure: van Leeuwen, E. (2022, July 18). Faith Kipyegon of Kenya at the 2022 World Championships in Oregon [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. GFDL. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Faith_Kipyegon_Oregon_2022.jpg

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the middle-distance family — 800m Run, 5000m Run and Steeplechase — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The 1500 m starts from a standing start behind a curved start line, with no lane restrictions after the start; athletes jostle for position at the front of the pack. Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

The 1500 m has been on the Olympic programme since 1896 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Hicham El Guerrouj of Morocco set the 3:26.00 world record in Rome 1998 and won Olympic gold in 2004; Sebastian Coe of Great Britain, the 800 m Olympic champion, also ran 1500 m at world level in the early 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, Faith Kipyegon won Olympic gold at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 and broke the 3:49 barrier in 2025, while the Chinese runner Qu Yunxia held the previous record of 3:49.11 from 1993 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
