Shovel Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shovel racing is a winter novelty racing sport in which riders sit on an ordinary snow shovel — in the scoop, facing the handle, feet pointed forward and leaning back — and race head-first down a groomed snowy slope; it originated in the 1970s among ski-resort workers in New Mexico (credited around Angel Fire Resort, and also Crested Butte, Colorado) who used shovels to get down the mountain quickly, and it peaked as a Winter X Games event in 1997 before being dropped after a fatal crash (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). There is no world federation; the best-known competition was the annual World Championship Shovel Races at Angel Fire Resort, New Mexico — the 40th edition ran in January 2019 — which the resort suspended around 2005 over liability concerns and resumed in 2010 only after "super-modified" shovel-sleds were banned, and today the sport survives in community festivals such as the Beaver County Snow Shovel Riding Championship in Utah (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Figure: Eubank, L. (2011, February 12). AngelFireShovelRaces2011 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AngelFireShovelRaces2011.jpg

Shovel racing is open to all genders and to a wide age range from students to retirees, and a women's class exists at elite level — the American Nadia Gonzales was clocked at 64 mph (103 km/h) in 2017 — while the best-known competitors are Damien Deleon, unofficially dubbed the "king of shovel racing", and John Schrader, the "Shovelmeister" record holder (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Riders race on a basic snow shovel: seated in the scoop facing the handle, leaning back with feet forward, and they typically wax the underside of the blade to gain speed; sleds are often given custom paint jobs, and elite racers reach speeds up to 72 mph (115.87 km/h) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The "super-modified shovel" category — sleds running on skis with an aerodynamic pod and a roll cage around the driver, with the shovel merely as decoration — was banned from competition after the fatal 1997 X Games crash of Justin Williams, and the Winter X Games dropped the sport entirely (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Races are timed runs down the prepared slope, fastest time wins, and events are split into classes by shovel type and by age and gender, including a women's class (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Competitive shovel racing grew out of 1970s New Mexico ski resorts, where lift operators and other workers slid down on shovels after their shifts; it reached the zenith of its popularity as a Winter X Games event in 1997, but the Games organisers removed it afterwards when athlete Justin Williams was killed during the 1997 competition (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Angel Fire Resort hosted the annual World Championship Shovel Races — the 40th edition in January 2019 — dropped the event in 2005 because of liability, and resumed it in 2010 after banning the super-modified sleds; the resort ultimately stopped hosting the races around 2024 for budget reasons (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport also appeared in the opening scene of the 1946 Frank Capra film It's a Wonderful Life, and today it lives on in community events such as the Beaver County Snow Shovel Riding Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
