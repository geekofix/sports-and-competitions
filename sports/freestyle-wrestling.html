Freestyle Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freestyle wrestling is the Olympic wrestling style in which both takedowns and exposure of the opponent's back to the mat score points; leg attacks are allowed and the bout is decided by technical superiority or a fall (pin). It is governed by United World Wrestling (UWW), which runs the World Wrestling Championships and the Olympic wrestling program (freestyle has been at every Olympics since 1904 for men; women's freestyle since Athens 2004).

Rules

Under UWW rules, a freestyle match is two 3-minute periods. A wrestler scores points for takedowns (2-4 points depending on amplitude and control), exposure of the opponent's back (2-4 points for 2-5 seconds), reversals and penalties for fleeing/caution (1 point to the opponent). A fall — holding both the opponent's shoulder blades to the mat — wins the match immediately. If no fall, the wrestler with more points wins; a technical superiority (10-point lead) also ends the match. Attacks on the legs are allowed, and a match can also be won by pinning the opponent. Weight classes, weigh-ins and anti-doping follow UWW regulations; video review is used at major events. Greco-Roman (no leg attacks) is the other Olympic style.

History

Freestyle wrestling has roots in catch wrestling and became an Olympic sport for men at the 1904 St. Louis Games (the 1896 Athens wrestling event was Greco-Roman). The UWW (founded 1924 as FILA, renamed United World Wrestling in 2014) governs the sport, holding World Championships since 1922. The USSR/Russia dominated the men's freestyle for decades (legends such as Aleksandr Medved, three-time Olympic champion, and Buvaisar Saitiev), with the United States (John Smith, Cael Sanderson), Iran and Cuba also strong. Women's freestyle was added to the Olympics in 2004, dominated by Japan (gold in every Games since), the US and Canada. Stars include Cael Sanderson (159-0 college record, Olympic gold 2004), Jordan Burroughs and Kyle Snyder.
