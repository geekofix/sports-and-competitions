Freestyle Wrestling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freestyle wrestling is the Olympic wrestling style in which both takedowns and exposure of the opponent's back to the mat score points and leg attacks are allowed; a bout ends immediately on a fall (both shoulder blades held to the mat), on technical superiority (a 10-point lead) or on points after three two-minute periods (United World Wrestling [UWW], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by United World Wrestling (UWW), founded in 1912 as FILA and renamed in 2014, which runs the World Wrestling Championships — held since 1922 — and the Olympic wrestling program: men's freestyle has been on the Olympic program at every Games since St. Louis 1904, and women's freestyle since Athens 2004 (UWW, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). Men compete in six Olympic weight classes (57, 61, 65, 74, 86, 97 and 125 kg — seven at world championships, which add 79 and 92 kg non-Olympic classes) across ten non-Olympic world classes from 57 to 125 kg; women wrestle eight weight classes from 50 to 76 kg (UWW, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 World Wrestling Championships were held at Barys Arena in Astana, Kazakhstan, from 24 October to 1 November 2026 — the second edition hosted by Kazakhstan after Nur-Sultan 2019 — with close to 900 wrestlers from 30 weight classes across Greco-Roman, women's and men's freestyle, and the United States winning the men's freestyle team title by nine points over Russia (UWW, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women include Japan's wrestlers, who have won Olympic freestyle gold at every Games since 2004, with the 2026 European champion at 55 kg being Andreea Ana of Romania, who beat Liliia Malanchuk of Ukraine 10–0 (UWW, n.d.-b). On the men's side, the American team that triumphed in Astana included reigning Olympic champions and 2026 world champions Kyle Snyder (97 kg), Bo Bassett (65 kg), Levi Haines (79 kg) and Kyle Dake (86 kg) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the UWW Wrestling Rules, a freestyle bout consists of three periods of two minutes each, with a 30-second break between periods; the match ends on a fall (touchfall — both shoulder blades of the opponent held to the mat for about one second), on technical superiority (a 10-point difference), on injury or disqualification, or on points when time expires (UWW, n.d.-a). Scoring values are 1 point for a takedown with control or a penalty against the opponent (fleeing the mat, fleeing a hold, passivity), 2 points for a standard takedown, 4 points for a takedown with large amplitude and control, and 2 points for exposing the opponent's back to the mat; a reversal is worth 2 points, and a grand amplitude throw can score 4 or 5 points (UWW, n.d.-a). Leg attacks are fully legal in freestyle — the key difference from Greco-Roman wrestling — and the mat is a 12 m diameter circle (9 m at the center of action). Each weight class awards one gold, one silver and two bronze medals; bronze positions are decided through a repechage system for wrestlers who lost to the finalists (UWW, n.d.-a). Weigh-ins take place the evening before or the morning of competition, athletes compete only in the class they weighed in for, and anti-doping follows the World Anti-Doping Code; video challenge is available to coaches at major events (UWW, n.d.-a). Men and women follow identical rules and formats, with separate weight categories and separate world rankings; age-group classifications U23 and U17 exist alongside the senior category (UWW, n.d.-a).

History

Freestyle wrestling grew out of the 19th-century catch-as-catch-can and Lancashire catch wrestling traditions of Europe and North America, in which all holds — including holds on the legs — were permitted; it became an Olympic sport for men at the 1904 St. Louis Games, while the 1896 Athens program featured Greco-Roman only (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). The international federation was founded in 1912 in Duisburg as the Internationaler Amateur-Ringer-Verband and has been known as United World Wrestling (UWW) since 2014, running the World Championships since 1922 (UWW, n.d.-a). The Soviet Union and Russia dominated the early decades — Aleksandr Medved (1927–2003) won three Olympic gold medals (1964, 1968, 1972), and Buvaisar Saitiev won three Olympic and six world titles — followed by the rise of the United States with John Smith, Cael Sanderson (a 159–0 college record and Olympic gold in 2004), Jordan Burroughs and Kyle Snyder, who at 19 became the youngest American Olympic wrestling champion in 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's freestyle entered the World Championships in 1987 and the Olympics in 2004, and has been dominated by Japan — Saori Yoshida won gold in 2004, 2008 and 2012, and Kaori Icho became the first woman to win the same individual event at four consecutive Olympics (2004–2016) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
