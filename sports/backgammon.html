Backgammon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Backgammon is a two-player board game of strategy and luck played on a board of 24 points (triangles) with 15 checkers per player, two dice and a doubling cube. The goal is to move all your checkers around the board and bear them off before your opponent. The World Backgammon Federation (WBGF) is the international governing body, and the Backgammon World Championship in Monte Carlo (since 1967) is the most prestigious event.

Rules

Players roll two dice and move checkers the combined number of points, following the direction of play; a point occupied by a single enemy checker (a "blot") can be hit and sent to the bar, from where it must re-enter in the opponent's home board. The doubling cube lets either player offer to double the stake; the opponent can take (play on at double stakes) or drop (resign and lose the current stake). Matches are played to a set number of points (commonly 7, 11 or 13 in tournaments), with match play using the doubling cube and money play using it freely. Variants: many regional rule differences exist (e.g. starting position variants, " acey-deucey " rules in the Middle East).

History

Backgammon's ancestors (games like senet and the Royal Game of Ur) are over 5,000 years old; the modern form developed in 17th-century England from the game of tables. The World Backgammon Championship began in Las Vegas in 1967 and moved to Monte Carlo in 1979. Online play exploded after 2000 with servers like Backgammon Galaxy.
