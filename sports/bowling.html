Bowling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bowling (ten-pin bowling) is a precision sport in which a player rolls a heavy ball down a polished wooden lane 60 ft (18.29 m) long to knock down ten pins arranged in a triangle at the far end. It is governed internationally by World Bowling, the IOC-recognized world federation founded in 1952 as the Fédération Internationale des Quilleurs and reorganized as World Bowling in 2014, which runs the World Bowling Championships (World Bowling, n.d.). In the United States the United States Bowling Congress (USBC) sets the official rules of play, the Professional Bowlers Association (PBA) runs the top men's professional tour, and the Professional Women's Bowling Association (PWBA) runs the top women's tour (United States Bowling Congress [USBC], n.d.) (Professional Bowlers Association [PBA], n.d.) (Professional Women's Bowling Association [PWBA], n.d.).

Rules

Under the USBC rules of play, a game (line) consists of ten frames. In each of the first nine frames the bowler has up to two deliveries to knock down all ten pins; knocking down all ten with the first ball is a strike, with both balls a spare. In the tenth frame, a bowler who strikes or spares earns a third delivery. A strike scores 10 plus the pins knocked down with the next two balls; a spare scores 10 plus the next one ball; a perfect game is 300 (twelve consecutive strikes) (USBC, n.d.).

Equipment and field: the lane is 41.5 in (105 cm) wide from gutter to gutter, with a foul line 60 ft from the head pin; pins are 15 in (38 cm) tall and weigh up to 3 lb 10 oz (1.64 kg); the ball may weigh at most 16 lb (7.26 kg) and measure 8.500 to 8.595 in (21.59 to 21.83 cm) in diameter, with finger holes drilled to the bowler's hand. Lane oil patterns are applied by machine and standardized for sanctioned competition; bowlers may use rosin on their hands and must wear approved bowling shoes on the approach (USBC, n.d.).

Formats and classifications: besides individual stroke play, team events (doubles, trios, fives) and match-play formats exist, and league play uses handicap (a percentage of the difference between a team's and a baseline average), which lets bowlers of different abilities compete. Men's and women's competitions use identical rules at World Bowling and USBC level; youth divisions adjust lane length and ball weight, and the PBA50 senior tour is open to bowlers aged 50 and over. At the international level, World Bowling sanctions the World Bowling Championships, the World Bowling Women's Championships and the World Youth Bowling Championships, played in singles, doubles and team formats (World Bowling, n.d.). Fouls include crossing the foul line after delivery (the delivery counts but the pins knocked down are not scored) and touching the lane beyond the foul line.

History

Bowling descends from ancient pin games — polished stone balls and pins have been found in Egyptian tombs from around 3200 BC — and the modern ten-pin game was codified in the United States in 1895 by the American Bowling Congress, the forerunner of today's USBC (World Bowling, n.d.). The International Bowling Federation was founded in 1952 and became World Bowling in 2014. Professional bowling boomed with television coverage from the 1950s onward: the PBA Tour was founded in 1958, and legends such as Walter Ray Williams Jr. (47 PBA titles), Earl Anthony and Pete Weber — whose play-by-play calls on PBA broadcasts made him the voice of the sport for decades — defined the era (PBA, n.d.). On the women's side, the PWBA Tour was founded in 2003 (as the Women's Professional Bowling Association), and pioneers like Kelly Kulick, who became the first woman to win a PBA major in 2010, and Liz Johnson, the most successful PWBA bowler of her era with 25 titles, opened the door for today's generation of stars (PWBA, n.d.).
