Monoski

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The monoski (mono-ski) is adaptive skiing on a single wide ski mounted under a molded seat, steered with two outrigger crutches that end in small skis; it is a Paralympic alpine skiing discipline for athletes with lower-limb impairments who sit, contested in downhill, super-G, giant slalom, slalom, super combined and the team event, in the sitting classes LW10–LW12 (IPC, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate fields; at the Milano Cortina 2026 Paralympics (6–15 March 2026) the 30-event alpine program included all sitting disciplines (IPC, n.d.).

Figure: Eusebius. (2009-02-15). A monoski — the single ski with seat used in adaptive alpine skiing [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Monoski.JPG

Within the winter skiing and Para family on this site its closest relatives are Para Alpine Skiing, Alpine Skiing Downhill, Para Snowboard, Biathlon — which also splits standing and sitting classes — and Wheelchair Racing as the track counterpart; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Paralympic monoski rules mirror able-bodied alpine skiing with adaptations: the athlete sits in a bucket seat mounted on a single ski, uses two outriggers ending in mini-skis for balance and turning, and wears a helmet; courses, timing and gate rules follow FIS alpine regulations; the sitting classes LW10–LW12 determine the events; men's and women's races are held in every discipline (IPC, n.d.).

History

The monoski was developed in the 1970s–80s (early designs in Europe and North America), entered the Paralympics at Lillehammer 1994 as part of alpine skiing for sitting athletes, and has been a medal discipline ever since; it is governed by the IPC's World Para Alpine Skiing technical committee together with FIS equipment rules (Wikipedia, n.d.).
