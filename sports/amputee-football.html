Amputee Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Amputee Football (amputee soccer) is football played by teams of players with limb amputations or limb differences, using forearm crutches and with adapted rules. It is governed internationally by the World Amputee Football Federation (WAFF), which organizes the men's and women's Amputee Football World Cup every four years.

Rules

Amputee football follows the FIFA Laws of the Game with adaptations: teams have seven players (six outfield players plus a goalkeeper), matches are played in two 30-minute halves on a smaller pitch, and players use forearm crutches instead of one leg. Offside applies only in the opponent's half. One-handed throw-ins are allowed, and tackling rules protect crutch users. The WAFF World Cup is the premier event.

History

Amputee football grew from rehabilitation programs in the 1980s; the first World Cup tournaments were held from 1986 under Amputee Soccer International, and the WAFF was formed in 2003 as the global governing body. Turkey won the men's 2022 World Cup, beating Angola 4-1 in the final, and the first Women's World Cup was held in 2024.
