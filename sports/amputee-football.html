Amputee Football

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Amputee football (amputee soccer) is football played by teams of athletes with limb amputations or limb differences, using forearm crutches under adapted FIFA-based rules. It is governed internationally by the World Amputee Football Federation (WAFF), founded in November 2003, which organizes the men's and women's Amputee Football World Cup (World Amputee Football Federation [WAFF], n.d.-a). The 20th edition of the men's World Cup was held in San Juan de los Lagos, Mexico, November 13–22, 2026, with 24 teams in six groups; Turkey, the 2022 champion from Istanbul, is the most successful nation in the tournament's history. The women's game reached a milestone in 2024 with the first official women's international matches and a WAFF Women's World Cup (WAFF, n.d.-b). Among the best-known players is Poland's Marcin Oleksy of Warta Poznań, who in 2022 became the first amputee footballer to win the FIFA Puskás Award for the most beautiful goal of the year (WAFF, n.d.-a) (World Amputee Football Federation, n.d.-a) (World Amputee Football Federation, n.d.-b). Amputee football is one of the disability football formats within the wider football family — Football, Blind Football, Cerebral Palsy Football, Powerchair Football, Five-a-side Football, Seven-a-side Football, Futsal, Indoor Soccer, Beach Soccer, Six-man Football, Swamp Soccer, Walking Football, Fire-ball Soccer, Royal Shrovetide Football, Calcio Fiorentino and Cuju.

Within the Para sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Blind Football, Cerebral Palsy Football, Powerchair Football, Para Table Tennis, Para Badminton; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

WAFF rules adapt the FIFA Laws of the Game: teams have seven players — six outfield players plus a goalkeeper — with outfield players having one leg amputated or a comparable limb difference, and goalkeepers one hand or arm. Players use forearm (loftstrand) crutches, which may not be used to strike the ball; prostheses are not worn during play (bilateral amputees may use them). The pitch is smaller — at most 70 x 60 m — and the goals at most 5 x 2.2 x 1 m. Matches last two 25-minute halves, there is no offside rule, substitutions are unlimited, and shin guards are mandatory. One-handed throw-ins are allowed. Contact between a crutch and an opponent's body is a foul, and a crutch striking a player is punished with a red card and a penalty kick (WAFF, n.d.-a).

History

Amputee football grew out of rehabilitation programs in the 1980s: Canadian Don Bennett, inspired in 1982, founded Amputee Soccer International in 1985, and the first international tournament was held in Seattle in 1984. The WAFF was formed in November 2003 as the global governing body and has run World Cups since; Turkey beat Angola 4–1 in the 2022 final in Istanbul. The women's World Cup and the first official women's internationals followed in 2024, and stars such as Marcin Oleksy have brought the sport into the mainstream — his Puskás Award-winning goal in 2022 made him the first amputee footballer to receive the honor (WAFF, n.d.-a; WAFF, n.d.-b).
