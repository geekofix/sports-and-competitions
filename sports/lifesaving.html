Lifesaving

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lifesaving sport (lifesaving sport) is the competitive side of lifesaving: athletes race against the clock in pool and ocean events that simulate real rescues — swimming with fins, towing manikins, negotiating obstacle courses and racing surf craft. It is governed by the International Life Saving Federation (ILS), formed on 24 February 1993 by the merger of the Fédération Internationale de Sauvetage (FIS, founded 1928) and the World Life Saving Society, and the ILS organizes the Lifesaving World Championships every two years.

Rules

Competitions are split into pool (stillwater) events and ocean (beach) events, plus surfboats, inflatable rescue boats and simulated emergency response (SER). Pool events include the 200 m obstacle swim (climbing over and under floating barriers), the 100 m manikin carry, the 50 m manikin tow with fins, the 100 m rescue medley, the 25 m line throw and the 4 x 50 m obstacle relay; each has strict rules on how the manikin or line may be handled, and false starts are penalized. Ocean events include the surf race (swimming out around buoys), the board race, the ski race, the ironman (combined swim, board and ski) and the 4 x 90 m relay combining the disciplines. Points and team scores decide placings, and world records are ratified by the ILS.

History

Organized lifesaving began in the early 20th century as volunteer surf lifesaving clubs on the beaches of Australia and New Zealand, where the Bronze Medallion, the proficiency award for surf lifesaving, was introduced in the early 20th century. The FIS held the first Pool Lifesaving World Championships in Nantes, France, in 1953, and the first combined Lifesaving World Championships took place in 1994 in Cardiff and Newquay, UK. Lifesaving sport has been on the World Games program and is strongest in Australia, New Zealand, Europe and South Africa, with national federations in more than 40 countries.
