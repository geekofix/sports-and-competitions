Para Canoe Marathon

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para canoe marathon is the long-distance paracanoe race for athletes in the KL1, KL2 and KL3 classes (kayak) and VL1–VL3 (va'a, the outrigger canoe with stabilizing float), paddled on flat water with portages — and it is one of the newest disciplines on the international program: paracanoe marathon was contested for the first time at the 2025 ICF Canoe Marathon World Championships in Győr, Hungary, and para canoe is confirmed for The World Games 2029 in Chengdu. At the Paris 2024 Paralympics, paracanoe sprint (200 m) was contested for the first time; the marathon remains a World Games / ICF World Championship discipline rather than a Paralympic event (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the paddling family on this site its closest relatives are Canoe Marathon, Paracanoe, Canoe Sprint, Va'a and Outrigger Canoeing; among the Para sports it sits with Para Rowing and Para Triathlon; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

ICF para canoe marathon follows the sprint rules with adaptations: shorter loop courses (6 km and 10 km at the 2025 world championships) with mandatory portages around dams or obstacles, mass start, and kayak (KL) or va'a (VL, with stabilizing outrigger float) classes KL1/VL1 (no trunk function), KL2/VL2 (partial), KL3/VL3 (near-full function). Races are timed; turns and portages are marshalled; men's and women's events are held in every class (ICF, n.d.).

History

Paracanoe appeared as an exhibition ('paddleability') at the 2009 Canoe Sprint Worlds in Dartmouth, Canada, became official in 2010 (Poznań), and debuted at the Rio 2016 Paralympics as 200 m sprint in KL1–KL3 and VL1–VL3. Paracanoe marathon was contested for the first time at the 2025 ICF Canoe Marathon World Championships in Győr, Hungary (4–7 September 2025), where Poland's Katarzyna Kozikowska won women's KL3 6 km, Germany's Anja Adler won KL2 and Britain's Paralympic champion Chippington won KL1; Hungary's Katalin Varga remains one of the most decorated paracanoe marathon paddlers (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
