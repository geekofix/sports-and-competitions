Paramotoring

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Paramotoring (powered paragliding) is foot-launched motorized paragliding: a backpack engine with propeller drives a paraglider wing, combining the accessibility of paragliding with powered, level flight (FAI, n.d.). It is an FAI air sport under the Microlight and Paramotor Commission (CIMA) and belongs to the air-sports family with paragliding, microlights and gyrocopters.

Figure: Basile Morin. (2024-12-21 16:36:55). Two-seater paramotor in flight over Don Det, Laos [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Two-seater_paramotor_-_Powered_paragliding_in_flight_-_3_quarter_view_a_sunny_afternoon_in_Don_Det_Laos.jpg

Competitive paramotoring covers navigation tasks, precision landing ("bowling landing"), slalom courses with pylons, and economy/distance tasks; the FAI World Paramotor Championship is the pinnacle — the 12th edition was held at Chambley, France, 23–30 August 2025 with 81 pilots from 16 nations across three classes, won overall by 21-year-old Andrea Cecchetto of Italy in the PF1 class, the youngest paramotor world champion ever; France won the team title ahead of Qatar and Czechia (FAI, 2025).

Rules

Paramotor classes are defined by the wing/engine combination: PF1 (foot-launched solo paraglider control), PL1 (landplane, trike) and the new PF1f/PF1s/PL1s classes, including a female class, added to the World League Championship from 2025 (FAI, n.d.). Records — distance, altitude, duration — are ratified by the FAI; in March 2025 British pilot Dan Jones flew a 500 km closed-circuit triangle, beating the 13-year-old record of Germany’s Peter Schulz (FAI, 2025). Safety rules require pilot certification, airspace rules and reserve parachutes on competition tasks (FAI, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same classes, with dedicated women’s classes now ratified (FAI, n.d.).

History

The paramotor was born in the early 1980s when British pilot Mike Byrne combined a powered unit with a paraglider wing — his first flight lasted four and a half minutes on 8 June 1980 in Essex (FAI, n.d.). Ramon Morillas of Spain pioneered the modern backpack unit in the late 1980s, and the FAI created CIMA’s paramotor structure in the 1990s (FAI, n.d.). The first world championships were held in the 2000s, and the sport is now flown on every continent, with Qatar and Spain hosting the World Slalom Championships in the 2020s (FAI, 2025).
