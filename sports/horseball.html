Horseball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Horseball is an equestrian team sport that combines elements of polo, rugby, basketball and netball: two teams of four riders (plus two substitutes) pass a football-size ball fitted with six leather handles and score by throwing it through a 1 m diameter vertical hoop mounted 3.5 m above the ground at each end of the pitch (Fédération Internationale de Horseball [FIHB], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is one of the ten disciplines officially recognized by the International Equestrian Federation (FEI) since 2015, and the FIHB — founded in 1999 — governs the sport worldwide with 18 member nations (as of March 2025), a World Horseball Ranking (WHR) for players, horses, coaches, clubs and referees, and the FIHB Champions League for clubs since 2007 (FEI, n.d.; FIHB, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is mixed-gender by design: senior national teams are mixed, while dedicated ladies' competitions have been held since 2003 (European Ladies Championship) and a women's Champions League since 2014 — among today's leading players are the French internationals at the top of the WHR, and the 2024 Champions League in Le Mans brought together top clubs from eight nations (FIHB, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

A horseball match is played on a rectangular equestrian surface, normally about 65 × 25 m (a 60 × 20 m dressage arena is also permitted), and lasts 20 minutes — two halves of 10 minutes with a 3-minute half-time during which the teams change ends; each team may take one 30-second timeout per half (FIHB, n.d.-a). Each team fields four players on horseback plus two substitutes, who may enter at any break of play; before a goal can be scored, the ball must be passed at least three times between three different players, and a player may hold the ball for less than 10 seconds (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ball is about the size of a football but enclosed in a leather harness with six handles so it can be grabbed while riding; if it falls to the ground, players may pick it up only without changing pace and without dismounting (ramassage) (FIHB, n.d.-a). Goals are scored by throwing the ball through the 1 m vertical hoop, whose bottom is 3.5 m above the ground and which hangs 1 m into the pitch; the team with the most goals wins (FIHB, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Horse welfare is a core rule: the FEI/FIHB regulations require fit, properly equipped horses and penalize dangerous play; teams are mixed-sex at senior level, while ladies', U21, U16 and U10 events provide dedicated categories (FEI, n.d.; FIHB, n.d.-a).

History

Horseball was invented in France by the army captain Clave, a show jumping champion, who borrowed the ball and the goal concept from the Argentine national game pato (with roots in the ancient Afghan game buzkashi); the rules were established in 1978 by a French group under Jean-Paul Depons, a riding instructor and rugby player from Bordeaux, and the French Equestrian Federation accepted horseball as a discipline in the late 1970s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). International organization began in 1992 with an International Commission of Horseball (France, Belgium, Germany, Great Britain, Italy and Portugal) and the first European Cup at the Salon du Cheval in Paris in December 1992; the FIHB was formed in 1999 (FIHB, n.d.-a). The senior European Championship (mixed teams) has been held since 1992, the European Ladies Championship since 2003 in Abano Terme, Italy, the first World Championship in 2008 in Ponte de Lima, Portugal — won by France — with world titles every four years since (2025 in Argentina), and the club-level FIHB Champions League started in 2007 in Stockholm, won that year by Chambly of France; the FEI recognized horseball as a discipline in 2015 and signed a memorandum of understanding with FISU the same year (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; FEI, n.d.).
