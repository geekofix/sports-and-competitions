Underwater Cycling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Underwater cycling is the novelty sport of riding a bicycle at the bottom of a swimming pool: competitors in full scuba gear mount heavily ballasted bicycles and pedal along the pool floor, racing each other or the clock over set distances and slalom courses while judges and buddies watch from the surface (Wikipedia, n.d.). Its best-known showcase is the world championship of underwater cycling staged annually at the bottom of a pool in the Pays de Caux, Normandy, France, as part of the Rencontre Subaquatique Antoine de Saint-Exupéry diving festival, held since 1997 (Normandie Tourisme, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Fins are prohibited — the bike's ordinary pedals do all the work — and standard diving safety rules apply, with buddy supervision throughout (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women and men compete in the same races under identical rules, with results recorded separately by gender (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Events run on the floor of a swimming pool a few metres deep: competitors in mask, regulator and scuba set mount purpose-built bicycles ballasted with weights so they stay on the bottom, and pedal a set distance along marked lanes or a slalom course around markers, without fins (Wikipedia, n.d.). Races are timed from the start signal to the touch at the finish line, and event categories differ by distance and course layout — straight sprint lanes or slalom — rather than by gender, so women's and men's results are recorded in the same races under identical rules (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety rules follow standard diving practice: each rider is paired with a buddy, surface judges and officials supervise, and competitors may surface at any time without penalty in the recreational format (Wikipedia, n.d.). There is no international federation; the rules are set by the hosting diving club or festival, above all the French organizers of the Pays de Caux championship (Normandie Tourisme, n.d.).

History

Underwater cycling is a creature of the late-20th-century novelty-sport era: its organized heart is the Rencontre Subaquatique Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, the diving festival in the Pays de Caux, Normandy, which has staged a world championship of underwater cycling at the bottom of a swimming pool every year since 1997, drawing dozens of divers and international media coverage (Normandie Tourisme, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Diving clubs worldwide have imitated the idea, using pool-bottom cycling as a training challenge for buoyancy control, fitness and confidence as well as a spectacle for open days and festivals (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport remains a niche festival event — contested annually by diving enthusiasts and covered as one of the world's most unusual championships — beloved precisely for the absurdity of pedaling in full scuba gear a few metres underwater (Wikipedia, n.d.).
