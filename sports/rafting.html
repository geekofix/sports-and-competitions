Rafting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rafting is the navigation of rivers in an inflatable raft, ranging from guided recreational descents of whitewater to organized international racing. As a competitive sport it is governed by the International Rafting Federation (IRF), whose World Rafting Championship crowns the world's best teams in disciplines that test flat-out speed, head-to-head aggression, technical slalom and endurance downriver. IRF competitions use crews of four or six paddlers (the R4 and R6 categories) in men's, women's and mixed classes.

Rules

IRF racing is contested in four disciplines at world championship level: the sprint, a timed run from a standing or flying start; the head-to-head, in which two rafts race the same course simultaneously after a mass start; the slalom, a run through hanging gates on whitewater, with time penalties for touched and missed gates; and the downriver, a long-distance mass-start race of roughly 25 to 60 minutes of paddling, historically deciding the world title on combined performance. Teams qualify through national selections and continental championships, and rankings combine the results of all four disciplines. Rafts are standardized by the IRF (self-bailing, with fixed dimensions by category), paddling technique is commanded by a guide, and races demand both power and river-reading skill; the same whitewater grading system (Classes I to VI) governs which rivers are raceable.

History

Whitewater rafting as an organized activity grew from the great expeditions of the early 20th century — notably John Wesley Powell's 1869 Colorado River expedition in the US — through military surplus rubber rafts after World War II and the first commercial outfitters in the 1960s and 70s. Racing followed almost immediately, with unofficial world rafting championships from 1985 onward and the International Rafting Federation founded in the 1990s to unify the rules and, since the 2000s, standardize the modern four-discipline format. The sport has grown worldwide — notable participant nations such as the Czech Republic, Japan, New Zealand, Slovakia, Russia and the United States have collected many of the world titles — with continental circuits in Europe (run by the European Rafting Federation) and beyond and youth events feeding the R4 and R6 elite ranks. The IRF is a member of the Global Association of International Sports Federations and continues to press for inclusion in multisport games.
