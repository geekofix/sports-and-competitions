Piquet

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Piquet is the classic French two-player trick-taking game with a 32-card deck (7–A): players bid (declare) the points they will score in melds (sequences, aces, etc.) and tricks; it is one of the oldest European card games (16th century). Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Écarté, Bezique, Whist; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Meissonier, J.-L.-E, 1861).

Figure: Polybius. (2005-08-05). The piquet deck layout [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piquet_deck.png

Rules

32-card deck; 12 cards each + talon; bidding/declaring (sequences of 3/4/5 ('tierce', 'quatorze', 'quinte') and aces/kings); then play 12 tricks; scoring of declarations and tricks; the higher scorer wins the stake (Meissonier, J.-L.-E, 1861).

History

Piquet appeared in France in the 16th century (Rabelais mentions piquet); Meissonier's 1861 painting 'A Game of Piquet' shows the game; it remains a French classic (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
