Arctic Sports

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Arctic sports — also called Inuit games or traditional Arctic games — are the strength, skill and endurance contests of the Indigenous peoples of the Arctic: the one-foot and two-foot high kick, the Alaskan high kick, the kneel jump, the knuckle hop, the airplane, the one-hand reach, the head pull and arm pull, the blanket toss (nalukataq), the ear pull, the sledge jump, the triple jump, the toe kick, the four-man carry and the "drop the bomb". They are contested at every Arctic Winter Games since 1974, at the World Eskimo-Indian Olympics (WEIO) in Fairbanks, Alaska, since 1961 — with women's events added in 1970 — and at the Native Youth Olympics; alongside them run the Dene Games and winter sports such as Snowshoe Biathlon on this site (Arctic Winter Games [AWG], n.d.-a; World Eskimo-Indian Olympics [WEIO], n.d.). Women compete in all events at the AWG and WEIO, and the legendary high-kicker Lou Lefaive of Alaska is among the most decorated athletes in the history of the games (Arctic Winter Games, n.d.-a) (Arctic Winter Games, n.d.-b).

Within the multi-sport events family on this site, its closest relatives are Arctic Winter Games, Dene Games, Firefighter Games, Electrician Games, Lumberjack Championships; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In the two-foot high kick, the athlete jumps with both feet together and kicks a seal-skin target suspended about 2.6–2.7 m (8.5–9 ft) above a padded platform; the target is raised after each successful kick until only one competitor remains. In the one-foot Alaskan high kick, the athlete kicks with one foot while holding the other. The blanket toss (nalukataq) is a team event: eight throwers toss a teammate high into the air on a sealskin blanket, and both height and a stick landing are judged. The kneel jump, knuckle hop and triple jump test explosive power and balance over measured distances, while the head pull and arm pull are pure strength contests and the ear pull tests grip and pain tolerance (AWG, n.d.-a; WEIO, n.d.).

History

The games descend from the hunting and survival skills of Arctic peoples — jumping, pulling and balancing on ice and snow — and were formalized as competitions in the 20th century: the World Eskimo-Indian Olympics began in Fairbanks in 1961, with women's events added in 1970, and the Arctic Winter Games have included Arctic sports in their program since the first Games in 1970. Lou Lefaive of Alaska, a multiple high-kick world champion, is one of the most iconic athletes of the tradition (AWG, n.d.-b; WEIO, n.d.).
