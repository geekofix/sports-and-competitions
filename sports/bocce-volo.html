Bocce Volo

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bocce volo (boule lyonnaise in France) is the throwing specialty of the boules family: players take a run-up and throw the ball through the air to hit the pallino or an opponent's ball directly. It is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Boules (FIB) and contested at the FIB World Championships, under the umbrella of the IOC-recognized World Pétanque & Bowls Federation.

Rules

Under FIB/CBI regulations for volo, the player may take a run-up of up to four steps and must throw from behind the C-C1 line (the volo throwing area at the front of the lane); the ball must first strike the ground within 50 cm of the target it is aimed at (the pallino or an opponent's ball) to be a valid hit. After each throw, balls are measured and points awarded: one point per ball closer to the pallino than the opponent's closest. Matches are played to a set score (commonly 15-18 points at elite level) over a lane of about 27.5 m, in singles, doubles and team formats, men and women.

History

Volo (Italian for "flight") developed in Italy and southeastern France (where it is called boule lyonnaise) in the 19th century as the most spectacular of the boules specialties. The FIB was one of the three founding federations of the CMSB (now WPBF) in Monaco in 1985, and FIB World Championships for volo/lyonnaise have been held since the 1940s-50s at national level and internationally under the WPBF umbrella. Lyon is the traditional heartland of the game.
