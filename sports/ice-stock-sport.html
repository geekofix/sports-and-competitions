Ice Stock Sport

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ice stock sport (German: Eisstockschießen, also called Bavarian curling or simply Stockschießen) is the Alpine winter sport in which players slide ice stocks — a gliding sole with a handle about 30 cm long, typically 4–6 kg — over an ice surface toward a rubber-ring target called the Daube, or in distance shooting for maximum range (International Federation Icestocksport [IFI], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Federation Icestocksport (IFI), founded in 1950 in Zurich, which organizes the European Championships (since 1951) and the World Championships for men and women (since 1983); the 14th World Championship was held in 2022 in Ritten/Klobenstein, Italy, and the 15th in 2025 in Kapfenberg and Stanz, Austria — where India won bronze (IFI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The disciplines contested internationally are team target shooting, individual target shooting and distance shooting; in team play, two teams of four players alternate over six turns (Kehren), each player sliding one stock per turn, and the team whose stock lies closest to the Daube scores 3 stock points plus 2 points for every further own stock closer than the opponent's next stock; the team with more stock points takes 2 game points (1 each on a tie), and the most game points wins the match (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Ice stock sport was a demonstration sport at the 1936 and 1964 Winter Olympics (not an Olympic medal sport in 2026), the summer tarmac version is called Stocksport, and the sport is part of India's Khelo India Winter Games program; women compete in team and individual events at the European and World Championships on equal terms with men (IFI, n.d.-a, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (International Federation Icestocksport, n.d.-a) (International Federation Icestocksport, n.d.-b).

Rules

All equipment must be IFI-registered: stocks carry a type letter for their weight class (L, M, P, E) and interchangeable soles (e.g., IFI-registered rubber soles for ice or summer floors) are permitted, with the shortest distance between stock and Daube measured by feeler gauge (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; IFI, n.d.-a). In the team game, the Daube is placed on the centre cross about 24.5 m from the throwing line at the start of each turn; the teams shoot until all stocks are played, always the team not holding the best position, and stocks outside the target field are removed; after six turns the team with the higher stock-point total receives 2 game points, a tie gives 1 each, and equal game points are broken by the stock quotient (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Individual target shooting ranks players by accumulated stock points over several rounds, and distance shooting measures the longest valid throw; fouls include crossing the throwing line before release and touching the Daube illegally (IFI, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate team and individual classifications with identical rules; junior categories (U23) exist (IFI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The first written mention of ice stock sport dates to shortly before Christmas 1192, when messengers brought news of the capture of Richard the Lionheart to Leopold V, Duke of Austria, who was playing ice stock on the frozen Danube at Vienna; the game was probably also played in Scandinavia by the 13th century, and Pieter Brueghel the Elder's painting The Hunters in the Snow (1565) shows players on a frozen pond (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It became a formal sport only in the 1930s: a German federation was founded in 1934 and German championships began in 1936 (IFI, n.d.-b). The IFI was founded in 1950 in Zurich, the European Championships began in 1951 and the World Championships in 1983 for both men and women; ice stock sport was demonstrated at the 1936 Garmisch-Partenkirchen Winter Olympics (on Lake Riessersee) and again at the 1964 Innsbruck Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; IFI, n.d.-b).
