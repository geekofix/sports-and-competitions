Street Luge

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Street luge is a gravity-powered downhill racing sport in which the rider lies supine, feet first, on a long low board — an extended skateboard with trucks, no mechanical brakes — down closed mountain roads or city streets at speeds that can exceed 100 km/h (IGSA, n.d.). It is governed internationally by the International Gravity Sports Association (IGSA) and the International Downhill Federation (IDF), which sanction world cup races and world championships, and it was an X Games event from 1997 to 2001 under IGSA rules (Wikipedia, n.d.). Around 1,200 active licensed riders compete worldwide, and the sport is open to men and women in the same events, with separate men's and women's world rankings (IGSA, n.d.).

Rules

A street luge board is at most 122 cm (48 in) long and 30 cm (12 in) wide with four wheels on skateboard trucks; the rider lies on the board with head uphill, steers by leaning with shoulders and legs, and has no brakes — speed control is by carving, sliding the board sideways, or a controlled foot-down slide (IGSA, n.d.). Mandatory safety equipment is a full-face hard-shell helmet, a leather or Kevlar one-piece suit, gloves and shoes, plus front and rear padding on the board; courses of 0.5 to 3 miles (1–5 km) are closed to traffic, marshaled and inspected before each event (IGSA, n.d.). Race formats include single and double elimination heats of two, four or six riders, timed trials, points series and mass-start runs limited to 20 riders; the fastest time or the elimination order decides, with fouls such as leaving the course or dangerous overtaking penalized by disqualification (Wikipedia, n.d.). The classic luge variant uses a wooden board under 49 x 12 in with four wheels and is raced on tighter courses. There are no separate women's rules — women and men race together under identical equipment and safety rules, with women's world records tracked separately (IGSA, n.d.).

History

Street luge grew out of the skateboard and luge culture of 1970s Southern California, where riders first lay down on longboards on mountain roads; the first organized street luge events were the EDI Gravity Games in the 1990s, and the IGSA was founded in the late 1990s to sanction racing, bringing street luge to the X Games from 1997 to 2001 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The classic luge class preserves the original wooden-board form, and the sport continues as a world cup series under IGSA and IDF with around 1,200 active riders (IGSA, n.d.).
