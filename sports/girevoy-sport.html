Girevoy Sport

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Girevoy sport (Russian: girevoy sport, "kettlebell sport") is the competitive lifting of cast-iron kettlebells (giri) for repetitions within a set time, standardized in the Soviet Union in the 1960s — the first rules date to 1962 — and recognized as a sport in the USSR in 1985, with an international federation founded in 1992 (International Union of Kettlebell Lifting [IUKL], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The classic disciplines are the snatch (one kettlebell, lifted from the ground to overhead lockout with one hand change allowed in the 10-minute set), the jerk (two kettlebells from the chest to overhead lockout), the long cycle (clean plus jerk with two kettlebells), and the biathlon (jerk then snatch, 10 minutes each), alongside marathon formats — 30-minute half marathon and 60-minute marathon — and 12-minute sets and relays (IUKL, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed internationally by the IUKL and the World Kettlebell Sport Federation (WKSF, founded 2017), with the IKMF covering marathon lifting; the WKSF Kettlebell World Championship 2026 was held in Chișinău, Moldova, from 4–8 June 2026 with 500+ athletes from about 30 countries, and the IUKL World Championship in Riga, Latvia, in 2026 (WKSF, n.d.; IUKL, n.d.-b). Weight classes at the IUKL Worlds 2026 were men's −63, −68, −73, −78, −85, −95 and +95 kg and women's −58, −63, −68 and +68 kg, with events including 10-minute long cycle, biathlon, one-arm long cycle (OALC), 10- and 12-minute snatch, the 30-minute half-marathon snatch and 4 × 3-minute relays, plus para-athlete categories (IUKL, n.d.-b) (International Union of Kettlebell Lifting, n.d.-a) (International Union of Kettlebell Lifting, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the Russian and Eastern European lifters dominating the IUKL and WKSF podiums in long cycle and snatch, while on the men's side the 2026 world champions in Riga and Chișinău included record-chasing lifters such as Poland's Maciej Łuczak, whose 2016 world record of 1,207 snatch repetitions in 60 minutes with a 24 kg kettlebell still stands among the sport's benchmark marks (IUKL, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

In all girevoy sport disciplines, the athlete performs as many technically valid repetitions as possible within the time limit, with each repetition worth 1 point; the classic competition set lasts 10 minutes, with 30-minute half-marathon and 60-minute marathon formats in which hand switches are unlimited (IUKL, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the snatch, the kettlebell is lifted from between the legs to a straight-arm overhead lockout in one continuous motion; only one hand change is allowed in the 10-minute set (marathon: unlimited) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the jerk and long cycle, two kettlebells are racked on the chest and pressed overhead with a straight-arm lockout; in the long cycle each rep begins with a clean of both bells to the chest (IUKL, n.d.-a). The biathlon combines a 10-minute jerk set and a 10-minute snatch set, the scores being added (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Judges count only repetitions with full lockout and no press-out; rest is allowed only with the kettlebell(s) on the platform (IUKL, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate weight classes — the kettlebell weights in competition range from 8 kg to 32+ kg, with 24 kg and 32 kg standard for men and 16 kg and 24 kg for women at elite level — under identical technical rules; para-athlete classes and junior categories also exist (IUKL, n.d.-b).

History

Kettlebells (giri) appeared in Russia in the 18th century as weights for weighing goods at markets, and from the 19th century they were used by strongmen and circus athletes; the first kettlebell lifting competitions were held in the Russian Empire around 1900, and the sport was standardized in the Soviet Union in the 1960s, with the first official rules published in 1962 and the first USSR championship in 1969 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Girevoy sport was officially recognized in the USSR in 1985, and the international federation was founded in 1992 with 56 member countries today (IUKL, n.d.-a). Among the icons of the sport are the multi-time world and Olympic-adjacent strongman Valery Fedorenko of Russia, a multiple world champion in girevoy sport who popularized the kettlebell worldwide in the 2000s, and modern record holders such as Maciej Łuczak of Poland (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
