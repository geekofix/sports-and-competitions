Canal Vaulting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canal vaulting — known in Friesland as fierljeppen and in the Netherlands as polsstokverspringen — is a traditional Dutch sport in which an athlete runs with a long pole, plants it in the bed of a canal and swings across the water. The pole is 8 to 13 meters long and is now made of carbon fiber. The sport is governed in Friesland by the Fierljep Bûtenriks Feriening (FLB) together with the national fierljep bond, and polsstokverspringen was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2018. A German variant, the Pultstockspringen, is practiced in East Frisia.

Rules

The vaulter takes a run-up along the bank, plants the pole in a clay-lined spot in the canal bed and swings across the water; only the width of the canal counts as the result. Competitions are held in age and sex categories, and the distance is measured to the point where the vaulter lands or falls back into the water. The men's record stands at 22.21 m (Jaco de Groot, 2017) and the women's record at 18.19 m (Marrit van der Wal, 2019). The Dutch championship has been held in Grijpskerk since the 1970s, and the sport is also contested at international meetings of traditional sports.

History

Fierljeppen developed from the practical need of Frisian farmers to cross the region's many ditches and canals with a pole. The first official competition was held in Winsum on 10 August 1957, and organized fierljeppen with standardized rules spread from the 1960s onward. In 2018 UNESCO inscribed polsstokverspringen on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognizing the living tradition in Friesland. The related Pultstockspringen in East Frisia keeps the older vaulting style alive across the German border.
