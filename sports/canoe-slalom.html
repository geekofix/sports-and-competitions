Canoe Slalom

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canoe slalom is whitewater racing through a course of hanging gates on rapids, governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF). Paddlers in kayaks (K1) and Canadian canoes (C1) negotiate the course downstream and upstream, and the fastest time wins. Since Paris 2024 the program also includes kayak cross, a head-to-head race in which four paddlers start together and the first across the line advances. The ICF Canoe Slalom World Championships and World Cup are the premier events.

Rules

Under the ICF Canoe Slalom Competition Rules, a run is penalized with 2 seconds for touching a gate and 50 seconds for missing a gate or passing it in the wrong direction; the competitor takes two timed runs in qualifying and one in each knockout round, with the best time counting. Gates are hung from cables across the river and colored green (downstream) or red (upstream). In kayak cross, four boats race simultaneously down a shorter course with jumps and obstacles; the top two advance from each heat until the final. Olympic slalom has been contested since Munich 1972, with kayak cross added at Paris 2024.

History

Slalom canoeing began as a timing game between paddlers on Alpine rivers in the early 20th century. The first ICF Canoe Slalom World Championships were held in Geneva in 1949, and the discipline joined the Olympic program at Munich 1972 (returning permanently in 1992). Course design has become ever more technical, and the addition of kayak cross at Paris 2024 brought a spectator-friendly sprint format to the Olympics. Stars of the sport include Australia's Jessica Fox and Great Britain's Joseph Clarke.
