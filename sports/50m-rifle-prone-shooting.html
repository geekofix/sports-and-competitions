50m Rifle Prone Shooting

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 50 m rifle prone is the ISSF's classic precision rifle event: 60 shots from the prone position at 50 m with a .22 Long Rifle single-shot rifle, scored ring by ring on an electronic target; it is governed by the International Shooting Sport Federation under its rifle technical rules (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was an Olympic event for men until London 2012 — the women's event has never been on the Olympic programme, but both genders contest it at the World Championships and World Cups (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

At the 2026 Asian Rifle/Pistol Championships in New Delhi (2–14 February 2026), Kazakhstan's Islam Satpayev won the men's 50 m rifle prone gold and India's Priya Brijpal Singh the women's (Wikipedia, n.d.-d). Among the athletes defining the current era, the women lead: Priya Brijpal Singh (IND), together with India's Sift Kaur Samra and Tejaswini Sawant, who also won the team title, while the men's side is led by Satpayev and by Norway's Jenny Vatne, a team world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Rules

Under the ISSF rifle rules the 50 m prone match is 60 shots in the prone position, with the rifle supported by the elbows and sling (no rests); the course of fire has a time limit at major championships, scoring is whole-ring up to 600 with inner tens as X, and since 2018 the Olympic-style final has been discontinued — placings are decided by score, then by the number of inner tens (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The rifle must be .22 LR single-shot, within the regulated weight and dimension limits, with a minimum trigger pull and open or aperture sights; shooting clothing (jacket, trousers, shoes, gloves) must pass equipment control with ISSF seals under the 2026 rulebook (ISSF, n.d.-c; ISSF, 2026). Men and women compete in separate classifications, and junior (under 21) categories exist; the Olympic rifle events are 10 m air rifle and 50 m three positions (link below), which include a prone phase (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The 50 m rifle prone is based on the traditional English Match, which also consisted of 60 shots in the prone position with a .22 rifle at distances between 50 yards and 100 m; it was added to the World Championship programme at Stockholm 1929 and was an Olympic event for men until London 2012, where Belarus's Sergei Martynov won the last Olympic title (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

Sergei Martynov, pictured, won the last Olympic 50 m prone title at London 2012 after a silver at Sydney 2000; other icons: America's Matthew Emmons, Athens 2004 gold medallist, and Ukraine's Artur Ayvazyan, Beijing 2008 champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-b, n.d.-c).
