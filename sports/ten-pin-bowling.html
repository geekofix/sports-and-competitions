Ten-Pin Bowling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ten-pin bowling is the indoor sport of rolling a drilled ball down a glossy wooden lane to knock down ten pins standing in a triangle: strikes, spares and the classic 300 perfect game define its scoring. Played by tens of millions of people in thousands of centers worldwide, it is organized from league night to the professional tours of the Professional Bowlers Association (PBA) and the international federation.

Rules

Games run ten frames: each frame allows up to two rolls to knock down all ten pins; a strike (all ten on the first roll) scores ten plus the next two rolls, a spare (ten over two rolls) scores ten plus the next roll, and the tenth frame awards extra balls for strikes or spares, making the maximum game 300. Equipment rules govern the ball (up to 16 pounds, with regulated balance and hole drilling), the pin (standard weight and dimensions), the oiled lane pattern (regulation distance of 60 feet of oil in professional events) and fouling: crossing the foul line counts zero. League play uses 3- to 5-player teams alternating frames, match formats race to a set game total or pinfall, and professional events add qualifying blocks, match play brackets and televised stepladder finals.

History

Bowling's ancestry runs from ancient Egyptian and German religious games to European kegling carried to America, where ten pins became the standard and pinboys were replaced by automatic pinsetters after 1946, the technological leap that made the modern bowling center possible. The sport boomed in mid-twentieth-century America as a family pastime and professional organization matured (the PBA was founded in 1958), and the international federation staged world championships and pushed Olympic recognition while professional women's and senior tours grew. The modern sport features high-tech ball surfaces and engineered oil patterns, a PBA Tour with stars, majors and millions of viewers, and a worldwide league infrastructure that keeps the 300 game the sport's grail.
