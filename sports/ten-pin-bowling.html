Ten-Pin Bowling

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ten-pin bowling is the game of rolling a heavy ball down a wooden lane to knock down ten pins standing in a triangle at the far end; the world governing body is the International Bowling Federation (IBF, bowling.sport), founded in 1952 in Hamburg as the FIQ, renamed World Bowling in 2014 and IBF in 2020, with Sheikh Talal M. Al-Sabah as president (International Bowling Federation, n.d.). In the United States the United States Bowling Congress (USBC) issues the playing rules used in league and tournament play (United States Bowling Congress, n.d.), while the professional tours are the PBA for men and the PWBA for women, both played under USBC rules with specified lane oil patterns (Professional Bowlers Association, n.d.). The 2026 season brought major wins for — women first — Jillian Martin (U.S. Women's Open and PWBA Tour Championship), Erin McCarthy (USBC Queens, her third PWBA major) and New Hui Fen (U.S. Open, her second major), and for men EJ Tackett (U.S. Open) and Boog Krol, who won the 2026 USBC Masters over Tackett 196–195 (Professional Bowlers Association, n.d.; United States Bowling Congress, n.d.). Ten-pin differs from the other pin-bowling variants — nine-pin bowling, candlepin bowling and duckpin bowling — in pin count and shape, ball size and finger holes, and in allowing only two deliveries per frame.

Rules

A bowling lane is 60 ft (18.29 m) from the foul line to the head pin and about 41.5 inches (105 cm) wide, with the ten pins set in an equilateral triangle in the pin deck; the ball may weigh at most 16 lb (7.26 kg) and have a diameter of at most 8.595 inches (United States Bowling Congress, n.d.). A game consists of ten frames; in each frame the bowler gets up to two deliveries (a third in the tenth frame if the first is a strike or the first two complete a spare) (United States Bowling Congress, n.d.). Scoring: a strike (all ten pins on the first ball) earns 10 plus the next two balls, a spare 10 plus the next ball, an open frame just the pins knocked down, so a perfect game of twelve strikes is 300 (United States Bowling Congress, n.d.). Crossing the foul line is a foul and the delivery counts zero (Wikipedia, n.d.). USBC playing rules govern equipment, lane specifications and conduct at sanctioned events, and the PBA and PWBA tours apply the same rules with tournament-specific oil patterns (Professional Bowlers Association, n.d.). Men and women compete under identical rules on the same lanes — on separate PBA and PWBA tours — alongside youth, adult and senior (50+) divisions (Professional Bowlers Association, n.d.).

History

Bowling-like games with balls and pins are documented in ancient Egypt, and the modern ten-pin game took shape in 19th-century America: the tenth pin was added in the 1840s in Connecticut to evade laws banning the nine-pin gambling game, and the American Bowling Congress, founded in 1895, standardized the rules (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Women's International Bowling Congress was founded in 1917, the two merged with other bodies into the USBC in 2005, and internationally the FIQ was founded in 1952 in Hamburg (International Bowling Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Record icons of the professional era include Walter Ray Williams Jr. (b. 1943, a record 47 PBA titles), Earl Anthony (1938–2020, 41 PBA titles) and, on the women's side, Lisa Wagner, one of the most successful PWBA players ever (Wikipedia, n.d.).
