Cricket

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cricket is a bat-and-ball team sport played between two teams of eleven on a large oval field with a 22-yard (20.12 m) pitch at the center. The batting side scores runs by hitting the ball and running between the wickets, while the bowling and fielding side tries to dismiss the batters (bowled, caught, LBW, run out and others). It is governed by the International Cricket Council (ICC), founded in 1909 as the Imperial Cricket Conference and headquartered in Dubai, which sanctions the Laws of Cricket (maintained by the MCC at Lord's) and runs the World Cup and World Test Championship.

Rules

The Laws of Cricket govern play: the bowler delivers six legal balls per over from one end, and the fielding side changes ends each over. Batters can be out bowled, caught, leg before wicket (LBW), stumped, hit wicket or run out. The batting side's innings ends after ten dismissals or when the overs limit is reached; formats are Test cricket (up to five days, unlimited overs with two innings per side), One Day Internationals (50 overs per side) and T20 (20 overs per side). Fielding restrictions, powerplays, the wide and no-ball laws, and the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method for rain-affected matches are defined in the ICC playing conditions.

History

Modern cricket originated in England in the 16th-18th centuries and was codified in the Laws of Cricket first published by the MCC in 1744. The first international match was played in 1844 between the USA and Canada, and the first Test match in 1877 between England and Australia at Melbourne. The Ashes series began in 1882. The ICC was founded in 1909, expanded to full members over the 20th century, and launched the Cricket World Cup in 1975 (won first by the West Indies) and the T20 World Cup in 2007. Today cricket is hugely popular in South Asia, Australia, England, the Caribbean and beyond, with the Indian Premier League as the richest league.
