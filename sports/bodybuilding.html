Bodybuilding

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bodybuilding is the sport of developing muscle mass, symmetry and definition through progressive resistance training, nutrition and rest, judged in posed rounds at competitions. The world's premier events are the IFBB Professional League's Olympia Fitness and Performance Weekend (IFBB Professional League, n.d.-a). At the 62nd Mr. Olympia in Las Vegas (September 24–27, 2026), Nick Walker of the United States won the Men's Open title — his first — with a $600,000 first prize, ahead of Samson Dauda and Derek Lunsford (IFBB Professional League, n.d.-b).

On the women's side, Andrea Shaw of the United States won her record seventh Ms. Olympia title at the same 2026 weekend — the most successful female bodybuilder in history — while Jasmine Gonzalez won Bikini, Eduarda Bezerra took second in Wellness, Michelle Fredua-Mensah second in Fitness, Shealynn Burnett became the first Fit Model champion and Ryan Terry won his fourth straight Men's Physique title; in total twelve divisions were contested (IFBB Professional League, n.d.-b).

Rules

Competitors perform mandatory poses (front double biceps, side chest, back double biceps, etc. — seven for men, five for women) plus a free posing routine to music, judged on muscularity, symmetry, proportion, conditioning and presentation; prejudging determines the top 15, and the finals include a posedown (IFBB Professional League, n.d.-a). Divisions include Men's Open bodybuilding, Classic Physique, Men's Physique, 212, and for women Ms. Olympia (Open bodybuilding), Bikini, Fitness, Figure, Wellness, Women's Physique and Fit Model. Qualification runs through national federations of the IFBB (205 member federations worldwide) via pro-card events; amateur world championships crown the World Amateur champion. Men's and women's divisions are separate with different pose requirements and, in Physique/Classic, height-weight classes; drug testing follows the World Anti-Doping Code with in-competition and out-of-competition testing (IFBB Professional League, n.d.-a).

History

Modern bodybuilding was founded by Eugen Sandow (1867–1925), the Prussian-born strongman who staged the first physique contests in London in the 1890s and popularized posing and muscular ideals; Sandow's 1901 show at London's Royal Albert Hall drew 15,000 spectators. The IFBB was founded in 1946 in Canada by brothers Joe and Ben Weider, and the first Mr. Olympia was held in 1965, won by Larry Scott. Arnold Schwarzenegger won seven Mr. Olympia titles (1970–1975, 1980), Lee Haney and Ronnie Coleman share the record eight wins each (1984–1991, 1998–2005), and on the women's side Rachel McLish won the first Ms. Olympia in 1980 and Corinna Everson won six consecutive titles from 1984 to 1989 (IFBB Professional League, n.d.-b).
