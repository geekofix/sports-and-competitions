50m Rifle 3 Positions Shooting

Basic Information Rules History

Basic Information

50m Rifle Three Positions is the classic ISSF rifle event, with shots fired from the prone, standing and kneeling positions at 50 meters. It has been on the Olympic program since Rome 1960 (removed for Tokyo 2020, reinstated for Paris 2024) and anchors the ISSF World Championships and World Cups.

Rules

Under ISSF rules shooters use a .22 Long Rifle (5.6 mm) rimfire rifle at 50 m. The qualification is 40 shots in each position — prone, standing and kneeling — for 120 shots in total. The top eight advance to a final of further strings in each position, with the lowest scorer eliminated after set stages until one athlete remains. Equipment rules differ by position: a sling may be used for prone and kneeling, but in standing the rifle is held unsupported. Variants: the ISSF also runs 300m Rifle Three Positions at the World Championships — the same three positions at 300 m with centerfire rifles — so the discipline exists in both 50 m and 300 m versions.

History

The event has been an Olympic staple since 1960. Wu Xiaoxuan of China won the first Olympic gold medal ever for China in this event at Los Angeles 1984. Other legends include Matt Emmons of the USA (gold Athens 2004), Sergei Martynov of Belarus (gold London 2012) and Niccolò Campriani of Italy (gold Rio 2016).
