Bottle-kicking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bottle-kicking is an ancient Leicestershire custom played every Easter Monday in the village of Hallaton: teams from Hallaton and neighboring Medbourne battle across a mile-long playing area to move wooden barrels ("bottles") to the opposing team's stream. It is claimed to be one of Britain's oldest continuing sporting events, with roots possibly predating Christianity.

Rules

The game is best of three "bottles": two small barrels filled with beer and one solid wooden "dummy" painted red and white. Starting from the Buttercross on Hallaton's village green, each village tries to move the bottles across the other village's stream at the far end of the mile-long course — by any means, in a huge scrummage through fields, hedges and the brook. The first village to get two of the three bottles over the opponent's boundary wins; the winning team takes the beer barrels home to drink on the village green. The day also features the hare pie scramble and penny loaves.

History

Local legend says the custom began when two Hallaton ladies were saved from a raging bull by a startled hare; in gratitude they gave land to the church on condition the vicar provide a hare pie, penny loaves and ale each Easter Monday — and the rivalry with Medbourne, which once stole the beer, began. Records date to the late 18th century, and a vicar's attempted ban in 1790 was defeated by the slogan "No pie, no parson." The event was cancelled only in 2001 (foot-and-mouth) and 2020 (pandemic), and draws thousands of spectators.
