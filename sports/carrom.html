Carrom

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Carrom is a tabletop board game from South Asia in which players flick a striker disc with a finger to shoot carrom men into one of the four corner pockets of a square board; a set has 19 pieces — nine white, nine black and one red queen — plus a heavier striker (ICF, n.d.). It is played casually across India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh and by the diaspora worldwide, and competitively under the International Carrom Federation (ICF), an association governed by Swiss law founded on 15 October 1988 at the first World Carrom Congress in Madras (Chennai), India, by delegates of India, Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Malaysia, Germany and Switzerland; the ICF is headquartered in Zurich, its president is Josef Meyer, and as of November 2024 it had 19 member associations plus continental confederations such as the Asian Carrom Confederation (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The unified Laws of Carrom were adopted at the second ICF Congress in Malé, Maldives, in 1991 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The flagship event is the World Carrom Championship: the 9th edition was held at the Grand Hotel Le Fonti in Chianciano Terme, Italy, from 30 November to 6 December 2026, organized by the Federazione Italiana Carrom under ICF aegis with the patronage of the city, with team, singles and doubles competitions; the federation also runs the Carrom World Cup (5th edition in Chuncheon, South Korea, 2018) and the ICF Cup (8th edition in Pune, India, 2019) (CWC 2026, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the leading players of the modern era, the women's stars come first: Rashmi Kumari of India — the "Queen of Carrom," a three-time world champion (most recently in 2022) with a record 12 national titles, playing internationally since 1992 — S. Apoorva, singles winner at the 2016 World Championship, the 2018 World Cup and the 2019 ICF Cup, and Kajal Kumari, the first woman to win the overall combined Swiss League title (2018); on the men's side, Nishantha Fernando of Sri Lanka (2012 world champion), Chamil Cooray of Sri Lanka (two-time Swiss League winner) and Prashant More of India (2018 World Cup men's singles champion) (Wikipedia, n.d.) Rules

Under the ICF Laws of Carrom, the board's playing surface is a square of 73.5–74 cm with a wooden frame and bumpers and a pocket of about 4.5 cm in diameter in each corner; the 19 pieces are arranged with the red queen in the center circle and the white and black men alternating in the two concentric circles around it (ICF, n.d.). The carrom men are 3.02–3.18 cm in diameter, 7–9 mm thick and weigh 5.0–5.5 g; the striker is about 4.1 cm in diameter and weighs roughly 15 g (ICF, n.d.). Players draw lots for their color (white or black) and take turns flicking the striker from the baseline — placed between the two arrows — to pocket their own men; only the striker may be touched by hand, and it must be placed on the baseline for each shot (ICF, n.d.). Each pocketed man scores one point and the queen three points, but the queen must be "covered" by immediately pocketing one of the player's own men, otherwise she is returned to the board (ICF, n.d.). Pocketing the striker is a foul: the striker is returned and one of the player's already-pocketed men comes back onto the board, and the turn passes (ICF, n.d.). A game is decided at 25 points or eight boards, whichever comes first; international singles and team matches are typically played as best-of-three-board encounters, and men's and women's singles, doubles and team events are all contested at world championships (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Carrom originated in the Indian subcontinent — its precise birthplace is debated between India and Pakistan — and was popular at the courts of Indian maharajas in the 18th century before spreading through South Asia and, with the diaspora, to the United Kingdom, North America, the Middle East and beyond (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitive play was organized internationally only in the late 20th century: on 15 October 1988, delegates of India, Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Malaysia, Germany and Switzerland met in Madras and founded the International Carrom Federation, adopting an international set of rules; the unified Laws of Carrom followed at the 1991 congress in Malé (Wikipedia, n.d.). The World Carrom Championship has been held since 1991 (editions in 1991, 1995, 2000, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016 and 2022), alongside the Carrom World Cup (since 2001) and the ICF Cup (since 1989); the 9th World Championship takes place in Chianciano Terme, Italy, in November–December 2026 (ICF, n.d.; CWC 2026, 2026). Women have been part of international carrom from the first world championships, and the most decorated player of either sex is Rashmi Kumari of Bihar, India — three world titles, 12 national titles and the nickname "Queen of carrom" (Wikipedia, n.d.).
