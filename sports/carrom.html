Carrom

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Carrom is a tabletop board game from South Asia in which players flick a striker with their finger to shoot carrom men (discs) into one of the four corner pockets. A set has 19 pieces: nine white, nine black and one red queen, plus a heavier striker. It is hugely popular in India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, and is played competitively under the International Carrom Federation (ICF), founded in 1988 in Chennai with its office in Zurich, which runs the World Carrom Championship and the Carrom World Cup.

Rules

Under the ICF rules, the board's playing surface is a square of 73.5 to 74 cm with wooden bumpers and a pocket in each corner. Players take turns flicking the striker from the baseline to pocket their assigned color; the queen must be pocketed and then "covered" by pocketing one of the player's own men immediately after. Pocketing the striker is a foul and costs a man; the first player to pocket all their men wins the board, and matches are usually best of three boards. The 8th World Carrom Championship was held in 2022.

History

Carrom's origins are debated, with claims linking it to games from the Indian subcontinent and to a game invented in the 1870s by a maharaja's courtier, but it became standardized in the 20th century in India and Sri Lanka. The International Carrom Federation was formed at the first World Carrom Congress in October 1988 in Madras (Chennai), when delegates from India, Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Malaysia, Germany and Switzerland adopted international rules. Today the ICF has 19 member associations across five continents.
