Rogaining

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rogaining is a team endurance sport of long-distance cross-country navigation: teams of two to five people plan a route across wilderness terrain to visit as many checkpoints as possible within a time limit — championship rogaines last 24 hours, with shorter 3-, 6-, 8-, 12- and 15-hour variants — and the team with the highest score wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed by the International Rogaining Federation (IRF), based in Melbourne, Australia, whose Rules of Rogaining (current edition 1 January 2024) set the technical standards for national championships and the World Rogaining Championships (IRF, n.d.). The World Championships were first held in 1992 in Beechworth, Australia, ran biennially from 1996 to 2012, annually from 2012 to 2017 and biennially again since; as of January 2026 the IRF had ten member associations, with Julie Quinn of Australia serving as interim president from 4 July 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Competition classes are Men's, Women's and Mixed teams, plus age categories Youth (up to 23), Veteran (45+), Super Veteran (55+) and Ultra Veteran (65+) — women have been world champions since the first edition in 1992, when the Australian pair Drusilla Patkin and Alexandra Tyson won the women's class (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the 2026 European Rogaining Championships in Orlické Záhoří, Czech Republic, with 213 teams, the Estonian pair Timmo Tammemäe and Sander Linnus won the men's open class, the Estonian women Mariann Sulg and Kersti Jääger won the women's class, and a Ukrainian mixed team won the mixed class (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the IRF Rules of Rogaining, all championship rogaines last 24 hours and start and finish around noon so that daylight is balanced before and after the night period (IRF, n.d.). Teams of two to five choose which checkpoints to visit; each checkpoint is worth points based on its tens digit — a checkpoint numbered 31 is worth 30 points — so distant, harder controls score more, and the map area for a 24-hour event is around 250 km² on flat terrain (IRF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Maps are distributed only shortly before the start, forcing genuine route planning in the field, and each checkpoint has a hash house where teams stamp their pass card (IRF, n.d.). Teams are penalized for late return — under the championship rules the score is reduced for every minute past the deadline and a team more than 30 minutes late is disqualified (IRF, n.d.). Mandatory equipment includes a first-aid kit, water, food, whistle, torch and warm clothing, and competitors must carry a mobile phone; there are age classes Youth (under 23), Veteran (45 and over), Super Veteran (55 and over) and Ultra Veteran (65 and over) alongside the open class, and the three gender classes — Men's, Women's and Mixed — compete for separate titles at the World and European Championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; IRF, n.d.). Both women and men compete in all classes, and mixed teams of women and men are a standard category (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

The name "rogaine" was coined in Australia in 1947, reportedly as a humorous backronym for the first event, and the sport spread through Australian bushwalking clubs in the following decades, with monthly rogaines held near full moons and annual state and national championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Australian Rogaining Association coordinated national development, and the International Rogaining Federation was later established to govern the sport worldwide, based in Melbourne (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first World Rogaining Championship took place in 1992 in Beechworth, Victoria, won in the men's class by Australians James Russell and Michael Walters and in the women's class by Drusilla Patkin and Alexandra Tyson; the event has since grown to several hundred teams, with strong scenes in Estonia, Latvia, the Czech Republic, Ukraine and the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). European Rogaining Championships have been held annually since 2003, with the 2026 edition in the Czech Republic (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
