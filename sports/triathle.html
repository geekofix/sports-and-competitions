Triathle

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Triathle is the fast, spectator-friendly development sport of the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM): athletes race a continuous sequence of laser-pistol shooting, swimming and running — a pack start of up to 25 m to the shooting station, five targets hit with a laser pistol within 50 seconds, a 25–50 m swim, and a 200–800 m run, repeated two to four times depending on the age group, with the first athlete across the finish line winning (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Triathle was approved by the UIPM in November 2012 and made its international debut at the first UIPM Biathle/Triathle World Championships in Limassol, Cyprus, in 2013, alongside biathle and (from 2015) laser-run (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2025 combined Biathle-Triathle and Laser Run World Championships were held in Mossel Bay, South Africa, in December 2025, and the senior world titles went to France's Léa Fernandez in the women's race and Switzerland's Alexandre Dällenbach in the men's race (Wikipedia, n.d.). The UIPM itself was founded in London in 1948 and also governs modern pentathlon, biathle, laser-run and tetrathlon (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.).

Rules

Under the 2025 UIPM Biathle/Triathle Competition Rules, a triathle race starts with a pack start of up to 25 m from the shooting station; the shooting bout consists of five shots with a UIPM-homologated laser pistol at a 10 m electronic target, all five hits required within a 50-second limit per bout, followed directly by the swim leg (25–50 m freestyle) and the run leg (200–800 m), with the shoot-swim-run sequence repeated two to four times according to the age category, and the first athlete to cross the finish line is the winner (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Equipment rules cover the laser pistol (single-shot, open sights, 800–1500 g for adults) and the 170 × 170 mm electronic target, and transition and open-water safety rules apply at the venue (Wikipedia, n.d.). World Championships are held annually in senior, junior, youth and masters categories, alongside para triathle, and women and men race the same course categories and distances — the women's and men's fields start together in the same heats (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Triathle belongs to the UIPM's family of accessible racing formats that grew out of modern pentathlon: the combined run-shoot event was introduced in 2009, and laser shooting replaced the air pistol across the sport's disciplines from 2011 (first non-youth use) and at the Olympic Games from London 2012 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The UIPM — founded in London in 1948 to govern modern pentathlon, which Pierre de Coubertin had brought onto the Olympic programme in 1912 — approved triathle as a development sport in November 2012 (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Triathle made its debut on the international stage at the UIPM Biathle/Triathle World Championships 2013 in Limassol, Cyprus, and the World Championships have been held annually since (2013–2019 and 2021–2025, with the 2025 edition in Mossel Bay, South Africa, combined with the Laser Run World Championships), with editions in Puerto San José (2014), Batumi (2015), Sarasota (2016), Viveiro (2017), Hurghada (2018), St. Petersburg, Florida (2019), Weiden (2021), Machico (2022), Bali (2023) and Port Said (2024) (Wikipedia, n.d.). The format serves as an entry point into pentathlon's running, swimming and shooting disciplines for athletes from developing nations (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.).
