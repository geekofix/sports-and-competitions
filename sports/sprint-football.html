Sprint Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sprint football (formerly lightweight football) is the college variant of American football played under standard collegiate rules with one defining constraint: an athlete weight limit, today 178 pounds (81 kg), which turns the game into a showcase of speed, agility and technique rather than size. It is a varsity sport at United States colleges organized in the Collegiate Sprint Football League (CSFL), whose members include Army, Navy, Cornell, Penn and Princeton.

Rules

The game is played under NCAA college football rules "+E+" the field, downs, formations and officiating are those of the regular college game "+E+" with weight certification as the great exception. Players must weigh in at or below the limit at two official weigh-ins, four days and two days before each game, and may regain weight afterward; body-fat and hydration standards guard against unhealthy cutting. Because every player on the field is roughly the same size, the game emphasizes execution, timing and quickness: precision blocking schemes, fast defensive pursuit and crisp special teams are the hallmarks of a sport in which a running back can stand shoulder to shoulder with a defensive tackle. League rules have also traditionally restricted preseason practice periods and prohibited scouting of opponents to keep the emphasis on amateur, student-athlete competition.

History

Sprint football began in 1934 as the Eastern 150-Pound Football League, founded by seven charter members (Cornell, Lafayette, Pennsylvania, Princeton, Rutgers, Villanova and Yale) as a way for lighter athletes to play intercollegiate football. As American college students grew taller and heavier, the weight limit rose in steps "+E+" to 165 pounds in 1996 and 172 pounds in 2005, with the figure since lifted to 178 "+E+" and the league changed its name to the Eastern Lightweight Football League and then to the Collegiate Sprint Football League in 1998 as it sought expansion. Famous alumni filled its rosters: future president Jimmy Carter played at Navy, future defense secretary Donald Rumsfeld captained Princeton, and New England Patriots owner Robert Kraft played at Columbia. Army and Navy remain the league's marquee rivalry, and a Midwest Sprint Football League operated briefly in the 2020s before the discipline re-consolidated around the CSFL.
