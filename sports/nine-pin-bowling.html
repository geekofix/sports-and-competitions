Nine-Pin Bowling

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Nine-pin bowling (also called kegeln or kegel) is a pin bowling game played mainly in Europe, in which a ball without finger holes is rolled at nine pins arranged in a diamond formation at the end of a lane (World Ninepin Bowling Association, n.d.). It is governed internationally by the World Ninepin Bowling Association (WNBA), a disciplinary association within World Bowling, with about 250,000 members in 28 countries across Europe, South America and Asia (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It differs from ten-pin bowling and general bowling in pin count, ball and lane types; the other small-ball pin variants are candlepin bowling and duckpin bowling. Both women and men compete in national leagues and at the World Championships under the same rules (World Ninepin Bowling Association, n.d.).

Rules

The nine pins stand in a diamond (rows of 1-2-3-2-1) at the end of the lane, with the centre pin (the "king") usually marked; the ball is a solid sphere without finger holes, rolled from a run-up of about 23-24 m on a wooden or synthetic lane (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A frame consists of up to three balls per player in singles, or a set number of throws in team play; knocking down all nine pins with the first ball scores the strike bonus, and knocking down eight pins leaves a "spare" situation — the full ninepin scoring (Kegeln) awards points for pins plus bonuses for clearing the lane, and the highest total after the set number of frames wins (World Ninepin Bowling Association, n.d.). At the World Championships, events are contested in singles, doubles, team and sprint formats over 120/200 throws, with separate men's and women's classifications but identical scoring (World Ninepin Bowling Association, n.d.). Fouls include crossing the foul line and touching pins; men's and women's athletes use the same lane specifications and ball weights adapted only by age group (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Nine-pin bowling developed from medieval European kegeln games played in guild halls and taverns, evolving into a lane sport in the 19th century; the standardized nine-pin diamond and the ball without holes distinguish it from the American ten-pin game derived from the same roots (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The international governing structure was formalized with the founding of the World Ninepin Bowling Association (WNBA) in 1953 in Hamburg, which today organizes World Championships for men and women every two years within World Bowling (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Ninepin Bowling Association, n.d.). The sport remains strongest in Germany, Austria, the Czech Republic, the Balkan countries and South America, with professional Bundesliga leagues in Germany and a large recreational base across Central Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
