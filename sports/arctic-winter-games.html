Arctic Winter Games

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Arctic Winter Games (AWG) are a biennial multi-sport event for athletes from Arctic regions, founded in 1970 and first held in Whitehorse, Yukon; eight contingents — Alaska, Alberta North, Greenland, Northern Alberta, Northwest Territories, Nunavut, Nunavik (Quebec) and Yukon — take part. The 2026 Games were held in Whitehorse, Yukon, from March 8–15, 2026, and the 2028 Games will be held in Fairbanks, Alaska, from March 5–11, 2028 (awarded on July 31, 2026) (Arctic Winter Games [AWG], n.d.-a). The program uniquely mixes conventional winter sports — ice hockey, basketball, volleyball, cross-country skiing, snowshoe racing, biathlon — with the traditional Arctic Sports and the Dene Games on this site; the Hodgson Trophy is awarded to the contingent showing the greatest sportsmanship and fair play (AWG, n.d.-b) (Arctic Winter Games, n.d.-a) (Arctic Winter Games, n.d.-b).

Women and men compete in the same events at the Arctic Winter Games, with female athletes among the medalists.

Rules

Contingents register teams and athletes by region, age and gender; men and women compete in separate divisions in every sport, and youth categories run alongside the senior events. Medals are awarded per event, and the overall medal standings together with the Hodgson Trophy decide the prestige of the Games (AWG, n.d.-b).

History

The Games were founded in 1970 through the initiative of Alaska Governor Wally Hickel together with northern leaders, and the first edition was held in Whitehorse, Yukon, the same year. They have been held every two years since, with Arctic sports on the program from 1974. The 2026 edition in Whitehorse (March 8–15) brought together eight contingents, and Fairbanks, Alaska, was awarded the 2028 Games on July 31, 2026 (AWG, n.d.-a; AWG, n.d.-b).
