Artistic Cycling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Artistic cycling (German: Kunstradfahren) is gymnastics on a bicycle: athletes perform choreographed routines to music in an indoor hall, combining balance tricks, pair work and choreography on specially built competition bikes. It is one of the disciplines of the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), which organizes the UCI Artistic Cycling World Championships for individuals and pairs.

Rules

Under UCI rules, the competition area is a hall of about 14 x 11 m. Elite individual riders perform a 5-minute routine containing a set number of exercises of varying difficulty; pairs perform shorter programs. Judges award points for difficulty, execution and artistic presentation, with deductions for falls or time overruns. The bike must be propelled by the rider at all times; pushing by another person is not allowed. At the UCI World Championships, men's and women's individual titles and pairs titles are awarded.

History

Artistic cycling grew out of trick cycling shows in the late 19th century, especially in Germany and the United States, where riders entertained crowds with wheelies and balance acts. It became an organized competitive discipline in the early 20th century and has been part of the UCI program since the first UCI Indoor Cycling World Championships in the 1950s. Germany remains a traditional powerhouse of the sport.
