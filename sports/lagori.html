Lagori

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lagori — also called seven stones, pithoo, pittu, satoliya or dabba kali — is a traditional Indian throwing game in which a team tries to knock down a stack of seven flat stones with a ball and then rebuild the stack while the opposing team tries to hit them with the ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is played across South Asia and by diaspora communities in more than 30 countries, governed internationally by the International Lagori Federation, which organized the inaugural Lagori World Cup in 2015 and the second edition in 2017 with teams from India, Bhutan, Hong Kong, Brazil, Turkey, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh and Nepal (Amateur Lagori Federation of India, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In India the game was professionalized with the Indian Lagori Premier League, launched in November 2017 by the Amateur Lagori Federation of India (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Girls and women play lagori widely in Indian schools and clubs, and women's teams compete at the World Cup — the sport is one of the most gender-inclusive traditional games of India (Amateur Lagori Federation of India, n.d.-a).

Rules

A team has 12 players, of whom 6 are on the court in each set; the game is played on a court with a throwing circle and a stack of seven flat stones (traditionally stone discs, in modern competition fibre discs) piled in the center (Amateur Lagori Federation of India, n.d.-a). The seeking team (breakers) throws a soft ball from the throwing line to knock the stack down — each thrower gets up to three attempts — and then the teams switch roles: the hitters must rebuild the stack stone by stone in the original order while the opponents throw the ball at them to tag them out (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A player hit by the ball is out for the set; points are scored for each stone knocked down, each stone rebuilt and each opponent tagged (Amateur Lagori Federation of India, n.d.-a). A set lasts 3 minutes with a 30-second break, a match consists of three sets, and the team with the most points wins (Amateur Lagori Federation of India, n.d.-a). Men's and women's matches use identical rules; mixed teams are common in school play (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rule source: International Lagori Federation / Amateur Lagori Federation of India rules, https://www.indialagori.in/ (Amateur Lagori Federation of India, n.d.-a). See also kabaddi on this site.

History

Lagori is among the oldest known ball games of India — the Bhagavata Purana describes Krishna playing a seven-stone game, and the game is traditionally associated with Maharashtra, where it has been played for centuries at festivals (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It spread with Indian traders and migrants to Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, and in the 20th century to the Indian diaspora in the Caribbean, Africa and the Gulf (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Modern organized competition began with the Amateur Lagori Federation of India, which launched the Indian Lagori Premier League in November 2017, and internationally with the first Lagori World Cup in 2015 and the second in 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
