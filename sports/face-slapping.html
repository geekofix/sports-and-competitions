Face Slapping

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Face slapping (also called "Russian slap" or professional slapping) is a combat novelty sport in which two competitors stand across a small table and take turns slapping each other in the face with an open hand; the bout ends by knockout, submission or a judges' decision. The first official Male Slapping Championship was held in Krasnoyarsk, Russia, in March 2019 as part of the Siberian Power Show.

Rules

Two male competitors face each other across a table about 1 m wide with a white line marking the center; hands must stay behind the line until the referee allows the slap. Competitors alternate slaps — each may deliver one open-palm slap to the opponent's face (no closed fists, no palm-heel strikes to the chin in most rule sets). A fighter can win by knockout (opponent cannot continue), by submission (opponent gives up or signals), or on points after the judges decide who landed the more effective slaps; some formats allow a set number of rounds. Protective gear is minimal: mouthguard and sometimes cotton in the ears; vaseline may be allowed to reduce friction. Weight classes exist in some promotions, and women's divisions have been added by some organizers.

History

Competitive slapping grew out of Russian street fight culture and was formalized in 2019 at the Siberian Power Show in Krasnoyarsk, with Vasiliy "The Mountain" Kamotskiy winning the first championship; the sport gained international attention through viral videos and coverage by Vice, NDTV and ESPN. The Professional Slapping League / slap fighting promotions later staged events in Las Vegas (e.g., "Power Slap," backed by Dana White, launched in 2022 with televised events on TBS/ESPN), which formalized rules including weight classes, referees and medical checks. The sport remains controversial, with critics comparing it to bare-knuckle boxing, while promoters market it as a test of pain tolerance and technique.
