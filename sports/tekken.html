Tekken

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive Tekken is the esports scene of Bandai Namco's 3D fighting-game series, currently Tekken 8 (2024) — 1v1 matches of best-of-3 rounds (60-second round timer), with health bars, walls, throws and Tekken 8's Heat system (Heat Smash, Heat Burst) deciding sets (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Bandai Namco, n.d.). The premier circuits are the Tekken World Tour (TWT, run by Bandai Namco since 2017), EVO, and the Esports World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At EVO 2026 (Las Vegas, 26–28 June) Pakistan's Arslan Ash won the Tekken 8 tournament, beating Korea's Rangchu 3–2 in the grand final — his eighth EVO title overall, tying the all-time record (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The 2025 TWT Finals in Malmö were won by Korea's LowHigh, and the 2026 TWT Finals are set for the inaugural EVO Singapore (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Women compete in the same open brackets as men at all majors — there are no gender divisions in the FGC (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Rules

A Tekken match is best-of-3 rounds; each round lasts 60 seconds, and a round is won by reducing the opponent's health bar to zero (K.O.), by ring-out, or on time by higher remaining health (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tekken 8 adds the Heat gauge: Heat Engager/Heat Smash for pressure, Heat Burst as a comeback mechanic, plus rage arts at low health (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Tournaments are double-elimination, best-of-3 (best-of-5 at grand finals of majors), with stage select and counterpick rules; the Tekken World Tour awards points across Master+, Master, Challenger and Dojo tiers, with the top of the global and regional leaderboards plus Master+ winners qualifying for the season Finals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). EVO runs open brackets with anti-collusion rules; men's and women's players compete together (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

The Tekken series began with the 1994 arcade game; competitive play exploded with Tekken 3 (1997) and the Korean scene. Bandai Namco launched the Tekken World Tour in 2017 with Twitch exclusivity; seasons ran 2017–2019, 2022–2026 (2020 cancelled), with Finals champions Qudans (2017), Rangchu (2018), Chikurin (2019), Atif Butt (2022), Arslan Ash (2023 — the first "Triple Crown" of EVO + TWT in one season), Rangchu (2024) and LowHigh (2025) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Legends include Korea's Bae "Knee" Jae-min (multiple EVO and TWT titles), Yoon "Qudans" Young-dae, Knee-era rivals JDCR and Kkokkoma, and Pakistan's Arslan Ash, whose 2018 Dubai breakthrough beat Knee and put Pakistani Tekken on the map; Arslan's eighth EVO title came at EVO 2026 over Rangchu (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
