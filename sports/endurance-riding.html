Endurance Riding

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Endurance riding is an equestrian discipline governed by the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI) in which horse-and-rider pairs cover marked cross-country courses of 40 to 160 km; the horse's fitness is checked at veterinary gates after every loop, and the first combination to finish with its horse judged sound wins (FEI, n.d.-b). The 2026 FEI Endurance World Championship was originally awarded to Al Ula, Saudi Arabia, for November 2026, but the FEI Board withdrew the event from Al Ula on 1 July 2026 citing the region's geopolitical situation and moved it to Butheeb, near Abu Dhabi in the United Arab Emirates, where it is scheduled for 14–20 December 2026 with the 160 km championship ride on 19 December (FEI, n.d.-a). A major FEI rule overhaul that took effect in 2020 introduced speed penalties for horses that repeatedly fail vet checks at high average speeds, mandatory rest periods tied to distance — more than a month after a full 160 km ride — limits on crew assistance and higher minimum horse ages (FEI, n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current riders are the combinations qualified for the world championship at Butheeb, dominated as ever by the elite squads of the United Arab Emirates, with men and women competing together in the same classes (FEI, n.d.-a) (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.-a) (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.-b).

Rules

Endurance rides are run under the FEI Endurance Rules as CEI competitions over 40, 80, 100, 120 or 160 km; championship rides are 160 km, usually split into five or six loops that start and finish at a central venue (FEI, n.d.-b). At the end of every loop the horse must enter the vet gate, where it must present within 20 minutes with a heart rate at or below 64 beats per minute and pass the veterinary inspection; a horse that fails metabolically or is pulled is eliminated (FEI, n.d.-b). Riders may be accompanied by crews, but under the 2020 rules crews may assist only at designated points, and outside assistance is prohibited (FEI, n.d.-b). Horses must meet FEI minimum-age rules — six years for national rides and older for international championships — and must be vaccinated and identified (FEI, n.d.-b). The winner is the first combination across the line whose horse passes the final vet check within the time allowed; ties and placings are decided by the final veterinary inspection and ride time (FEI, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in the same classes with identical rules, and there are separate team and individual world titles but no separate women's ride (FEI, n.d.-b).

History

Endurance riding descends from the cavalry endurance tests of the nineteenth century; one of the first great long-distance rides was the 1892 Berlin–Wien Distanzritt over roughly 512 km (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the United States the 100-mile Tevis Cup, first run in 1955 in Virginia within a 24-hour limit, became the most famous endurance ride (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FEI made endurance an official discipline in the 1970s and has run the FEI World Endurance Championship since 1978, with the FEI European Championships preceding it (FEI, n.d.-b). The sport has been dominated since the 1990s by the United Arab Emirates, whose ruler Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum is himself an FEI world endurance champion and whose stables have set the standard for horse care and training (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women compete on equal terms in the same classes and have been among the sport's medalists at world championships (FEI, n.d.-b).
