Memory Sport

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Memory sport is competitive memorization: athletes memorize shuffled decks of cards, long strings of binary digits, names, words, images and dates under strict time limits, recalling them for points in standardized disciplines. Champions memorize a full deck of cards in well under a minute and thousands of binary digits in an hour.

The sport's flagship is the World Memory Championships founded in 1991 by Tony Buzan and Raymond Keene, and the modern circuit spans national championships, international association rankings and online leagues.

Rules

Standard disciplines fix the challenge and scoring: memorizing as many shuffled 52-card decks as possible in one hour, or a single deck at speed; strings of random decimal and binary digits; abstract images; names and faces; words and fictional dates, each with set memorize and recall times. Correctly recalled cards, digits and words score points, missed or wrong items are penalized, and championship titles go to the athletes with the highest totals across all disciplines in their age and category classes.

History

Memory techniques go back to the ancient method of loci of Greek and Roman orators, but memory as a measurable contest dates from 1991, when Tony Buzan and Raymond Keene staged the first World Memory Championships in London. The sport grew with national championships, memory clubs and junior leagues, and grandmaster titles awarded for norm performances. The founding of the International Association of Memory and the rise of online memory leagues have spread competition across Asia, Europe and the Americas, with speed discipline formats suited to online duels.
