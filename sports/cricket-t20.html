Cricket T20

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

T20 cricket (Twenty20) is the shortest international format: each team bats for a maximum of 20 overs, producing fast, aggressive matches of about three hours. T20 Internationals (T20Is) are played under the ICC T20I Playing Conditions, and the flagship event is the ICC Men's T20 World Cup, held every two years; the 2026 edition is co-hosted by India and Sri Lanka with 20 teams.

Rules

Under the ICC T20I Playing Conditions, each innings is 20 six-ball overs. The powerplay lasts the first six overs, during which at most two fielders may be outside the 30-yard circle; from overs 7 to 20 a maximum of five fielders may be outside. Each bowler may bowl up to four overs, and there is a limit of two bouncers per over. A fixed 15-minute interval is scheduled between innings (reducible to 10 minutes when time has been lost). The chasing side wins by wickets in hand (ten wickets available) or by runs on a tie; rain-affected matches use the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method, and a Super Over decides tied matches in knockout games.

History

T20 was created in England in 2003 by the England and Wales Cricket Board to boost domestic attendances, with the first official T20 match played in June 2003. The format exploded globally with the Indian Premier League, founded in 2008 as a franchise T20 league, and the first ICC World Twenty20 was held in South Africa in 2007 (won by India). The T20 World Cup is now held every two years, and T20 leagues exist in Australia (Big Bash), England (The Hundred, Blast), the Caribbean (CPL) and many other countries.
