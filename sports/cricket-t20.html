Cricket T20

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Twenty20 (T20) cricket is the shortest international format: each team bats once for a maximum of 20 overs, with a white ball, colored clothing and floodlit day/night matches; the side with more runs wins, and ties in knockout games are settled by a Super Over (International Cricket Council [ICC], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). T20 is governed by the ICC under the T20I Playing Conditions, and its pinnacle is the biennial ICC Men's T20 World Cup and ICC Women's T20 World Cup (ICC, n.d.-b). The 2026 Men's T20 World Cup, co-hosted by India and Sri Lanka from 7 February to 8 March 2026, featured 20 teams and 55 matches at eight venues; the final on 8 March 2026 at the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad ended with India beating New Zealand by 96 runs (255/5 to 159) — India's third title, the first back-to-back title defense in the tournament's history and the first won by a host nation — with Sanju Samson's 89 and Jasprit Bumrah's 4/15 leading the way (ICC, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the 2026 Women's T20 World Cup in England, the final at Lord's on 5 July 2026 saw Australia beat England, chasing down the target with 17.1 overs to spare — Australia's record seventh title, under captain Sophie Molineux (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Domestically, T20 drives the biggest leagues in the world, above all the Indian Premier League (IPL), founded in 2008, whose 2026 season is the latest edition of the most-watched T20 league (ICC, n.d.-b). T20 is played in men's and women's internationals under identical playing conditions; women's T20 has its own World Cup (since 2009) and rankings, and at the end of 2026 the leading current players are — in women's T20 — the 2026 world champions Sophie Molineux, Ashleigh Gardner and Alyssa Healy of Australia and England's Nat Sciver-Brunt, and in men's T20 — the 2026 champions Sanju Samson, Jasprit Bumrah and Hardik Pandya of India alongside Suryakumar Yadav, the T20I world No. 1 batter (ICC, n.d.-b).

Rules

A T20I is played under the ICC's T20I Playing Conditions: each innings is limited to 20 overs of six legal balls; a bowler may bowl a maximum of 4 overs; the fielding restrictions allow two fielders outside the 30-yard circle in the first 6 overs (powerplay), a maximum of four outside until the 15th over, and five in the last 5 overs (ICC, n.d.-a). The innings ends after 20 overs, when ten batters are out, or when the chasing side passes the target; a tied match is decided by a Super Over — each side bowls one over, and if still level, by "eliminator" one-ball shoot-outs (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The equipment, pitch, wickets, dismissal modes and DRS are as in other formats, with the white ball used in all limited-overs cricket; no-balls and wides count as extra deliveries and free hits (ICC, n.d.-a). The T20 World Cup format in 2026: 20 teams in four groups of five, the top two from each group advancing to the Super 8 stage, then semi-finals and the final (ICC, n.d.-b). There are no gender or age rule differences — women's T20Is use the identical 20-over structure, and U19 T20 World Cups exist for both genders (ICC, n.d.-a).

History

The first Twenty20 match was played in England in 2003 between Hampshire and Sussex in the domestic T20 Blast forerunner, and the first men's T20 International followed on 17 February 2005 at Eden Park, Auckland, when Australia beat New Zealand; the first women's T20I was England v New Zealand in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The format exploded with the inaugural ICC World Twenty20 in South Africa in 2007 — pictured below, Bangladesh v South Africa at Newlands in Cape Town — where India, captained by MS Dhoni, beat Pakistan by five runs in the final at Johannesburg (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Since then the men's title has been shared by India (2007, 2024, 2026), England (2010, 2022), the West Indies (2012, 2016) and Australia (2021); in 2024 India beat South Africa in the final at Barbados with Virat Kohli's 76, and in 2026 they beat New Zealand in Ahmedabad (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Women's T20 World Cup began in 2009 in England, won by the hosts over New Zealand; Australia has dominated with seven titles through 2026, and the 2024 edition in Dubai was won by New Zealand over South Africa by 32 runs with Amelia Kerr player of the tournament (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons of T20 cricket include Chris Gayle (b. 1979), the only man with two T20 World Cup centuries and record six-hit figures; AB de Villiers (b. 1984) of South Africa; and in the women's game Alyssa Healy (b. 1988), the first woman to a T20I century for Australia, and England's Danni Wyatt-Hodge (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
