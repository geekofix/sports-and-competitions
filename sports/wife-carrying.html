Wife Carrying

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wife carrying is a comic obstacle race in which a male competitor carries a female partner — his wife or any teammate over 17 — through a themed course against the clock, using the piggyback, the fireman's carry over the shoulder, or the crowd-pleasing "Estonian carry," in which the carried woman hangs upside down from the man's shoulders (Sonkajärvi Wife Carrying World Championship, n.d.). The sport's spiritual home is Sonkajärvi in Finland, whose annual Wife Carrying World Championships have drawn international teams every summer since 1992, and the winners famously receive the carried woman's weight in beer as their prize (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Satellite qualifying events are held in several countries — including the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia — feeding the Finnish world final, and the contest is a staple of rural and harvest festivals, where men and women take part alike in the light-hearted spirit of the game (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the folk and festival games family on this site, its closest relatives are Cheese Rolling, Zorbing, Egg Tapping, Shovel Racing, Lawn Mower Racing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

The official World Championship course at Sonkajärvi is 253.5 m long over sand, grass and gravel and includes obstacles such as log hurdles, a water dip and a dry obstacle in the form of a foam pool; teams race singly against the clock and the fastest time wins (Sonkajärvi Wife Carrying World Championship, n.d.). Any carry style is permitted as long as the carried partner is held throughout the run — a belt is commonly used for the Estonian carry — helmets are worn as safety gear, and deliberate obstruction of other teams is forbidden (Wikipedia, n.d.). If the carried partner is dropped, the team receives a time penalty added to its run, and the prize for the world champions is the wife's weight in beer, paid out in the beverage of her choice (Sonkajärvi Wife Carrying World Championship, n.d.).

History

The tradition is traced to a nineteenth-century Finnish legend about the brigand Ronkainen, who is said to have tested prospective gang members by making them carry heavy sacks — and, in some tellings, to have stolen wives from neighbouring villages — while a folk custom of "wife stealing" provided another ancestor of the modern game (Wikipedia, n.d.). The contemporary competition began at Sonkajärvi in 1992, deliberately treating the legend as comedy, and it quickly became an international hit, spawning the official World Championships and satellite events in the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia and beyond; Estonian competitors using the Estonian carry style soon dominated, and the beer prize remains the sport's signature reward (Wikipedia, n.d.).
