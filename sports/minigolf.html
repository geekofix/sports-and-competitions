Minigolf

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Minigolf is miniature golf played competitively on standardized 18-lane courses under the World Minigolf Sport Federation (WMF), founded in 1963 as the International Minigolf Federation (IMF) and renamed WMF in 1993; the federation has 65 member nations, is a member of GAISF/TAFISA and AIMS and a WADA affiliate, and was a demonstration sport at the 1989 World Games in Karlsruhe (World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The WMF publishes an official Rulebook — statutes, worldwide international sport rules and regulations — and runs the biennial World Championships in odd-numbered years, continental championships in even years, and annual junior and European Open events (World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-c; World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-b; World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-a).

Competitions are open to women and men in separate categories across three age groups — juniors (under 20), adults and seniors (45 and over) — so a full international programme includes men's and women's titles at every age level (World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The WMF recognises four course systems: minigolf (fibrocement lanes, about 600–800 m² for 18 holes), feltgolf (carpet lanes, common in Sweden and Finland), béton (concrete with 18 standardized obstacles, common in German-speaking countries) and the Minigolf Open Standard (artificial turf, common in the US and UK) (World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

In competition, each player completes all 18 lanes in a set order, teeing off only from the marked teeing-off area, with homologated balls and putters; a maximum of six strokes is allowed per lane — a seventh stroke scores seven points, making seven the maximum per lane — and the player with the lowest total strokes wins, with the world record being 18 strokes on 18 lanes (“enterne”) (World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-b; World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-c).

History

The first standardized minigolf course was laid out on 19 March 1954 by the Swiss landscape architect Paul Bongni, and national federations followed in Austria in 1962–1963; the IMF was founded in 1963 and became the WMF in 1993 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Since then the sport has grown to 65 member nations with world championships in odd years, European and Asian championships in even years, and a strong junior scene; minigolf was also a demonstration sport at the 1989 World Games in Karlsruhe (World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-d; World Minigolf Sport Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
