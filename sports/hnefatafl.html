Hnefatafl

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hnefatafl ('king's table') is the Norse tafl game documented since the 8th century (Gokstad ship burial pieces): on an 11x11 board the king with 12 defenders faces 24 attackers; the king wins by escaping to the edge/corner, the attackers by capturing the king. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Tablut, Brandubh, Latrunculi; the related pages are listed under Important Links (NTNU Vitenskapsmuseet, n.d.).

Figure: Berig. (2008-02-23). Hnefatafl pawns [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hnefatafl_pawns.jpg

Rules

11x11 (or 9x9/13x13 variants); the king moves like other pieces but must escape to the edge (or corner in some rules); attackers capture the king by surrounding him on four sides (two on the throne/edge). Vikings played it on carved boards (Trondheim 1100s) (NTNU Vitenskapsmuseet, n.d.).

History

Hnefatafl is the best-documented Viking board game (boards from the Gokstad and Oseberg ship burials, amber pieces from Kuddby 9th c.); it spread with the Vikings across the North Atlantic. Modern world championships (e.g. the Kolomenskoe historical festival 2015) use reconstructions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
