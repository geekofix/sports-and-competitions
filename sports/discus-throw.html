Discus Throw

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The discus throw is one of the two throws on the programme of the first modern Olympics (Athens 1896): from a 2.50 m circle protected by a cage, the athlete spins one and a half turns and releases a disc-shaped weight — 2 kg for men, 1 kg for women — into a 34.92-degree sector. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world records are 75.56 m for men, set by Mykolas Alekna of Lithuania in Ramona, Oklahoma, on 13 April 2025 (breaking his own 74.35 m of 2024 and Jürgen Schult's 74.08 m of 1986, the longest-standing men's world record in athletics), and 76.80 m for women, set by Gabriele Reinsch of East Germany in Neubrandenburg on 9 July 1988 — both standing in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by Valarie Allman of the USA (pictured), the first American woman to win back-to-back Olympic discus titles (Tokyo 2020, Paris 2024) and the 2025 world champion, with Silja Kosonen of Finland and Jorinde van Klinken of the Netherlands; the men's event was led by world record holder Alekna (pictured at the 2026 European Championships in Birmingham, where he took silver in 69.89 m) and Matthew Denny of Australia (74.25 m in Ramona, April 2026, second-best ever) (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the throws family — Shot Put, Javelin Throw and Hammer Throw — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The discus is a convex disc of wood or other material with metal rims, weighing 2 kg for men and 1 kg for women, thrown from a 2.50 m circle surrounded by a safety cage into a 34.92-degree sector (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Throws at major championships begin with a qualification round; in the final each athlete has three attempts, and the best eight receive three further attempts in reverse order of placings, with a one-minute time limit per attempt (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The implement must land completely inside the marked sector, the athlete must not touch the ground or the stop board before the implement lands, and must leave the circle from its rear half after the throw (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The distance is measured to the nearest centimetre from the mark made by the implement to the inside edge of the throwing circle's line (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

Men and women use different discus weights; U18/U20 athletes use lighter discs (1.75 kg for U20 men, 1.5 kg for U18 men and 1 kg for women at both levels), and para athletics contests the event in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

Throwing the discus goes back to the discus of the ancient Greek pentathlon, immortalised by Myron's Discobolus; the event has been on every modern Olympic programme since 1896, where the USA's Robert Garrett won both throws (Wikipedia, n.d.). Al Oerter of the USA won four consecutive Olympic titles from 1956 to 1968, and Gerd Kanter of Estonia won in 2008; Robert Harting of Germany won three straight world titles from 2009 to 2013 (Olympics, n.d.). Jürgen Schult of East Germany, pictured, set the world record of 74.08 m in 1986 — it survived 37 years and 9 months until Alekna's 74.35 m in 2024 (World Athletics, n.d.-e). On the women's side, Lillian Copeland and Tamara Press were early Olympic champions, Sandra Elkasević of Croatia won two Olympic and two world titles, Gabriele Reinsch, pictured, set the world record of 76.80 m in Neubrandenburg 1988, still standing in 2026, and Valarie Allman added the 2025 world title to her two Olympic golds (World Athletics, n.d.-e).
