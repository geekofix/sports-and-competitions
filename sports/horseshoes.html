Horseshoes

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Horseshoes (pitching horseshoes) is the North American game in which players alternate tossing U-shaped iron or steel shoes (regulation shoes weigh up to 2.5 lb / 1.13 kg) toward a steel stake (the "pin") 40 ft (12.19 m) away in a sand or clay pit. A shoe encircling the pin (a "ringer") scores highest, and shoes landing within 6 inches (15 cm) of the pin score one point each. It is governed in the US by the National Horseshoe Pitchers Association (NHPA) and internationally by the World Horseshoe Pitchers Association (WHPA), which runs the annual World Horseshoe Championship in Canton, Ohio.

Rules

Under WHPA/NHPA rules, the pitching distance is 40 ft from the pitcher's box to the pin (30 ft for women, juniors and some classes), the pin is a steel stake about 36-38 mm in diameter leaning slightly toward the pitcher, and each player throws two shoes per turn (frame), alternating with the opponent. Only one player's shoes score per frame: a ringer scores 3 points, and each other shoe within 6 inches of the pin scores 1 point. Games are played to a set point total (commonly 40 at championships) or a set number of frames. Clay pits with a backstop are standard at tournaments, and shoes must meet regulation weight, width and opening limits.

History

Pitching horseshoes became popular in North America in the 18th-19th centuries (with European ring-throwing games such as quoits as ancestors) and was played by soldiers in the American Civil War. It became organized with the National Horseshoe Pitchers Association, founded in 1938, and the World Horseshoe Pitchers Association in the mid-20th century; the WHPA World Championship has been held annually in Canton, Ohio since the 1980s. The game remains a staple of American county fairs and campgrounds, with multi-time world champions from the Ohio and Indiana circuits.
