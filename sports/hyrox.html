Hyrox

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

HYROX is a global indoor fitness racing competition, self-billed as "the world's biggest fitness race," in which athletes complete eight functional workout stations interleaved with 1 km runs: 1,000 m SkiErg, 1,000 m Concept2 RowErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jumps, farmer's carry, sandbag lunges and wall balls, for a total of 8 km of running. It was founded in 2017 in Hamburg, Germany, by Christian Toetzke and Moritz Fürre, and events now run in dozens of cities worldwide, with the annual HYROX World Championship (Nice 2024, Hamburg 2025).

Rules

All competitors run the same standardized course: 8 rounds of 1 km run plus one work station each, in a fixed order (SkiErg, RowErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jump, farmer's carry, sandbag lunges, wall balls), with weights scaled by division (Open, Pro, Women, doubles, relays). The clock runs from the start; the finish time decides the ranking within each division, with world rankings per age group. Judges enforce movement standards (e.g., full range on wall balls, burpee broad jump distance), and penalties (or reps) are applied for missed standards. There is no cut-off at most events, though some have time caps.

History

HYROX was founded in Hamburg in 2017 after founders Toetzke and Furre were inspired by a CrossFit-style event and wanted a standardized, measurable fitness race; the first event in September 2017 drew about 650 athletes. It grew rapidly across Europe, the US, Asia and Australia, with over 500,000+ participants to date and events in cities from London and New York to Singapore and Melbourne. The HYROX World Championship crowns the season champion — the 2024 edition was held in Nice, France, won by Hunter McIntyre (USA) and Lauren Weeks (UK). The race has become a mass-participation phenomenon combining endurance running with functional fitness.
