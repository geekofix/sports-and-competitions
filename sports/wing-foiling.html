Wing Foiling

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wing foiling (wingfoiling) is the water sport in which a rider stands on a hydrofoil board and holds a handheld inflatable wing that catches the wind — with no mast, boom or lines connecting wing to board — so the rider powers up, rises on the foil and glides above the water's surface (World Sailing, n.d.). It blends windsurfing, kitesurfing and foiling, and it excels in light wind where other sail sports struggle, with formats ranging from course racing to freestyle and wave riding (Wikipedia, n.d.).

The sport is governed through a partnership of World Sailing with the International Wing Sports Association (IWSA) and the Global Wingsports Association (GWA), which run endorsed world tours; the WingFoil Racing World Cup is a World Sailing Special Event crowning the annual Open WingFoil Racing World Champion, and the one-design Formula Wing class provides Olympic-style racing on equal equipment (International Wing Sports Association, n.d.). The first women's WingFoil Racing World Champion was Paula Novotná of Czechia, crowned at Jericoacoara, Brazil, in December 2022, and the first women's Formula Wing World Champion was the 18-year-old Italian Maddalena Spanu, who shares the honour with France's Mathis Ghio as the first Formula Wing world champions of any kind (International Wing Sports Association, n.d.).

Rules

Course racing uses fleet races around buoy tracks, with right-of-way borrowed from sailing's racing rules — port gives way to starboard, windward keeps clear of leeward, overtaking keeps clear — plus equipment class rules covering wing, board and foil dimensions (World Sailing, n.d.). In the Open class riders use any approved equipment, while the Formula Wing class is one-design, so races are decided by sailor skill on identical gear; safety requirements include a leash and buoyancy aid, and federated events define start procedures, penalties and rescue rules (International Wing Sports Association, n.d.). Freeride and wave winging contests are judged subjectively on jumps, rotations and wave rides, similar to windsurfing freestyle and wave formats, and the hydrofoil lifts the board at speed, so riders manage flight altitude through pressure on the wing and board (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's and women's fleets race separately in the same classes at world championships (International Wing Sports Association, n.d.).

History

The handheld-wing concept is old — aeronautical engineer Jim Drake, inventor of windsurfing, and European windsurfing pioneer Uli Stanciu built the first wing in 1981 — but the modern sport ignited when hydrofoil boards matured through kitesurfing and SUP foiling in the late 2010s, with purpose-built inflatable wings taking off between 2018 and 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Riders and brands from the windsurfing and kitesurfing communities drove explosive growth, national sailing federations added wing racing programmes, and World Sailing-sanctioned class racing and youth circuits emerged; wing foiling was added to the ANOC World Beach Games programme in Bali 2023, replacing kitefoil, and the IWSA and GWA began organising international tours from 2021 (International Wing Sports Association, n.d.). The first WingFoil Racing World Cup events were held from 2022, crowning Paula Novotná as the first women's world champion at Jericoacoara in December 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
