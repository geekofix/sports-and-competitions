Gouren

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gouren (also called Okinawan sumo) is a traditional wrestling sport of Okinawa, Japan, in which two wrestlers grapple in a small ring or circle of sand (dohyo-style) and the first to touch the ground outside the ring or be pushed out loses. It is organized by the Okinawan Gouren Federation and featured in Okinawan cultural festivals; it is related to but distinct from Japanese sumo (which uses a raised clay dohyo).

Rules

In gouren, matches are fought in a circle (about 2-4 m diameter) marked on sand or grass; wrestlers start gripping each other (mawashi-style grips on clothing or belt) and the bout ends when one wrestler is thrown, forced out of the circle, or touches the ground with any body part other than the feet. No ring-out rules of professional sumo apply (no dohyo edge); the bout is decided purely by fall or exit. Matches are short (best of three in competition), with weight classes in some tournaments. Fouls such as hair-pulling or striking are prohibited. At festivals, open-weight divisions and children's bouts are common.

History

Gouren has been practiced in Okinawa for centuries as part of village festivals (matsuri) and samurai training, with written references from the Ryukyu Kingdom period (15th-19th centuries); it survived into the modern era as a folk sport distinct from mainland sumo, which arrived in Okinawa in the Meiji era. The Okinawan Gouren Federation organizes the annual All-Okinawa Gouren Championships, and the sport is demonstrated at Okinawan festivals, attracting both local competitors and visitors. It is one of the traditional martial arts (bugei) of the Ryukyu islands alongside karate and kobudo.
