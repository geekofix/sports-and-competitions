Para Taekwondo

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para taekwondo is the kyorugi (sparring) discipline of taekwondo for athletes with physical impairments, governed by World Taekwondo and added to the Paralympic programme at Tokyo 2020 (World Taekwondo, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Classes are K44 (lower-limb and trunk impairments) and K46 (arm impairment or amputation), each with five weight categories for men and women at the Paralympics — K44: -58/-63/-70/-80/+80 kg (men) and -47/-52/-57/-65/+65 kg (women) (World Taekwondo, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Paris 2024 at the Grand Palais staged about 120 athletes in 10 categories; the 2026 season includes the World Para Taekwondo Championships in Muju, South Korea, and Para Grand Prix events in Rome, Muju and Paris-Levallois (8 October 2026, 113 athletes from 30 countries) (World Taekwondo, n.d.-a; 2026, November).

Leading the women today: Ana Carolina Silva de Moura of Brazil (pictured), K44 -65 kg gold at both Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 and a 2026 Grand Prix winner; Silvana Fernandes of Brazil, K44 -57 kg, who won three consecutive 2026 Grand Prix titles after her Paris 2024 bronze; Guljonoy Naimova of Uzbekistan, K44 +65 kg, Tokyo 2020 champion and 2026 Muju world champion; and Zakia Khudadadi of France (pictured), K44 -47 kg, bronze medallist at Tokyo 2020 (the Refugee Paralympic Team's first medal) and Paris 2024 (2024, August; 2026, November). Among the men, Asaf Yasur of Israel won the K44 -58 kg gold at Paris 2024, and Mahmut Bozteke of Türkiye the K44 -63 kg gold there and the 2026 Muju world title (2026, November; 2024, August).

Rules

World Taekwondo competition rules apply: three two-minute rounds with electronic protective gear (hogu) and sensors scoring head and body kicks and punches, with the higher score after three rounds winning; ties go to the "golden point" round (World Taekwondo, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

K44 athletes may wear permitted protective gear and compete standing; K46 athletes compete with the affected arm; weigh-ins and classifications are checked before the draw, men and women compete separately per weight class, and the world ranking (points from World Championships and Grand Prix) seeds qualification for LA28 (World Taekwondo, n.d.-c; World Taekwondo, n.d.-a).

History

Para taekwondo is the Paralympics' newest martial art: the IPC decided in 2015 to include it from Tokyo 2020, and it debuted there with eight weight categories (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Pioneers of the first Games include Denmark's Lisa Gjessing (women's -58 kg gold) and Brazil's Nathan Troquato (men's -61 kg gold) (2024, August).

At Paris 2024, Peru's Angelica Espinoza won the K44 -49 kg, breaking Peru's Paralympic gold drought since Sydney 2000; Great Britain's Amy Truesdale took K44 +65 kg over Naimova, and Uzbekistan's Asadbek Toshtemirov won K44 -80 kg 38–17 over Mexico's Luis Mario Nájera (2024, August).
