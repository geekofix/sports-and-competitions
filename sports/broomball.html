Broomball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Broomball is a team sport played on ice or snow — like ice hockey, but without skates: players wear special rubber-soled shoes and hit a ball with a broom-shaped stick toward the opponent's goal. The International Federation of Broomball Associations (IFBA), founded in 1998 and based in Canada, is the world governing body and runs the biennial World Broomball Championships (Challenge Cup).

Rules

Under IFBA rules, each team fields six players: one goaltender and five skaters, on a standard ice hockey rink. Players use a "broom" — a stick with a rubber-molded triangular head (historically a real corn broom with bristles taped) — and may not swing it above waist height. The IFBA goal net is 5 x 7 ft (1.5 x 2.1 m), smaller than the 6 x 8 ft American net; IFBA offside uses only the center red line, while American rules use a floating blue line. Games are two halves of 20-30 minutes with two on-ice referees; face-offs, penalties and icing-style rules follow ice hockey adapted to broomball. At the World Championships, national teams play round-robin and knockout stages.

History

Broomball originated in Canada in the late 19th/early 20th century, when farmers and lumberjacks played on frozen ponds with brooms and a ball. It became organized in Manitoba in the 1960s; the IFBA was founded in 1998 after an Australian team visited Canada in 1988, sparking the idea of international competition. The first World Broomball Championships were held in the early 2000s and are now biennial, traditionally dominated by Canada and the United States, with strong teams from Switzerland, Italy, Japan and Australia. In the US, USA Broomball (the former national body) folded in 2020, succeeded by the United States Broomball Association (USBA).
