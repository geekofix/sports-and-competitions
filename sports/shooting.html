Shooting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shooting is the family of precision target-sports governed internationally by the International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF), in which competitors fire rifles, pistols and shotguns at stationary paper or electronic targets or at flying clay targets. It is one of the oldest Olympic sports — shooting was on the programme of the first modern Games in 1896 and has appeared at every Games since 1908 except 1928 — and today's ISSF disciplines span rifle, pistol, shotgun (trap and skeet) and running-target events for men, women and mixed teams.

Rules

Rifle and pistol events are shot at paper or electronic targets from set distances and positions: the flagship events include 10 metre air rifle and air pistol (on the Olympic programme since 1984 and 1988 respectively), 25 metre rapid fire pistol, 50 metre rifle three positions (kneeling, prone and standing) and 50 metre rifle prone, scored by decimal ring values with finals decided to 0.1 precision. Shotgun events — trap and skeet — require hitting clay targets launched on fixed trajectories, with trap machines beneath the shooters' stations and skeet thrown from a high and a low house crossing in front of the shooter; each missed target is a lost point in a set number of shots. Since the 2020 era, Olympic programmes feature rifle, pistol and shotgun events for men and women separately together with mixed team events — 10 metre air rifle and air pistol mixed teams and trap mixed team — in which scores of the male and female team members are combined. Competition is run under strict safety and equipment rules laid down by ISSF, with equipment control designed to keep the sport about marksmanship, not technology.

History

Target shooting's governing body, the International Shooting Sport Federation, was founded on 17 July 1907 in Z\u00fcrich by seven national federations and — after changes of name early in its history, including spells as the Union Internationale de Tir — has been known as the ISSF since 1998. Shooting was one of the founding sports at the first modern Olympic Games in 1896 and has been contested in almost every subsequent Games, apart from the 1928 Games, where it was absent because of a lack of international rules; the first world championships were staged in 1897. The women's programme was formally separated from the open events in the 1980s and 1990s, airgun events joined the Olympics in 1984\u20131988, skeet and trap women's events were added by 2000, and the mixed team events debuted on the Olympic programme at Tokyo 2020 (held 2021), where the sport received wide attention through its youngest-ever Olympic champion. The sport's heartlands remain Europe, China, the Republic of Korea, India and the United States.
