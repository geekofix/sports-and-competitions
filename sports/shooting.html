Shooting

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shooting is the family of precision target sports governed internationally by the International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF), in which competitors fire rifles, pistols and shotguns at paper or electronic targets or at flying clay targets; it has been on the Olympic programme since the first modern Games in 1896 (ISSF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ISSF disciplines span rifle (10 m air rifle, 50 m rifle prone, 50 m rifle three positions), pistol (10 m air pistol, 25 m pistol, 25 m rapid fire pistol, 50 m pistol), shotgun (trap, skeet, double trap) and running target, contested by men, women and mixed teams, and the 55th ISSF World Shooting Championships take place in Doha, Qatar, from 1 to 14 November 2026 as the first qualification event for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ISSF also publishes an official world ranking in every event (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

At the top of the women's game stand the ISSF world-ranking leaders — Yao Qianxun in 50 m rifle three positions, Esha Singh in 10 m air pistol, and Erica Sessa and Jiang Yiting in trap — together with the Paris 2024 Olympic champions Ban Hyo-jin (10 m air rifle, gold at the age of 16), Oh Ye-jin (10 m air pistol) and Yang Ji-in (25 m pistol), while the men's elite is led by Sheng Lihao (10 m air rifle, Paris 2024 gold), Xie Yu (10 m air pistol, Paris 2024 gold) and six-time Olympic medallist Kim Rhode of the United States in skeet (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Rules

Rifle and pistol events are shot at paper or electronic targets from set distances and positions — 10 m air rifle and air pistol (Olympic since 1984 and 1988), 25 m pistol and 25 m rapid fire pistol, 50 m rifle three positions (kneeling, prone, standing) and 50 m rifle prone — scored by decimal ring values up to 10.9 per shot, with qualification rounds followed by finals decided shot by shot to 0.1 precision (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; ISSF, n.d.). Shotgun events — trap, skeet and double trap — require breaking clay targets launched on fixed or crossing trajectories, scored purely hit or miss over set numbers of targets (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment is strictly controlled: air guns in .177 (4.5 mm), small-bore .22 LR rifles and pistols, and 12-gauge shotguns, with rules for clothing, shoes, triggers and sights designed to keep the contest about marksmanship (ISSF, n.d.). Safety rules govern loading, transport and handling at the range, and all athletes are subject to the ISSF anti-doping code (ISSF, n.d.). The Olympic programme features men's and women's events in each discipline plus mixed team events (10 m air rifle, 10 m air pistol and trap mixed teams), and para shooting is contested under World Shooting Para Sport; women compete in every event and in mixed teams on exactly the same terms as men, and shooting was again on the programme of the Victoria 2026 Commonwealth Games with trap and 10 m events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

History

The first ISSF World Shooting Championships were held in 1897, and the International Shooting Sport Federation itself was founded on 17 July 1907 in Zürich by seven national federations, originally as the Union Internationale de Tir, taking its present ISSF name in 1998 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Shooting has appeared at every modern Olympic Games from 1896 except 1904 and 1928, and its roll of retired icons includes Germany's Ralf Schumann, three-time Olympic rapid fire pistol champion (1996, 2004, 2008), China's Zhu Qinan (10 m air rifle gold 2004) and Du Li (10 m air rifle gold 2004 and multiple world titles), and Russia's Sergei Alifirenko (rapid fire pistol gold 2000) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The women's programme was separated from the open events in the 1980s and 1990s, airgun events joined the Olympics from 1984, and mixed team events debuted at Tokyo 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
