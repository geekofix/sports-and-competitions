Tai Chi Chuan

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tai chi chuan (taijiquan) is a Chinese internal martial art built on slow, flowing movement, balance and breath control; internationally its competitive side is governed by the International Wushu Federation (IWUF), founded on 3 October 1990 with 164 national and territorial members and recognized by the IOC (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). In December 2020 UNESCO inscribed taijiquan on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and the UNESCO General Conference established 21 March as International Taijiquan Day in 2025 (UNESCO, n.d.). The flagship event is the World Taijiquan Championships: the 5th edition was held in Burgas, Bulgaria, from 23 to 30 April 2026 with 38 gold medals in compulsory, optional and newly standardized routines for men and women alike, plus demonstration events in the Yang, Chen, Wu, Sun and Wu (hao) styles (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Women compete on equal terms with men in every taolu (routine) and push-hands category at world, continental and national championships (International Wushu Federation, n.d.; UNESCO, n.d.).

Rules

IWUF taijiquan competition comprises taolu (choreographed routines) and push hands (tuishou). Compulsory routines include the 24-form simplified Yang-style set codified by China's State Sports Commission in 1956 and the 42-form competition routine, shot within 2 minutes 45 seconds to 3 minutes 15 seconds at the 2026 World Taijiquan Championships; optional and traditional routines cover the Yang, Chen, Wu, Sun and Wu (hao) styles plus weapons such as the straight sword (jian) and the spear (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Routines are judged under the IWUF Taolu Competition Rules (2024 edition) on difficulty, execution and overall impression (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Push-hands bouts are contested in weight classes with separate men's and women's divisions, and age groups range from children through juniors to seniors and veterans (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). The official rule source is the IWUF competition rules and regulations (International Wushu Federation, n.d.).

History

Taijiquan is traditionally credited to Chen Wangting (c. 1600–1680) of Chenjiagou in Henan province, who compiled the art in the late Ming dynasty; his student lineage through Chen Changxing produced Yang Luchan (1799–1872), who carried the art to Beijing and founded the Yang style, and whose grandson Yang Chengfu (1883–1936) spread it nationwide and published The Essence and Applications of Taijiquan in 1934 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The simplified 24-form set was codified in 1956, the IWUF was founded in Kuala Lumpur in 1990, the first World Wushu Championships were held in Beijing in 1991, and UNESCO's 2020 inscription recognized taijiquan's living heritage (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (UNESCO, n.d.) (International Wushu Federation, n.d.) — followed by the 5th World Taijiquan Championships in Burgas in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
