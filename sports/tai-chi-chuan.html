Tai Chi Chuan

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tai chi chuan (taijiquan) is the Chinese internal martial art of slow, continuous, flowing movement: sequences of postures practiced for health, meditation and self-defence, rooted in the traditional styles of the Chen, Yang, Wu and Sun families. Practiced by hundreds of millions worldwide, it is both a daily health practice and a competitive sport with standardized forms, push-hands competition and international championships.

Rules

Traditional practice centers on the taolu (hand and weapon forms), long choreographed sequences performed slowly with precise posture, breath and weight transfer in styles such as Chen (with its silk-reeling and bursts of power), Yang (the long, slow sets most recognized worldwide), Wu and Sun. As a sport, standardized competition forms (including the 24-posture simplified Yang form and combined 42-form) are judged on execution, dynamics and presentation, while tuishou (push hands) contests two athletes in a circle who try to uproot and unbalance each other using sensitivity, yielding and explosive neutralization rather than brute force: touch rules, weight classes and prohibition of wrestling grips keep the contest true to the art. International federations govern ranking and events, with tai chi events now also on the programme of international wushu championships and world taijiquan championships.

History

Tai chi descends from seventeenth-century Chen village in Henan province, where the Chen family developed the art; the Yang family's nineteenth-century adaptation made it accessible to a broader public and produced the style that spread worldwide through the twentieth century, carried by diaspora masters and later promoted within China as a health practice (the simplified 24-form, created in 1956, became the most practiced exercise sequence on earth). The late twentieth century brought competitive standardization through international wushu bodies, with tai chi events at the world wushu championships and the International Health Qigong and Taijiquan communities growing rapidly. Today taijiquan is both a UNESCO-recognized element of Chinese cultural heritage (inscribed 2020) and an enormous global wellness practice, from hospital rehabilitation programs to world championship forms and push-hands competition contested across the traditional family styles.
