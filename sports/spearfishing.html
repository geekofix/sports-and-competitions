Spearfishing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Spearfishing is the ancient art of catching fish with a hand-launched spear or speargun, both a subsistence practice dating back to the earliest human cultures and a modern underwater sport. In its competitive form \u2014 governed internationally by the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS) \u2014 freediving spearfishers hunt with band-powered spearguns in world championships that test breath-hold diving skill, fish knowledge and ocean judgement over hours in open water.

Rules

Competitive spearfishing under CMAS rules is freedive-only: athletes breathe up at the surface and dive to ambush fish, with the use of underwater breathing apparatus forbidden in competition. Each competitor fishes in an individually assigned water zone for a set competition duration, using a speargun or pole spear, and is scored on the fish captured \u2014 each species carrying a specified point value \u2014 with local rules usually setting minimum sizes and protected species, and penalties for undersized or forbidden captures. The winner is the competitor with the highest points total, after verification of each catch against the rules; team events accumulate the scores of individual divers from each nation. Competition requires precise breath-hold fitness (dives of one to two minutes to depths of 15\u201325 m are common), local knowledge of fish behaviour, and careful safety practice \u2014 many events use buoys, flags, boat check-ins and partner watching, since shallow-water blackout is the sport's ever-present danger.

History

Spearing fish from the surface is one of humanity's oldest hunting techniques \u2014 prehistoric rock art, ancient Egyptian reliefs and Polynesian, Mediterranean and Pacific indigenous free-diving traditions document the art \u2014 with the multi-pronged spear evolving into the streamlined modern speargun during the twentieth century, when goggles, masks, fins and finally the band-powered guns of the 1930s\u201350s created a new generation of underwater hunters. Recreational and competitive spearfishing organised quickly: CMAS (founded 1959 to govern underwater sports including spearfishing) staged its first world spearfishing championships in the 1950s and 1960s, and the sport's historic powers \u2014 Italy, France, Spain and later the strong national traditions of Croatia, Greece, Turkey and, across the Atlantic, the USA and South American nations \u2014 have contested world and continental championships ever since. Modern competitive spearfishing is a highly regulated sport with licenses, size limits and marine protected areas increasingly shaping when and where the sport can be practiced; the sport also continues as sustainable selective harvesting outside competition (only capturing targeted, table-size fish), a point its federations stress in an era of overfishing \u2014 and today's freediving boom has brought new athletes into the competitive sport, whose world championship gold is one of underwater sports' most prized titles.
