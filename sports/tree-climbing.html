Tree Climbing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive tree climbing (arborist climbing) tests the rope-work skills of professional tree workers: climbers ascend a tall tree and complete tasks at marked stations, scored by judges — governed internationally by the International Society of Arboriculture (ISA) through its International Tree Climbing Championship (ITCC) (ITCC-ISA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). 2026 marks the 50th anniversary of the ITCC (ISA, n.d.).

The ITCC format has five preliminary events — Work Climb (80 points), Aerial Rescue (50), Throwline (30), the head-to-head ascent (20) and Speed Climb — plus the Masters' Challenge for the top climbers; chapter champions qualify for the annual world championship (ITCC-ISA, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate divisions at every ITCC — the world championship crowns a men's and a women's champion (ITCC-ISA, n.d.).

Within the climbing and mountaineering family on this site, its closest relatives are Rock Climbing, Sport Climbing, Ice Climbing, Free Solo Climbing, Lead Climbing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Events (ITCC rule book, 2026 edition): Work Climb — climb to five stations in the tree and complete a task at each (e.g. ring a bell, unscrew a spindle), 80 points max, with time penalties; Aerial Rescue — reach a simulated casualty 10 m up and lower them to the ground safely, 50 points, heavy deductions for unsafe technique; Throwline — set a rope over a target branch with a throw bag, 30 points; Head-to-Head Ascent (formerly Footlock) — race a rival up a 15 m secured line, 20 points; Speed Climb — first to ring a bell at 15 m wins (ITCC-ISA, n.d.).

The Masters' Challenge combines all skills in one final for the top-placed men and women. Safety is paramount: harness, helmet, eye protection and a belayed system are mandatory; unsafe acts can disqualify (ITCC-ISA, n.d.). Men's and women's world champions are crowned separately (ITCC-ISA, n.d.).

History

Tree climbing competitions began in 1976 with the first ISA Tree Trimmer's Jamboree in St. Louis, Missouri, created to preserve the classic rope skills needed for aerial rescue — saving a stranded climber with nothing but a rope (ITCC-ISA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The Jamboree evolved into today's ITCC, which now celebrates its 50th anniversary in 2026 (ISA, n.d.).

Events were developed to test the knowledge and skill of professional arborists, with chapter and associate-organization qualifiers feeding the annual world championship; related commercial series include STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, which packages similar disciplines (chop, saw, climb) as spectator sport (ITCC-ISA, n.d.).
