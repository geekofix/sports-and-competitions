Canoe Marathon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canoe marathon is long-distance racing on flat or slow-moving water over distances of roughly 20 to 30 km, governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF). Races are held in kayaks (K1, K2) and Canadian canoes (C1, C2) with mass starts, and competitors must carry their boats around mandatory portages at dams, weirs or other obstacles. The ICF Canoe Marathon World Championships are an annual event, and canoe marathon was also on the program of the 2025 World Games in Chengdu.

Rules

ICF marathon rules set minimum distances by age and sex category, with senior world championship races over about 30 km for men and shorter distances for women and juniors, and a required number of portages depending on the course. The race starts as a mass start or in heats; paddlers may draft behind other boats, and the first boat to cross the finish line wins. K1 and C1 classes are paddled kneeling or sitting with a single-bladed paddle; K2 and C2 use double-bladed paddles. Safety equipment such as life jackets is mandatory, and time penalties apply for rule infringements.

History

The ICF, founded in 1924 in Copenhagen, added canoe marathon to its world championship program in 1988. The discipline combines endurance racing with the practical skill of portaging around obstacles, reflecting canoeing's roots in travel and expedition paddling. National marathon series and the ICF Marathon World Cup feed into the annual world championships, which have produced legendary long-distance paddlers such as Hungarian star Krisztina Bedecs and multiple world champions from Spain and France.
