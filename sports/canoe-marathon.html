Canoe Marathon

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Canoe marathon is the long-distance racing discipline of canoeing and kayaking, governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF, rebranded Paddle Worldwide in 2026). Races run on rivers, lakes or the sea over distances typically from about 3.4 km (short race) to 30 km or more, usually starting and ending at the same point and including mandatory portages where paddlers carry their boat around weirs or dams (ICF, n.d.). The 2026 ICF Canoe Marathon World Championships were held in Gualeguaychú, Argentina, from 22 to 25 October 2026 — preceded from 21 October by the Masters World Championships on the same course, where Olympic champion Hugh Fisher (K2 1000 m gold at Los Angeles 1984) won the men's K1 70–74 title by 0.65 seconds and South Africa's Bridgitte Hartley (London 2012 K1 500 m bronze, chair of the ICF Athletes' Committee) defended her women's title (ICF, 2026; PaddleStats, 2026). Race categories cover K1, K2, C1 and C2 for men and women, plus junior, under-23, senior, masters and paracanoe classes; the senior K1 distance at the worlds is about 26–30 km and the short race 3.4 km with heats and finals (ICF, n.d.). Top current paddlers include South Africa's Hank McGregor — widely considered the greatest marathon canoeist of all time — and Australia's Jemma Smith in ocean racing, while Hungarian Renáta Csay and Spain's Manuel Campos and Mara Santos dominated the 2000s–2010s worlds (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Under the ICF Canoe Marathon Competition Rules, races are time-trial events on a marked course with mandatory portages; paddlers may draft behind other boats, and the classic tactical element is the portage, where boats are carried around obstacles. Distances at world championships: senior K1/C1 races of about 26–30 km with up to three portages, K2/C2 slightly shorter, and a short race of 3.4 km with heats and a final; junior and U23 distances are reduced (e.g., 19–22.6 km), and the masters program runs on the same course ahead of the seniors (ICF, n.d.). Boat classes: K1, K2, C1, C2 for men and women; minimum boat weights apply (10 kg for C1, 14 kg for C2 in sprint; marathon boats are lighter and longer). Paracanoe marathon (since 2022) uses KL and VL classes over reduced distances. The start is a mass start from a pontoon or beach; the first paddler across the line wins, with photo-finish timing. Fouls: obstructing another paddler deliberately or leaving the marked course; penalties are time additions or disqualification (ICF, n.d.). Men's and women's races follow identical rules, with women's K1 debuting at the 1988 worlds in Nottingham (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Marathon canoe racing has roots in the voyageur canoe brigades of North America and in European long-distance paddling; the ICF Canoe Marathon World Championships have been held since 1988 (first in Nottingham, England), and canoe marathon was an Olympic demonstration/medal event from 1936 to 1956 (men's 10,000 m). Legendary races include the Devizes to Westminster International Canoe Race (125 miles/201 km on the Thames, since 1891), the Berg River Canoe Marathon in South Africa (4 days, ~240 km), the AuSable River Canoe Marathon (26.5 miles) in New York, the Adirondack Canoe Classic and the Dusi Canoe Marathon in South Africa. South African Hank McGregor, who won the Berg River Marathon and multiple world titles, is widely considered the greatest marathon canoeist of all time; Canada's Serge Corbin is another icon with multiple world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.).
