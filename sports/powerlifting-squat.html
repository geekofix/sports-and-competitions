Powerlifting Squat

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The squat is the first of the three lifts in competitive powerlifting: the barbell is taken on the back, the lifter bends the knees and descends until the hip crease is below the top of the knee, then stands erect again under the referee's commands. It opens every full powerlifting competition and is widely regarded as the most technically demanding of the three lifts to judge, because depth and control are assessed visually by three referees.

Rules

Under IPF technical rules, the lifter takes the bar from the uprights and steps back, then stands upright and motionless with knees locked, at which point the chief referee gives the "squat" command and the lifter descends. The lifter must bend the knees and lower the body until the top surface of the legs at the hip joint is lower than the top of the knees, then recover to an upright position with knees locked and stand motionless before the "rack" command. Faults include double bouncing out of the bottom, any downward movement during the recovery, failure to reach required depth, shifting the feet, contacting the bar or supports with the plates, or dumping the bar. Equipped lifters wear a supportive squat suit and, in equipped divisions, knee wraps, while classic lifters may use only knee sleeves. Each lifter receives three attempts and single-lift squat championships are contested on the same technical basis.

History

Squatting with a barbell on the shoulders descends directly from 19th- and early 20th-century strongman "deep knee bends", and the lift assumed its modern form as racks and standardized barbells spread through mid-century gyms; in the "odd lift" contests it was fixed as the opening discipline of powerlifting and became the sport's record-setting lift in the equipped era, when squat suits and knee wraps allowed totals far beyond raw lifting. In the IPF's classic (raw) era, squat world records have been reset repeatedly — among the recent greats, the American champion Bonica Brown set classic and equipped world records in the women's 84+ kg class, including an equipped squat of 310.5 kg at the 2017 World Games. The squat remains the standard measure of lower-body strength across strength sports, weightlifting's snatch and clean and jerk being its closest technical relatives.
