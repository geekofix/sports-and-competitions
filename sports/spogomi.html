SpoGomi

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

SpoGomi (from the Japanese for "sport" and gomi, garbage) is a competitive litter-picking sport in which teams of three jog through a defined area for one hour, collecting as much trash as possible, and then sort it for 20 minutes; the team scoring the most points wins, with points awarded by the amount and type of waste — cigarette butts score highest (World Spogomi Federation, n.d.). The sport was invented in 2008 by Kenichi Mamitsuka of the Social Sports Initiative with support from the Nippon Foundation, and is governed by the Nippon Foundation Spogomi Federation, which stages a World Cup; the first World Cup was held in Tokyo in November 2023 with 21 national teams and was won by Great Britain, and the second in Tokyo on 27–31 October 2025 drew 33 teams, with Japan's team "Smile Story" defeating Germany's "The Grandline-Cleaners" in the final (Wikipedia, n.d.). By November 2024 the movement had collected about 193,120 kg of litter with roughly 190,000 participants worldwide, and the next World Cup is planned for 2027. SpoGomi is open to women and men alike, teams are usually mixed, and women compete on the same terms as men in every division (World Spogomi Federation, n.d.).

Rules

A standard SpoGomi competition fields teams of three (some rule sets allow three to five) players on a course of roughly one square kilometre; the picking phase lasts exactly one hour and is followed by a 20-minute sorting phase in which the collected bags are weighed and categorized (World Spogomi Federation, n.d.). During the picking phase teammates must stay within 10 metres of each other and running is forbidden — jogging is allowed — and each team may only collect litter in public areas designated by the organizers. Points are awarded by weight and category, with cigarette butts and small plastics scoring the most per kilogram, so strategy favors hunting high-value waste; prohibited items include waste from public bins, other people's property, bulky or hazardous waste, and anything from gutters, rivers, seas or private property, and collecting prohibited items can disqualify the team (Wikipedia, n.d.). A team that arrives more than 10 minutes late, is caught running, or falsifies the weight of its bags is disqualified, and official World Cup matches are played in a bracket format with group stages leading to semifinals and a final. Men and women compete together in mixed or single-gender teams under identical rules, and there are no age or gender classifications beyond the open team format (World Spogomi Federation, n.d.).

History

SpoGomi was invented in 2008 by Kenichi Mamitsuka, founder of the Social Sports Initiative, with backing from the Nippon Foundation, which established the Nippon Foundation Spogomi Federation to run international competition (Wikipedia, n.d.). The movement grew from Japanese local cleanups into an international sport: the inaugural Spogomi World Cup took place in Tokyo in November 2023, where 21 teams from 21 countries competed and Great Britain's team collected the most, about 60 kg, to win the first title. The second World Cup, held in Tokyo from 27 to 31 October 2025 with 33 teams, was won by the Japanese team "Smile Story" with roughly 165 points, ahead of Germany's "The Grandline-Cleaners"; cumulative collection passed 193,120 kg by November 2024 with about 190,000 participants, and the next edition is scheduled for 2027 (World Spogomi Federation, n.d.).
