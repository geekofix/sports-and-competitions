Combined Driving

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Combined driving is the equestrian three-phase test for horse-drawn vehicles, governed by the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI). A driver (with grooms) takes a single horse, pair or four-in-hand through three phases: a dressage test, a marathon cross-country phase with obstacles, and a cones phase in the arena. It is contested at FEI World Championships for singles, pairs and fours, as well as at European Championships and the FEI Driving World Cup.

Rules

Under the FEI Driving Rules (2026 edition, in force from 1 January 2026), each phase is judged by penalty points. The dressage phase is a memorized test in a 100 x 40 m arena. The marathon phase runs over roughly 10 km of roads and tracks with up to eight obstacle complexes (water, bridges, gates), timed with allowed times. The cones phase is a timed precision course through a field of cones in the arena; each ball knocked off adds penalty points. The lowest total penalties after all three phases wins. Safety rules require helmets and body protectors for drivers and grooms.

History

Horse driving as a sport grew from 19th-century carriage driving clubs, and the FEI, founded in Lausanne in 1921, took over international driving. Combined driving became the FEI's championship format, with biennial world championships for singles, pairs and teams. Four-in-hand driving is especially associated with Hungary and Germany, whose teams have dominated world championships for decades. The FEI Driving World Cup and the CAI (concours d'attelage international) circuit form the international calendar.
