Dressage

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dressage is the equestrian discipline often called "horse ballet": horse and rider perform a memorized sequence of movements (a "test") in a rectangular arena, judged on precision, impulsion, obedience and harmony (Fédération Equestre Internationale [FEI], n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the FEI and is one of the three Olympic equestrian disciplines alongside eventing and show jumping. At the 2026 FEI World Championships in Aachen, 11–23 August 2026, Great Britain's Charlotte Fry on Glamourdale defended the individual dressage title, winning both the Grand Prix Special (82.295%) and the Grand Prix Freestyle (91.907%), ahead of Denmark's Cathrine Laudrup-Dufour, who took silver in both tests (80.927% and 91.236%), while Germany won the team title; at the 2026 FEI World Cup Finals in Fort Worth, 4–8 April 2026, Great Britain's Becky Moody won the dressage title on Jägerbomb (FEI, n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current riders are — in women's dressage — 2026 world individual champion Charlotte Fry of Great Britain, 2026 World Cup Final winner Becky Moody and Paris 2024 Olympic team silver medallist Cathrine Laudrup-Dufour of Denmark, pictured below (FEI, n.d.-b) (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.-a) (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.-b).

Rules

Dressage is played under the FEI Dressage Rules, the official rulebook, at national, continental and world level, including the Olympic Games and FEI World Championships (FEI, n.d.-a). The standard arena is 20 m × 60 m (a 20 m × 40 m arena is used at lower levels) and is marked with the letters A, K, V, E, S, H, C, M, B and F; riders perform a prescribed test of movements — walk, trot, canter, transitions, lateral movements, pirouettes and, at the highest level (Grand Prix), movements such as piaffe, passage and flying changes (FEI, n.d.-a). A panel of judges awards a mark from 0 to 10 for each movement and for collective marks (gaits, impulsion, submission, rider's position and aids), and the total is expressed as a percentage that determines the ranking (FEI, n.d.-a). At the Olympic Games and World Championships the team competition is decided in the Grand Prix test with three riders per nation, and the individual medals are decided through the Grand Prix, the Grand Prix Special and, for the leading combinations, the Grand Prix Freestyle, in which riders perform to music (FEI, n.d.-a). Riders must wear an approved helmet and formal dress, horses must meet the FEI minimum-age rules (seven years at the Olympic Games), and the FEI Veterinary Regulations define lameness checks, the elimination for horse welfare concerns ("blood rule") and doping and medication control (FEI, n.d.-a). Illegal equipment and any abuse of the horse are penalized up to elimination (FEI, n.d.-a). Men and women compete directly against each other in the same classes — dressage is one of the very few Olympic sports with fully mixed individual competition — and there are separate team and individual podiums only at championship level, not separate classes (FEI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Dressage derives from classical cavalry training: the Greek Xenophon wrote the first known treatise on horsemanship in the fourth century BC, and the Renaissance masters Federico Grisone (1550) and Antoine de Pluvinel systematized the art, which reached its institutional peak with the Spanish Riding School of Vienna, founded in 1735 and the oldest classical dressage school in the world (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The modern sport was formalized in the late nineteenth century, entered the Olympic program at Stockholm 1912 — the first individual gold went to the Swedish officer Axel von Fersen Sr. on Snaefell — and was one of the first Olympic events open to women, who have competed since Helsinki 1952 (Olympics.com, n.d.). The Fédération Equestre Internationale was founded in 1921 in Lausanne and has run the FEI World Championships since 1966 (FEI, n.d.-b). Icons of the sport include the German Hans Günter Winkler (1926–2018), seven Olympic gold medals, and his compatriot Reiner Klimke (1936–1999), six Olympic golds; on the women's side, Isabell Werth of Germany (born 21 July 1969 in Rheinberg), pictured below, holds the record of twelve Olympic medals and is the most decorated equestrian Olympian of all time, while Great Britain's Charlotte Dujardin won individual gold with Valegro at London 2012 and Rio 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
