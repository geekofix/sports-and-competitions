Calcio Fiorentino

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Calcio Fiorentino — also called calcio storico — is the historic full-contact football game of Florence, Italy, played every June since 1930 on a sand-covered pitch in Piazza Santa Croce: four teams of 27 players, distinguished by the colours of the city's quarters — Azzurri of Santa Croce (blue), Bianchi of Santo Spirito (white), Rossi of Santa Maria Novella (red) and Verdi of San Giovanni (green). Each match lasts 50 minutes without a halftime; a goal scored by getting the ball across the end line (a caccia) counts one point, and getting it over the side lines counts half a point (mezzo caccia). The 2026 final on June 24, 2026, saw the Azzurri of Santa Croce defeat the Rossi of Santa Maria Novella 19–18 — decided by a single chasse — the Azzurri's first title since 2022, with Flavio Cobolli as Magnifico Messere (master of ceremonies) (Federazione Italiana Giochi e Sport Tradizionali [FIGEST], n.d.). The game is organized today by the Comune di Firenze together with the FIGeST, and since 2008 players must be under 40 and born in or resident in Florence for at least ten years (Comune di Firenze, n.d.).

On the women's side, calcio storico is traditionally a men's game — women have not played the official Piazza Santa Croce match — but women's football in Florence and Tuscany is strong, and women's calcio storico exhibitions have been organized in recent years; the women's game in Italy is governed within the FIGC's football programme (FIGEST, n.d.) (Visit Tuscany, n.d.).

Rules

Under the rules codified in the 16th century and still used today, the pitch is a rectangle of about 80 × 40 m covered with sand, marked with a centre line and a centre circle; each of the four teams fields 27 players — forwards (combattenti), midfielders and defenders — plus a captain (capitano) and a standard-bearer. There is no goalkeeper in the modern game: the whole team defends its end line. The match lasts 50 minutes with no halftime; a caccia — carrying or throwing the ball over the opponent's end line — scores 1 point, and a mezzo caccia over the side lines scores half a point. Players may use hands and arms but not in a way that constitutes foul play: punching, kicking below the knee, gouging and tripping are forbidden, and a referee (collegio dei Giudici) and marshals enforce the rules; rough play brings warnings and ejections (FIGEST, n.d.). Equipment: a leather ball (modern: 540–560 g, 68–70 cm circumference) and the team colours; barefoot or studded play is at the organizers' discretion — modern players wear football boots on the sand (FIGEST, n.d.). Formats: the annual tournament in Florence on the last Sunday of June (three matches: Azzurri vs Bianchi, then the winners and Verdi etc. — the exact pairing rotates), plus invitational matches abroad. Men's and women's rules are identical in principle, but the official Florentine match is men-only; youth and amateur calcio storico games use modified team sizes and shorter halves (FIGEST, n.d.) (Comune di Firenze, n.d.).

History

Calcio Fiorentino dates back to at least the 16th century; the rules were codified around 1530, and Giovanni de' Bardi published a set of 33 articles in 1580 that standardized teams, the pitch and scoring (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). The most famous match was played on February 17, 1530, during the siege of Florence by the imperial troops of Charles V — the Florentines defied the besiegers by playing calcio on the Piazza Santa Croce. The game was banned at times (notably after the Medici restoration in 1530 and again in the 18th century) and was revived in its modern form in 1930 by the Opera Nazionale Dopolavoro under the Fascist regime, with interruptions from 1942–45 and in 2020 (pandemic); it has been played every June since, with players required since 2008 to be under 40 and long-time Florence residents (FIGEST, n.d.) (Comune di Firenze, n.d.). Early depictions of the game survive in art, including a 1688 print of a match at Piazza Santa Croce (Visit Tuscany, n.d.).
