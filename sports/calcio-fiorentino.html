Calcio Fiorentino

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Calcio Storico Fiorentino ("historic Florentine football"), also called calcio in costume or calcio in livrea, is a 16th-century ball game unique to Florence, Italy, that mixes elements of association football, rugby and wrestling. It is still played once a year in Piazza Santa Croce, which is covered with sand for the occasion, by four teams of 27 players each representing one of the city's historic quarters: the Azzurri of Santa Croce, the Rossi of Santa Maria Novella, the Bianchi of Santo Spirito and the Verdi of San Giovanni. The tournament is organized by the Comune di Firenze and the teams are affiliated with FIGeST, the Italian federation of traditional games and sports.

Rules

The annual tournament consists of two semifinals in June and a final on 24 June, the feast day of Saint John the Baptist, Florence's patron saint. A match lasts 50 minutes without a halftime break on a sand-covered pitch roughly 80 by 40 meters with a full-width netted goal at each end. A goal (caccia) is scored when the ball enters the opponent's net and counts one point; a ball that goes over the goal line without entering the net counts half a point (mezzo caccia). The ball may be played with any part of the body. Players may attack each other physically, but kicking to the head, attacking from behind and ganging up on one opponent are banned and lead to expulsion. Since 2008, players must be under 40, born in Florence or resident there for at least ten years, and free of serious criminal convictions.

History

Calcio storico dates back to the 15th and 16th centuries and is possibly a descendant of the Roman ball game harpastum. The first written rules were recorded in 1580 by Giovanni de' Bardi in a 33-article regulation written in the Florentine dialect. A famous match was played in Piazza Santa Croce on 17 February 1530, during the siege of Florence by the imperial troops of Emperor Charles V, as a defiant gesture by the besieged city. Interest in the game waned in the early 17th century, and it was revived in 1930 under Benito Mussolini together with the Corteo Storico, a historical parade of over 500 costumed figures. The modern tournament has been held continuously since then except for breaks such as 1942-1945 and 2020. The most successful team since 1979 is the Azzurri of Santa Croce with more than 20 titles; in the 2026 final they beat the Rossi 19 to 18.5.
