Mallakhamb

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mallakhamb (malla = wrestler, khamba = pole — “wrestling pole”) is the traditional Indian sport in which a gymnast performs aerial yoga and gymnastic poses in concert with a vertical wooden pole, a hanging pole or a rope. It is practiced by men and women — rope mallakhamb is the primary competitive apparatus for women — and has its own world championships (first held in Mumbai in 2019 with 150+ athletes from 15+ countries; Himani Uttam Parab won gold in the rope long set). Madhya Pradesh declared mallakhamb its official state sport on 9 April 2013 (by 2017 more than 20 states had followed), with the Prabhash Joshi Award and a dedicated academy in Ujjain; the Mallakhamb Federation of India (founded by Bamshankar Joshi and others in Ujjain, first all-India nationals 28–29 January 1981) governs the sport with 28 affiliated state associations.

Rules

Three apparatus are contested: pole mallakhamb (a vertical teak/sheesham pole about 2.25–2.75 m high, polished with castor oil, with head, neck and tapering body), hanging mallakhamb (a shorter pole suspended from hooks and chains so it swings) and rope mallakhamb (a hanging cotton rope without knots). The athlete climbs, grips, wraps legs and performs static and dynamic poses and dismounts; judges score difficulty, execution and artistry. Competitions are held in age and gender categories under the Mallakhamb Federation of India's rules (competitors wear langot and shorts, or jangiya for pole/hanging; rope has no compulsory set). Variants include weapon (hatyari), unsupported (niradhar) and double-pole forms.

History

Pole exercises appear on Chandraketugarh pottery (2nd century BC–1st century AD), the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzhang witnessed pole climbing at Prayagraj in the 7th century, and the earliest literary mention is in Someshvara III's Manasollasa (1135 AD). The sport was revived in the early 19th century by Balambhatta Dada Deodhar, teacher of Peshwa Baji Rao II; Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi learned it with Nana Saheb and Tantia Tope. Mallakhamb was demonstrated at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, entered the National Gymnastics Championships in 1958 (first national championship Gwalior 1962), was removed from the Gymnastics Federation of India in 1977, and was reorganized under the Mallakhamb Federation of India from 1981. The first World Mallakhamb Championship was held in Mumbai in 2019.
