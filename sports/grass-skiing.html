Grass Skiing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Grass skiing is alpine skiing on grass: athletes race down prepared grass slopes on special grass skis with caterpillar-track bindings, using poles and hard boots, in the FIS disciplines slalom, giant slalom, super-G and super combined (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It was developed in Germany in the 1960s as summer training for alpine skiers, grew rapidly in the 1970s, and has been an FIS discipline since the 1985 Congress in Vancouver; the first World Championships were held in 1979 (Germany's Vincent Riefe won three golds) and the first overall World Cup in 2000 (Stefano Sartori of Italy and Sylva Lipníčková of Czechia) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b; International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a).

The FIS Grass Skiing World Cup runs each summer from the Czech Republic to Switzerland, with venues such as Rettenbach (Austria) and Marbachegg (Switzerland) in the 2025/26 season; the biennial World Championships were last staged in 2025, and the 2024 Junior World Championships at Orlické Záhoří saw Andrea Iori of Italy complete a hat-trick (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c). Current stars include Edoardo Frau of Italy and Martin Batak of Czechia; the picture shows a slalom race at Rettenbach (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c; International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a).

Rules

The FIS International Competition Rules (ICR) apply as in alpine skiing on snow: timed runs through a gate course (two runs in slalom/giant slalom with the second course reversed, one run in super-G and super combined), electronic timing, and the FIS points system (100-75-60-50-45… down to 30th) for World Cup standings (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c).

Equipment is regulated: grass skis are 65–105 cm long depending on category, with tracked binding systems, plus poles, helmet and hard boots; courses are prepared with artificial grass mats or natural turf and must be homologated by FIS; men and women race separately, with junior and U21 categories at the World Championships (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

The sport's all-time legends include Austria's Ingrid Hirschhofer with 22 world championship titles (pictured with her grass skis at the 2009 Worlds in Rettenbach), the Czech Jan Němec with nine overall World Cup titles, Italy's Stefano Sartori, first men's overall World Cup winner in 2000, and Japan's Chisaki Maeda with 13 world titles between 2015 and 2023 (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The 2009 World Championships at the Rettenbachschale in Austria remain a landmark edition, with flower ceremonies for each discipline and the super combined deciding the overall champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
