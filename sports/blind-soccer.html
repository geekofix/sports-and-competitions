Blind Soccer

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Blind soccer is the common American name for blind football 5-a-side — the same IBSA-governed game for B1 (blind) players — and is also used for partially sighted football (futsal for B2/B3 players). The game is played with a rattling ball on a boarded pitch, with sighted goalkeepers and guides directing play. It is governed by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA) and contested at the Paralympics (B1) and IBSA World Games.

Rules

In blind soccer (5-a-side, B1), the rules mirror blind football: a 40 x 20 m pitch with boards, five players per team including a sighted goalkeeper, two 20-minute halves, no offside, no throw-ins, and a noise-making ball; players must shout when challenging for the ball. In partially sighted futsal (B2/B3), teams play FIFA futsal rules with adaptations: at least two B2 players on court at all times, a smaller ball that makes less noise, and no four-second goalkeeper possession rule. At the Paralympics, only the B1 game is played; B2/B3 futsal is contested at the IBSA World Games and regional championships.

History

Blind soccer grew from special-school football in Europe and South America in the 1980s; Brazil organized a tournament in 1980 and Spain held the first national championship in 1986. The IBSA has governed the sport since 1996, held the first World Championships in 1998, and the game entered the Paralympics in 2004. Brazil dominated the early Paralympic golds; France won the first non-Brazilian title at Paris 2024.
