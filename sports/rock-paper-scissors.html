Rock Paper Scissors

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rock Paper Scissors (RPS) is a hand game in which two players simultaneously throw one of three shapes — fist (rock), flat hand (paper), or V-shaped fingers (scissors) — with rock beating scissors, scissors beating paper, and paper beating rock, and identical throws counting as ties. Played worldwide as a quick decision-making game, it is also organised as a competitive pursuit with official rulesets, most prominently by the World Rock Paper Scissors Association (WRPSA), which has run the game as a sport since 2015, with the Toronto-based World RPS Society having staged world championships from 2002 to 2009.

Rules

Under the WRPSA ruleset the referee calls "Rock, Paper, Scissors, Shoot!" with both players pumping a closed fist in unison and revealing their throw on the final beat, so both hands land at the same moment; priming a different number of times or exposing a hand late is a foul, and changing the shape after the reveal is an automatic forfeit. Only the three legal shapes count — invented gestures such as "fire", "well" or "gun" are not moves. Matches are typically best-of-three, with the first player to win two rounds taking the match; in WRPSA competition two out of three throws decide a set and two sets decide a match, with ties simply re-thrown. Disputes are resolved by the referee, whose call is final in tournament play.

History

The game's roots lie in the Chinese finger game shoushiling, recorded as early as the Han dynasty, and via Japan, where it is known as jan-ken, it spread through the world during the 20th century, reaching global awareness under names including "roshambo" and "jan-ken-pon" and becoming the world's default randomising ritual for settling disputes. The competitive era began in Toronto, where brothers Douglas and Graham Walker founded the World RPS Society, published The Official Rock Paper Scissors Strategy Guide in 2004 and staged international championships from 2002 to 2009 that at their peak drew hundreds of competitors such as the 495 who entered the 2005 World Championships. In the USA, the USA Rock Paper Scissors League ran televised championships in the mid-2000s with prize money reported at up to $50,000. Since 2015 the World Rock Paper Scissors Association has operated the modern circuit, running numbered official rules, online and in-person tournaments and world rankings, with its events and online platforms staging world championship events and a world championship held annually, complete with live stream broadcasts and national championships feeding the world final.
