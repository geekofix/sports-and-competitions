Land Sailing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Land sailing — also called sand yachting or land yachting — is the sport of driving a wind-powered, wheeled vehicle (a land yacht or sand yacht) across beaches, dry lake beds or even frozen lakes with skates (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Sand et Land Yachting (FISLY), founded in 1962, which sets the international classes and the International Sand and Land Yacht Racing Rules (ISRR) (FISLY, n.d.-a). The classes range from the single-sail Class 1 and the Standard class to the high-speed Class 5, Class 7 (speed sail) and Class 8 (kite buggying — see kite buggying on this site) (FISLY, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete in all classes at international and record level; there are no women-only classes, and mixed crews are standard in multi-seat yachts (NALSA, n.d.-a). The absolute world land speed record is 202.9 km/h (126.1 mph), set by Richard Jenkins in the jet-black Greenbird on 26 March 2009 at Ivanpah Dry Lake, California, under FISLY verification (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

International competition and records are run under the FISLY ISRR: each class has defined sail area, dimensions and minimum crew age — for example, Class 5 yachts carry about 5.5 m² of sail, and the one-design Blokart (New Zealand) has a 3 m² or 5.5 m² rig option — and record attempts are timed over a measured kilometre with wind-speed limits (FISLY, n.d.-a). Races are held on beaches, dry lake beds and grass airfields; on ice, yachts run on skates instead of wheels (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the United States, records are verified by NALSA (North American Land Sailing Association), the national member body of FISLY, and the ISRR Appendix sets minimum ages per class for youth competition (NALSA, n.d.-a). Men's and women's competitors race in the same classes under identical rules (FISLY, n.d.-a). Rule source: FISLY International Sand and Land Yacht Racing Rules, https://fisly.org/ (FISLY, n.d.-a).

History

Wind-driven carriages date back centuries — a Chinese land-sailing carriage is depicted in a 1626 illustration, and in 1785 Henry Oxenden exhibited a sailing carriage in England — but modern land sailing as a sport began with beach racing in California and Europe in the early 1900s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The international governing body FISLY was founded in 1962 and standardized the class system; the one-design Blokart was developed in New Zealand in the 1990s and became the entry-level racing class worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The speed era began in the 1990s at Ivanpah Dry Lake, culminating in Richard Jenkins's 202.9 km/h record in Greenbird on 26 March 2009 — faster than any previous land sailing record and verified under FISLY rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Blokart also holds the 24-hour distance record of 589 km, set in 2022 at Loring Air Force Base, Maine (NALSA, n.d.-a).
