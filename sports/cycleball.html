Cycleball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cycle ball (Radball) is an indoor cycling team sport played in a gymnasium: two teams of two riders (a field player and a goalkeeper) on bicycles try to score goals with a football-sized ball into the opponent's goal, using their bike's front wheel. It is one of the two disciplines of UCI indoor cycling, alongside artistic cycling, and is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), which runs the Indoor Cycling World Championships.

Rules

Under the UCI indoor cycling rules, cycle ball matches consist of two halves of seven minutes (senior men) with a short break. Each team has two riders: the goalkeeper defends the 2 m wide goal and may use hands within the goal area, while both players may play the ball with the front wheel (never the rear wheel or hands elsewhere). Free kicks and penalty kicks are awarded for fouls such as handball or dangerous riding; a goal from a penalty is allowed. The team with more goals wins; in case of a tie, extra time or penalty shootouts decide at championships. Players wear helmets and indoor shoes.

History

Cycle ball originated in Germany and Switzerland in the late 19th century, inspired by polo on bicycles, and was codified in the early 20th century. The UCI included indoor cycling in its world championships from 1929. The sport remains strongest in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, the Czech Republic and Japan, with professional clubs and a lively world championship scene.
