Sepak Takraw Doubles

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sepak takraw doubles is the two-a-side variant of the Southeast Asian kick-volleyball game, played on a smaller court with a lighter ball and one substitute per side under the same ISTAF laws that govern the full game (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Doubles has been on the Asian Games programme since 2006 for the men and since 2010 for the women, and it is also contested at the King's Cup World Championship in Bangkok, where the Philippines famously beat Brunei 3–2 in an early doubles final (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the current leading pairs, the women's game is headed by Myanmar's Kyu Kyu Thin and Phyu Phyu Than, gold medallists in the women's doubles at the 2014 Asian Games in Incheon — a year after the same Myanmar trio had won gold at Guangzhou 2010 — and by South Korea's Kim I-seul and Lee Min-ju, who contested that 2014 final, while on the men's side Myanmar's Zaw Zaw Aung and Zaw Lat took the 2014 doubles title and Japan's Yuki Sato added a team-doubles bronze at the 2018 Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Rules

In doubles, two players per side plus one substitute take a court that is smaller than the full 13.40 m × 6.10 m regu court, with a lighter ball, while the net stays at 1.52 m for men and 1.42 m for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A side may play the ball a maximum of three times before returning it over the net, no player may touch it twice in a row, and hands and arms are forbidden in play except for the server's toss; the tekong serves from the centre service circle while the partner waits in a quarter circle at the net (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Faults include touching the net or posts, more than three touches, carrying or rolling the ball, serving out of position and playing the ball in the opponents' court (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A set is won at 15 points with a two-point margin — the 15-point set replaced the old 21-point set in the 2011 ISTAF rules revision — and matches are best of three sets, with a deciding set extended to 17 points at 14–14; team doubles at multi-sport games is decided by three doubles regus per nation, the first to win two (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women compete with the lower net and the lighter 150–160 g ball, and men's and women's doubles are separate events at the Asian Games, the SEA Games and the King's Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Doubles emerged alongside the standardisation of sepak takraw, when officials from Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Myanmar met in Kuala Lumpur in 1960 to unify the regional rules, and the compromise name sepak takraw — Malay sepak ("kick") plus Thai takraw ("woven ball") — became established from the 1965 Southeast Asian Peninsular Games, the first international medal edition (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's doubles entered the Asian Games programme at Doha 2006 and women's doubles at Guangzhou 2010; Myanmar's Zaw Zaw Aung and Zaw Lat won the 2014 men's doubles gold in Incheon, and the Myanmar women Kyu Kyu Thin, Phyu Phyu Than and Khin Hnin Wai took the women's doubles title there as well (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the King's Cup era the Philippines defeated Brunei 3–2 in an early doubles final, cementing the event's place in the Bangkok championship held annually since 1985 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The world governing body, the International Sepaktakraw Federation (ISTAF), was formed in 1988 out of the Asian Sepaktakraw Federation (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
