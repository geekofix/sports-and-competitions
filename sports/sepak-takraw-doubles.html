Sepak Takraw Doubles

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sepak takraw doubles is the two-player format of sepak takraw, Southeast Asia's kick-volleyball, in which two players per side contest a woven ball over a 1.5-metre net using only feet, knees, chest and head. Whereas the standard regu format fields three players with a specialist server and strikers, doubles demands all-round excellence from both players — each must serve, defend and attack — and it is contested internationally alongside regu at the sport's major events, including the King's Cup World Championship in Thailand, where doubles enjoys full medal status.

Rules

Doubles is played on the same court as regu — 13.4 by 6.1 metres with a net at 1.52 m for men and 1.42 m for women — but with only two players per team, who rotate between serving and the other positions between sets. The server kicks the ball over from the serving circle, and thereafter each side has up to three touches to return it; rallies end on faults such as the ball touching the ground, the net or an illegal contact. Under the rolling substitutions permitted by international rules, teams may substitute between sets, and matches follow the same scoring as regu: rally points to 21 per set (two-point lead, or first to 25 at 21–21), best of three sets. The game's most demanding feature is that both players must be able to cover the full court, producing spectacular diving saves and the acrobatic roll spike; additionally, there is no tekong-server-specialist division of labour, both players must be able to deliver the serve and cover the opponent's two-player attack.

History

The doubles format grew directly out of the standardised sepak takraw of the 1960 Kuala Lumpur agreements: the new sport allowed different team sizes — circle formats, regu (three) and doubles (two) — and international events soon included doubles alongside regu. The King's Cup World Championship in Thailand, the sport's most prestigious annual event since 1985, has long featured both regu and doubles categories, and national championships worldwide from Malaysia to the Philippines include doubles doubles gold-medal events because the format travelled well to smaller school and club settings. The International Sepaktakraw Federation continues to oversee doubles internationally through its King's Cup, ISTAF SuperSeries and World Cup events, with Thailand, Malaysia and Myanmar historically strong, and the format remains popular at the Southeast Asian Games' and Asian Games' sepak takraw tournament as an official doubles medal event.
