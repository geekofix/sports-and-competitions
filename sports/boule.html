Boule

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Boule is the French boules game in which players throw or roll hollow metal balls to land as close as possible to a small wooden target ball, the jack (but or cochonnet). The modern competitive form is pétanque, played standing in a circle with feet together; it is governed by the Fédération Internationale de Pétanque et Jeu Provençal (FIPJP), which organizes the Pétanque World Championships under the umbrella of the IOC-recognized World Pétanque & Bowls Federation.

Rules

Under FIPJP rules, pétanque is played by teams of three (triplettes) in men's competition, two (doublettes) or one (tête-à-tête), with three balls each (two in singles). The terrain is open ground, typically 15 x 4 m for competition. Players throw from inside a circle with both feet on the ground; the jack is thrown 6-10 m away. Points are scored for each ball closer to the jack than the opponent's closest ball, and games are played to 13 points. The FIPJP also sanctions the World Precision Shooting Championship (tir de précision).

History

Boules games date to ancient Greece and Rome; pétanque was invented in 1910 in La Ciotat, Provence, when Ernest Pitiot adapted the Provençal game (jeu provençal, played with a run-up) so his friend with a rheumatic leg could play standing still — "pèd tanca" ("feet together"). The FIPJP was founded in 1958 and the first men's triplette World Championship was held in 1961; pétanque is now played in over 100 countries and is part of the World Games.
