Riichi Mahjong

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Riichi mahjong is the Japanese rule set for the four-player tile game mahjong, distinguished by the riichi declaration — a 1,000-point wager that a player's hand needs no further changes — the use of dora bonus tiles revealed during play, and an elaborate system of scoring patterns called yaku, at least one of which is required to win a hand. It is played competitively in Japan by professional leagues and worldwide by clubs organized in national federations under the European Mahjong Association, with the World Riichi Championship as the top international competition.

Rules

Riichi mahjong uses 136 tiles (three suits of 1-9 in circles, bamboo and characters, plus honor tiles — winds and dragons; flowers are not used). Players draw and discard in turn, forming sets and a pair while calling discarded tiles for melded sets (chii, pon and kan). The heart of the game is riichi: a player with a closed hand one tile from winning may declare riichi with a 1,000-point stake, after which the hand may not be changed; a riichi declaration is itself a yaku and unlocks the ura dora — bonus indicators revealed under the dead wall only for riichi winners. A winning hand must contain at least one yaku (a scoring pattern such as riichi, tanyao, pinfu or yakuman class patterns like kokushi musou); dora and red fives add han value but are not yaku. Hands are scored in han (doubles) and fu (minipoints), with the thresholds — mangan and the successive limit hands haneman, baiman, sanbaiman and yakuman — determining point payments between players. Competition formats such as the World Riichi Championship use agreed international rule sets; the WRC rules specify head-bump resolution of multiple winning calls, no counted yakuman, and two-day or multi-round individual and team events scored cumulatively on table points.

History

Mahjong reached Japan from China in the early 20th century, and the Japanese rule set — formalized with the riichi bet, dora and rich yaku catalog — had taken its modern shape by the mid-20th century, becoming a mass pastime of mahjong parlors and, from the 1970s and 80s, of manga, anime and video game culture. Professional competition organized in Japanese leagues and rating systems, and the international scene followed: the European Mahjong Association certified riichi competition in Europe from the 2000s, with the European Riichi Championship first held at Hanover in 2008, and clubs spread across North America, China, and beyond. The World Riichi Championship, founded in 2014 by the French club Tri Nitro Tiles, has been staged every few years — Puteaux in 2014, Las Vegas in 2017, Vienna in 2022 and Tokyo in 2025 — with a growing field of over 250 players, Japanese professionals prominent among the champions, and a team event added in 2025.
