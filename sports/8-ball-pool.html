8-Ball Pool

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

8-Ball Pool is the most widely played pool (pocket billiards) game in the world: on a 9-foot table, 15 object balls — seven solids, seven stripes and the black 8-ball — are played with the cue ball. Professional play follows the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA) rules, and the WPA sanctions an official World Eight-ball Championship (World Pool-Billiard Association [WPA], n.d.-c). See also 9-Ball Pool, 10-Ball Pool, Billiards, Straight Pool and Carom Billiards on this site (World Pool-Billiard Association, n.d.-a) (World Pool-Billiard Association, n.d.-b) (World Pool-Billiard Association, n.d.-c).

Among the players shaping the game today are the Philippines' Chezka Centeno, who won the 2026 WPA Women's World Eight-ball Championship 9–6 over Kelly Fisher in Green Bay, Wisconsin, on July 26, 2026, and Singapore's Aloysius Yapp, who won the 2026 WPA World Eight-ball Championship 10–4 over Francisco Sánchez Ruiz in St. Louis on April 8, 2026 (WPA, n.d.-b). Austria's Jasmin Ouschan, the 2025 women's world champion (9–8 over Centeno) and head of the WPA women's ranking, is another leading figure on the women's side (WPA, n.d.-a; WPA, n.d.-b).

Rules

After the break the table is "open" until a player legally pockets a ball; that player is then assigned either the solid or the striped group. The assigned player must pocket all seven balls of their group and then legally call and pocket the 8-ball to win the rack. Pocketing the 8-ball early, on a foul, or off the wrong ball loses the rack immediately (WPA, 2025). The WPA break requires the cue ball to start behind the head string with ball-in-hand, and a push-out is allowed after a legal break. Standard fouls — scratch, contacting the wrong ball first, no rail after contact — give the opponent ball-in-hand anywhere on the table, and three consecutive fouls by one player lose the rack. The equipment follows standard pool specifications: a 9-foot table (playing surface about 254 × 127 cm / 100 × 50 in), six pockets and 57.15 mm (2 1/4 in) balls (WPA, 2025). Men, women, junior and wheelchair athletes all play the identical rule set; the WPA simply runs separate divisions of its championships (WPA, n.d.-c). Bar and league rules differ from the WPA game — some casual rule sets treat an 8-ball pocketed on the break as an instant win or an instant loss, and call-shot requirements vary between BCA and WPA events (Cue Sports International, n.d.).

History

8-ball evolved from earlier pool games in the United States in the 19th century and became the standard pub and league game in the mid-20th century. Professional 8-ball boomed from the 1980s onward with events such as the WPA World Championship and the US Open (WPA, n.d.-c). The WPA world 8-ball title ran from 2004 to 2012 in Fujairah — Efren Reyes of the Philippines won the inaugural 2004 edition — and was revived in 2022 under Predator sponsorship. Recent men's world champions include Francisco Sánchez Ruiz of Spain (2022, 10–6 over Carlo Biado's compatriot Wojciech Zieliński of Poland), Shane Van Boening of the USA (2023), Joshua Filler of Austria (2024) and Albin Ouschan of Austria (2025, 10–5 over Eklent Kaçi), before Aloysius Yapp's 2026 win (WPA, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the WPA Women's World Eight-ball Championship has been won in recent years by Jasmin Ouschan (2025) and Chezka Centeno (2026) (WPA, n.d.-b). Legendary players of the game include Earl Strickland, Efren Reyes and Shane Van Boening (Cue Sports International, n.d.).
