50m Pistol Shooting

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 50 m pistol — still often called free pistol — is the ISSF's classic long-distance precision pistol event: 60 shots at 50 m with a .22 Long Rifle pistol, fired one-handed within a maximum time of two hours; it is governed by the International Shooting Sport Federation and remains a World Championship and World Cup event, although it was removed from the Olympic programme after Rio 2016 (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 50 m pistol target has not changed since 1900 and the 50 m distance has been standard since 1912 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

At the 2026 Asian Rifle/Pistol Championships in New Delhi (2–14 February 2026) India's Saurabh Chaudhary won the men's 50 m pistol gold, and at the ISSF World Championship in Doha (1–14 November 2026) India's Ravinder Singh, Saurabh Chaudhary and Amanpreet Singh took the men's 50 m pistol team gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-b, n.d.-c). The world record is 583, set by South Korea's Jin Jong-oh at the 2014 World Championships in Granada; the Olympic record of 581, set by Alexander Melentyev at Moscow 1980, stood for 34 years (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the ISSF pistol rules the 50 m pistol course of fire is 60 shots within a maximum of two hours; the pistol must be .22 LR, loadable one round at a time, held and fired with one hand only (no support from the other hand or body), with conventional open sights — optical sights are not allowed — while trigger weight, grip shape, size and weight are otherwise largely unrestricted, which is why the event was called free pistol (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring is whole-ring on the 50 m target, maximum 600, and ties are broken by inner tens (ISSF, n.d.-c).

Men and women compete in separate classifications at the ISSF World Championships and World Cups (shooting has been mixed at ISSF level since 1966), but the event was men-only at the Olympics from 1900 to Rio 2016 and was dropped from the Olympic programme in 2017 in favour of mixed-team events; women's Olympic precision pistol is the 10 m air pistol and 25 m pistol (link below) (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Junior (under 21) categories also contest the event (ISSF, n.d.-a).

History

The 50 m pistol is one of shooting's oldest disciplines, tracing back to 1870s European indoor parlour shooting; it was added to the World Championship programme in 1900 and was an Olympic event for men from 1900 until Rio 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

South Korea's Jin Jong-oh, pictured at London 2012, is the event's most successful athlete — four Olympic gold medals (10 m air pistol and 50 m pistol at Beijing 2008 and London 2012) plus two silvers, and the 50 m pistol world record holder; other icons include Kyrgyzstan-born Alexander Melentyev, whose 1980 Olympic record stood for 34 years, and Russia's Mikhail Nestruyev (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
