Shotokan Karate

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shotokan is the first and most widely practised style of modern karate, founded by the Okinawan master Gichin Funakoshi (1868–1957) and named after his pen name Shōtō ("pine waves") plus kan ("hall"); it is taught worldwide by organisations such as the Japan Karate Association (JKA), founded in 1949 with Funakoshi as honorary head, while international competition is governed by the World Karate Federation (WKF) (JKA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the 2025 WKF World Championships in Cairo, the women's kata title went to Grace Lau of Hong Kong and the men's kata title to Kakeru Nishiyama of Japan, and the 61st European Championships were held in Frankfurt in 2026; kata has been on the Olympic programme since Tokyo 2020, where Japan's Ryo Kiyuna won gold (WKF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Figure: Funakoshi, G. (1924). Funakoshi Gichin [Photograph]. Source: Rentan Goshin Karate Jutsu. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain (PD-Japan-oldphoto). https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Funakoshi_Gichin.jpg

Among current elite athletes, the women's competition is led by kata world champion Grace Lau of Hong Kong (2025 Cairo) and by the women medallists of the WKF Premier League kumite circuit, while the men's side is headed by Kakeru Nishiyama of Japan (2025 world kata champion) and by Olympic kata champion Ryo Kiyuna of Japan (Tokyo 2020); women and men compete under identical rules, in separate kata events and in weight-categorised kumite (WKF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Within the combat sports and martial arts family on this site, its closest relatives are Karate, Karate Kumite, Shito Ryu Karate, Judo, Taekwondo; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Shotokan training rests on three pillars: kihon (basics), kata (forms) and kumite (sparring) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The style has 26 official kata — the five Heian kata derived from Ankō Itosu's Pinan forms and renamed with Japanese readings in 1936, plus advanced kata such as Kanku Dai, Bassai, Empi, Jitte and Hangetsu, and the Taikyoku series created by Funakoshi's son Gigō — and Funakoshi's Twenty Precepts (niju kun) govern dojo etiquette, beginning with "karate ni sente nashi" (there is no first attack in karate) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c; JKA, n.d.). Under WKF competition rules, kata are judged by a panel on technical and athletic performance, while kumite bouts last three minutes (two minutes at cadet level), with yuko (1 point), waza-ari (2 points) and ipon (3 points) scoring, eight foul categories from excessive contact to evading, and hansoku-chui or hansoku (disqualification) for serious offences; women's kumite weight categories are −55, −61, −68, −76 and +84 kg and men's −60, −67, −75, −84 and +84 kg, while kata is open-weight, and both genders follow the same rulebook (WKF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Gichin Funakoshi was born on 10 November 1868 in Shuri, Okinawa, trained under Ankō Asato and Ankō Itosu in both Shōrei-ryū and Shōrin-ryū, and demonstrated karate for Crown Prince Hirohito in Okinawa in 1921 before giving the first public demonstration of Okinawan karate on the Japanese mainland at the Ministry of Education exhibition in Tokyo in 1922, invited to the Kodokan by judō founder Kanō Jigorō (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). He opened the first Shotokan dojo in Mejiro, Tokyo, in 1936 — destroyed by bombing in 1945 — changed the written characters of "karate" from "China hand" to "empty hand" the same year, and in 1936 his students renamed the kata to Japanese readings (Kushanku became Kanku Dai, Naihanchi became Tekki, Chinto became Gankaku, Wanshu became Empi) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). His son Gigō (Yoshitaka) Funakoshi (1906–1945) developed the modern technical syllabus and the Taikyoku kata before dying of tuberculosis in 1945; after Gichin Funakoshi's death on 26 April 1957, his students founded the Japan Karate Association on 27 May 1949 — with Funakoshi as honorary head — and the style later split into Shotokai (Egami and Hironishi), the Shotokan Karate-Do International founded by Tsutomu Ohshima (born 1930) in Los Angeles in 1969, and SKI under Hirokazu Kanazawa (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The first All Japan Karate Championships were held in 1957, the WUKO world championships began in 1970 (WKF since 1998), and the JKA's quadrennial Funakoshi Gichin Cup is the successor world tournament named after the founder (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
