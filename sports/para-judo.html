Para Judo

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para judo is judo for athletes with a vision impairment, governed by IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation) using the International Judo Federation rules with IBSA modifications; since December 2021 athletes compete in two classes — J1 (severe vision impairment) and J2 (acute vision impairment) — inside their weight category and gender (IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation), n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It made its Paralympic debut at Seoul 1988 with men only, women's events were added at Athens 2004 (France's Karima Medjeded won the first women's title), and Paris 2024 at the Champ de Mars Arena staged 16 medal events for 146 athletes from 43 countries, the largest field ever (IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation), n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.).

Leading the women today: Yijie Shi of China won the -60 kg J1 gold at Paris 2024 and again at the 2026 IBSA World Championships in Tbilisi, where Chunfang Tian took +70 kg J1, Yi Liu the -52 kg J2, and Brazil's Brenda Freitas — Paris 2024 silver medallist — defended her -70 kg J1 title; the pictured ceremony is the women's -70 kg J2 final at Paris 2024 (2026, September 30; Olympics, n.d.). Among the men, Ion Basoc of Moldova became his country's first world champion with +95 kg J1 gold in Tbilisi 2026, beating Brazil's Wilians Silva de Araújo, the Paris 2024 +90 kg gold medallist (2026, September 30).

Rules

Bouts follow IJF rules — ippon, waza-ari, osaekomi, shido and hansoku-make — with two IBSA adaptations: every contest starts with both judoka already gripping each other's gi (kumikata), and if contact is broken the referee calls "mate", the pair returns to the centre and regrips; there is no penalty for stepping off the mat (IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation), n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Competitors are divided by gender, weight category (eight categories per gender at the Paralympics) and vision class J1/J2; the IBSA world ranking (points from IBSA competitions since July 2026 also serve LA28 qualification) seeds the draws (IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation), n.d.-c; IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation), n.d.-a).

History

Para judo began in England in the 1950s, spread to other countries in the 1970s, and entered the Paralympics at Seoul 1988 as the first sport of Asian origin on the programme; women joined at Athens 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The IBSA Judo World Championships are the top non-Paralympic event — Astana 2025 and Tbilisi 2026 (25–27 September 2026) — with European Championships in between; the pictured ceremony is the blind judo men's +90 kg awarding at the 2015 European Games in Baku (IBSA (International Blind Sports Federation), n.d.-a).
