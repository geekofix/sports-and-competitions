Belt Wrestling Alysh

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Alysh is the national belt wrestling style of Kyrgyzstan: an upright wrestling form in which competitors wear a jacket and a belt, must hold the opponent's belt at all times, and try to throw the opponent onto the mat. It is recognized by United World Wrestling (UWW, since 2008) and governed internationally together with the World Alysh Federation; the UWW World Belt Wrestling Championships include Alysh events.

Rules

Under UWW/World Alysh Federation rules, wrestlers wear blue or green jackets, white trousers and red belts and start with a fixed grip on the opponent's belt. Technical points are awarded by the amplitude of the throw: 1 point if the opponent lands on a knee or buttocks, 2 points sideways/on the hip, and 6 points for landing flat on the back; the first competitor to score 6 points wins the match. Alysh is contested in two styles — freestyle (leg attacks allowed) and classic (no leg attacks) — in men's and women's weight categories, with round-robin groups and semifinals at championships.

History

Alysh is considered one of the most ancient sports of Asia and the national traditional sport of Kyrgyzstan, where it is a core event of the World Nomad Games. The modern international discipline was codified in the early 2000s, FILA (now UWW) recognized it in 2008, and it has been contested at the Asian Games, the Summer Universiade and the World Combat Games. The World Alysh Federation coordinates international competition.
