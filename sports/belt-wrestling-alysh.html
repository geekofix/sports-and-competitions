Belt Wrestling Alysh

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Alysh is the Kyrgyz national form of belt wrestling — an upright, standing style in which the competitors must hold on to each other's belt at all times and try to throw each other onto the mat; it is recognised by United World Wrestling (UWW) as "wrestling alysh" since 2008 and regulated internationally by the International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh (IFWA) (United World Wrestling [UWW], n.d.-a). The 2026 season featured the Belt Wrestling Asian Championships in Bishkek in March 2026, the VI World Nomad Games in Kyrgyzstan (August 31–September 6, 2026), and the UWW World Belt Wrestling Championships scheduled for December 16, 2026 in Goa, India (UWW, n.d.-b). Competitors fight in weight classes in both the classic style (gripping the kushak) and freestyle (gripping the belt), and the women's programme is well established — Turkmenistan's Zuhra Madraimova and Nasiba Surkiewa were 2017 world champions, and Israel's Gregory Rudelson is among the best-known international alysh wrestlers (International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh, n.d.). Kyrgyzstan celebrated the sport officially with a 2018 postage stamp depicting alysh (United World Wrestling, n.d.-a) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-b).

Within the combat sports and martial arts family on this site, its closest relatives are Belt Wrestling, Wrestling, Judo, Boxing, Bare Knuckle Boxing; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Alysh is wrestled standing up: both competitors wear a jacket, knee-length trousers and a red belt, and each must hold the opponent's belt at all times — letting go is penalised (UWW, n.d.-a). The goal is to throw the opponent to the mat so that a part of the body other than the feet touches the ground; throws are scored 1, 2 or 6 points depending on which part of the body lands, and the first wrestler to reach 6 points wins the bout. A bout normally lasts about five minutes, with shorter times for children, women and elders. Classic style requires gripping the kushak (the belt loop) while freestyle allows gripping the belt anywhere, and the two styles are contested separately at world championships. Men's and women's bouts are run in parallel weight classes under identical rules, and junior categories use shorter bouts (International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh, n.d.).

History

Alysh has deep roots in Kyrgyz folk wrestling; the Epic of Gilgamesh (circa 2000 BC) already describes belt wrestling, and Kyrgyzstan issued a postage stamp depicting alysh in 2018 (see figure). The modern international sport was started in 2001 by Bayaman Erkinbayev, who was assassinated in 2005, and was developed with Rif Gaynanov until 2005, when it split into the styles now called Alysh and Kurash; UWW's predecessor FILA recognised alysh in 2008 (UWW, n.d.-a). Since then alysh has been a permanent fixture of the World Nomad Games — the VI edition in August–September 2026 in Kyrgyzstan included it alongside kurash and Qazaq Kuresi — of the Asian Games and the Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games programme (since 2013 Universiade), and of the UWW World Belt Wrestling Championships, whose 2026 edition is scheduled for December 16, 2026 in Goa, India (UWW, n.d.-b). On the women's side, Turkmenistan's Zuhra Madraimova and Nasiba Surkiewa won world titles in 2017, and Israeli judoka Gregory Rudelson has competed internationally in alysh (International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh, n.d.).
