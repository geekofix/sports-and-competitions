Boomerang Throwing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Boomerang throwing is the sport of throwing returning boomerangs for accuracy, distance, time aloft and trick catches; the word boomerang comes from an Aboriginal Australian language of the Sydney region, and competitive throwing is coordinated internationally by the International Federation of Boomerang Associations (IFBA), which sets the international rules and helps organise the biennial World Championships (Wikipedia, n.d.; International Federation of Boomerang Associations, n.d.-a). More than a dozen countries now field national teams, and the 2026 World Boomerang Championships were held in Bandung, Indonesia, from 26 July to 5 August 2026, where Team USA “Raditude” won the overall team title (United States Boomerang Association, n.d.; International Federation of Boomerang Associations, n.d.-b).

Competitions are open to women and men in the same events, with separate women's and men's placings recorded at the World Championships; the sport's disciplines are judged by international officials under IFBA rules, so both genders throw identical equipment — sport boomerangs weigh under 100 g, and the lightest maximum-time-aloft (MTA) models under 25 g (Wikipedia, n.d.; International Federation of Boomerang Associations, n.d.-a).

Rules

In every discipline the boomerang must travel at least 20 m from the thrower, and the thrower stands at the centre of concentric rings on an open field; the Accuracy events (Accuracy 100 and Accuracy 50) award points for how close the returning boomerang lands to the centre over five attempts, and the thrower may not touch the boomerang after release (Wikipedia, n.d.).

The other classic disciplines are the Aussie Round (one throw scored for distance, catch and accuracy — a perfect throw scores 6 + 4 + 10), endurance (most catches within five minutes), consecutive catch, fast catch, maximum time aloft (MTA 100 inside a 100 m circle and MTA unlimited; five throws, best three times), distance, trick catch and doubling; points, not clocks, decide the standings in each event (Wikipedia, n.d.; International Federation of Boomerang Associations, n.d.-b).

History

Returning boomerangs are among the oldest human inventions: the oldest known example is the ivory boomerang from Obłazowa Cave in Poland, dated to roughly 30,000 BC and pictured here, while Aboriginal Australians developed the returning boomerang as a hunting and sport tool thousands of years ago (Wikipedia, n.d.).

The sport reached Europe and the United States in the 20th century — the first European boomerang festival and competition was organised by Jacques Thomas in Amsterdam in 1980, shown in the photograph — and the biennial World Boomerang Championships, now coordinated with the IFBA, have been held since the 1980s, with the 2026 edition in Bandung, Indonesia (United States Boomerang Association, n.d.; International Federation of Boomerang Associations, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.).
