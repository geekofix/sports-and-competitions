Mud Snorkeling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mud snorkeling (also called bog snorkelling) is an unusual sport in which competitors swim through a water-filled trench cut into a peat bog, wearing a snorkel, diving mask and flippers, without using conventional swimming strokes. The world's premier event is the World Bog Snorkelling Championships, held every August bank holiday at the Waen Rhydd peat bog near Llanwrtyd Wells in mid-Wales, the smallest town in Britain. Related events at the same venue include mountain bike bog snorkelling and the bog snorkelling triathlon.

Rules

Organizers cut a trench 60 yards (about 55 m) long, roughly 4 feet deep and 3 feet wide through the peat bog. Competitors must wear a snorkel, mask and flippers (wetsuits are optional but advisable) and complete two consecutive lengths of the trench, totalling 120 yards (about 110 m), in the shortest time possible. Conventional swimming strokes are not allowed; competitors rely on flipper power alone, face down in the murky peat water. The fastest time wins, with separate men's and women's titles plus a fancy dress section. World records are ratified at the championship: Neil Rutter holds the men's record in 1:18.81 (2018) and Kirsty Johnson the women's in 1:22.56 (2014), both set at Llanwrtyd Wells.

History

Bog snorkelling was conceived in 1976 in a conversation between regulars at the Neuadd Arms pub in Llanwrtyd Wells, where local landlord Gordon Green and other business owners were looking for quirky events to draw visitors back to the former spa town. The first World Bog Snorkelling Championship was held in 1985 at the Waen Rhydd bog, organized by Green Events, and it has taken place every August bank holiday since, attracting competitors from more than a dozen countries. The same bog hosts the World Mountain Bike Bog Snorkelling Championship and a bog triathlon (snorkel, bike ride, run), and proceeds from the championship go to a local charity each year. Llanwrtyd Wells is also home to the Man versus Horse Marathon.
