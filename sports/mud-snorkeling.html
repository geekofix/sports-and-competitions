Mud Snorkeling

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mud snorkeling — also called bog snorkelling — is a novelty endurance sport in which competitors swim through a water-filled trench cut into a peat bog, wearing a snorkel, diving mask and flippers, without using conventional swimming strokes. The world's premier event is the World Bog Snorkelling Championships, held every August bank holiday weekend at the dense Waen Rhydd peat bog near Llanwrtyd Wells in mid Wales, the town that claims to be the smallest in Britain (Green Events, n.d.; Visit Wales, n.d.). Competitors from more than a dozen countries take part, and separate men's and women's titles are awarded alongside a popular fancy dress category; proceeds go to a local charity each year (Wikipedia, n.d.). Related events at the same venue include the Bog Triathlon and the World Mountain Bike Bog Snorkelling Championship (Green Events, n.d.).

Rules

The organizers cut a trench 60 yards (about 55 m) long, roughly 4 feet deep and 3 feet wide through the peat bog (Green Events, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitors must wear a snorkel, a diving mask and flippers; a wetsuit is not compulsory but is advisable in the cold peat water. Each competitor must complete two consecutive lengths of the trench — 120 yards (about 110 m) in total — in the shortest possible time, relying on flipper power alone, face down in the murky water: conventional swimming strokes are not allowed (Wikipedia, n.d.). The fastest time wins, with separate men's and women's world titles plus a fancy dress section judged on costume, and world records are ratified at the championship in Llanwrtyd Wells — the men's record stands at 1:18.81 (Neil Rutter, 2018) and the women's at 1:22.56 (Kirsty Johnson, 2014) (Green Events, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Age and gender classifications are simple: men's, women's and fancy dress divisions, open to adults and, in the shorter charity courses, to younger participants.

History

Bog snorkelling was conceived in 1976 in a conversation between regulars at the Neuadd Arms pub in Llanwrtyd Wells — among them the landlord Gordon Green and other local business owners, who were looking for a quirky event to put the tiny town on the map (Wikipedia, n.d.). The idea became a reality in the mid-1980s: a trench was dug through the Waen Rhydd peat bog, and the first World Bog Snorkelling Championships were held in 1985; the event has run every August bank holiday since and has grown into an international charity fundraiser attended by hundreds of competitors and thousands of spectators (Green Events, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The event gained national media attention through celebrity participants such as athletes Ewan Thomas, Gareth Thomas and Donovan Bailey, and was featured by the Royal Mail in a 2019 stamp collection of UK weird and wonderful customs (Wikipedia, n.d.).
