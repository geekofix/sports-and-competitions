Mounted Archery

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mounted archery — horseback archery — is the sport of shooting arrows from a galloping horse along a straight track, governed internationally by the International Horseback Archery Alliance (IHAA), established in late 2013 (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-a). The IHAA's major championships run the combined discipline of Horseback Archery Eventing (HBAE), which adds up the three phases Tower, Raid and Hunt (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-a). The classic tracks are the 99 m Hungarian straight (90/110), the 235 m Korean track (K-233/K-235), the 233 m Kassai track, and the Polish and Australian variants, with a team Grand Prix event for teams of three riders per nation (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-b). The biennial IHAA World Championships were held in Hungary in July 2018, in Mongolia in September 2023 and — as the third edition — in Lewisburg, Tennessee, USA, on 19–21 September 2025, welcoming more than 50 riders from about 20 countries; the European Championships run biennially (France 2017, Poland 2019, France 2022, Hungary 2024) and the European Grand Prix series has run every year since 2014 (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-a). Women and men compete in the same classes. At the end of the 2026 season the leading women include Elizabeth Tinnan of the United States, a leading American rider and organizer of the 2025 world championships at Tennessee Valley Archery, and France's Chloé Simons, junior world champion (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-a).

Rules

In the HBAE discipline, the Tower phase uses the 99 m Hungarian straight track, where the rider makes timed runs shooting three arrows at the target, scoring points for hits and bonus points for speed; the Raid phase uses the 235 m Korean track with three targets (front, side and back); and the Hunt phase is a roughly 1,700 m cross-country course with a series of ground targets — at the 2025 world championships the Hunt was 1,700 m and the Tower 110 (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-b). The Kassai track is 233 m, and the Polish and Australian tracks follow their own national formats; in the team Grand Prix, three riders per nation combine their scores (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-b). Riders wear a thumb ring or glove, carry a quiver, and may use any horse; women and men compete in the same classes under identical rules, and there are junior (under-17) and age-group categories (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-a).

History

Horseback archery is one of the oldest skills of mounted warriors: the Huns and Mongols of the Eurasian steppes perfected the Parthian shot — firing backwards while retreating — and Korean horse-archer traditions of the hwarang era survived into the modern game (Wikipedia, n.d.). The modern competitive system was codified by the Hungarian master Lajos Kassai Jr., whose 233 m Kassai track and training method became the basis of today's sport; he died in 2012 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The IHAA was established in late 2013 to govern the sport internationally, and the first IHAA World Championships followed in Hungary in July 2018 (International Horseback Archery Alliance, n.d.-a).
