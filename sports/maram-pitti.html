Maram Pitti

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Maram pitti (picchi banti in Telugu — “mad ball”) is the Indian version of dodgeball: a player holding the ball hits opponents with it from a fixed spot while they run to dodge, and the last player standing wins. It is played by children and adults — men's and women's groups — in lanes, courtyards and streets across India, and a Pakistani cognate is maram kutti. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are Pithoo, Seven Stones, Kancha; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Usually 5–6 players (solo or in teams of 3+) play in a small enclosed area or lane. The game starts with the ball thrown straight up; it must bounce three times before a player may pick it up. The holder may not move from their spot and must hit opponents with the ball; hits to the head are not allowed. Hit players are out; the last player remaining (or the last team) wins. With many players, two winners may be chosen.

History

Maram pitti is one of the classic street games of South India (Telugu-speaking regions especially), alongside picchi banti's namesakes; the Pakistani maram kutti is the same game. Like other dodgeball-type games worldwide it derives from children's throwing games, and it remains a staple of schoolyards and village festivals across the subcontinent.
