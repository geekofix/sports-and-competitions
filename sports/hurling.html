Hurling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hurling is Ireland's ancient field sport, played by two teams of 15 with ash sticks (hurleys/camán) and a small hard ball (sliotar) on a large grass pitch (130-145 m x 80-90 m) with H-shaped goals; a goal (under the crossbar, 3 points) or over it (a point, 1 point) is scored by striking the sliotar between/above the posts. It is governed by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), founded 1884, which runs the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship — the final at Croke Park, Dublin, draws 80,000+ spectators.

Rules

Under the GAA Official Guide, each team fields 15 players (1 goalkeeper plus 14 outfield) in championship matches of four 15-minute quarters. The sliotar may be carried for four steps, then must be bounced or struck on the run (a "solo"), or struck along the ground; only the goalkeeper may lift it with the hand inside the goal area. Dangerous or holding play is penalized with free pucks and cards, including a 10-minute sin-bin for cynical fouls. A goal (the sliotar under the crossbar between the posts) is worth 3 points; over the crossbar is 1 point. Restarts are by kick-outs and free pucks, and an offside rule applies near the goals.

History

Hurling is one of the oldest field games in the world, with references in Irish mythology (Cú Chulainn) and laws (Brehon laws) from the early medieval period; the GAA codified the rules in 1884 and organized the first All-Ireland championship in 1887 (won by Tipperary). The sport is Ireland's national game, strongest in Munster and Leinster; legends include Christy Ring of Cork, DJ Carey, and Henry Shefflin of Kilkenny, the championship's all-time top scorer. Women play camogie under the Camogie Association; the GAA also governs Gaelic football, handball and rounders. Shinty-hurling, a hybrid with Scottish shinty, is played annually as an international match.
