Hurling

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hurling is Ireland's ancient field sport — often called "the fastest field sport in the world" — in which two teams of 15 players use wooden ash sticks (hurleys) to strike a small hard leather ball called the sliotar over the opponent's crossbar (1 point) or into the goal (3 points); it is governed by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) (Gaelic Athletic Association [GAA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The premier competition is the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship, first held in 1887; the 2026 final on 19 July 2026 at Croke Park was won by Limerick over Galway — Limerick's 13th title and their first since 2023, after the 2018 and 2020–2023 streak — and Limerick also won the 2026 National Hurling League (GAA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The women's equivalent of hurling is camogie, governed by the Ladies' Gaelic Football Association (LGFA), played with identical rules and the same sliotar; leading current players include the 2026 Limerick senior hurlers on the men's side and the top camogie county panels on the women's side (Ladies' Gaelic Football Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (Gaelic Athletic Association, n.d.-a) (Gaelic Athletic Association, n.d.-b).

Rules

Hurling is played on a rectangular pitch 130–145 m long and 80–90 m wide, with a goal at each end (6.4 m wide posts and a crossbar 2.44 m above the ground); each team fields 15 players — one goalkeeper and 14 outfield players (GAA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sliotar is 69–72 mm in diameter and 110–116 g in mass; since 2010 a helmet with a faceguard is compulsory for all players in GAA games, and other protective equipment (gloves, mouthguard) is recommended (GAA, n.d.-a). A player may carry the ball in the hand for a maximum of four steps, may solo it (bounce it on the hurley) while running, and may strike it with the hurley; catching the ball is allowed, and the square-ball rule requires play to restart when play is stopped in the small square in front of goal (GAA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Free hits are awarded from where the foul occurred, the 65-m free is taken from the 65-m line, sideline and 21-m (small) frees similarly; a goal is worth 3 points and a point over the crossbar 1 point (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Inter-county matches last 70 minutes (two 35-minute halves) with extra time if tied in championship games, club matches 60 minutes; the referee, two umpires and four linesmen officiate under the GAA's Official Guide (Rule Book, current edition April 2026) (GAA, n.d.-a). Men and women play identical rules; camogie uses the same pitch, ball and scoring (Ladies' Gaelic Football Association, n.d.).

History

Hurling is one of the oldest field sports in the world — the Irish epic Táin Bó Cúailnge and the hero Cú Chulainn describe hurling-like contests in Iron Age Ireland, and the game was codified in the 19th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Gaelic Athletic Association was founded on 1 November 1884 in Thurles, County Tipperary, by Michael Cusack (with Maurice Davin among the founders), to promote Irish games and athletics; the first All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship was played in 1887 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the sport's icons are Henry Shefflin of Kilkenny, who won a record ten All-Ireland titles between 2000 and 2014, and Christy Ring of Cork, who won eight titles between 1943 and 1966 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
