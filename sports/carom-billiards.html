Carom Billiards

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Carom billiards is a cue sport played on a pocketless table in which the cue ball must strike both object balls, with a required number of cushions before the second object ball in the main disciplines. It is governed by the Union Mondiale de Billard (UMB) and the World Carom Billiards Sports (WCBS), which run world championships in three-cushion, one-cushion, straight rail (balkline) and artistic billiards. Three-cushion is the most prestigious discipline and has been played at the World Games.

Rules

In three-cushion billiards, the cue ball must hit one object ball, then contact at least three cushions before hitting the second object ball; each successful carom scores one point, and the match is played to a set number of points (40 or 50 at world championships). A shot clock of about 30-40 seconds applies at major events. In straight rail (balkline), the table is divided by lines and at least one cushion must be hit before the second object ball. Fouls (missing both object balls) give the opponent points or the turn. Balls are 61.5 mm, and the cloth is heated at championship level.

History

Carom billiards originated in 15th-17th century Europe, with the pocketless table developing in France and spreading through the courts of Europe; straight rail was codified in the 18th century and three-cushion became the dominant form in the 19th century. The UMB, founded in 1923, and later the WCBS govern the sport internationally. Legends of three-cushion include Raymond Ceulemans and Torbjörn Blomdahl, who dominated world championships for decades.
