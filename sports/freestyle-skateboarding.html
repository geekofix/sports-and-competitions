Freestyle Skateboarding

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freestyle skateboarding in competition means the Olympic disciplines street and park, governed by World Skate, the IOC-recognized federation, and contested at the Olympics since Tokyo 2020, at the World Skate Games and the World Championships (World Skate, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the Paris 2024 Olympics, Yuto Horigome (JPN) defended the men's street title and Coco Yoshizawa (JPN) won women's street, while Keegan Palmer (AUS) defended men's park and Arisa Trew (AUS) won women's park with 93.18 — ahead of Cocona Hiraki (JPN) and Sky Brown (GBR) (Olympics, n.d.). The 5th World Skate Games were held in Asunción, Paraguay, October 2–18, 2026: Ginwoo Onodera (JPN, 16) won men's street ahead of Juni Kang (KOR) and Taiga Nagai (JPN), Yumeka Oda (JPN) repeated as women's street champion ahead of Chloe Covell (AUS) and Coco Yoshizawa, Egoitz Bijueska (ESP) took men's park ahead of Tom Schaar (USA) and Gui Khury (BRA), and Mizuho Hasegawa (JPN) won women's park ahead of Sky Brown and Cocona Hiraki (World Skate, n.d.-b). The 2026 Asian Games in Aichi (September 24–27) added street golds for Kang Jun-i and Sasaki Toa and park gold for Hasegawa (World Skate, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Yumeka Oda (JPN), the 2026 world street champion, and Mizuho Hasegawa (JPN), the 2026 world park champion, followed by Sky Brown (GBR), two-time world park champion (2026 São Paulo WST), Chloe Covell (AUS) and Arisa Trew (AUS); on the men's side Ginwoo Onodera (JPN) and Egoitz Bijueska (ESP) head the 2026 World Skate rankings (World Skate, n.d.-b).

Rules

World Skate's skateboarding competition rules (street and park) apply at the Olympics, World Championships and World Skate Games (World Skate, n.d.-a). In each discipline, athletes perform timed runs — three runs in the final, with the best run counting (plus a best-trick section in street at major events) — and each run is scored 0–100 by judges for difficulty, execution, speed, height, variety and flow, covering the whole course (all obstacles in park; a set of street features in street) (World Skate, n.d.-a). Qualification rounds determine the finalists; at the Olympics, 8 men and 8 women per discipline reach the final from semifinals. The park course is a bowl-like arena with walls, coping, hips and extensions; the street course is a line of stairs, handrails, ledges, benches and banks in a plaza-style layout (World Skate, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate events on identical courses, and junior age classes (U18) exist at World Championships, while breaking (added at Paris 2024) is a separate World Skate discipline (World Skate, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Safety and equipment rules — including mandatory helmets at junior events — follow the World Skate regulations (World Skate, n.d.-a).

History

Skateboarding emerged in California in the late 1940s–1950s when surfers wanted something to do when the waves were flat ("sidewalk surfing"), and the first boards were wooden planks with roller-skate wheels; the 1960s–70s brought urethane wheels and the empty-pool era that created vertical skating, pioneered by Tony Alva and the Z-Boys, while street skating was born in the late 1970s–80s with Rodney Mullen's flat-ground freestyle inventions and later Mark Gonzales and Natas Kaupas (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tony Hawk became the icon of vert skating with his first 900 at the 1999 X Games; the X Games (ESPN, since 1995) and the video game era globalized the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Skateboarding debuted as an Olympic sport at Tokyo 2020 — street and park, won by Yuto Horigome, Sakura Yosozumi, Keegan Palmer and Momiji Nishiya — with breaking added at Paris 2024, and the World Skate Games (Rome 2024, Asunción 2026) serve as the combined world championships for all skateboarding disciplines (Olympics, n.d.; World Skate, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
