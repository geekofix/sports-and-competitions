Freestyle Skateboarding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freestyle skateboarding is the technical flat-ground discipline of skateboarding — manuals, flip tricks, shuvits and combinations performed on flat ground or in a small area — judged on difficulty, execution, variety and style. It is governed by World Skate, the IOC-recognized federation for roller sports, and is part of the World Skate Championships alongside street and park.

Rules

At World Skate freestyle events, competitors perform runs (typically 1-2 minutes, sometimes to music) consisting of a sequence of tricks on flat ground; judges score difficulty, execution, variety, composition and style, with falls and hand-downs reducing the score. Events include men's and women's freestyle, with preliminary rounds and finals. Street and park formats are separate Olympic disciplines of Skateboarding. In casual freestyle ("flatland"), the goal is a clean run without putting a foot down; in "best trick" contests, riders take turns attempting their hardest trick.

History

Freestyle skateboarding developed in the 1960s-70s when surfers rode skateboards on flat ground (sidewalk surfing), with pioneers such as Larry Stevenson (inventor of the kicktail, 1969) and the dogtown/Z-Boys era; freestyle competitions flourished in the late 1970s-80s with stars like Rodney Mullen, who invented the flat-ground ollie and kickflip and is considered the father of modern street skating. Freestyle declined as street skating took over in the 1990s, but it survives in World Skate events and longboarding. Mullen's appearances in 1980s freestyle contest videos made him a legend, and he was inducted into the Skateboarding Hall of Fame.
