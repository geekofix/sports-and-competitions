Konane

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Konane ('Hawaiian checkers') is the traditional jumping game of Hawaii played on an 8x8 (or larger) board filled with alternating black/white stones; players jump over an adjacent enemy stone into an empty space, removing it; the player left without a move loses. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Draughts, Alquerque, Senet; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Hawai'i Volcanoes NPS, n.d.).

Figure: Nobody(not jj). (Taken on 2). Konane being played at Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau National Historical Park [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Konane_at_Puʻuhonua_o_Hōnaunau_National_Historical_Park.jpg

Rules

Board starts full alternating; multiple jumps in one turn are allowed (and often compulsory in some rule sets); no diagonal moves. The player unable to move loses. Played at Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau and taught in Hawaiian schools (NPS photos) (Hawai'i Volcanoes NPS, n.d.).

History

Konane is ancient in Hawaii (documented in 19th-century accounts); it is a cousin of draughts/checkers spread by Polynesians; the NPS teaches it at Hōnaunau (2008 photo) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
