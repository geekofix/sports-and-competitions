Rugby League Nines

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rugby league nines is a shortened, festival version of rugby league played with nine players per side instead of 13; it is governed internationally by the International Rugby League and is popular at school, social and workplace tournaments because a full day of games can be completed in one weekend (International Rugby League, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The flagship event was the Downer Rugby League World Cup 9s, held in Sydney in October 2019 with 12 men's national teams and four women's teams at Bankwest Stadium, won by Australia over New Zealand in a controversial final (NRL, 2019; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play under the same nines format in their own tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Nines keeps the laws of 13-a-side rugby league with a set of exceptions: squads of up to 15 players with no more than nine on the field at any time and unlimited substitutions from a named bench of four; matches last 18 minutes in two nine-minute halves with a two-minute half-time (some competitions use 15 minutes in two 7.5-minute halves) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 2019 World Cup 9s, extra modifications included no video referee, a five-minute golden-try period for drawn matches, a tap restart after a 40/20, a five-point bonus for a try scored in the zone between the posts, and a drop-kick kick-off by the scoring team (Rugby League Zone, n.d.). Scoring otherwise follows league — try 4, conversion 2, penalty 2, drop goal 1 — and misconduct is punished by a five-minute sin bin (reduced to two minutes at the Carnegie Floodlit Nines) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Short-sided rugby league grew out of Australian pre-season tournaments in the 1960s–1990s, when the ARL and later Super League organized sevens and nines events; nines became established in 1996 as a Super League-backed rival to sevens and was promoted during the Super League War (Rugby League Zone, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Carnegie Floodlit Nines, founded in Leeds in 2005, became the biggest annual club nines tournament in England. The inaugural World Cup 9s in Sydney 2019 brought 12 nations to Parramatta's Bankwest Stadium; Australia beat New Zealand in the final (NRL, 2019; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). No further World Cup 9s has been held, but nines remains a staple of festivals, schools and corporate events worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
