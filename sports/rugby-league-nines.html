Rugby League Nines

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rugby league nines is a nine-a-side version of rugby league, with the same fundamental laws as the thirteen-a-side game but shorter matches and modified set pieces, designed so that a whole tournament can be completed in a single day or weekend. It is governed under the International Laws of the Game with nines variations, and its highest-profile events have included the NRL Auckland Nines (2014–2017) and the first Rugby League World Cup 9s in Sydney in 2019. International Rugby League has examined nines as a possible format for future Olympic inclusion.

Rules

Nines squads contain up to fifteen players with no more than nine on the field at any time, and unlimited substitutions are allowed from a named bench of four. Matches last 18 minutes, played in two nine-minute halves with no half-time interval (teams change ends within a minute), and each half begins with a place kick. Scrums are reduced to five forwards (three in the front row, two in the second), and conversions after a try are drop kicks rather than place kicks; after any score the non-scoring team restarts with a tap from halfway, and penalties are taken by a tap ten metres in advance of the infringement. Misconduct earns a five-minute suspension from the referee. Tournament organisers add their own variations — the World Cup 9s in 2019 used a golden-try extra-time period, bonus-zone tries worth five points and two-point conversions — but the core game remains rugby league's six-tackle, play-the-ball structure on a shortened format.

History

Rugby league's short formats have recurred since the early 20th century, with sevens festivals spreading and the first rugby league nines regularised in Australia in the postwar decades; the game's modern tournament era arrived through the Rugby League World Sevens, launched in Sydney in 1988, whose success encouraged condensed formats across the sport. Professional nines took centre stage with the NRL Auckland Nines, held each pre-season from 2014 to 2017 and won by teams including the Parramatta edition of 2016, whose title was later stripped in a salary-cap ruling; the event moved to Perth for a final edition in 2020 before being cancelled. The Sheffield-based Carnegie Floodlit Nines established a respected British tournament with two-minute suspensions replacing the standard five, and Super League staged World Nines events in the 1990s during Australian rugby's Super League war era. The flagship arrived in October 2019, when twelve men's and four women's national teams contested the inaugural Downer Rugby League World Cup 9s at Parramatta's Bankwest Stadium, with Australia beating New Zealand in the men's final; the event was planned as a recurring world championship, and International Rugby League has since floated nines as a potential Olympic vehicle for the 2032 Brisbane Games.
