Para Va'a

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para va'a is the Paralympic outrigger canoe sprint: athletes with limb, trunk or balance impairments race a va'a (outrigger canoe with a second pontoon and a single-blade paddle) over 200 m in the VL1, VL2 and VL3 classes, with lower numbers indicating greater impairment (ICF, n.d.). Paracanoe has been fully integrated into the ICF Canoe Sprint World Championships since 2010 and has been on the Paralympic programme since Rio 2016; the 2026 ICF Canoe Sprint and Paracanoe World Championships on Lake Malta, Poznań, 26–30 August 2026, included the full VL programme (Wikipedia, 2026; ICF, n.d.). Women's VL events are raced over the same 200 m in the same classes; at Paris 2024 Charlotte Henshaw (GBR) won VL2 gold in 56.80 and VL3 in 56.91, and Emma Wiggs (GBR) took VL2 silver — the British women's VL squad has been a Paralympic powerhouse since 2016 (Wikipedia, 2024). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are Va'a, Paracanoe, Canoe Sprint, Waka Ama, Outrigger Canoeing, Surf Ski; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

All international para va'a races are 200 m straight-line sprints from aligned starts, timed electronically; classification into VL1–VL3 is based on functional ability in trunk, legs and balance, assessed by ICF classifiers. Athletes may use a backrest and straps within the va'a specifications; boats and paddles are standardized under ICF paracanoe equipment rules. Men's and women's events use identical classes and distance (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Paracanoe emerged from adaptive paddling programmes in the 1990s and was first included in the ICF World Championships in 2010; it debuted at the Paralympics at Rio 2016. Curtis McGrath of Australia won the men's VL2 200 m at both Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024, becoming one of the most decorated paracanoeists in history (Wikipedia, 2024; ICF, n.d.).
