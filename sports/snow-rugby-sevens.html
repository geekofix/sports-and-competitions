Snow Rugby Sevens

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snow rugby sevens is the winter adaptation of rugby sevens played on a snow-covered pitch: teams of six or seven players contest an oval ball under the World Rugby sevens laws, with Rugby Europe staging a Snow Rugby Sevens European Championship since 2019 (Rugby Europe, n.d.). Both women's and men's national teams compete, and the 2019 edition in Moscow, Russia, was played six-a-side, while events staged in Finland used the full seven-a-side line-up (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Because sevens is the fastest form of rugby union, the snow variant keeps short halves and a high tempo on a smaller, packed-snow field.

Rules

Snow rugby sevens applies the World Rugby Laws of the Game, sevens variation, with winter modifications approved by the organising body: two teams of six or seven players on a rectangular snow pitch, a standard rugby ball, and matches of two short halves of typically seven minutes each (World Rugby, n.d.-a). Play restarts with a drop-kick after points, tackling is permitted below the shoulders, and rucks and mauls follow sevens law with the offside line at the hindmost foot (World Rugby, n.d.-a). Scoring is identical to sevens: a try is worth five points, a conversion two, and a penalty or drop goal three; the team with the most points at the final whistle wins, while knock-ons, forward passes and offside play are penalised with a scrum or free kick (World Rugby, n.d.-a). Tournaments run as round-robin pools followed by knockout cup, plate and bowl stages, mirroring the World Rugby Sevens Series structure (World Rugby, n.d.-b). Women's and men's teams play under identical laws on the same pitches, with under-18 age-group events alongside the senior categories; on snow, players wear studded or multi-stud footwear for grip instead of standard rugby boots (World Rugby, n.d.-b).

History

Rugby sevens originated at Melrose Rugby Club in Scotland, which staged the first sevens tournament in 1883 as a fundraiser, and the first international sevens match followed in 1973 at Murrayfield in Edinburgh, where England beat Ireland 22–18 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Hong Kong Sevens, first held in 1976, globalised the shortened format; the Rugby World Cup Sevens began in 1993, won by England, and sevens entered the Olympic programme at Rio 2016, where Fiji's men won the inaugural gold — the nation's first Olympic medal of any kind (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's sevens played its first international in 1982 and joined the Olympic programme at Rio 2016 alongside the men (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Snow rugby sevens emerged in the 2010s in Russia, Finland and Austria as a winter offshoot of the sevens circuit, and Rugby Europe formalised the Snow Rugby Sevens European Championship from 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
