Cycling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cycling is the umbrella sport of racing and riding bicycles, governed worldwide by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), founded in Paris in 1900 by 14 national federations. Its main disciplines are road, track, mountain bike, BMX, cyclo-cross, indoor (cycle-ball and artistic cycling) and gravel, contested at the Olympic Games, the UCI World Championships and the UCI World Cup. The Grand Tours (Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, Vuelta a España) are the pinnacle of professional road racing.

Rules

Each discipline has its own UCI rule book: in road races, riders compete in a mass start over a set distance and the first across the line wins, with team tactics, feed zones and time bonuses; in track cycling, events like the sprint, keirin, omnium and team pursuit are run on a velodrome with specific formats; time trials are individual against the clock. Doping, equipment and safety rules (helmets) are enforced under UCI regulations, and rankings decide World Cup and championship entries.

History

Bicycle racing began in the 1860s with velocipedes and the first races in France (Paris-Rouen 1869). The UCI was founded in 1900, the Tour de France began in 1903, the Giro in 1909 and the Vuelta in 1935. Track cycling has been Olympic since 1896 (with gaps), road racing since 1896, and mountain biking joined at Atlanta 1996. The UCI now has over 170 national federations; legends include Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, Miguel Induráin, Marianne Vos and Tadej Pogačar.
