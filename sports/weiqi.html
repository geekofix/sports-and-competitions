Weiqi

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Weiqi is the Chinese name of the ancient board game known as Go, in which two players take turns placing black and white stones on the intersections of a grid — classically 19 by 19 lines — with the aim of surrounding more territory than the opponent. Stones that become completely surrounded are captured and removed, and the game ends when neither side can improve its position, with the winner determined by territory and captures counted together.

It is one of the oldest board games still played in its original form, created in China thousands of years ago and spreading to Korea (where it is called baduk) and Japan (where the name Go derives from the Japanese pronunciation), becoming a professional sport with title matches, master ranks and a rich strategic literature.

Rules

Black moves first, placing a stone on any empty intersection; stones or groups that lose their last adjacent empty point (liberty) are captured. Suicide is illegal except where the move itself captures, and the ko rule forbids immediately recreating a previous board position, sometimes through extended superko rules in professional play. In traditional Chinese scoring, the winner is decided by stone placement area scoring counting surrounded points plus stones on the board, with compensation (komi) for white offsetting black's first-move advantage. Handicap stones allow uneven games, letting weaker players start with extra stones.

History

The game originated in China, with early references appearing in written records over two and a half thousand years ago as a court pastime and strategic exercise; it was listed among the four arts of the Chinese scholar.
