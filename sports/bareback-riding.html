Bareback Riding

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bareback riding is rodeo's original roughstock event: the cowboy grips a rigging — a single leather handhold — with one hand, rides without a saddle, and must stay on a bucking horse for 8 seconds. Rides are scored out of 100 points (up to 50 for the horse's performance and 50 for the rider's), and the event is governed in the United States by the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA), with the Professional Bull Riders (PBR) running its own elite tour.

Rules

Under PRCA official rules, the horse must be ridden with one hand in the rigging; the free hand may not touch the rider, the horse or the rider's equipment, or the ride is disqualified ("mark-out" and foul rules apply). The 8-second buzzer starts when the horse's shoulders clear the chute; the rider must keep spurs on and feet over the horse's shoulders until the buzzer. Judges score both horse and rider on a 50-point scale each. The top scorers advance through rounds to the finals.

History

Bareback riding grew out of the working ranch traditions of the American West and Mexican vaqueros, who broke horses bareback. Organized rodeo emerged in the late 19th century — Cheyenne Frontier Days began in 1897 — and the Rodeo Cowboys Association, founded in 1936, became today's PRCA. Bareback riding has been part of every major rodeo since, and stars like the legendary cowboys of the 1920s-50s made the event famous worldwide.
