EnduroCross

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

EnduroCross (Enduro-X) is indoor motorcycle racing that mixes supercross jumps with hard enduro obstacles — rock beds, log piles, tire walls, water crossings — inside arenas; in Europe the same format is the FIM SuperEnduro World Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the motorcycle racing family with supercross, enduro and motocross.

Figure: Excf23. (2020-02-01). Rider on a dirt track during a SuperEnduro round, Kraków 2019 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Teodor_Kabakchiev_at_FIM_Superenduro_World_Championship_in_Krakow_-_December_2019.jpg

The AMA EnduroCross Championship has run in North America since 2007 (first one-off event at the Orleans Arena, Las Vegas, 2004, organized by Eric Peronnard and Tim Clark); it was an X Games event in 2011–2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The European FIM SuperEnduro World Championship debuted with the Barcelona Indoor Enduro in 2000 and runs October–March in arenas across Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Races are short, intense mains (typically 8–10 laps / about 10 minutes) with a grid start; the course is rebuilt each round and includes extreme sections (Wikipedia, n.d.). Champions: Taddy Blazusiak holds the EnduroCross record with 29 main-event wins (through 2013), and Billy Bolt has won the FIM SuperEnduro World Championship six times (2020, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Equipment: 250/450 cc four-strokes or 250/300 cc two-strokes with lights, mousse tyres and hard-parts protection (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — women’s classes exist at several SuperEnduro rounds.

History

Indoor enduro debuted in 2000 as a support event at the Barcelona Trial Indoor, organized by Jaime Alguersuari Sr.; the hybrid spread to European cities in the 2000s, and the FIM created the Indoor Enduro World Cup in 2007, renamed SuperEnduro (Wikipedia, n.d.). Peronnard brought the concept to the US in 2004, where it became the AMA EnduroCross (Wikipedia, n.d.).
