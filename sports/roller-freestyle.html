Roller Freestyle

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller freestyle is the competitive side of aggressive inline skating: athletes on inline skates perform grinds, slides, aerials and technical tricks in skateparks, on street courses or on vert ramps, judged like skateboarding (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Under World Skate's Roller Freestyle Rulebook 2026 the official disciplines are street, park and vert, contested in heats of five to six riders with two runs of 40–60 seconds each, the best run counting, and a qualification-to-final format at world championships; scores are given out of 100 (World Skate, n.d.). The 2025 Roller Freestyle World Championships were held in Sakai, Japan, and vert returned to the 2026 World Skate Games programme in Asunción (World Skate, n.d.). A related slalom discipline, freestyle slalom skating, runs cone courses — classic slalom, battle, speed slalom, slides and free jump — under the World Skate Inline Freestyle Rulebook 2026 with senior and junior U19 categories for women and men (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Both women and men compete in all disciplines and categories (World Skate, n.d.).

Rules

In park and street, riders get two runs of 40–60 seconds on a course of bowls, ramps, rails, ledges, stairs and gaps; a panel of four to six judges scores each run out of 100 on difficulty, execution, variety and use of the course, and the best run counts toward the ranking (World Skate, n.d.). World championship formats narrow qualification heats to a final; the 2/3/2 final variant is permitted for street (World Skate, n.d.). In vert, athletes ride a halfpipe with two runs, best run counting (World Skate, n.d.). In freestyle slalom, classic slalom runs a line of 20 cones spaced 50, 80, 120 or 150 cm apart depending on the class, with penalty seconds added for each cone knocked over; skaters get multiple runs and the best counts; battle pits two skaters head-to-head on parallel lines; speed slalom is a timed race through gates on an incline; free jump is a judged jump over a height bar (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Inline freestyle world championships cover speed slalom, classic slalom, battle, slides and free jump for junior U19 and senior men and women (World Skate, n.d.). Safety gear — helmet, knee and elbow pads — is required at World Skate events, and women and men compete in separate categories (World Skate, n.d.).

History

Aggressive inline skating grew out of skateboarding culture in the late 1980s and 1990s, when riders began grinding handrails and stairs on inline skates; the X Games added aggressive inlines in 1995, and the discipline flourished at events like the X Games and the ASA/ASA tour before World Skate consolidated it as roller freestyle (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Freestyle slalom skating developed from the same inline scene, with the IFSA (International Freestyle Skating Association) organizing international slalom competitions from the late 1990s; World Skate now runs the Inline Freestyle and Roller Freestyle commissions with annual world championships and a world ranking (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Skate, n.d.).
