Rally Raid

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rally raid is long-distance cross-country rallying over deserts, steppes and mountains — the Dakar Rally is the most famous event — with cars, motorcycles, quads, SxS buggies and trucks racing against the clock over stages of hundreds of kilometres (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the rallying family of motorsport, distinct from stage rallying (WRC).

Figure: EKSRX. (2021-01-13 11:49). Dakar Rally 2021, stage 10 — car in the dunes [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Rally_2021_-_Stage_10_%2850831571643%29.jpg

The FIA World Rally-Raid Championship (W2RC) is the top series, with the Dakar Rally (run by ASO since 1979, in South America 2009–2019 and Saudi Arabia since 2020) as its flagship; categories include cars (T1+ Ultimate), SSV (T3/T4), motorcycles and quads, and the new sustainable-fuel Mission 1000 category, won by Spain’s Jordi Juvanteny in 2024, 2025 and 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Rally raid rules: crews of driver and co-driver navigate by GPS waypoints to checkpoints, with liaison sections on public roads; only the special stage times count, and assistance vehicles follow a separate route (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety: Sentinel warning system, GPS tracking, medical helicopters (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — women have won Dakar stages and titles in SSV and bikes.

History

The Paris–Dakar Rally was founded by Thierry Sabine, who got lost in the Ténéré desert during the 1975 Abidjan–Nice rally and conceived a race to Dakar; the first edition ran in December 1978–January 1979 from Paris to Dakar, Senegal, with 182 vehicles (Wikipedia, n.d.). Cyril Despres (5 car titles), Stéphane Peterhansel (14 titles) and Nasser Al-Attiyah are the record holders; Martin Macík won the truck class in 2024 and 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
