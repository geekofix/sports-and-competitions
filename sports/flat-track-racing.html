Flat Track Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Flat track motorcycle racing is speedway-style racing on an oval dirt or shale track of 250-400 m with no brakes, where riders powerslide through corners at speeds over 80 mph. In the US it is governed by the American Motorcyclist Association (AMA), which runs the AMA Flat Track Grand Championship; internationally the FIM sanctions the FIM Speedway Grand Prix (which uses 400 m tracks) and the FIM Flat Track World Championship.

Rules

At AMA Flat Track events, races run counterclockwise on ovals (the "mile" is 1609 m, "half-mile" 805 m, "short track" 400 m or less — AMA classes include Grand Championship (mile/half-mile), short track and TT). Bikes are single-cylinder 450 cc machines with no brakes, one gear and a clutch; riders left-foot on the ground for balance while powersliding. Races are heat-based: heats, semifinals and a main event of typically 15-20 laps (mile) or 10 laps (short track), with points awarded per finishing position. Riders wear a leather suit, helmet, gloves, boots and a chest protector. The FIM Speedway GP format uses 400 m tracks, 20 heats of 4 riders, semifinals and a final, with the world champion decided over a series of GPs.

History

Flat track racing grew out of early 20th-century board-track and dirt-track motorcycle racing in the US; the AMA (founded 1924) has sanctioned national championships since the 1930s, with the Springfield Mile (Massachusetts) the oldest continuously running motorcycle race in America (since 1925). The half-mile became the classic discipline, with legends such as Eddie Muns, Chris Carr (10-time Grand National champion) and Jared Mees. In Europe, speedway developed in the UK in the 1920s (West Ham, Wimbledon), and the FIM Speedway World Championship ran from 1949; the FIM reorganized 500 cc speedway into the Speedway Grand Prix series in the 1990s. Flat track remains hugely popular in the US at events like the Springfield Mile and the Sacramento Mile.
