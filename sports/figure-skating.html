Figure Skating

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Figure skating is the sport of performing choreographed programs on ice to music, combining jumps, spins, step sequences and, in pairs and ice dance, lifts, twists and partner work; it is judged under the ISU Judging System with a technical element score (TES) and a program component score (PCS) minus deductions (ISU, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Skating Union (ISU), founded in 1892 in Scheveningen and headquartered in Lausanne, and it has been part of every Winter Olympic Games since Chamonix 1924 — singles and pairs entered the Olympic program in 1908, ice dance in 1976 (ISU, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). The pinnacle event is the ISU World Figure Skating Championships; the 2026 edition was held in Prague from 25 to 28 March 2026 (ISU, n.d.-a). See also Speed Skating and Short Track Speed Skating on this site for the other ice skating disciplines (International Skating Union, n.d.-a) (International Skating Union, n.d.-b) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Amber Glenn of the United States, Olympic team champion at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics and a three-time U.S. champion, together with Isabeau Levito, Japan's Mone Chiba (silver at the 2026 Worlds) and Belgium's Nina Pinzarrone (bronze); Japan's Kaori Sakamoto, who won her fourth world title in Prague in March 2026, retired immediately afterwards (ISU, n.d.-a). On the men's side the American Ilia Malinin won his third consecutive world title in Prague with a world-record 329.40 points, ahead of Japan's Yuma Kagiyama and Shun Sato (ISU, n.d.-a).

Rules

The Olympic and World Championship program consists of three disciplines — men's and women's singles, pairs and ice dance — each skated in two segments: the short program (2 min 40 s) and rhythm dance (3 min) respectively, followed by the free skating (4 min) and free dance (4 min); in singles and pairs the elements are limited to seven for women (three jumps, three spins, one step sequence, one choreographic sequence) and eight for men, including a jump combination, a single jump and an Axel for women, and a jump combination, a single jump of any type and a spin combination (ISU, n.d.-b). Jumps are judged in six types (toe loop, Salchow, loop, flip, Lutz, Axel — the Axel, invented by Axel Paulsen in 1882, is the only jump taking off forward), spins in three basic positions (upright, sit, camel) with variations, and pairs add lifts, throw jumps, twists and a death spiral (ISU, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each element earns a base value plus a grade of execution from −5 to +5 from the nine judges, the technical panel identifies elements and levels, and the program components (skating skills, transitions, performance, composition, interpretation) are marked out of 10 with a factor; deductions apply for falls, time violations, music violations and costume violations (ISU, n.d.-b). Men and women compete under identical rules and in separate events; since the 2024–25 season skaters must be at least 17 years old on 1 July before the season to compete as seniors, while the junior category covers skaters who have not reached 19 by 1 July, and novice categories exist below (ISU, n.d.-b). At the Olympics and World Championships, the short programs qualify the field for the free skating segments, and the combined TES + PCS totals decide the medals; team events combine the disciplines in a points system (ISU, n.d.-a). Para figure skating for athletes with impairments is governed by World Abilitysport and contested at the Paralympic Winter Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Figure skating grew out of utilitarian ice skating in the 18th century: the American Jackson Haines (1865–1926), "the father of modern figure skating", introduced ballet positions, spins and the Viennese waltz to Vienna and St. Petersburg in the 1860s–70s and made skating an art form (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Swede Ulrich Salchow (1877–1949) won ten world titles between 1901 and 1911 and gave his name to the Salchow jump, and his compatriot Axel Paulsen (1855–1938) invented and landed the Axel jump in 1882 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Skating Union was founded on 23 July 1892 in Scheveningen, Netherlands, and organized the first World Championships in 1896 (men only; women joined in 1906) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Figure skating debuted at the London 1908 Summer Olympics with men's singles, women's singles and pairs, and moved to the first Winter Olympics in Chamonix 1924, where Sonja Henie of Norway (1912–1969) won the first of her three consecutive Olympic gold medals (1928, 1932, 1936) and ten world titles, turning the sport into a spectator attraction (Olympics.com, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Other icons include East Germany's Katarina Witt (b. 1965, Olympic champion 1984 and 1988), America's Dorothy Hamill (b. 1956, Olympic champion 1976), Kristi Yamaguchi (b. 1971, Olympic champion 1992) and Michelle Kwan (b. 1980, five-time world champion, retired), Canada's Tessa Virtue and Scott Moir (ice dance gold 2018), and Russia's Evgeni Plushenko (b. 1982, four-time world champion, retired) on the men's side (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
