Gravel Cycling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Gravel cycling (gravel racing) is the discipline of riding drop-bar bicycles on unpaved gravel roads, forest tracks and trails, blending road racing with light off-road terrain; the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) recognized gravel as an official discipline in 2021 and launched the UCI Gravel World Series in 2022, whose leading finishers qualify for the annual UCI Gravel World Championships, where the winners wear the rainbow jersey (Union Cycliste Internationale [UCI], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Elite races are mass-start events over roughly 130–170 km on mixed gravel, dirt and tarmac, and alongside the professional peloton the sport has a huge amateur scene with flagship events such as Unbound Gravel in Emporia, Kansas, and the Belgian Waffle Ride series (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-a) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-b).

As of the end of the 2026 season, the leading women are Marianne Vos of the Netherlands — winner of the 2024 and 2026 UCI Gravel World Championships and one of the most decorated riders in the history of cycling — and Lorena Wiebes of the Netherlands, the 2025 world champion, while on the men's side the reigning world champion is Magnus Bak Klaris of Denmark, who won the 2026 UCI Gravel World Championships in Nannup, Western Australia, on 10–11 October 2026 (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Gravel racing is governed by the UCI Cycling Regulations and the dedicated UCI Gravel Regulations; at the UCI Gravel World Championships the course must be at least 60% unpaved, races are mass-start, and drop handlebars are mandatory for all competitors, while tire width is capped under the UCI equipment regulations and helmets are compulsory (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Feed zones and technical assistance are permitted only in designated zones, and national federations may enter licensed riders in the elite and age-category races; in the UCI Gravel World Series, the top 25% of each age category earn a starting place at the World Championships (UCI, n.d.-a). The formats are the UCI Gravel World Series races, continental championships such as the UEC European Gravel Championships, and the UCI Gravel World Championships, whose elite winners receive the rainbow jersey (UCI, n.d.-b). Women and men race in separate elite events over the same mixed-terrain course — the women's race is typically shorter — under identical rules, and both genders compete in five-year age categories from 19–29 up to the oldest class at the World Championships (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Gravel riding grew out of the American Midwest "gravel grinder" scene of the early 2000s, most famously the Dirty Kanza 200, founded in 2006 in Emporia, Kansas, which was acquired in 2016 and renamed Unbound Gravel and grew to thousands of riders; the COVID-era boom in drop-bar off-pavement riding pushed manufacturers to develop dedicated gravel bikes, and the UCI created the discipline in 2021, the Gravel World Series and the first UCI Gravel World Championships in 2022 in Veneto, Italy, won by Gianni Vermeersch of Belgium and Pauline Ferrand-Prévot of France (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). The subsequent world champions were Matej Mohorič and Katarzyna Niewiadoma (2023), Mathieu van der Poel and Marianne Vos (2024), Florian Vermeersch and Lorena Wiebes (2025), and Magnus Bak Klaris and Marianne Vos (2026) (UCI, n.d.-b). Among the icons of the young discipline are Ferrand-Prévot — also a world champion in mountain biking and cyclo-cross — and van der Poel, who won the 2024 rainbow jersey (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
