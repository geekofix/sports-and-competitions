Pop Tennis

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

POP Tennis (until 2015 usually called paddle tennis) is a racquet sport adapted from tennis, played on a court smaller than a tennis court with a lower net, solid perforated paddles and a depressurized tennis ball. It was invented in 1898 by the Episcopal minister Frank Peer Beal and has been played for over a century; since 2015-16 the American governing body has branded the game as POP Tennis to distinguish it from platform tennis and padel. The same court is used for singles and doubles, with doubles the dominant form.

Rules

The court measures 50 feet baseline to baseline and 20 feet across, with the service line 3 feet in from the baseline, creating service boxes of 10 by 22 feet; the net stands 31 inches high. Players use a solid, stringless paddle that may be perforated and a reduced-pressure tennis ball. Serves are underhand and only one serve is allowed per point, struck from behind the baseline and alternating between deuce and ad courts, and it must land in the diagonally opposite service box. Scoring mirrors tennis, with conventional game, set and match scoring (15, 30, 40, advantage) and tiebreak sets; the small court strongly favors net play and produces a fast, reaction-based doubles game.

History

Frank Peer Beal invented the game in 1898 in Albion, Michigan, as a recreation for children and a stepping stone to tennis, on a court of 18 by 39 feet — exactly half a tennis court — and in 1915 persuaded New York City's parks department to lay out courts in Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village; the first tournament followed in 1922 and the United States Paddle Tennis Association in 1923. By 1941 the game was played in almost 500 American cities, aided by Murray Geller's post-war rules modernization, which enlarged the court and instituted the underhand serve to make the game more attractive to adults. Scott Freedman dominated the world championships in the late 20th century, winning 19 world singles titles. In 2015-16 the USPTA rebranded the sport as POP Tennis and renamed itself the International POP Tennis Association to gain marketability alongside padel and platform tennis; today POP Tennis is played on public courts across the United States, most famously at the historic courts built in 1962 at Venice Beach, California.
