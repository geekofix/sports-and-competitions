Lelo Burti

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Luciano Pucci Burti, known as "Lelo" Burti (born 27 March 1975 in São Paulo, Brazil), is a Brazilian racing driver who competed in Formula One in 2000 and 2001 for Jaguar Racing, making 26 Grand Prix starts without a podium (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After Formula One he became a long-time competitor in the Brazilian Stock Car Pro Series and in the A1 Grand Prix for A1 Team Brazil, and he won the 2008 Race of Champions at Wembley Stadium in London — one of the biggest individual honors in motorsport's all-star event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Since 2010 he has also worked as a motor-racing commentator for TV Globo in Brazil (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The list entry "Lelo Burti" refers to this driver, and women have increasingly taken part in Brazilian touring-car and stock-car series in the decades since his career began, competing in the same national championships he raced (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The disciplines Burti competed in each have their own rulebooks. In Formula One at the time, cars raced on FIA-homologated circuits, scored under the 10–8–6–5–4–3–2–1 points system for the top eight, with one-off qualifying sessions and refuelling allowed during races (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The Stock Car Pro Series races silhouette-style touring cars on a mix of street circuits and ovals in Brazil under a points championship, with a race format of a single long race per round and a grid set by qualifying (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Race of Champions is an invitational all-star event in which drivers race identical cars in a knockout bracket on a short stadium circuit, with the winner taking the Champion of Champions trophy (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Safety rules in all three series mandate helmets, fireproof suits, harnesses and, increasingly from the 2000s, the HANS device (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Men and women compete under the same technical regulations in these series; the Race of Champions has also invited leading women drivers from series such as the W Series (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Burti came up through Brazilian karting and the single-seater ladder — Formula Chevrolet, Formula Renault and Formula 3 — before stepping into Formula One with Jaguar Racing for the 2000 season, where he was teammate of Eddie Irvine (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). He remained with Jaguar in 2001, then moved to Brazilian domestic racing, where he became a fixture of the Stock Car Pro Series driving for Peugeot and other manufacturers, and represented Brazil in the A1 Grand Prix nations cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). His crowning individual result came at the 2008 Race of Champions at Wembley, which he won ahead of Sébastien Loeb and other world champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). His idol and the defining icon of Brazilian motorsport was Ayrton Senna, the three-time Formula One world champion from São Paulo, whose 1991–1994 title years Burti grew up watching (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
