Varpa

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Varpa is the traditional throwing game of the Swedish island of Gotland: players throw a flat, heavy stone or metal disc (the varpa) to land as close as possible to a wooden peg (the pinne) planted in the ground, a precision game played in team or singles form. A beloved fixture of Gotland summer life with organized leagues and a big island championship, it belongs to the same northern throwing tradition as Finnish molkky and the boules family.

Rules

The target is a wooden pin set at a set distance (commonly 15 to 20 metres, longer in veteran play) and players throw the varpa (traditionally a suitably flat stone, today often a purpose-made metal or rubber disc) as close as possible to the pin; like boules, the closest throw scores a point per round, with teams alternating throws in order and the winner of each round throwing first in the next. Matches play to a set total (commonly 12 points in team play), the throw's validity requires landing legally (on the ground, within the field's boundaries), and the rules also govern the varpa's material and weight, pin placement after each round, and the customary alternating-order conventions that keep the game flowing for players of every age.

History

Varpa descends from ancient throwing games played across the Baltic and Fennoscandian world, where throwing stones at a mark was both pastime and training; on Gotland the game survived as a living folk sport and became the island's identity game, with organized village leagues (the island's varpa clubs) and the annual Gotland championship staged each summer. The sport modernized its equipment (metal varpas replacing stones of choice) while keeping its village character, and it remains a fixture of Gotland summer culture, played by visiting tourists and champions alike with the seriousness of a folk institution.
