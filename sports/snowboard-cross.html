Snowboard Cross

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snowboard cross (SBX) is a mass-start race in which four to six riders speed down a course of banked turns, jumps and rollers, with the first riders across the finish line advancing; it is governed by the FIS and has been an Olympic event since Turin 2006 (FIS, n.d.-b). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, Alessandro Hämmerle of Austria won the men's gold ahead of Éliot Grondin of Canada, while Josie Baff of Australia won the women's gold ahead of Eva Adamczyková of Czechia (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the current leading women are 2026 Olympic champion Josie Baff and silver medallist Eva Adamczyková; among the men, 2026 Olympic champion Alessandro Hämmerle and silver medallist Éliot Grondin (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

In snowboard cross, four to six riders race simultaneously down a course — the Livigno Snow Park course for Milano Cortina 2026 was about 1,100 metres long — featuring berms, jumps, rollers and table-top jumps (FIS, n.d.-b). The format runs a time qualification, then heats of four to six riders, semi-finals, and a big final plus a small final; in each race the first riders across the line advance, and the winner of the big final takes gold (FIS, n.d.-a). Riders wear helmets, back protectors and padded suits; deliberate blocking, cutting across or pushing an opponent leads to relegation or disqualification, while incidental racing contact is generally allowed (FIS, n.d.-a). The mixed team event, added at Beijing 2022, pairs one woman and one man per nation in a relay-style race. Women's and men's individual events use identical courses and formats with separate classifications, and juniors compete at the FIS Junior World Championships (FIS, n.d.-a).

History

Snowboard cross was invented as "boardercross" in 1991 at Blackcomb Mountain in British Columbia, Canada, where the first course of jumps and berms was built that year; ESPN X Games added boardercross in 1997, and the FIS staged the first snowboard cross World Championships the same year (Wikipedia, n.d.). The discipline debuted at the Turin 2006 Winter Olympics, where Seth Wescott of the United States and Tanja Frieden of Switzerland won the first gold medals (Wikipedia, n.d.). Retired icons of the sport include Wescott, who repeated as Olympic champion at Vancouver 2010, Pierre Vaultier of France (Sochi 2014 and PyeongChang 2018) and Eva Samková of Czechia (Sochi 2014) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
