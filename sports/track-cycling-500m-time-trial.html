Track Cycling 500m Time Trial

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 500 m time trial is the women's track cycling sprint against the clock — a single rider covers 500 m (two laps on a 250 m velodrome) from a standing start in the gate on an aerodynamic bike, timed electronically to the thousandth of a second (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men race the 1 km time trial instead; the event has been on the Olympic programme for women since Seoul 1988 and at every World Championships (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At the 2026 UCI Track Cycling World Championships in Shanghai, 14–18 October 2026, Britain's Emma Finucane — pictured during the women's 500 m time trial — took silver in the individual sprint and team sprint and bronze in the keirin, establishing herself among the world's best sprinters, with the world record in the 500 m around 32–33 seconds (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

This is part of the track sprint and time-trial disciplines — Individual Pursuit, Individual Sprint, 1km Time Trial — within the cycling family (parent Cycling); the full list is under Important Links.

Rules

Under UCI Regulations Part 3, the elite women's time trial is 500 m and the elite men's 1 km; junior and under-23 riders use shorter distances; at the Olympics each rider contests one timed ride, while World Championships may add a qualification round (UCI, n.d.-b).

The equipment rules are the track rules — fixed-gear bike, no brakes, minimum weight 6.8 kg, aerodynamic clip-on handlebars allowed, mandatory helmet — and men and women compete in separate classifications on identical equipment standards (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The women's 500 m time trial became an Olympic event at Seoul 1988 and has been the showcase of sprint power on the track; Germany's Kristina Vogel, pictured, won eleven world titles between 2009 and 2016 including the 500 m time trial, and set the world record around 32 seconds before a serious training crash in 2018 ended her career (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Earlier icons include Australia's Anna Meares, Olympic sprint champion in 2004, and the Dutch sprinters who set the early world records in the 1990s and 2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).
