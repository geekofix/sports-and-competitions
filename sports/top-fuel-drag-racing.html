Top Fuel Drag Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Top Fuel drag racing is the premier class of drag racing: 11,000-horsepower, nitromethane-burning dragsters cover the 1,000-foot course in around 3.6 seconds at over 330 mph (530 km/h), making them the quickest accelerating vehicles in motorsport (National Hot Rod Association, n.d.). The sport in America is governed by the National Hot Rod Association (NHRA), founded in 1951 by Wally Parks, which sanctions the NHRA Mission Foods Drag Racing Series with its flagship U.S. Nationals at Brainerd, Minnesota (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete at every level, including Top Fuel itself — the leading current female drivers are Brittany Force, the 2022 NHRA Top Fuel world champion shown racing below, and Leah Pruett, the 2018 champion, who race against the men on equal terms (National Hot Rod Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Since 2008 the professional distance is 1,000 ft (305 m) — shortened from the quarter-mile (1,320 ft) for safety after a fatal crash — and the winner is the first across the finish line; elapsed time and terminal speed are recorded for the record books (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Cars launch from a standing start down a drag strip under a "Christmas tree" starting light system: in the professional classes the green light comes 0.400 s after the last amber, and a reaction time earlier than the green (a "red light") is a foul start that loses the race (National Hot Rod Association, n.d.). Qualifying sessions seed a 16-car elimination ladder in which pairs race head-to-head and the winner advances; in eliminations the driver with the lower qualifying time gets lane choice (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A Top Fuel dragster is built around a 500-cubic-inch (8.2 L) supercharged V8 derived from the Chrysler Hemi, fueled by up to 90% nitromethane, producing roughly 11,000 hp; after every run the engine is completely torn down and rebuilt in about an hour, and cars slow with dual parachutes after crossing the line (National Hot Rod Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The NHRA sanctions many classes beyond Top Fuel — Funny Car (nitromethane with production-style bodies), Pro Stock and the sportsman bracket categories — and women compete in all of them with no separate women's class; the official rulebook is published by the NHRA (National Hot Rod Association, n.d.).

History

Organized drag racing grew out of 1950s Southern California hot-rodding, and Wally Parks — founder of Hot Rod magazine — created the National Hot Rod Association in March 1951 to sanction the sport safely; the first NHRA Nationals were held in 1955 at Great Bend, Kansas (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). "Big Daddy" Don Garlits, driving his dragsters "Swamp Rat", became the first driver to exceed 200 mph in 1964 and set numerous elapsed-time records over a career spanning five decades (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Shirley Muldowney (b. 1940) broke the sport's gender barrier in 1973 as the first woman licensed to drive a Top Fuel dragster, went on to win 18 NHRA national events and three Top Fuel world championships (1977, 1980, 1982), and later became a team owner (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Other icons include Connie Kalitta (1938–2004), "the Minnesota Catalpa" Tony Schumacher, a record seven-time Top Fuel champion, and John Force, with 16 Funny Car titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
