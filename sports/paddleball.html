Paddleball

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Paddleball is an American wall sport played with a solid paddle and a small rubber ball, in two main formats: four-wall paddleball, played indoors on a standard 40 by 20 foot handball court, and one-wall paddleball, played outdoors against a single rebound wall (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Four-wall paddleball was invented in 1930 by Earl Riskey, a physical education instructor at the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is popular in the Upper Midwest of the United States (Michigan and Wisconsin), on the West Coast (especially southern California) and around Memphis, Tennessee, and men and women compete in separate national divisions under the same rules (USPA, n.d.). It is closely related to handball, racquetball, squash and paddle tennis. Paddleball is the solid-paddle wall game of the pelota/wall family — American Handball, Matkot, Basque Pelota, Valencian Pilota — listed under Important Links.

Rules

In four-wall paddleball, two players (singles), three (cutthroat) or four (doubles) play on the same 40 by 20 foot (12.2 by 6.1 m) court used for American handball, but with a solid paddle and a slower, slightly larger rubber ball; games are played to 21 points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The server stands in the service zone and must hit the ball against the front wall so that it lands in the opponent's court; a serve that hits the floor first (short), flies over the back wall (long), hits the ceiling, or touches two walls before the floor is a fault, and two faults end the serve (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After the serve, players may hit the ball against any wall, and the ball must be struck before it bounces twice; contact with another player is a hinder (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). One-wall paddleball uses a single rebound wall outdoors: the ball must hit the wall without touching the ground more than once in a valid rally, and games are played to 11 or 21 points (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The United States Paddleball Association (USPA) publishes the official rules and runs national championships with men's, women's and senior divisions that share identical court and scoring rules (USPA, n.d.).

History

Four-wall paddleball was invented in 1930 by Earl Riskey at the University of Michigan, who used wooden paddles from paddle tennis on the intramural handball courts and found that a de-fuzzed tennis ball had the right bounce (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game spread through Michigan and the Midwest in the 1930s-1950s, with national tournaments at Ann Arbor's Intramural Sports Building; the Riskey Trophy is still awarded for service to the game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). One-wall paddleball developed in New York City in the 1960s-1970s, where outdoor handball walls were adapted for paddle play; the US Paddleball Association (founded 1960) and later the revived USPA (2005, headed by Henry Marquez) have governed the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Champions such as eight-time national champion Mike Wisniewski of Bay City, Michigan, and one-wall legend Howard Hammer kept the competitive scene alive (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
