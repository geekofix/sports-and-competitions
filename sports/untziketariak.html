Untziketariak

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Untziketariak is the Basque milk-can carrying race: runners carry two heavy milk churns (traditionally 41 kg each, one in each hand) over a course; the fastest time wins. It tests the endurance of the dairy carriers (untziketariak) who once carried milk from farm to market. Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are Zaku Lasterka, Giza Probak, Lasto Altxatzea; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

Figure: Golden 55. (2010-06-20). A traditional metal milk churn of the type carried in untziketariak races [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Milk_churn_-_4716901937.jpg

Rules

Two metal milk cans of the set weight are carried, one per hand, arms locked; the runner must complete the course without setting a can down (a set-down ends the run or adds penalty time). Distances are typically 100–400 m; men's and women's heats with adjusted weights at modern events (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

History

Dairy transport by hand was everyday work in the Basque mountains until the mid-20th century; the carrying race preserves it as a fiesta sport. Historic milk churns and can stands are still photographed at heritage sites (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
