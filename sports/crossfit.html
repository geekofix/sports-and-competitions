CrossFit

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

CrossFit is a fitness and competition program built around constantly varied functional movements performed at high intensity: Olympic weightlifting, gymnastics (pull-ups, muscle-ups, handstands) and cardio (running, rowing, double-unders). It is owned by CrossFit, Inc., which runs the annual CrossFit Games to crown the "Fittest on Earth", in divisions for individuals, teams of four, and age groups (teens, masters). Workouts are published as the "Workout of the Day" (WOD) and are adapted for local gyms worldwide.

Rules

At the CrossFit Games, athletes face a series of unknown workouts revealed only shortly before competition; each event is scored, with the winner of an event receiving 100 points and others ranked by finish time, reps completed or load lifted (points decrease by set steps). The athlete or team with the lowest total points after all events wins; a tie is broken by comparing event-by-event finishes. Movements must meet published standards (e.g., a full-depth squat, a locked-out overhead position), and video review and judges enforce them. In the Open (the worldwide qualification stage), athletes complete workouts at their affiliate gym and submit scores online.

History

CrossFit was founded by Greg Glassman in 2000 in Santa Cruz, California, after he developed the program in the 1990s while training police officers. The first CrossFit Games were held in 2007 on a ranch in Aromas, California, with about 70 athletes; the Games moved to the StubHub Center in Carson (2010s), then Fort Worth, Texas (2017), and Madison, Wisconsin from 2019. Affiliate gyms grew to several thousand worldwide. The sport has faced criticism over injury risk (rhabdomyolysis cases), which CrossFit addressed with safety guidelines and medical partnerships.
