Tetris

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tetris is Alexey Pajitnov's 1984 tile-matching puzzle game (falling tetrominoes in a 10 by 20 well); beyond casual play it is a serious esport: the Classic Tetris World Championship (CTWC) plays the 1989 NES version under hypertapping rules and has crowned champions since 2010, while guideline events play modern Tetris (Classic Tetris World Championship, n.d.). Henk Rogers holds the Tetris rights; top players include Jonas Neubauer (seven-time CTWC champion, died 2021) and Joseph Saelee (2020 CTWC champion), whose deciding final game at the 2020 CTWC was a famous comeback (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Puyo Puyo, Pac-Man, Donkey Kong and E-Sports; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

CTWC rules (classic NES Tetris): the 1989 NES version has no hold piece and a one-piece preview; hypertapping is allowed; matches start at level 18, 19 or 29 (the so-called kill screen); matches are best-of-5 games decided by score. Guideline events include 40-line sprint, marathon and battle modes with garbage lines. Open to all genders (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Pajitnov created Tetris at the Soviet Academy of Sciences in 1984; it became the pack-in game of the Nintendo Game Boy in 1989 (over 35 million copies) and the center of the first big esport scoring controversy; the CTWC began in 2010 and moved to a Las Vegas arena stage from 2018 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
