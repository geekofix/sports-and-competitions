Hillclimbing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Hillclimb (hill climb) is a motorsport time-trial in which one car at a time races up a closed mountain road against the clock, from a standing start to a finish line at the summit. It is sanctioned by the FIA (FIA European Hill Climb Championship, running since the 1950s) and by national clubs; the most famous events are the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb (Colorado, since 1916) and the Goodwood Festival of Speed hill climb (since 1993).

Rules

At FIA European Hill Climb Championship events, each driver gets one or two timed runs (usually one timed run plus one practice/qualifying run) up a course of 2-20 km with a start at the bottom and a standing start; the fastest time wins within each class (touring cars, GT, single-seaters, electric, historic). Cars must pass technical inspection (roll cage, fire extinguisher, harness, helmet per FIA Appendix K/8856 standards), and drivers must wear FIA-approved helmets and suits. At Pikes Peak, each driver gets one run (three in practice days), with a standing start at 2,862 m and a finish at 4,301 m (14,115 ft) over 19.99 km with 156 turns; the Unlimited class allows prototype cars, and Romain Dumas set the standing record of 7:57.148 in 2018 in the electric VW I.D. R. Safety rules include mandatory pace cars, red flags and medical cover.

History

Hillclimbing began in Europe in the late 19th century (the first organized events in France and Austria in the late 19th century, and the FIA European Hill Climb Championship has been organized since the 1950s, with classic courses such as Mont Ventoux (France, early 20th century), Schauinsland (Germany), Trento-Bondone (Italy) and St. Ursanne-Les Rangiers (Switzerland). Pikes Peak began in 1916 (won by Rea Lentz in a Romano), and Goodwood's Festival of Speed hill climb (1.16 miles) has been a highlight since 1993, with a shootout final. Legends include Sébastien Loeb, who won Pikes Peak in 2013 with a Peugeot 208 T16 in a record 8:13.878, Ari Vatanen, whose 1988 Peugeot 405 T16 record stood for decades, and Michèle Mouton, who won Pikes Peak in 1985 in an Audi Sport quattro.
