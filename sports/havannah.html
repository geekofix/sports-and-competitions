Havannah

≈ 1 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Havannah is a hexagonal connection game invented in 1981 by Christian Freeling: played on a hexagon of 54 cells (side 10), a player wins by forming a ring, a bridge (connecting two opposite sides) or a fork (three corners). Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Hex, Go, Amazons; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Figure: Dvortygirl. (2007-01-27). A hexagonal board (r=6 shown) used for hex-family games [Diagram]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hex11.PNG

Rules

Hex board shaped as a hexagon with 10 cells per side; win by ring around a cell/group, bridge connecting two opposite sides, or fork touching three corners. World championships exist (Havannah world championship) (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Invented by Christian Freeling (Netherlands) in 1981; it belongs to the connection-game family with Hex and Y. The Havannah world championship is held regularly (Wikipedia, n.d.).
