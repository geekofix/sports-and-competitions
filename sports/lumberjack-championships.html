Lumberjack Championships

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lumberjack championships (woodchopping) are competitions in which athletes use axes and chainsaws to chop, split and buck timber against the clock: events such as the underhand chop, standing block chop, springboard, single buck, double buck and hot saw. The modern professional series is STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, which runs national series and an annual World Championship; chainsaw carving (sculpting with a chainsaw) is also contested.

Rules

In chopping events, the athlete's time is measured from the starting signal to the completion of the task: chopping through a standing log (standing block chop) or a horizontal log on trestles (underhand chop), sawing through a log with a chainsaw (single buck, double buck, hot saw — the fastest chainsaws), or chopping footholds into a vertical plank (springboard) and then chopping through the top block. In each heat, competitors race head-to-head or against the clock, and the fastest clean time advances; a false start or incomplete cut means elimination. Safety rules require protective chaps, helmets, hearing and eye protection, and steel-toed boots; only homologated saws and axes are allowed.

History

Woodchopping contests grew out of logging camps and forestry fairs in the 19th century in North America, Scandinavia and Australia, where axemen competed for prestige and prize money. The sport became formalized in the 20th century with standardized events and safety rules, and STIHL has run the TIMBERSPORTS World Championship annually since the mid-1980s, with national series in more than a dozen countries. Champions such as the Australian David Foster (multiple world titles in the standing block chop and springboard) became national celebrities, and the sport remains a highlight of country shows and agricultural exhibitions.
