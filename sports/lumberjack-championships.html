Lumberjack Championships

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lumberjack championships (timbersports) are competitions in which athletes are timed using axes and chainsaws in disciplines taken from professional forestry work: Stock Saw, Single Buck, Underhand Chop, Standing Block Chop, Springboard and Hot Saw (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-a). The flagship series is the STIHL TIMBERSPORTS Series, founded in 1985, whose season culminates in the World Championship — held at the Porsche-Arena in Stuttgart, Germany, on 30–31 October 2026 as part of STIHL's 100th anniversary — alongside the World Trophy and the Rookie World Championship (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-b). A historic milestone came in 2026: the first-ever STIHL TIMBERSPORTS Women's World Championship was held on 6 June 2026 in Budapest, Hungary, where Canada's Andrea Hand became the first women's world champion, with ten athletes from four continents competing and six national records falling in the Single Buck alone; the inaugural North American Women's Cup was won by Lauren Bergman at JACKALOPE Virginia Beach on 1 June 2026 (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-c). A separate tradition is the Lumberjack World Championship in Hayward, Wisconsin, USA, held annually since 1960 with events such as log rolling, axe throwing and springboard (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Each discipline is a timed run, and faults add penalty seconds to the clock (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-a). In Stock Saw the athlete cuts two complete through-cuts in a horizontal log lying on a stand; Single Buck uses a standing vertical log with a single bucking cut; Underhand Chop and Standing Block Chop are axe disciplines in which the athlete severs a vertical block of wood in two chops from opposite sides; Springboard combines axe work on a springboard inserted into a standing tree trunk with a final Hot Saw cut; Hot Saw is a chainsaw race through a horizontal log (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-a). Athletes compete in heats and finals, national records and personal bests are recorded in the official database, and safety rules require helmets, eye and ear protection, chainsaw chaps and steel-toe boots (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-a). Men's and women's events use the same six disciplines, the same time-scoring and the same safety rules; the 2026 season introduced equal prize payouts and the first women's world and continental titles (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-c). The Hayward Lumberjack World Championship uses its own event set — log rolling, boom walking, axe throwing, chainsaw speed cuts and tree climbing — scored by points or time depending on the event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Competitive wood chopping and sawing grew directly out of logging culture in North America, Scandinavia and the Alpine countries, where workers competed informally at county fairs and logging camps from the 19th century onward (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Lumberjack World Championship was founded in Hayward, Wisconsin, in 1960 and became the longest-running event of its kind (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The STIHL TIMBERSPORTS Series was founded in 1985 by the German chainsaw manufacturer STIHL and professionalized the sport with standardized disciplines, safety standards and a world tour (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-b). New Zealand's Jason Wynyard dominated the 2000s and early 2010s with four consecutive world titles (2008–2011), and American Nate Hodges set world records in Stock Saw and Hot Saw en route to the 2025 US title; on the women's side, three-time US champion Martha King has been a leading figure, culminating in Andrea Hand's first women's world title in Budapest in June 2026 (STIHL TIMBERSPORTS, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
