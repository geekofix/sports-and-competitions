Para Karate

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para karate is the World Karate Federation's competitive format for athletes with intellectual (class K21/K22) or physical (class K30) impairments, contested as kata (forms) at the World Para Karate Championships and on the World Games program — para karate is confirmed for the Chengdu 2029 World Games after its exclusion from the Paralympic program. Classes K21/K22 cover intellectual impairment and K30 physical impairment; kata is judged by panels of judges scoring technical execution, with elimination rounds and medal matches (WKF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). At the 2022 European Championships in Azerbaijan the national para karate teams competed in kata team events, and women compete in the same classes as men (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the combat sports family on this site its closest relatives are Karate, Karate Kata, Karate Kumite and the style pages such as Shotokan Karate; among the Para sports it is grouped with Para Judo and Para Taekwondo; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

WKF para karate is kata-only in competition: athletes perform two katas per round (different katas in later rounds), judges score from 5.0 to 10.0 per criterion and the totals decide; in K21 (intellectual) classes support persons may assist with the start position but not during the performance. Kumite is not contested under WKF para rules because of contact-safety requirements. Divisions are men's and women's per class; equipment follows standard WKF rules (gumshield, gloves optional in kata) (WKF, n.d.).

History

Competitive para karate was launched as a demonstration at the 2012 World Championships in Paris and got its first official World Para Karate Championships in Bremen 2014 (~50 athletes from 20 countries), followed by Linz 2016, Madrid 2018, Dubai 2021 and Budapest 2023; the WKF was recognized by the IPC as the Para-sport federation for karate in 2015, and the discipline appeared at the 2023 World Combat Games in Riyadh. Classes today are K10 (visual impairment), K21 (intellectual) and K30 (physical, lower limbs); early world champions include Franck Duboisse (Belgium, wheelchair kata 2014) and Uliana Smyrnova (Ukraine, women's wheelchair kata 2014) (WKF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
