Dahi Handi

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dahi Handi is the Govinda sport performed during the Hindu festival of Krishna Janmashtami, in which teams of govinda pathaks build a human pyramid to reach a clay pot (handi) filled with dahi — yogurt — suspended high above the ground, and the team that breaks it wins the bout and the prize money (Government of Maharashtra, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 festival fell on Saturday 5 September 2026, the day after Krishna Janmashtami on 4 September, with major celebrations in Mumbai, Thane and Pune (Wikipedia, n.d.). Pyramids of up to nine or ten tiers are attempted, each team getting three tries, and spectators traditionally throw water at the climbers; the event is accompanied by the chant "Ala re ala, Govinda ala" (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Guinness World Record for the highest nine-tier dahi handi pyramid stands at 43.79 ft (13.35 m), set in Thane in 2012 by the Jai Jawan Govinda Pathak (Guinness World Records, n.d.). Both men and women take part, and women's mandals now compete in their own events alongside the men's (Government of Maharashtra, n.d.).

Rules

There is no international federation; the sport is organized by the participating mandals (neighborhood associations) under safety guidelines issued by the Government of Maharashtra (Government of Maharashtra, n.d.). The handi — a clay pot of yogurt, sometimes with prizes tied inside — is hung at a height fixed by the organizers, historically up to the record 13.35 m (43.79 ft) for a nine-tier pyramid (Guinness World Records, n.d.). A team forms a pyramid of several tiers, the topmost govinda climbs and smashes the pot, and the yogurt rain marks victory; each mandal gets three attempts per height (Wikipedia, n.d.). Prizes range from a few thousand rupees for small pots to over ₹1.2 million (12 lakh) for the biggest 2010s events (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety rules govern the minimum age of participants, the height-to-tier ratio, protective gear and insurance, after a series of injuries in the 2010s (Government of Maharashtra, n.d.). Men's and women's teams compete under the same rules, in separate or mixed events depending on the mandal (Government of Maharashtra, n.d.).

History

Dahi Handi reenacts the legend of the infant Krishna as a butter thief who stole makhan (butter) from the pots of the gopis of Gokul, and the celebration of Krishna Janmashtami with human pyramids became popular in Maharashtra in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Mazgaon mandal in Mumbai built the first nine-tier pyramid in 2008, and the Jai Jawan Govinda Pathak of Thane set the Guinness-recognized 13.35 m record in 2012 (Guinness World Records, n.d.). A variant called Utlotsavam is held at the Tirupati temple, and the festival was paused during the COVID-19 pandemic before returning at full scale (Wikipedia, n.d.).
