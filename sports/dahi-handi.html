Dahi Handi

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dahi Handi is the Mumbai pyramid festival of Krishna Janmashtami: teams of young Govindas form human pyramids several tiers high to reach and break a pot of curd (dahi) hung high above the street, re-enacting the young Krishna's butter theft from Hindu tradition. Fed by festival crowds, prize money and sponsorship, the best teams build pyramids of nine tiers or more in seconds — climbing to records of over 40 feet.

The state of Maharashtra classifies Dahi Handi as an adventure sport, with regulations on tier limits and participant safety.

Rules

A handi — the earthen pot filled with curd and often money — is hung from a rope at heights commonly between 20 and 40 feet above the street. Each team, often dozens strong, forms a pyramid with base tiers of the strongest boys standing shoulder to shoulder, layers climbing to a light top climber who shimmies up and breaks the pot with a coconut stroke; water balloons and hoses thrown by opposing crowds add to the challenge. Prizes reward height and speed, with tier limits per category in recent years, helmets increasingly used and time limits per attempt.

History

The tradition celebrates Krishna's childhood raids on hung pots of butter and curd, and Dahi Handi grew into a public festival across Mumbai's lanes in the 20th century, with neighbourhood mandals organising competing pyramids and prizes. In the 2000s corporate sponsorship and media turned top teams — such as the record-setting Jai Jawan Govinda Pathak mandal of Dadar, which built a nine-tier pyramid in 2012 — into professional-style crews with training gyms and paid appearances. The 2010s brought safety regulation (age and height rules) after campaigning by activists, and courts asked organizers to insure participants.
