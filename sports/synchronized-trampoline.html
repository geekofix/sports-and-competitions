Synchronized Trampoline

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Synchronized trampoline is a discipline of trampoline gymnastics in which two athletes perform identical 10-skill routines at the same time on adjacent trampolines, judged on execution, difficulty, horizontal displacement and — the defining element — synchronization. It is governed by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG), into which the International Trampoline Federation (FIT, founded 1965) merged in 1999; the first World Trampoline Championships were held in London in 1964, and individual trampoline became Olympic at Sydney 2000, while synchronized trampoline remains a non-Olympic world championship discipline (FIG, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring follows the FIG Code of Points 2025–2028: execution, difficulty, horizontal displacement and synchronization scores minus penalties, with dedicated judges scoring the timing between the pair (FIG, n.d.). The 38th FIG Trampoline World Championships in Pamplona (5–9 November 2025) contested four disciplines — men's and women's individual, synchronized and double-mini — with men's, women's and mixed synchronized events (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). China has dominated the discipline since 2007, and Canada's Jason Burnett holds the difficulty world record of 18.00 (2010, Pacific Rim Championships, Melbourne) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Each synchronized pair performs a routine of 10 skills on side-by-side trampolines; the routine is judged by execution (out of 10 per skill), difficulty (open-ended DD value), horizontal displacement and synchronization — the time difference between the two athletes' skills is measured and scored — minus penalties for falls or stepping off the bed (FIG, n.d.). Routines are limited to about 10 skills with no required "risers" at world level, and pairs compete in men's, women's and mixed categories at the world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Qualification rounds lead to a final where the highest total wins; ties are broken by the higher synchronization score (FIG, n.d.). Age-group and double-mini-trampoline variants follow the same synchronized principle, and all genders compete under identical scoring rules (FIG, n.d.).

History

Trampoline gymnastics grew out of circus diving acts and the 1930s–50s American trampoline boom; the International Trampoline Federation was founded in 1965 and held the first world championships in London in 1964 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The discipline entered the Olympics at Sydney 2000 as individual trampoline, while synchronized trampoline — added to the world programme in the 1970s–80s — stayed non-Olympic (FIG, n.d.). The FIT merged into the FIG in 1999, unifying governance (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
