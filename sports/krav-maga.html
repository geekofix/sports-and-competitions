Krav Maga

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Krav Maga is the Israeli self-defense and close-combat system developed by Imi Lichtenfeld (1910–1998) for the Haganah and later the Israel Defense Forces (IDF), combining techniques from boxing, wrestling, judo, aikido and karate (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is designed as a practical self-defense system rather than a sport — there are no regular competitive tournaments — and is taught worldwide by several international organizations, above all the International Krav Maga Federation (IKMF), founded in 1996 at Lichtenfeld's request, plus Krav Maga Global (KMG, 2010), Krav Maga Worldwide and the original Israeli Krav Maga Association (IKMA, 1978) (International Krav Maga Federation, n.d.-a). Women are a core part of the system: Krav Maga was explicitly adapted for civilian self-defense including women's seminars in the military, and women train and instruct alongside men in every major organization — the IDF's women's Sexual Assault Self-Defense Seminar at Lackland-style programs is a well-known example (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Progression is tracked through a belt and patch grading system (Practitioner levels white to green, Graduate levels blue and brown, Expert black in the IKMF; P1–P5, G1–G5 and E1–E5 in KMG) (International Krav Maga Federation, n.d.-a).

Rules

Because Krav Maga is a self-defense system and not a sport, it has no competition ruleset — the governing principle is to neutralize a threat quickly and legally and "walk away in peace" (International Krav Maga Federation, n.d.-a). The technical curriculum covers verbal de-escalation, stance and movement, strikes with fists, palms, elbows, knees and feet to vulnerable targets, defenses against punches, kicks, chokes, holds and weapon attacks (knife, gun), releases from grabs, fighting multiple attackers and stress drills, with sparring and slow-fights at higher levels (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The only formalized "rules" are the grading curricula of the international federations: IKMF Practitioner levels (white, yellow, orange, green belts), Graduate levels (blue, brown) and Expert level (black), each requiring defined techniques and sparring; KMG uses P1–P5, G1–G5 and E1–E5 grades (International Krav Maga Federation, n.d.-a). There are no weight classes because there are no matches; instead, techniques are adapted to age, size and ability, and the system is explicitly taught to women, men, teenagers and seniors alike, with military, police and civilian curricula (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Curriculum source: International Krav Maga Federation, https://kravmaga-ikmf.com/ (International Krav Maga Federation, n.d.-a).

History

Imi Lichtenfeld, born 26 May 1910 in Budapest and died 9 January 1998 in Netanya, grew up in Bratislava, where in the 1930s he defended Jewish neighborhoods against anti-Semitic street gangs and became a champion boxer and wrestler (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). He fled Europe in 1940 on the last ship out, reached the British Mandate of Palestine, and from 1944 trained fighters of the Haganah and Palmach in wrestling, swimming and close combat (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 1948, when the State of Israel was founded, he became chief instructor of Krav Maga at the IDF's Wingate Institute, a post he held for about 20 years until retiring in 1964; he then adapted the system for police and civilians (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). He founded the Israeli Krav Maga Association (IKMA) on 22 October 1978, and in 1996 — at his request — his senior students Eyal Yanilov, Avi Moyal and others established the International Krav Maga Federation (IKMF); Yanilov left in 2010 to found Krav Maga Global (KMG), and the American branch became Krav Maga Worldwide (International Krav Maga Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
