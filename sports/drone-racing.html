Drone Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Drone racing (FPV racing) is an esports and air-sport discipline in which pilots fly camera-equipped racing drones through obstacle courses at high speed, wearing first-person-view (FPV) goggles. It is governed by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI), which runs the FAI Drone Racing World Cup and World Championship (class F9U), while the commercial Drone Racing League (DRL) stages the biggest televised events.

Rules

At FAI Drone Racing World Cup events, pilots fly identical-spec or spec-limited quadcopters (class F9U: up to 330 mm motor-to-motor, max 6S battery) through a 3D course of gates, flags and tunnels, usually several laps, and the fastest total time wins; heats lead to semifinals and finals. The pilot sees only the onboard camera feed through FPV goggles and steers via radio; spotters and safety marshals monitor the course. At DRL events, custom-built drones (DRL Racer X reached 163.5 mph / 263 km/h, a Guinness world record in 2017) race on elaborate stadium courses under DRL regulations with standardized chassis, and the DRL Championship awards the season title. National federations under FAI membership organize qualifiers; anti-doping and safety rules follow the FAI Sporting Code.

History

FPV drone racing began around 2013-2014 in France and the US when hobbyists added cameras to racing quads and flew through gates in parks and forests. The FAI recognized drone racing as an official air sport discipline and launched the FAI Drone Racing World Cup in 2017, with the first FAI Drone Racing World Championship in 2022 (won by Kwan Theeraphong of South Korea). The Drone Racing League, founded in 2015 by Nicholas Horbaczewski, brought the sport to stadiums and ESPN broadcasts, culminating in the DRL Racer X speed record. Today drone racing is part of the World Games program and is a demonstration path toward future Olympic inclusion, with simulators and youth programs run by FAI member organizations.
