Two-Foot High Kick

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The two-foot high kick (akratcheak) is the most demanding Inuit event: the athlete jumps with both feet together, touches a hanging target with the soles of both feet, and lands on both feet maintaining balance (AWG, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's and women's records are kept at AWG and WEIO — the men's record stood at 2.64 m (8 ft 8 in) as of 2007, women's at 1.98 m; at the 2026 WEIO Danica Taylor (Rankin Inlet) won the women's event at 74 in and Colton Paul (Kipnuk) the men's at 104 in (ICT News, 2026). Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are Blanket Toss, Snow Snake, Two-Foot High Kick, Finger Pull, Hand Games, World Eskimo Indian Olympics; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Both feet must leave the ground together, strike the target together, and land together with balance held for a count. Three attempts per height (AWG, n.d.).

History

Originating in subsistence whale hunting — a messenger kicked both feet in the air to signal a whale to the village — the two-foot high kick became an AWG medal event in 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
