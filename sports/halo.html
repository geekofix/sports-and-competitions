Halo

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Halo is Microsoft/343 Industries' science-fiction arena shooter, the most successful console esport of the 2000s; its premier circuit is the Halo Championship Series (HCS), which ran on Halo Infinite through 2025 with the 2025 world championship won by FaZe Clan (343 Industries / Halo Waypoint, n.d.). The 2026 season marks Halo's 25th anniversary: a HCS World Championship is scheduled for the Seattle Convention Center, 23–25 October 2026, with a $1M prize pool, 16 teams and $400,000 for the winner, plus the Halo Fest invitational in Seattle (Esportsport, n.d.; Liquipedia, n.d.).

Figure: Kona X. (2013-02-27). Walshy (Halo pro) interview, 2013 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Walshy_2013_interview.jpg

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Call of Duty, Overwatch, Valorant, Counter-Strike, Apex Legends and E-Sports; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

HCS rules: 4v4 slayer, CTF, strongholds, oddball and king of the hill on the HCS map pool; double-elimination brackets with best-of-5 series and a best-of-7 grand final; $1M championship pools since 2024; open qualification through online cups and open circuits; all genders compete in the same open brackets (Liquipedia, n.d.).

History

Halo's esport began with MLG in the 2000s — Walshy, Ogre 2 and the Final Boss dynasty won multiple MLG titles — and 343 Industries launched the HCS in 2014 with Halo 2 Anniversary; the world championship grew to $1M+ pools in the Halo 5/Infinite era, and FaZe Clan lifted the 2025 Halo Infinite world title (Wikipedia, n.d.).
