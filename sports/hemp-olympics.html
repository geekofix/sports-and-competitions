Hemp Olympics

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Hemp Olympix (Hemp Olympics) were counterculture novelty games created in the mid-1990s as part of Nimbin's MardiGrass festival in Australia, parodying the Olympic Games with cannabis-themed events — artistic joint rolling, speed rolling, blindfolded rolling, bong throwing and more — to educate visitors about cannabis law reform. The idea began in 1996 and was staged internationally, including a 2000 edition in Sydney's Victoria Park timed with the real Olympics, covered by the BBC.

Rules

There is no formal rulebook; events are judged informally by organizers and the crowd. Typical events include: the artistic joint roll (a timed creative spliff, historically 10 minutes), the speed roll, the blindfolded roll (rolling while blindfolded), the bong throw (distance, parodying the javelin — organizers joked there are more bongs in Australian homes than javelins), and eating/"munchies" contests. Teams and individuals compete for humorous prizes; the tone is deliberately satirical, combining entertainment with political messaging for cannabis legalization.

History

The Hemp Olympix were created in 1996 at Nimbin's MardiGrass by activists (Chibo, Bob and Michael) as entertaining, educational events alongside the pro-legalization campaign "End Prohibition 2000"; a 2000 edition in Sydney's Victoria Park was timed to finish the week before the Sydney Olympics and drew BBC and international coverage. The Hemp Olympix continue annually as part of MardiGrass in Nimbin, New South Wales, which has been held since 1993 and is Australia's longest-running cannabis law reform festival. Related events include Seattle Hempfest (since 1991), the world's largest cannabis policy rally.
