Blackball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Blackball (also called English eight-ball or blackball eight-ball) is the British/Commonwealth variant of eight-ball pool played with red and yellow unnumbered balls instead of stripes and solids. The black 8-ball must be potted last to win. It is governed by the WPA, which runs the WPA Blackball World Championship (biennial since 2006), and by the International Eightball Pool Federation (IEPF, formerly WEPF), which uses "International Rules".

Rules

Under WPA blackball rules, the object balls are seven red and seven yellow plus the black 8. After the break, the table is open; the first pot decides the player's color, and a player must pot all of their color before the 8-ball. Potting the 8-ball early, potting the cue ball with the 8, or potting an opponent's ball loses the frame. Fouls give the opponent two visits (or ball in hand behind the baulk line depending on the rule set). At the WPA Blackball World Championship, frames are played in a knockout format with men's, women's and junior divisions.

History

Blackball developed in the UK in the 20th century from eight-ball, with standardized rules published by BAPTO in 1976 and later by the UK Pool Federation. The WPA adopted its own "blackball" ruleset to distinguish the British game from the American pool version and began the Blackball World Championship in 2006. Top players include Gareth Potts ("the Golden Boy") and Mick Hill of England, record WEPF/IEPF world champions.
