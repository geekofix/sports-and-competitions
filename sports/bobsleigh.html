Bobsleigh

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bobsleigh (bobsled) is a winter sliding team sport in which crews of two or four push a streamlined sled down an iced track and reach speeds over 150 km/h (93 mph). It is governed by the International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation (IBSF) and has been an Olympic sport since the first Winter Games in Chamonix 1924. The flagship events are the IBSF World Championships and the Olympic two-man and four-man competitions.

Rules

Under IBSF rules, each team gets up to four runs (two at the Olympics) down a 1,200-1,500 m track with 15-20 curves; the total time over all runs decides the ranking, with hundredths of a second separating medals. The sled is pushed from a starting ice section, then the crew loads; the brakeman (in the back) steers in the four-man, while the pilot steers in the two-man. Sleds are weighed with crew, with maximum weights of 210 kg (two-man) and 630 kg (four-man, men); women's two-man maximum is 165 kg. Skeleton is the related individual discipline under the same federation.

History

Bobsleigh began in the late 19th century in St. Moritz, Switzerland, where hotel guests raced sleds on snow roads; the first bobsleigh club was founded there in 1897 and the first ice track was built in 1903. It was part of the first Winter Olympics in 1924 and has been on every Winter Games since. Nations like Switzerland, Germany, the USA and Canada have dominated; legends include Eugenio Monti (ITA), who won the first Olympic sportsmanship medal for lending a bolt to British rivals in 1964.
