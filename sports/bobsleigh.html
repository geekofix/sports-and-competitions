Bobsleigh

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bobsleigh is the winter sliding sport in which a crew of two or four athletes races an aerodynamic sled down an ice track, reaching speeds above 150 km/h; the monobob is the solo women's discipline. It is governed by the International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation (International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation [IBSF], n.d.-a). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics at the Cortina Sliding Centre "Eugenio Monti" (February 15–22, 2026; 201 athletes from 24 nations), the women's monobob gold went to Elana Meyers Taylor of the United States (3:57.93, unbeaten in Olympic monobob), silver to Laura Nolte of Germany and bronze to Kaillie Humphries of the United States, who at 40 became the second-oldest woman to win a Winter Olympic bobsleigh medal with her sixth Olympic medal overall; the two-woman gold went to Nolte with Deborah Levi, silver to Lisa Buckwitz and Neele Schuten, bronze to Humphries and Jasmine Jones (IBSF, n.d.-b) (International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation, n.d.-a) (International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation, n.d.-b) (International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation, n.d.-c).

On the men's side, Johannes Lochner of Germany won both the two-man (with Georg Fleischhauer, 3:39.70) and the four-man (with Thorsten Margis, Jörn Wenzel and Fleischhauer, 3:37.57), becoming the most successful Olympic bobsleigh pilot in history ahead of Francesco Friedrich, who took silver in both events; the two-man bronze went to Germany's Adam Ammour and Alexander Schaller, and the four-man bronze to Switzerland's Michael Vogt, Andreas Haas, Amadou David Ndiaye and Mario Aeberhard (3:38.64). Jamaica's Mica Moore finished 14th in the monobob — the best ever result by a Jamaican woman — after winning the first international bobsleigh gold by a Jamaican at the North American Cup in Whistler in November 2025 (IBSF, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the IBSF International Bobsleigh Rules, the two-man and four-man sleds are pushed from a start ramp and crewed by a pilot (front) and brakemen; the monobob has a single athlete; the two-woman event pairs a pilot with one brakeman (IBSF, n.d.-a). Each event runs four heats over two days (two heats per day for the four-man), and the cumulative time decides the medals; sleds must meet weight limits (with crew: max 210 kg for two-man/monobob, 340 kg for two-woman, 630 kg for four-man; without crew the sled has its own minimum weight) and pass technical inspection, and crews must pass a push-start timing test (IBSF, n.d.-a). Women's and men's events use the same track and sleds within their class; the monobob is exclusively women's at the Olympics, and mixed team relay events (with skeleton) exist at World Cup level. Anti-doping follows the World Anti-Doping Code (IBSF, n.d.-c).

History

Bobsleigh began in the 1880s in St. Moritz, Switzerland, when hotelier Caspar Badrutt had a toboggan run built for guests; the first organized race took place in 1884, and the first five-man bob appeared in the early 1890s. The sport debuted at the first Winter Olympics in Chamonix 1924 (four/five-man only) and has been on every Winter programme since 1932; women's monobob joined in 2022 and two-woman in 2002. Bobsleigh was an Olympic sport at St. Moritz 1928 for the first time as a five-man event (IBSF, n.d.-b).

Legendary pilots include Italy's Eugenio Monti (six Olympic medals, 1956–1968, after whom the Cortina track is named), Switzerland's Meinhard Neuner, and the dominant German teams of the 1970s–2020s; on the women's side, Kaillie Humphries (Canada/USA) is the most decorated female slider with three Olympic golds and her sixth medal in 2026, and Elana Meyers Taylor (USA) is the most decorated American with five Olympic medals (IBSF, n.d.-b).
