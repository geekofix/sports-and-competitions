Eventing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Eventing is the equestrian triathlon: one horse-rider combination competes in three phases — dressage, cross-country and show jumping — with penalties from all phases added together; the lowest total wins. It is governed by the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI), is one of the three Olympic equestrian disciplines, and its premier events are the FEI Eventing World Championships and the Olympic Games.

Rules

At FEI Championships and the Olympics, each phase contributes penalty points. Dressage: the pair performs a test, judges award penalty points for errors (the best tests score close to 20-30 penalties). Cross-country: the pair gallops over a solid-fence course (at championship level around 4-6 km with 25-40 obstacles, within an optimum time; exceeding it adds time penalties, and a fall or three refusals eliminates). Show jumping: a final round over 10-12 fences in an arena; knockdowns and refusals add penalties, and exceeding the time limit adds time penalties. The total penalty score decides the placings; ties are broken by the cross-country score. Riders must wear approved helmets and body protectors on cross-country, horses must meet minimum age (5 years at Olympics/6 at World Championships for the long format), and anti-doping applies to horse and athlete under FEI rules. Team events (Olympics, World Championships) count the best three of four riders.

History

Eventing descends from cavalry tests of the 19th century, which combined endurance marching, steeplechase and dressage; it entered the Olympic program in Stockholm 1912 as the only event in which military officers and civilians competed together. The FEI (founded 1921) governs the sport, and the first FEI World Championships (then called the European Championships) were held in 1952 in Badminton. The sport's legends include the German and British teams of the 1950s-70s (Hans Günter Winkler, William Fox-Pitt, Ingrid Klimke) and modern icons Michael Jung (three-time Olympic individual gold, 2012-2024) and Ingrid Klimke. The Burghley Horse Trials (since 1961), Badminton (since 1949) and Kentucky Three-Day Event (since 1978) are the sport's "Majors" (5-star events), alongside the Olympics and World Championships.
