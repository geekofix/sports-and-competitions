Eventing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Eventing is the equestrian triathlon: each horse-and-rider combination contests three phases — dressage, cross-country and show jumping — over one to four days, and the pair with the fewest penalty points wins (Fédération Equestre Internationale [FEI], n.d.-b). It is governed worldwide by the FEI and is one of the three Olympic equestrian disciplines. At the FEI World Championships Aachen 2026, 11–23 August 2026, Great Britain took the eventing team gold with Rosalind Canter, Laura Collett, Tom McEwen and Gemma Stevens, while Germany's Michael Jung won the individual title — adding another world championship to his collection — in a finale at the historic Main Stadium where 20 of 85 starters failed to complete the demanding cross-country course (FEI, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current riders are — in the women's field — team gold medallist Rosalind Canter and her teammates Laura Collett and Gemma Stevens of Great Britain, and — in the men's field — individual world champion Michael Jung of Germany and team silver medallist Tom McEwen of Great Britain (FEI, n.d.-a).

Rules

Eventing is played under the FEI Eventing Rules: the dressage phase is a judged test in a 20 m × 60 m arena scored as a percentage and converted to penalty points, the cross-country phase is a timed ride over solid obstacles — fences, ditches, water and banks — where refusals, falls and exceeding the optimum time add penalties, and the show jumping phase is a round of 10–12 fences in an arena where knockdowns and refusals add penalties (FEI, n.d.-b). The lowest total of penalties across the three phases wins; at championships the order is dressage, cross-country, a veterinary inspection ("the trot-up"), then show jumping, and a second refusal or a fall on cross-country eliminates the pair (FEI, n.d.-b). Horses must meet the FEI minimum age (six years at national level, seven at the Olympic Games and world championships), wear approved protective equipment — body protectors and medical armbands are mandatory on cross-country — and riders wear helmets and body protectors (FEI, n.d.-b). At the Olympic Games and World Championships, teams of three or four riders per nation compete for team medals while all individuals are ranked in the same competition; men and women compete together in identical classes (FEI, n.d.-b).

History

Eventing descends from the all-round cavalry horse tests of the nineteenth century, in which officers' mounts were judged on dressage, endurance and jumping; the first three-day event was held in France in the 1890s, and eventing entered the Olympic program at Stockholm 1912 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FEI, founded in 1921, has run the Eventing World Championships since the 1960s, and the discipline has been part of every Olympic Games since 1912, with team and individual medals (FEI, n.d.-b). Icons include Germany's Michael Jung (born 1982), the most successful eventer of the modern era with multiple Olympic and world titles, and Britain's William Fox-Pitt, individual world champion and Olympic silver medallist; on the women's side, Britain's Pippa Funnell (2003 world champion) and New Zealand's Blyth Tait shaped the sport, and women have competed at the Olympics since 1952 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
