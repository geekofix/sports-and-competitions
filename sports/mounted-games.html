Mounted Games

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mounted games (gymkhana) are timed speed events and games played on horseback in an arena, combining racing patterns with tasks such as picking up objects, weaving through poles and handing over a baton. They are especially popular with children and young riders through Pony Clubs and 4-H clubs, and at the international level are governed by the International Mounted Games Association (IMGA), which runs an annual world championship. In the western United States the same type of competition is often called O-Mok-See.

Rules

A mounted games card consists of a series of timed games, each scored by the fastest clean run plus penalty seconds. Classic events include the relay race (teams of four riders handing over a baton), pole bending (weaving through six poles in a line), the keyhole race (a figure-eight pattern around a keyhole-shaped line), the keg race or "down and back" (around a barrel and back), stake racing and flag racing, plus mounting and dismounting at speed. Knocking over a pole, passing the wrong side of an obstacle or breaking the pattern brings penalties or elimination. The IMGA Rule Book governs international championships, where riders of all ages compete in age classes, and national associations such as the Mounted Games Association of Great Britain organize club shows.

History

Mounted games were devised at the request of Prince Philip, who in 1957 asked Col. Sir Mike Ansell, Director of the Horse of the Year Show, to create a competition for children who could not afford an expensive well-bred pony. The first Mounted Games Championship for the Prince Philip Cup was staged at the Horse of the Year Show at Harringay Arena in London in 1957 and was an immediate success. In 1984 Norman Patrick founded the Mounted Games Association of Great Britain to open the sport beyond Pony Club age limits, and in 2003 the International Mounted Games Association (IMGA) was founded, which now holds a world championship every year with riders from around the world.
