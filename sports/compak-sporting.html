Compak Sporting

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Compak sporting (also called "skeet universel" or universal trap) is a fast clay-target discipline governed by FITASC: from five stands arranged in a line, eight targets per stand are thrown — five simultaneously plus three on report — for 40 targets per round, with the shooter moving from stand to stand (FITASC, n.d.). It is the international cousin of sporting clays and part of the clay-target family.

Figure: 23IVAN. (2024-09-07 17:39:45). Compak sporting shooter on the line [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Compak_sporting_shooter.jpg

The course is compact — five traps in a straight line with a sixth optional trap behind, targets crossing at steep angles — which makes compak the fastest of the clay disciplines and a favourite for TV (FITASC, n.d.). A round is 40 targets (5 stands × 8); at World Championships shooters fire several rounds plus a final (FITASC, n.d.). Scoring is 1 point per broken target, 0 for dead (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Rules follow the FITASC sporting rules: 12-gauge shotguns only, maximum 24 g of shot (28 g in some national variants), no more than two shells loaded at a time, and the gun must be mounted before calling "pull" (FITASC, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate individual events plus mixed teams at world championships; junior and veteran categories exist (FITASC, n.d.).

History

Compak sporting was developed in France in the 1980s as a compact, spectator-friendly version of sporting, and FITASC — founded in 1921 as the international federation of clay-target shooting — adopted it as one of its five disciplines alongside universal trap, helice, parcours de chasse and combined (FITASC, n.d.). The 34th World Championship Finale Beretta World Cup was held in 2025, and the FITASC calendar lists more than 60 international competitions per year across five continents (FITASC, n.d.).
