Firefighter Games

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The "firefighter games" are competitive firefighting skills championships, best known as the Firefighter Combat Challenge (FCTC / Firefighter Challenge Championship Series), often called "the toughest two minutes in sports" (a phrase coined by ESPN). Firefighters in full gear race through a five-event obstacle course; internationally, the World Firefighter Games (organized under the International Sports of Firefighters Association) bring together brigades from dozens of countries.

Rules

In the Firefighter Combat Challenge, competitors wear full structural firefighting gear (helmet, coat, pants, gloves, SCBA — a 42 lb / 19 kg air pack) and complete five stations: a 6-story stair climb with a high-rise hose pack (another 42 lb), a 3-story hose hoist, forcible entry with a 160 lb (72 kg) Keiser Sled machine (5 ft / 1.5 m beam), a 140 ft (43 m) slalom around cones with a charged 1.75 in (45 mm) hose drag and water target, and a 100 ft (30 m) drag of a 175 lb (79 kg) rescue dummy. The clock runs from start to finish; the fastest times win, with penalties for rule violations. At the CFA (Competitive Firefighters Association) nationals and the Firefighter Challenge Championship Series, heats lead to semifinals and finals with age/gender categories. British equivalents (British Firefighter Challenge) use similar obstacle courses, and FireFit (Canada) runs its own challenge format.

History

The Firefighter Combat Challenge was created in 1991 by Dr. Bill Clark in the United States to measure real fireground fitness, inspired by the physical demands of the job; ESPN began broadcasting it and coined "the toughest two minutes in sports." It grew into an international circuit with events in the US, Canada, UK, Australia and the Middle East. The World Firefighter Games, held biennially since 1961 (founded by the International Sports of Firefighters Association), are a multi-sport event for professional and volunteer firefighters with track and field, swimming and firefighting disciplines. National bodies such as the CFA in the US and the British Firefighter Challenge run annual championships.
