Firefighter Games

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Firefighters Games are the biennial multi-sport event for professional and volunteer firefighters, organized by the World Firefighters Games Association (WFGA, based in Perth, Australia): athletes compete in more than 50 sports and disciplines, from track and field and swimming to the fire-service-specific competitions such as the "Toughest Firefighter Alive" (CTIF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.; WFGA, n.d.). The first Games were held in Auckland, New Zealand, from 22 to 29 April 1990 with about 1,800 athletes from 17 countries; the largest edition was Chungju, South Korea, in 2018 with roughly 6,600 athletes (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 16th edition was scheduled for Al-Khobar, Saudi Arabia, in November 2026 but was rescheduled to November 2027 (WFGA, n.d.). Parallel to the Games, the Comité Technique International de prévention et d'extinction du Feu (CTIF) runs the international firefighting sport championships of the Czech-style "fire sport" (Požární sport), which includes the 100 m obstacle course, the 4 × 100 m relay, tower climbing and the fire attack (CTIF, n.d.).

On the women's side, the Games and the CTIF competitions include full women's categories in all fire-service disciplines, and national teams such as the Czech and German women's fire-attack teams are among the leading sides in international fire sport; in 2023 more than 450 fire-sport competitions were held in the Czech Republic alone, with categories from preschool children to seniors over 35 (CTIF, n.d.).

Rules

The World Firefighters Games comprise more than 50 sports — athletics, swimming, cycling, Weightlifting, Wrestling, Judo, team sports and many more — plus the fire-service events, each governed by the rules of the respective international federation, with age groups from under-30 to 60+ in five-year steps (WFGA, n.d.). The flagship fire-service competition, "Toughest Firefighter Alive", combines wearing the full breathing apparatus set with a hose run, an obstacle course, a tower or ladder climb and a stair climb of 100–200 m against the clock (WFGA, n.d.). In CTIF fire sport, teams of seven (men's or women's) contest the fire attack: from a water source the team lays B-hoses (65–75 mm) and C-hoses (38–52 mm), and two jet targets wired to an electronic chronometer must be knocked down; the clock stops when both targets fall (CTIF, n.d.). The individual disciplines are the 100 m obstacle course (climbing a 2 m wall, carrying two 20 kg hose bundles across a beam, coupling hoses and laying a line), the 4 × 100 m hose relay, and tower climbing to the fourth floor of a training tower with a hose (CTIF, n.d.). Penalties add seconds for false starts, dropped equipment or rule infringements; men and women compete in identical disciplines with separate classifications, and age classes run from preschool to seniors 35+ and up (CTIF, n.d.). Competitors wear standard fire-service personal protective equipment, and medical checks are mandatory before the Games (WFGA, n.d.).

History

The idea of international firefighter competitions grew out of national fire-sport traditions, above all the Czech Požární sport, whose first competition was held in the USSR in 1937 and whose first Czechoslovak nationwide championship took place in 1970; the sport became institutionalized in Czechoslovakia and later the Czech Republic, where hundreds of competitions are held every year (CTIF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The CTIF, founded in 1929 in Paris as an international association of fire services, began organizing international firefighting sport championships in the 1960s and still runs the world championships of fire sport today (CTIF, n.d.). The World Firefighters Games were first held in Auckland, New Zealand, in April 1990 and have since been staged every two years — including Las Vegas 1992, Helsinki 1997, Lahti 2009 and Chungju 2018 — becoming the largest gathering of firefighters worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.). Women's participation has grown steadily since the 1990s, with women's teams now competing in all fire-service disciplines at both the Games and the CTIF championships (CTIF, n.d.).
