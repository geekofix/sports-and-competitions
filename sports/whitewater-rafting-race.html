Whitewater Rafting Race

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Whitewater rafting race is competitive rafting down river rapids in crewed inflatable rafts (typically R4/R6, four or six paddlers): teams race head-to-head or against the clock through slalom, sprint and downriver (RX) formats (IRF, n.d.). The International Rafting Federation, founded in 1997, runs the annual World Rafting Championships; in 2026 the IRF and the World Rafting Federation (WRF) merged into "United Rafting" as the single world governing body, with the 2026 Senior and Junior & U23 World Championships in Pau, France (IRF, 2026; World Rafting, 2026). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are Rafting, Canoe Slalom, Kayak Cross, Freestyle Kayak, Hydrospeed, Riverboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

IRF race categories classify events from World Championships (category A) down to national events; disciplines are sprint (short flat-water or rapid courses), slalom (gate lines on moving water) and RX (head-to-head downriver racing with obstacles). Rafts are classed by size (R2–R8+), must carry safety equipment (helmets, PFDs, throw lines), and teams are mixed or single-gender by category. Men's, women's and para-rafting categories are contested (IRF, n.d.; World Rafting, 2026).

History

Competitive raft racing began in the 1990s with the first IRF World Rafting Championships in 1997 on the Zambezi; the sport grew through commercial outfitters organising races, and the IRF gained IOC recognition. The 2026 unification of IRF and WRF created United Rafting, aiming at Olympic inclusion (IRF, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.).
