Powerchair Hockey

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Powerchair hockey (also called power hockey or electric wheelchair hockey) is a fast-paced team sport for athletes who use power wheelchairs, derived from ice hockey and floor hockey but played on a basketball court with a plastic ball instead of a puck (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Five players per side are on the court at any time — one center, one goaltender, two wingers and one defenseman (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The premier international event is the Powerchair Hockey World Cup: the 2026 edition was held 24 May – 1 June 2026 in Nastola, Finland, and was won by Switzerland, whose Jan Schläublin scored 56 goals; the Netherlands and Italy took silver and bronze (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Teams are mixed-gender at club level and both men's and women's national teams compete at the World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

All players must use a power wheelchair — manual wheelchairs and electric scooters are not permitted — and all must wear protective eyewear and a seat belt (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game is played on a regulation basketball court; goals are scored by hitting the plastic ball into the opponent's goal with a plastic stick mounted on the chair's footplate, and players with limited arm mobility may use a T-stick held in the mouth (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Each game consists of three 15-minute non-stop-time periods; the last two minutes of the third period are stop-time, and a tied game goes to a 5-minute golden-goal overtime (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Because goalies have limited mobility, the ball is not frozen: the referee whistles when the ball is under the goalie's chair and play restarts with a "keeperball" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Safety and conduct rules (e.g. CEWHA tournament regulations) govern contact, stick checks and unsportsmanlike conduct; men's, women's and mixed teams all compete under the same Laws of the Game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Power hockey began in the 1970s in public special schools in Germany and the Netherlands, where teachers adapted hockey for pupils with muscular dystrophy, cerebral palsy and similar disabilities — the sport uses only the maneuverability of the wheelchair, not gross motor power (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It gained public attention in the late 1980s, with the first World Games for power hockey held in Utrecht, Netherlands, in 1998, and a big international tournament in Minneapolis in 2001 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

World Cups followed in Helsinki 2004, Lignano 2010, Munich 2014, Lignano 2018 and Sursee 2022 (won by Denmark), before the 2026 edition in Nastola, Finland, won by Switzerland; European Championships are held between World Cups (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
