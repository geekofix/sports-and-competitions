Iaido

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Iaido is the Japanese martial art of drawing the katana, cutting the opponent, resheathing and finishing with a second cut, practiced as kata (forms) against imaginary opponents and judged on correctness, speed, power and spirit (kigime). It is governed internationally by the International Kendo Federation (FIK), which together with the All Japan Kendo Federation runs the biennial World Iaido Championships.

Rules

At FIK/AJKF events, competitors perform a set number of kata (typically 5-6 at World Championships) drawn from the ZNKR iaido kata set (12 kata, revised 2018, plus koryu kata in some events). Judges (shinpan) score each kata on the prescribed elements: nukitsuke (the draw-and-cut), kirioroshi, chiburi (blood-shaking), noto (resheathing) and zanshin (lingering awareness), plus correct etiquette (rei), distance (maai) and kiai. Events are individual and team (three per team). Iaido is practiced with a blunt iaito (or live shinken at dan examinations) in hakama and keikogi.

History

Iaido developed from samurai sword techniques and was systematized in the Edo period, notably by Hayashizaki Shigenobu, founder of Muso Jikiden Eishin-ryu in the 17th century. The All Japan Kendo Federation (ZNKR) standardized the modern kata set in 1969 (revised 2018), and iaido became one of the modern budo alongside kendo and jodo under the FIK, founded in 1970.
