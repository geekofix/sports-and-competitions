Bocce Ball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bocce ball is the modern, lightweight variant of bocce played with synthetic or resin balls instead of heavy wooden/metal ones, often on carpeted indoor courts, at parties, in restaurant venues and in recreational leagues. The rules follow classic bocce: get your balls closest to the pallino. In the United States the United States Bocce Federation (USBF) sanctions recreational and tournament play on various surfaces, including synthetic carpet.

Rules

The rules are the standard bocce rules with equipment and surface adaptations: eight balls (four per team) plus the pallino; the pallino is rolled or thrown into the court first, then teams alternate throwing until all balls are played; the team with the ball closest to the pallino scores one point per ball closer than the opponent's best. Synthetic balls are lighter and softer than regulation 107 mm wooden balls, so shots roll differently and courts may be shorter (backyard courts 50-60 ft, tournament courts about 76-87 ft long). Foul lines apply: players must not cross the throwing line when releasing the ball.

History

Classic bocce uses heavy balls (about 920 g) on groomed outdoor courts; lightweight "bocce ball" sets became popular from the late 20th century for indoor and party play, and venues such as restaurant-bocce chains run synthetic-carpet leagues in winter. The USBF codified "open rules" covering all surfaces — carpet, synthetic resin, crushed stone, clay, oyster shell — and sanctions bocce as part of the Special Olympics and Senior Olympics. Regulation tournament play still uses the 107 mm balls under CBI/WPBF rules.
