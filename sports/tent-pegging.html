Tent Pegging

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tent pegging is an equestrian sport in which a rider at full gallop picks up a small ground target (a peg) with a sword or lance; it is one of the ten disciplines of the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI), which recognized it in 2004 (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.). The international governing body, the International Tent Pegging Federation (ITPF), was founded as the World Tent Pegging Federation on 21 March 2013 at the World Tent Pegging Championship in Gurgaon, India, by representatives of 17 countries, renamed on 27 October 2013, and is headquartered in Muscat, Oman (International Tent Pegging Federation, n.d.). Its flagship event is the ITPF World Cup: the inaugural 2014 edition in Muscat was won by South Africa (758.5 points) ahead of Oman (693.5), and the 2023 edition in George, South Africa, by Saudi Arabia (581.5) ahead of Pakistan (570); the 2026 World Cup was held in Aqaba, Jordan (International Tent Pegging Federation, n.d.). Women riders compete alongside men at national and international events, particularly in Pakistan and India, where mixed teams are common (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Under ITPF and FEI rules, competitors ride a gallop course of roughly 120–130 m and must pick up a small wooden peg lying on the ground using a lance (minimum length about 2.2 m) or a sword, carrying it to the finish (International Tent Pegging Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Events are contested as individual sword, individual lance, pairs and teams, with scores combining time and penalties for missed or dropped pegs (International Tent Pegging Federation, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same events — mixed teams are standard in South Asian championships — and age groups range from juniors to seniors and masters (Wikipedia, n.d.). The official rule sources are the FEI tent pegging regulations and the ITPF competition rules (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.; International Tent Pegging Federation, n.d.).

History

Tent pegging's roots reach back to at least the 4th century BC on the Indian subcontinent, where cavalry trained by galloping at ground targets in drills against war elephants; it flourished in the Mughal era and was used by British colonial cavalry, while in Manipur the British weapons ban after the Anglo-Manipuri War of 1891 nearly suppressed the local arts (Wikipedia, n.d.). The modern sport was formalized in Pakistan and India in the 20th century, with international and Asian championships organized from 2010; at the World Tent Pegging Championship in Gurgaon in March 2013, 17 nations founded the World Tent Pegging Federation, later renamed ITPF with its seat in Muscat (International Tent Pegging Federation, n.d.). The FEI recognized tent pegging as a discipline in 2004, and the ITPF demonstrated it at the FEI World Equestrian Games 2014 in Normandy (Fédération Equestre Internationale, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
