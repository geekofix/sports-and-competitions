Four-Cross Mountain Biking

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Four-cross (4X) mountain biking is a timed gravity race in which four riders start simultaneously from a gate on a downhill course with jumps, berms and rollers, and the first two to cross the finish line advance (or the fastest times in some formats). It was part of the UCI Mountain Bike World Cup and World Championships and was an Olympic sport from Beijing 2008 to London 2012, governed by the UCI.

Rules

At UCI 4X events (historic format), four riders race head-to-head down a 300-600 m course with a start gate that drops automatically; the first two across the line in each race advance to the next round, and the final decides the placings (big final for 1st-4th, small final for 5th-8th). The course includes jumps, table-tops, berms and optional rollers; riders wear full-face helmets and full protection. Riders are seeded by timed qualifying runs, then paired into heats. Contact is allowed within reason but dangerous riding is penalized. The UCI ran 4X at World Cups and World Championships through the 2010s, with the last Olympic appearance at London 2012; the format has since been largely replaced by other gravity disciplines such as enduro in the modern calendar.

History

Four-cross developed from BMX-inspired mountain bike racing in the late 1990s and 2000s in Europe, notably France and the Czech Republic. It joined the UCI World Cup in the 2000s and became an Olympic sport at Beijing 2008, won by Maris Štrombergs of Latvia and Anne-Caroline Chausson of France; London 2012 was won by Julien Absalon and Anne-Caroline Chausson. After 2012 the Olympics dropped 4X, and the UCI phased it out of the World Cup in favor of other formats. The discipline remains popular in national and regional races, especially in France, the Czech Republic and Brazil.
