World Rally Championship

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Rally Championship (WRC) is the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile's premier international rally series, staged since 1973 across roughly thirteen three-day events on gravel, tarmac, snow and ice (World Rally Championship, n.d.). Each event pairs a driver with a co-driver who reads pace notes, and crews are scored on special stages — timed competitive sections linked by public-road liaison stages — with championship points for the top ten (25-17-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1) plus up to five Power Stage points (5-4-3-2-1) (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, n.d.). Women compete on equal terms as drivers and co-drivers in the WRC and its support categories, and the current leading drivers are Sébastien Ogier of France, who sealed a record-equaling ninth drivers' title at Rally Saudi Arabia on 29 November 2025 and became the oldest world champion in the process, 2024 champion Thierry Neuville of Belgium — the first Hyundai driver to take the drivers' title — and two-time champion Kalle Rovanperä of Finland, the youngest ever winner in 2022 (World Rally Championship, n.d.).

Rules

A WRC event runs over three days: a shakedown, then special stages with service parks between them, and a final-day Power Stage that awards bonus points (World Rally Championship, n.d.). Cars follow the Rally1 regulations — a 1.6-litre turbocharged engine with a hybrid boost used from 2022 to 2024, replaced from 2025 by a non-hybrid 1.6-litre unit running on 100% sustainable fuel, with Hankook as sole tyre supplier from 2025 to 2027 (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, n.d.). Titles are awarded for drivers, co-drivers, manufacturers and teams, alongside the WRC2, WRC3 and Junior WRC support championships, and women's and men's crews compete in identical machinery and point systems, with no separate classification (World Rally Championship, n.d.). Safety rules cover roll cages, homologated seats and fire systems, and crews must wear helmets and HANS devices on stages (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, n.d.).

History

The championship was inaugurated in 1973, with the Monte Carlo Rally as its opening round, and the official drivers' world title followed in 1979, won by Björn Waldegård of Sweden (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 1980s were revolutionised by Audi's quattro all-wheel-drive system, and the spectacular Group B era ended after the 1986 season, giving way to Group A; icons of the sport include Walter Röhrl, Ari Vatanen, Stig Blomqvist, Juha Kankkunen, Tommi Mäkinen, Colin McRae, Marcus Grönholm, and the two Sébastiens — Loeb, who won nine consecutive titles from 2004 to 2012, and Ogier, who matched that record in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.). On the women's side, Michèle Mouton of France remains the only woman to win world championship rounds, taking four victories between 1976 and 1982 and finishing runner-up in the 1982 drivers' title (Wikipedia, n.d.).
