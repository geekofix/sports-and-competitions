World Rally Championship

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Rally Championship (WRC) is the FIA's international rally racing series, contested over a season of rally events held on gravel, tarmac, snow and ice across the world, in which crews of a driver and co-driver race against the clock over special stages. Points from each rally count toward drivers' and manufacturers' championships, with Rally1 hybrid top-class cars at the head of the field.

Rallies run over several days with stages on closed public roads linked by road sections, and the combination of surfaces, weather and endurance makes the WRC one of motorsport's most varied championships, tracing its lineage back to the earliest intercity road rallies.

Rules

Each rally consists of timed special stages on closed roads (gravel, asphalt, snow or ice) plus untimed liaison sections between them; crews start stages at intervals and their stage times accumulate to a total, with the lowest total winning after penalties. Points follow the FIA scale for the top finishers of each rally toward season-long titles, with a power stage bonus points format rewarding the fastest on the closing stage, and regulations govern car specification by class (Rally1 hybrid and support categories), tyre allocations, reconnaissance and servicing restrictions for parc ferme rules penalties on repairs.

History

International rallying grew from European long-distance road events of the early twentieth century, with the modern world championship starting in 1973 as a unified series over iconic events like Monte Carlo, Sweden, Finland and Great Britain's RAC Rally — with Audi's quattro four-wheel drive revolutionising the sport in the 1980s and the Group B era leaving legends that ended in 1986. Championship history counts stars such as Sébastien Loeb and Sébastien Ogier, dominating the 2000s with nine and eight titles respectively. The modern era runs Rally1 hybrid cars with sustainable fuel requirements after the 2022 rules cycle, keeping the series at the technical forefront of production-based motorsport.
