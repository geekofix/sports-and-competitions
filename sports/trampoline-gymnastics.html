Trampoline Gymnastics

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Trampoline gymnastics is the Olympic discipline governed by the Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique (FIG) in which athletes bounce on a competition trampoline up to about 10 m high and perform routines of ten consecutive acrobatic elements — somersaults and twists in prescribed shapes — between an upright takeoff and landing (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.). Individual events for men and women have been contested at every Olympic Games since Sydney 2000, and the FIG World Championships programme also includes synchronized trampoline, team events and the companion disciplines double mini-trampoline and tumbling (Olympics, n.d.). At the top of the sport today, the leading women are China's Hu Yicheng, who won the 2025 world individual title in Pamplona, and Britain's Bryony Page, the Paris 2024 Olympic champion, while among the men Wang Zisai took the 2025 world title and Ivan Litvinovich won Olympic gold at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 — the first man to defend an Olympic trampoline title (Wikipedia, n.d.; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.).

Rules

The competition trampoline has a bed of about 4.28 m × 2.14 m, suspended about 1.15 m above the floor by springs inside a 5.05 m × 3.05 m steel frame with padded decks (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.). An individual routine consists of ten elements performed without pause; each element carries a declared difficulty value that builds the difficulty score, while a panel of execution judges deducts for faults such as bent knees, leg separation, poor shape, travel toward the frame and unstable landings, and at Olympic and elite international events an electronic time of flight is added as a third scoring component (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.). Routines must begin and end upright on the feet — landings on the feet, seat, front or back are allowed mid-routine — and a routine is interrupted if the gymnast touches the frame, the safety platform or a spotter (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.). Qualification consists of two routines with the best eight advancing to a one-routine final; synchronized trampoline pairs perform the same routine simultaneously and are judged additionally on synchronization, and team events aggregate the scores of the team's gymnasts (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.). World Cup series results and the FIG world rankings allocate quota places to the Olympic Games, and age-group categories run alongside the senior events (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.). Men and women compete in identical individual events on the same apparatus under the same rules — the only Olympic gymnastics discipline where both genders share one apparatus final format (Olympics, n.d.).

History

Competitive trampolining organized internationally in the postwar decades: the International Trampoline Federation (FIT) was founded in 1964 and staged the first Trampoline World Championships at the Royal Albert Hall in London on 21 March 1964, won by the Americans Dan Millman (men) and Judy Wills Cline (women), with Wills defending her title seven further times for a record eight world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.). European Championships followed from 1969, double mini-trampoline joined the world programme in 1976, and the FIT became a member of the FIG on 1 January 1999, bringing trampoline, double mini-trampoline and tumbling under one federation (Wikipedia, n.d.). Trampoline gymnastics made its Olympic debut at Sydney 2000 with individual titles for men and women, won by Russia's Irina Karavaeva and Alexander Moskalenko (Wikipedia, n.d.; Olympics, n.d.). Retired icons of the discipline include Canada's Rosie MacLennan, Olympic champion at London 2012 and Rio 2016 and world champion in 2013 and 2015, China's Dong Dong, Olympic champion at Beijing 2008 and London 2012, and Belarus's Uladzislau Hancharou, Olympic champion at Rio 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2025 Trampoline Gymnastics World Championships in Pamplona (5–9 November 2025) crowned Hu Yicheng and Wang Zisai of China as the women's and men's individual world champions (Wikipedia, n.d.).
