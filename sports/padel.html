Padel

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Padel is a racket sport that combines elements of tennis and squash, played almost always in doubles on a small enclosed court of 20 by 10 meters whose glass and mesh walls are part of the game (International Padel Federation, n.d.-a). It was invented in 1969 by the Mexican businessman Enrique Corcuera at his home Las Brisas in Acapulco — originally called "Paddle Corcuera" — after he walled in a space too small for a tennis court (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Padel Federation (FIP), founded in Madrid on 12 July 1991, governs the sport, and the Padel World Championship has been held every two years since 1992; men's and women's doubles are both played at the top level, with women's padel growing fastest in Spain and Latin America (International Padel Federation, n.d.-b). According to the FIP, more than 35 million people play padel in over 150 countries, and the sport is targeting Olympic inclusion at Brisbane 2032 (International Padel Federation, n.d.-a).

Rules

The court measures 20 by 10 meters with a net across the middle, and is enclosed by walls about 3 meters high in glass and 4 meters at the ends in glass plus mesh; players use a solid, stringless, perforated racket and a tennis-like ball (International Padel Federation, n.d.-a). The serve is underhand, struck at or below waist level into the diagonally opposite service box (International Padel Federation, n.d.-a). Scoring is identical to tennis — 15, 30, 40, game; six games win a set, with a tiebreak at 6-6, and matches are best of three sets (International Padel Federation, n.d.-a). The distinctive rule is the use of the walls: after the serve, the ball may bounce off the side or back walls and remain in play, and a player may also play the ball after it bounces off their own back wall (the "out" rule of squash does not apply) (International Padel Federation, n.d.-a). Games are always played in doubles (two per side) at FIP events; men's and women's events use identical courts, rackets and rules (International Padel Federation, n.d.-b).

History

Padel was invented in 1969 by Enrique Corcuera in Acapulco, Mexico, who built walls around his tennis court and played with wooden paddles; the first official court was built in 1974, and the sport spread to Spain and Argentina in the 1970s, where it exploded in popularity (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Padel Federation was founded in Madrid on 12 July 1991, and the first Padel World Championship was held in Madrid in 1992, won by the Spanish-Argentine pair (International Padel Federation, n.d.-b). Professional circuits grew from the 1990s: the World Padel Tour (WPT, founded 2013) and Premier Padel (founded 2022, backed by the International Tennis Federation's commercial arm) merged into a unified professional structure in 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Stars of the modern era include the Spaniards Alejandro Galán and Juan Lebrón on the men's side and the world No. 1 women's pairs from Spain and Argentina (International Padel Federation, n.d.-b).
