Ice Speedway

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ice speedway (ice racing) is motorcycle speedway racing on frozen ovals: four riders per heat race counter-clockwise over four laps (about 60 seconds) on ice tracks 260–425 m long, scoring points 3-2-1-0, with roughly 24 heats per championship round (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme [FIM], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIM Individual Ice Racing World Championship has been held since 1966, the U-21 title since 2009 and the Team (Nations) championship since 1979; in 2026 qualifying was held in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden (31 January), with finals in Inzell, Germany (14–15 March, two rounds) and at Thialf in Heerenveen, Netherlands (11 April) — the 2026 individual world champion was Niclas Svensson of Sweden, who won all six finals races at Heerenveen, while the defending champion Martin Haarahiltunen of Finland was injured at Inzell; a women's category has been added in recent FIM seasons (FIM, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

Rules

Ice speedway machines are 500 cc single-cylinder methanol-fuelled motorcycles with no brakes, a two-speed gearbox, a longer wheelbase and a more rigid frame than dirt speedway bikes; a rev limiter is compulsory for 500 cc four-valve engines (hard limit 13,500 rpm), and the minimum angle of inclination of the machine is regulated at 68° (FIM, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ice must be at least 20 cm thick; in the studded class — mandatory since 1982 — spikes up to 3 cm long are screwed into the treadless tyres, about 130–140 in the front and 170–190 in the rear, and special wheel guards extend almost to the ice surface (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Because the spikes give enormous grip, there is no broadsiding: riders lean the bike into corners until the handlebars nearly skim the ice, reaching about 130 km/h on the straights and about 100 km/h in the corners; barriers of straw bales or banked snow protect the track edges (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There are two classes — studded tyres and full rubber — and heats of four riders over four laps award 3-2-1-0 points; men and women race under identical rules and formats in separate categories (FIM, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Ice speedway is generally credited to Staten Lorenz of Michigan, who in 1975 organized the first event in Huntsville, Alabama; the third event was held at Kent State University's hockey field house, where speedway rider Gary Densford — invited by his friend Bob Hetrick, who won that event — went on to promote the sport and found ICE (International Championship Events) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Studded tyres became mandatory in 1982: after the single-track "Ice Getter" came the dual-track "Kold Kutter", and in 1991 ICE mandated the "Silver Rockets" studs, which offered the best grip with the least ice wear (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIM's individual world championship, running since 1966, was long dominated by the Soviet Union and later Russia; the sport's icons include Sweden's Per-Olof Serenius, with 22 Swedish championship titles, and Russia's Nikolai Krasnikov, seven-time world champion from 2005 to 2011 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
