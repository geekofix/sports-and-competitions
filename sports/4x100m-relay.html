4x100m Relay

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 4x100 metres relay is the sprint relay in which four runners each cover 100 m in lanes, passing a baton inside the changeover zone; it is the final track event of most championship programmes and is governed by World Athletics (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 36.84 s for men, set by Jamaica (Nesta Carter, Michael Frater, Yohan Blake, Usain Bolt) at the London 2012 Olympics, and 40.82 s for women, set by the USA (Tianna Madison, Allyson Felix — pictured, Bianca Knight, Carmelita Jeter) at the same Games — both also the Olympic records (World Athletics, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women's teams included the USA and Jamaica, while the men's event was headed by Jamaica, the USA and Japan (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the relay family — 4x400m Relay, 100m Sprint and 200m Sprint — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

Each of the four runners covers 100 m in the team's lane; the first leg starts from the same stagger as the individual 400 m and uses a crouch start in blocks, the other legs start from a standing start within the changeover zone (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since the rule change of November 2017 the 4x100 m changeover zone is 30 m long (the former 20 m box plus the 10 m acceleration zone); the baton must be exchanged within the zone — dropping the baton is not a disqualification if the runner who dropped it picks it up — while a false start, an out-of-zone exchange or impeding another team disqualifies the whole team (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The finish is judged by the torso of the fourth runner; photo finish and electronic timing apply, and the wind limit of +2.0 m/s applies to record ratification (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete separately, with a mixed 4x100 m relay at the World Athletics Relays, and U20 categories at age-group championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

History

Relay racing became popular at the end of the 19th century, and the men's 4x100 m has been on the Olympic programme since Stockholm 1912, the women's since Amsterdam 1928 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The USA men won eight consecutive Olympic titles from 1920 to 1956 but suffered a notorious string of disqualifications from 2008 to 2024; Jamaica, powered by Usain Bolt's anchor legs (pictured at Beijing 2008), won three consecutive Olympic golds from 2008 to 2016 and set the 36.84 world record in London 2012 (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). The World Athletics Relays, a relay-only world championship first held in 2014, continued in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-b).
