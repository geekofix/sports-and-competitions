MotoGP

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

MotoGP is the premier class of Grand Prix motorcycle road racing and the pinnacle of the FIM motorcycle World Championship, which has crowned world champions since 1949. Riders compete on 1000cc four-stroke prototype machines that exceed 360 km/h on the fastest circuits. The championship consists of three classes — MotoGP, Moto2 and Moto3 — racing at around 20 Grands Prix per season. The FIM sets the technical and sporting regulations, while Dorna Sports administers the championship and holds the commercial rights (acquired by Liberty Media in 2024/25).

Rules

In the MotoGP class, manufacturers build prototype 1000cc four-stroke machines within FIM technical regulations covering engine configuration, electronics, tires and fuel; top speeds exceed 360 km/h. A Grand Prix weekend comprises free practice, qualifying (with a Q1/Q2 shootout setting the grid) and a sprint race on Saturday plus the main race on Sunday (since 2023), roughly 40-45 minutes and 20-27 laps depending on the circuit, with points for the top 15 in both. The rider with the most championship points at season's end becomes world champion. The race distance, tire allocation and flag rules follow the FIM Grand Prix regulations, and the same weekend also hosts Moto2 and Moto3 races.

History

The FIM Road Racing World Championship began in 1949 with classes of 125cc, 250cc, 350cc, 500cc and 600cc sidecars, the first Grand Prix being held at the Isle of Man. For more than five decades the 500cc class was the premier category, first with four-strokes and then, from the 1970s, with two-stroke machines. In 2002 the modern MotoGP era began as the premier class was rebranded and reopened to four-stroke prototypes up to 990cc; the limit was reduced to 800cc in 2007 and raised to the current 1000cc in 2012. Giacomo Agostini remains the most successful rider with 15 world titles, Valentino Rossi won five consecutive premier-class titles from 2002 to 2005, and Marc Marquez has been the dominant force of the 2010s. In 2012 Moto3 replaced the 125cc class and in 2010 Moto2 replaced 250cc.
