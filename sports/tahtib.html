Tahtib

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tahtib (tahteeb) is the traditional stick game of Upper Egypt's Saeedy communities: two performers wield long wooden canes in a brief, controlled, non-violent interchange with folk music, inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016 (UNESCO, n.d.). Modern competitive tahtib is governed by the Egyptian Tahtib Federation (founded by Adel Paul Boulad, who formalized 12 standard forms), with world championships and an annual festival in Luxor (Traditionalsports, n.d.). Women's participation is growing in youth clubs, though traditionally male (UNESCO, n.d.). Within the combat sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Nguni Stick Fighting, Eskrima, Fencing, Kendo, [Baton Twirling](baton twirling.html), [Canne De Combat](canne de combat.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In competitive tahtib, two athletes face off and exchange controlled stick strikes and parries — no direct body contact is allowed; bouts are judged on technique, control and presentation, in rounds with elimination or points. The ash (cane) is about 1.2 m of flexible wood. Men's and women's categories exist at federation events (UNESCO, n.d.; Traditionalsports, n.d.).

History

Tahtib's roots reach the Old Kingdom of Egypt (2,700–2,200 BC temple reliefs); it evolved from warrior drill and cattle-herding stick use into a festive performance. Adel Paul Boulad drove the 2016 UNESCO inscription and the modern sport's 12 kata-like forms (UNESCO, n.d.; Arab Weekly, 2021).
