Blind Tennis

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Blind tennis — also called blind and low-vision (BLV) tennis or sound tennis — is tennis adapted for players who are blind or have a vision impairment. It is played with a larger ball that contains bearings so it makes noise, on a smaller court with tactile lines for the lowest vision class, and with audible calls before every serve. The International Blind Tennis Association (International Blind Tennis Association [IBTA], n.d.-a) runs the world championships; in September 2025 the IBTA signed a memorandum of understanding with the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA), and in October 2025 with the International Tennis Federation (International Tennis Federation [ITF], n.d.-a), after which IBSA formed a Blind Tennis Committee in August 2026 to oversee global governance. The sport is not on the Paralympic programme (International Blind Tennis Association, n.d.-a) (International Blind Tennis Association, n.d.-b).

At the 2026 IBTA World Championships at the SEB Arena in Vilnius, Lithuania (August 24–29, 2026), more than 120 athletes from over 20 countries played about 320 matches across the B1–B4 classes, and Australia defended its team title; the first world women's BLV tennis tournament followed in Benidorm, Spain (September 3–5, 2026) (IBTA, n.d.-a). On the women's side, national federations such as the United States Blind Tennis Association (founded 2022) are building the first permanently lined blind tennis courts, and the 2026 Benidorm event marked the first standalone world women's championship (IBTA, n.d.-b).

Rules

BLV tennis keeps normal tennis scoring, though sets may be shortened or timed; before serving, the server calls "ready", waits for the "yes" reply, then calls "play" and serves (IBTA, n.d.-a). The ball is larger than a tennis ball with bearings inside, usually black or fluorescent yellow; racquets have a shorter handle; and indoor courts are brightly lit. Rules are adapted to the vision class: B1 players wear a blindfold, play on a smaller court with tactile lines they may touch, over a lower net, and may let the ball bounce three times before returning it; B2 players also get three bounces; B3 two bounces; B4 and B5 one bounce (IBTA, n.d.-a). Singles and doubles are both played; men's and women's draws are separate at world championships, with identical rules for both genders and separate junior categories in national federations (IBTA, n.d.-b).

History

Blind tennis was created in 1984 in Japan by Miyoshi Takei (c. 1942–2011), who went on to win the Japanese national championship sixteen times in its first 21 years; the sport now has IBTA member countries across Africa, the Americas, Asia, Europe and Oceania. The International Blind Tennis Association was founded in 2014 and has organized world championships since, with Australia dominating the team event, notably at the IBSA World Games in Birmingham 2023, where BLV tennis was one of ten sports (IBTA, n.d.-a). In 2023, blind tennis was also showcased in exhibition matches at the Australian Open to raise its profile. The 2025 MoUs with IBSA and the ITF marked the sport's integration into the international sports movement, culminating in the IBSA Blind Tennis Committee formed in August 2026 (ITF, n.d.-a).
