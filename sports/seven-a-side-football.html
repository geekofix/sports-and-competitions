Seven-a-side Football

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

7-a-side football is small-sided football played with seven players per team on a reduced pitch, organized internationally by the International Federation of 7-a-side Football (IF7), which runs the IF7 World Cup, and in some countries by CP football (cerebral palsy football) bodies at the Paralympics, where 7-a-side has been a Paralympic sport since 1984 (as CP football, now football 7-a-side since Rio 2016). Youth and amateur 7s leagues exist worldwide.

Rules

At IF7 World Cup level, teams field seven players (including a goalkeeper) on a pitch of about 50-60 m x 30-40 m with proportionally smaller goals. Matches are two halves of 20-25 minutes (or 30 in some formats). The offside rule is usually adapted (offside line at halfway or in a designated zone), and kick-ins replace throw-ins in many 7s rule sets. Rolling substitutions are allowed. Free kicks, penalties, corners and goal kicks follow football laws with adaptations for the smaller field. At the Paralympics, football 7-a-side for athletes with cerebral palsy uses a 75 m x 56 m pitch (max) with 7 players, two 30-minute halves, no offside, and rolling substitutions; the goal is 5.5 m x 2.2 m.

History

7-a-side football grew from informal street and youth football in the late 20th century; the IF7 was founded in 2014 in Brazil and has held World Cups since 2015 (editions in Guatemala, Curitiba and Mexico, most recently 2023). Paralympic 7-a-side (originally for cerebral palsy footballers) debuted at Arnhem 1980? — it has been on the Paralympic program since 1984 (New York/Stoke Mandeville) and was rebranded "football 7-a-side" at Rio 2016. Ukraine won the Paralympic gold in 2016 and 2020 (defeating Brazil), and Brazil dominated earlier editions. Amateur 7s is hugely popular in Brazil, Japan and across Asia.
