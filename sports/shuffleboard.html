Shuffleboard

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Shuffleboard — in its competitive form, deck shuffleboard — is a precision game in which players slide weighted discs with a cue along a narrow court toward a triangular scoring area, aiming for the 10-point zone; it is governed internationally by the International Shuffleboard Association (ISA, founded in 1979 in St. Petersburg, Florida) and in the United States by the National Shuffleboard Association, and the 42nd ISA World Championship was held in Hohenroda, Germany, in 2025, won by Jessica Cegiela of Germany in the women's singles and Mark Malatt of the USA in the men's singles (ISA, n.d.; NSA, n.d.). The game descends from Tudor-era English pub games — shove ha'penny and shovillabourde, which Henry VIII played — that Dutch settlers brought to New York, and deck shuffleboard was laid out ashore at the Lyndhurst Hotel in Daytona Beach, Florida, in 1913 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Women have competed at the highest level since the early championships and the ISA runs separate women's and men's singles and doubles at its world championships; the current women's singles world champion is Jessica Cegiela of Germany (Hohenroda 2025, ahead of the USA's Crystal Zorich in the final), while the men's title went to Mark Malatt of the USA, and both genders play under identical rules (ISA, n.d.).

Rules

The court is 52 ft × 6 ft (about 15.85 × 1.83 m) with a 6 ft 6 in shooting area at each end and 39 ft between the baselines; the scoring triangle at each end is divided into zones worth 10, 8, 8, 7 and 7 points, with a small "10-off" area at the far tip beyond the baseline where a disc scores minus 10, and a disc must lie fully inside a zone to score — touching a line counts as zero (Wikipedia, n.d.; NSA, n.d.). Each player shoots eight discs per frame (four yellow, four black) in alternating shots; the cue may be up to 6 ft 3 in (1.90 m) long, may not have a metal tip on the court, and the last shot of the frame — the "hammer" — alternates between players (Wikipedia, n.d.). A game is played to 75 points (the frame must be completed) or as a fixed number of frames (8, 12 or 16), in singles or in doubles with two players per team, one shooting from each end; the discs are 6 in (15.2 cm) in diameter, 9/16 to 1 in thick and weigh about 15 oz (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate singles and doubles divisions but under the same rules and on the same courts (ISA, n.d.). A separate parlour version, table shuffleboard, is played on 9–22 ft tables with hand-slid pucks (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

Shuffleboard's ancestors are the Tudor-era English games shovillabourde and shove-groat — Henry VIII was an enthusiastic player — while shove-groat was banned by the 1541 Unlawful Games Act and shove ha'penny survived into the modern era (Wikipedia, n.d.). Dutch immigrants carried the game to New York in the 17th century, and in 1913 deck shuffleboard was laid out ashore at the Lyndhurst Hotel in Daytona Beach, Florida, by Robert Ball, from where it spread with the winter tourist trade to resorts across the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.). The St. Petersburg Shuffleboard Club was founded in 1924 and by 1939 the city boasted 116 courts at the height of the boom; the National Shuffleboard Association and, in 1979 in St. Petersburg, the International Shuffleboard Association were founded, and ISA world championships have been held annually since, including Hohenroda 2010 and 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.; ISA, n.d.; NSA, n.d.).
