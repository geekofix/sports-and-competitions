Briscola

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Briscola is the most popular Italian trick-taking game with a 40-card Italian/Spanish deck: a trump suit (briscola) ranks A,3,K,Q,J,7,6,5,4,2 ((points 11, 10, 4, 3, 2); capture tricks score points; played by 2–4 players. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Scopa, Truco, Belote; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Pagat.com, n.d.).

Figure: Boldrinus. (2022-09-17). Briscola card hierarchy [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gerarchia_carte_Briscola.jpg

Rules

40-card deck (Italian or Spanish suits); in classic briscola one card turns up as trump; players follow suit; highest trump or highest of led suit wins; card points: A=11, 3=10, K=4, Q=3, J=2; game to 120 points (61 to win) (Pagat.com, n.d.).

History

Briscola's name and rules are documented in Italy from the 18th century; paintings by Cammarano (1886) and Chierici (1897) show street games in Trastevere and Naples; the card hierarchy photo documents the deck (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
