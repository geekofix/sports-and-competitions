Ice Cross Downhill

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Ice cross downhill (commercially known as Red Bull Crashed Ice) is a downhill skating race in which up to four skaters race simultaneously down an icy track with jumps, banked turns and rollers, wearing full ice hockey protective gear. It was governed by the ATSX, which ran the Ice Cross Downhill World Championship from 2010 to 2019; the sport continues under new organizers as the Ice Cross world championship.

Rules

In the classic ATSX/Red Bull format, four riders per heat race down a course of about 300-600 m with a start gate, jumps, banked corners and rollers; the fastest two advance to the next round, and the final decides the podium. Riders wear full hockey gear (helmet with visor, chest and shoulder pads, elbow and shin guards, gloves) and modified skates, and timing is electronic at the finish. The course is built of ice on wooden or metal structures, and falls are common but not penalized unless a rider impedes others.

History

Ice cross downhill began in Quebec City in the early 2000s; the ATSX World Championship ran from 2010 to 2019 with finals in Quebec City and events in Belfast, Munich and St. Paul, Minnesota. Red Bull ended the Crashed Ice series after 2019, and the discipline continues under new organizers with world championships in Finland and elsewhere. Top riders come from Canada, the Czech Republic, Austria and Finland.
