Lizard Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lizard racing is a novelty "sport" in which lizards are placed in the center of a ring or at the start of a track and the first one to reach the edge or finish line wins. It is not an organized sport with a federation, but informal and festival-style races are held in several places — most famously the annual races in Eulo, Queensland, Australia (the Paroo Lizard Race Track, drawing thousands of visitors each year), and in Doyle, a small town in Lassen County, California, where races began as a local drinking game before becoming a family-friendly festival attraction.

Rules

There are no formal rules: lizards — typically small, fast local species such as blue-belly lizards in California — are placed in the center of a marked circle or at a starting gate, and the first to cross the boundary wins the heat; winners advance through heats to a final. Because the outcome depends on the lizards' mood and direction, races are as much entertainment as competition, and organizers use gentle handling to keep the animals safe. Spectators bet informally or simply cheer, and events are usually held alongside larger country fairs and festivals.

History

Racing small lizards is a quirky tradition in several rural communities. In Eulo, in outback Queensland, the Paroo Lizard Race Track has run races for decades and draws around 5,000 visitors a year to a town of about 70 people. In Doyle, California, the annual lizard races began in the mid-20th century as a drinking game in which locals raced western fence lizards in the street, before wildlife officials helped formalize the event with a proper track and family-friendly rules. Similar races have been reported in pubs and at fairs in the UK and elsewhere, always as lighthearted novelty entertainment rather than a recognized sport.
