Para-Equestrian Dressage

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para equestrian dressage is the Paralympic version of dressage, governed by the International Federation for Equestrian Sports (FEI), which brought para equestrian under its umbrella in 2006 as the first international federation to govern a sport for both able-bodied and disabled athletes (FEI, n.d.-a). Riders with physical or visual impairments are divided into grades according to their functional ability and ride prescribed dressage tests from memory, plus a freestyle test choreographed to music (FEI, n.d.-b). It has been on the Paralympic program since the Atlanta 1996 Games and is the only equestrian discipline at the Paralympics; roughly two-thirds of para dressage riders are women, and men's and women's events share the same tests within each grade (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). Related disciplines are show jumping and polo.

Rules

Riders are classified into grades — currently Ia, Ib, II, III and IV (with V planned) — by FEI classifiers, based on mobility, strength, coordination and vision, and each grade rides official FEI para dressage tests of increasing difficulty, from walk-only tests up to tests with canter, half-pirouettes and lateral work (FEI, n.d.-b). Most grades compete in a 40 by 20 m arena, while the highest grade uses the full 60 by 20 m arena (FEI, n.d.-b). In the freestyle, riders perform a choreographed routine to music within a time limit, judged on technical execution and artistic impression (FEI, n.d.-b). At the Paralympics each rider competes in the individual championship test and the freestyle for their grade; medals are awarded in individual and team events (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). Men and women compete together in the same grade tests — there are no separate women's tests — and the horse must be suitable for the rider's grade under FEI horse-welfare rules (FEI, n.d.-b).

History

Horse riding for people with disabilities has roots in therapeutic riding programs of the 1940s-1950s, notably in Britain and Scandinavia, and dressage was added to the Paralympic program at Atlanta 1996 as the only equestrian discipline (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). The FEI took over para equestrian in 2006, making equestrian the first sport governed jointly for able-bodied and para athletes at world level (FEI, n.d.-a). Pioneering riders include Britain's Lee Pearson, who won ten Paralympic gold medals between 2000 and 2024 in grade II, and Australia's Rosalie Fahey, a multiple national champion who competed at the Paralympics (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FEI Para Dressage World Championships and the Paralympic Games now form the top of the sport (FEI, n.d.-b).
