Para-Equestrian Dressage

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Para equestrian dressage is the Paralympic version of dressage, governed by the International Federation for Equestrian Sports (FEI), which brought para equestrian under its umbrella in 2006 as the first international federation to govern a sport for both able-bodied and disabled athletes. Riders with physical or visual impairments are divided into grades according to their functional ability and ride prescribed dressage tests from memory, plus a freestyle test choreographed to music. It has been on the Paralympic program since the Atlanta 1996 Games and is the only equestrian discipline at the Paralympics; related disciplines are show jumping and polo.

Rules

Riders are classified into grades (currently Ia, Ib, II, III and IV) by FEI classifiers, based on mobility, strength and coordination, and each grade rides official FEI para dressage tests of increasing difficulty, from walk-only tests up to tests with canter, half-pirouettes and lateral work; most grades compete in a 40 by 20 m arena, while the highest grade uses the full 60 by 20 m arena. In the freestyle, riders perform a choreographed routine to music judged on the same scale as able-bodied dressage. All class events are mixed gender, and in Paralympic team competitions each team of three must include at least one rider of grade Ia, Ib, II or III. Riders may use formally approved compensating aids, such as special reins or loops, and must wear the equipment stipulated in the FEI rules.

History

Para equestrian dressage was added to the Paralympic program at Atlanta 1996, and the FEI took over governance in 2006, previously handled by the International Paralympic Equestrian Committee. Riders with vision impairments became eligible from 2012, and para driving followed as a second FEI-sanctioned discipline. The Belgian rider Michèle George is one of the most successful para dressage athletes, winning seven Paralympic gold medals between 2012 and 2024 in the individual championship and freestyle tests.
