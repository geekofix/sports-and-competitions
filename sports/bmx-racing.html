BMX Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

BMX racing is bicycle motocross racing on a dirt track with jumps, berms and a starting gate: eight riders race head-to-head over one lap of about 350-400 m, and the first across the finish line wins. It is governed by the UCI and has been an Olympic sport since Beijing 2008.

Rules

Under UCI rules, races start from a 5 m high starting gate with eight riders on a track about 8 m wide at the start, with pro-straights, jumps and banked turns; the lap is roughly 350-400 m. The first four to eight riders across the line score points (Olympic and World Championship formats use motos and a final). Riders wear full-face helmets, and the bike must be a 20-inch BMX with one brake. At the UCI BMX World Championships, men's and women's elite and junior classes are contested, plus the UCI BMX World Challenge for amateurs.

History

BMX racing began in the early 1970s in Southern California when kids imitated motocross on Schwinn bicycles; the first organized race was held in Long Beach in 1971, and the first national championship in the US followed in 1977. The sport spread worldwide in the 1980s-90s, the UCI took it over in 1995, and it became an Olympic medal sport at Beijing 2008. The Netherlands, Colombia, Australia and the US are traditional powers; Olympic champions include Mariana Pajón (COL).
