Track Cycling 1km Time Trial

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 1 km time trial is the classic men's track cycling event against the clock: a single rider starts from the gate and races 1,000 m — four laps on a standard 250 m velodrome — on an aerodynamic bike with clip-on handlebars, and electronic timing to the thousandth of a second decides the medals (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It has been a World Championship event since the first championships in 1893 and an Olympic event for men since Tokyo 1964; women contest the 500 m time trial instead (see the link below) (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

At the 123rd UCI Track Cycling World Championships on Chongming Island, Shanghai, 14–18 October 2026 — 22 events, with Great Britain topping the medal table — the Netherlands' Harrie Lavreysen (pictured celebrating a world title in Berlin) completed a hat-trick of team sprint, keirin and individual sprint and remains the dominant time trialist of his generation, with the 1 km world record just under 60 seconds; since 2025 men and women contest the same event lengths in the sprint disciplines (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Figure: Tim Rademacher. (2020, February 27). Harrie Lavreysen of the Netherlands celebrating a world title at the 2020 UCI Track World Championships in Berlin [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:UCI_Track_World_Championships_2020-02-27_210315.jpg

This is part of the track sprint and time-trial disciplines — Individual Pursuit, Individual Sprint, 500m Time Trial — within the cycling family (parent Cycling); the full list is under Important Links.

Rules

Under UCI Regulations Part 3, the time trial is ridden over 1 km for elite men and 500 m for elite women (shorter distances for junior and under-23 categories); each rider starts alone from the start gate, and at the Olympic Games every rider contests a single timed ride, while World Championships may run qualification and final rounds for large fields (UCI, n.d.-b).

The bike must meet the track equipment rules — fixed gear, no brakes, minimum weight 6.8 kg, drop or aerodynamic handlebars with clip-ons permitted for time trials — and a helmet is mandatory; men and women compete in separate classifications, and the event is part of the Olympic and World Championship programme alongside the 500 m women's event (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The 1 km time trial has crowned some of cycling's biggest names: Britain's Sir Chris Hoy, pictured, won Olympic gold in the event at Beijing 2008 as part of his six Olympic titles and set world records in the discipline; Belgium's Eddy Merckx set the hour record twice (1972 and 1984), and in the modern era the world record fell below one minute with ever more aerodynamic equipment (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

On the women's side the 500 m time trial has been dominated by Germany's Kristina Vogel, eleven-time world champion, whose world record stood around 32–33 seconds; the two events have been run at equal prestige since women's track cycling joined the Olympics at Seoul 1988 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).
