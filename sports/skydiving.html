Skydiving

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Skydiving is the sport of jumping from an aircraft, free-falling, and descending under a parachute; its competitive disciplines are governed internationally by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its International Skydiving Commission (ISC) (FAI, n.d.-b). The disciplines are formation skydiving (4-way and 8-way), artistic events (freestyle and freefly), speed skydiving, canopy piloting (accuracy, distance and speed under the opened parachute) and wingsuit flying (FAI, n.d.-b). The 2026 FAI World Championships were held 18–24 October 2026 at Skydive Arizona in Eloy, Arizona, with 256 competitors from 29 nations: France won 4-Way Open, 4-Way Female, Freefly and Freestyle, the United States won 8-Way Open, and Curt Bartholomew of the United States defended the canopy piloting overall title ahead of Cornelia Mihai of the United Arab Emirates and George Sheather of Australia (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Canopy piloting will also be staged at The World Games 2029 in Karlsruhe. Among the current leading women are Cornelia Mihai in canopy piloting and the members of France's dominant 4-Way Female team (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Competitive skydiving is run under the FAI Sporting Code, Section 5, administered by the ISC (FAI, n.d.-b). In 4-way and 8-way formation skydiving, teams exit at about 12,500 ft and have a working time of 35–50 seconds to complete a drawn sequence of formations; each completed point scores, and the team with the most points in the allotted jumps wins — 4-Way Female is the women's event alongside the open class (FAI, n.d.-a). Artistic events score freestyle (a solo two-minute routine of acrobatics) and freefly (team vertical flight) on execution, artistry and presentation. Speed skydiving measures the fastest vertical speed over a 2.5-second window between 5,500 and 8,200 ft. Canopy piloting is flown under the fully opened parachute in three disciplines: accuracy, scored by distance in centimetres from the bullseye of a target on landing; distance, the longest straight-line ground distance before opening; and speed, the highest speed over a vertical measurement window (FAI, n.d.-a). Wingsuit flying is scored over horizontal and vertical windows. All competitors jump from aircraft at regulated exit altitudes, wear an automatic activation device (AAD) as backup, and are filmed for judging; men's and women's (open and female) classes exist in the team disciplines, and junior categories at championships (FAI, n.d.-b).

History

The parachute was sketched by Leonardo da Vinci in the 15th century, but the first descent by parachute was made by André-Jacques Garnerin from a balloon over Paris in 1797, and the first jump from an airplane was made by the American Grant Morton over Los Angeles in 1912 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Leslie Irvin founded the Irvin Air Chute Company in 1919 and pioneered the free-fall parachute used by military paratroopers, and formation skydiving began in California in the 1930s, with the first recorded formation jump in 1935 and the "Starfish" formation in 1938 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two icons of the high-altitude era were the American Joseph Kittinger, whose Project Excelsior jump from 31.3 km in 1960 stood as the highest free fall for 52 years, and Felix Baumgartner, whose Red Bull Stratos jump from 38,969 m in 2012 was in turn surpassed by Alan Eustace's 41,419 m stratospheric jump in 2014 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ISC was created within the FAI in 1949 and has run the world championships since (FAI, n.d.-b).
