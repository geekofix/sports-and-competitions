Rainbow Six Siege

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six Siege is a tactical five-on-five first-person shooter developed by Ubisoft Montreal and released in December 2015, in which attacking and defending teams of operators battle over objectives in destructible indoor environments; it is one of the world's established esports titles (Wikipedia, n.d.). The season-closing world championship is the Six Invitational, fielding 20 teams in a $3 million prize pool, and the Six Invitational 2026 was played at the Adidas Arena in Paris, 2–15 February 2026: on 15 February, Brazil's FaZe Clan beat Team Secret 3–1 in the grand final, becoming the first organization ever to win back-to-back Six Invitational titles (after Boston 2025 over Team BDS); FaZe also won the Rainbow Six title at the Esports World Cup 2026 in Paris in August 2026, beating FURIA 3–1 and completing the first-ever trifecta of Major, Six Invitational and Esports World Cup titles (Esports Observer, n.d.; Strafe, n.d.). Competition is open to players of all genders, and a dedicated women's scene runs in all four regions — women's leagues in North America and Europe (CCS Women), South America (Circuito Feminino, LSA) and Asia-Pacific (R6 Velvet) plus Ubisoft Brazil's HUB Women's ranked scenario with cash prizes (Siege.gg, n.d.). The 2026 circuit runs four regional leagues (NAL, EML, South America, APAC) plus a new China National League, Challenger Series in February–March, a Season Kickoff in April, two Six Majors (May and November, including the BLAST R6 Major Osaka) and the Six Invitational in February, under the Siege X ruleset launched 10 June 2025 with per-round operator bans and Ranked 3.0 (Strafe, n.d.). At the top of the current game stands the FaZe Clan roster — Eduardo "KDS" Chiste, Rocco "cyber" cigana, Leonardo "soulz" Andrade, Handyy and VITAKING — Six Invitational 2026 and Esports World Cup 2026 champions (Esports Observer, n.d.).

Rules

Competitive Siege is played as a best-of-series of maps: each map is contested in rounds, first to 7 round wins, with overtime at 6–6, and teams swap between attack and defense halfway through (Wikipedia, n.d.). Attackers pick from operator gadgets — drones for intel, breaches for walls — to assault bomb sites, hostage or secure area, while defenders reinforce walls, floors and hatches with gadgets and traps, creating vertical play and wall-bang duels rather than raw aim alone (Wikipedia, n.d.). Since Siege X (June 2025) each team votes to ban one operator every round, with bans resetting on role swap, and Ranked 3.0 brought new divisions, regional leaderboards and anti-cheat protection (Strafe, n.d.). At the Six Invitational, 20 teams play four groups of five, the top four of each group advance to a double-elimination playoff, playoff matches are best-of-three and the grand final best-of-five; qualification runs on Global Standings points earned in the regional leagues and the two Majors (Liquipedia, n.d.).

History

Siege descends from Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six (1998) and was developed by Ubisoft Montreal, first shown in 2013 as "Rainbow 6: Patriots" before being reworked into Siege and released in December 2015 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first Six Invitational was held in Montreal in February 2017, with Continuum winning on PC and Elevate on Xbox One; the prize pool reached $3 million from 2020, and the event has since been staged in Paris (2021), Stockholm (2022), Montreal (2023), São Paulo (2024), Boston (2025) and Paris again (2026) (Wikipedia, n.d.; Liquipedia, n.d.). Champions include PENTA Sports (2018), G2 Esports (2019, 2023), Spacestation Gaming (2020), Ninjas in Pyjamas (2021), TSM (2022), w7m esports (2024) and FaZe Clan (2025, 2026) (Strafe, n.d.). The PENTA/G2 core of Pengu and Goga set the first dynasty (2018–2019), and coach Fabian holds the unique record of three Six Invitational titles — twice as a player (2018, 2019) and once as G2's head coach (2023) — while FaZe's Brazilian core added the back-to-back crowns of 2025 and 2026 (Strafe, n.d.).
