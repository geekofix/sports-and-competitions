Rackets

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rackets (also called racquets or hard rackets) is an indoor racket sport played in a walled court, the direct precursor of squash, and is governed in Britain by the Tennis and Rackets Association (T&RA), founded in 1907, with the North American Racquets Association (NARA) governing the game in North America (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-e; North American Racquets Association, n.d.). The world singles championship has been decided on a challenge basis since 1820 and the world doubles since 1990; the 2026 singles final pits defending champion Ben Cawston against Richard Owen, with the first leg at Queen's Club in London on 5 December and the second at the Montreal Racket Club on 12 December (Tennis and Rackets Association, n.d.-a; Tennis and Rackets Association, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Rackets is the direct precursor of Squash and a cousin of Real Tennis within the racket-sports family listed under Important Links.

The women's game is now fully established but was long excluded: women were banned from playing rackets until 2008, the women's world singles championship began in 2015 and the women's doubles in 2019 (played out in 2020), and today there is a complete ladies' programme — in 2026, for instance, Cesca Sweet won the Ladies Amateur Singles at Queen's Club — alongside the men's championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Tennis and Rackets Association, n.d.-b).

Rules

A rackets court measures 60 by 30 feet (18.3 × 9.1 m) with a ceiling of at least 30 feet, and the walls are painted dark so the small white ball (about 38 mm, roughly 28 g) stays visible; the bottom of the front wall carries the “tin”, a sloped sounding board, and the floor is wood with service boxes and a halfway line (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Players use a solid wooden racket up to about 30.5 inches (77 cm) long; the serve must strike the front wall above the tin and land in the service box or beyond the service line, and play continues until the ball is dead — a let is replayed (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Scoring is hand-in, hand-out: only the server can score, and games are played to 9 points with the world championship contested over multiple games; men's and women's singles and doubles are separate titles, and junior (under-19) championships exist for boys and girls (Tennis and Rackets Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

History

Rackets began in 18th-century London, where debtors in the King's Bench and Fleet prisons played a modified form of fives against the prison walls — a game Charles Dickens later described in The Pickwick Papers; it spread to taverns such as the Belvedere Tavern in Pentonville and to Harrow School in the early 1820s, where the single-wall variant that became squash was developed (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Queen's Club in London became the sport's headquarters in 1887, the first Amateur Championships were held in 1888 (singles) and 1890 (doubles), the challenge-basis world title dates to 1820 with Robert Mackay as the first champion, and the classic rules manual, The Book of Racquets by C. E. Atkins, appeared in 1872; James Stout of Bermuda, world champion from 2008 to 2017 and winner of the British and US Opens, is regarded as one of the greatest players of the modern era, while Peter Latham remains a legendary name of the 19th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
