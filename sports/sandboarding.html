Sandboarding

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sandboarding is a boardsport in which a rider descends a sand dune standing on a sandboard with both feet strapped in — beginners often ride sitting or lying on a sand sled — similar to snowboarding but on sand; it is governed internationally by InterSands, the International Sandboarding and SandSki Association founded in Switzerland, which has hosted the Sandboard World Cup in the Ica region of Peru every two years since 2017, at Huacachina with its Duna Grande, the largest sand dune in the world (InterSands, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The classic world championship venue was Monte Kaolino in Hirschau, Bavaria — Europe's largest sand hill at about 90–120 m high with a water landing and the world's only sand lift — which hosted the Sandboarding World Championship annually until 2007; other major events are the Sand Master Jam at Sand Master Park in Florence, Oregon (since 1996), the Pan-American Sandboarding Challenge at Aquiraz, Brazil, and the European Sandboarding Championship "SandSpirit" at Monte Kaolino (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Figure: Weber, S. J. (U.S. Navy). (2006, November 12). Sandboarding in Dubai [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sandboarding_in_Dubai.jpg

Among the current leading riders, the women's field is headed by Chile's Constanza Albayay, double world champion 2022 in slalom and big air and 2025 European boardercross women's champion at SandSpirit, while the men's side is led by four-time world champion Josh Tenge, holder of three Guinness World Records, and by the 2019 Sandboard World Cup winners Luka Flachenecker (slalom), Vitor Semedo (boardercross) and Daniel Vonach (freestyle) (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Within the board and water sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Surfing, Windsurfing, Bodyboarding, Bodysurfing, Skimboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

A sandboard has a much harder base than a snowboard — usually formica or laminex, with terrain boards of hardwood ply and full-size boards built as a wood, fibreglass and plastic composite — and the base is waxed before a run with paraffin-based sandboard wax to cut friction on dry and wet sand (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitions are held on natural dunes or sculpted sand parks and comprise slalom (head-to-head knockout racing on a winding course, like parallel giant slalom), boardercross (several riders racing an obstacle course at once), freestyle/best-trick and big-air jump events; because mechanised lifts are almost impossible on dunes, riders climb back up on foot or ride dune buggies and ATVs, and Monte Kaolino uniquely offers a funicular sand lift and a water landing at the base (Wikipedia, n.d.; InterSands, n.d.). Men and women compete in all disciplines, with separate women's categories at the InterSands and European championships; there are no weight or age classes in the senior events — only the choice of dune, board and wax (InterSands, n.d.).

History

Sand sliding is ancient — Egyptians slid down dunes on wooden boards and Hawaiians practised heʻe one as a beach pastime in the early 20th century — but modern sandboarding is credited to Lon Beale, "Doctor Dune", who began riding in California's Mojave Desert in 1972; Jack Smith and Gary Fluitt popularised the sport in California in the early 1980s, and in Namibia Derek Bredenkamp was boarding near Swakopmund around 1974, leading to commercial tours from 1994 and the South African Sandboarding League founded in 2000 with competitions on the Matterhorn Dune between Swakopmund and Walvis Bay from 2002 to 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The related sand-skiing in Namibia produced a Guinness speed record of 92.12 km/h for the German Henrik May on 6 June 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.). At Monte Kaolino a ski club formed in 1956, and the dune hosted the Sandboarding World Championship from 1990 to 2007; the Copa Sandboarding Perú has run near Paracas since 2009, and since 2017 InterSands has organised the Sandboard World Cup at Huacachina–Ica — the 2019 edition crowned Luka Flachenecker, Vitor Semedo and Daniel Vonach — with the Dragon Cup at Cerro Dragón in Iquique, Chile, serving as the de facto world championship during the pandemic years (Wikipedia, n.d.; Sand-boarding.com, n.d.). An iconic rider is Josh Tenge, four-time world champion who holds three Guinness World Records, including a backwards somersault of almost 14 m (Wikipedia, n.d.).
