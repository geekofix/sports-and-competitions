Cutting

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cutting is a western riding event in which a horse and rider separate a single cow from a small herd and prevent it from returning, while judges score the run; the horse is credited for its "cow sense" — reading, mirroring and blocking the animal without rider cues (National Cutting Horse Association [NCHA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed in the United States by the National Cutting Horse Association (NCHA), founded in 1946 in Fort Worth, Texas, which sanctions the Triple Crown of Cutting — the Futurity for 3-year-olds, the Summer Spectacular and the Super Stakes — all staged at the Will Rogers Memorial Center in Fort Worth (NCHA, n.d.-b). The 2026 NCHA Metallic Cat World Championship Futurity runs 16 November to 12 December 2026 in Fort Worth, with Open, Non-Pro and Amateur divisions and their Intermediate and Limited levels (NCHA, n.d.-b). Women and men compete on equal terms in every division, and the 2026 NCHA Rulebook is the official rule source (NCHA, n.d.-a) (National Cutting Horse Association, n.d.-a) (National Cutting Horse Association, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under the NCHA Rulebook, each horse-and-rider pair works one cow for 2½ minutes (150 seconds); every run starts from a baseline score of 70, and the judges' marks take the total anywhere from about 60 to 80 (NCHA, n.d.-a). The rider must cut at least two cows during the run, and one of them must be a "deep" cow that drives well away from the herd (NCHA, n.d.-a). Two turnback riders and two herd holders assist each competitor, and the selected cattle weigh roughly 400–650 lb (180–295 kg) (NCHA, n.d.-a). Scoring rewards the horse for quietly selecting a cow, staying between the cow and the herd, and making the cow quit; penalties are assessed for losing the cow, hot fence contact, excessive hand use and other faults, and in major events the high and low of three preliminary runs are dropped (NCHA, n.d.-a). There are no separate rules for women — ladies and men compete in the same classes, with additional Amateur, Non-Pro and age-limited (4/5/6-year-old horse) categories (NCHA, n.d.-a).

History

Cutting grew out of the daily work of ranch cowboys separating cattle on horseback, and the first cutting exhibition was held at the Fort Worth Stock Show in 1919 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The National Cutting Horse Association was founded in 1946 in Fort Worth by thirteen horsemen at the Southwestern Exposition and Fat Stock Show, and the first NCHA-sanctioned cutting competition followed in Dublin, Texas, the same year (NCHA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport's most legendary performer was the mare Marion's Girl, world champion in 1954 and 1956 ridden by Buster Welch, who was inducted into the NCHA Hall of Fame in 1962; Welch remains one of the most decorated riders in the sport's history (Wikipedia, n.d.).
