100m Hurdles

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The 100 metres hurdles is the women's sprint hurdles event of athletics: ten hurdles at a height of 0.838 m are sprinted over a 100 m straight on a 400 m track, the first 13 m from the start, the rest 8.5 m apart and 10.5 m before the finish. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics, which ratifies the world record and runs the World Championships, the Olympic tournament and the inaugural 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship (World Athletics, n.d.-e). The world record is 12.09 s, set by Masai Russell of the USA at the 2026 Weltklasse Zürich on 27 August 2026, taking the 12.12 s record of Tobi Amusan of Nigeria (Eugene 2022) that had stood since 2022 — both marks still among the fastest ever (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the event was headlined by Olympic champion Masai Russell (pictured), who won the 100 m hurdles at the 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship in Budapest in 12.22 s, ahead of Tobi Amusan (12.34) and Nadine Visser of the Netherlands (12.39); other leading hurdlers included Grace Stark, Devynne Charlton of the Bahamas and Ackera Nugent of Jamaica (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

Together with Athletics, this discipline belongs to the hurdles family — 110m Hurdles, 400m Hurdles and 100m Sprint — and the full list of athletics disciplines is under Important Links.

Rules

The 100 m hurdles is run in separate lanes from a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks on the home straight, like all races up to and including 400 m; since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete, and the winner is the first whose torso crosses the finish line, judged by photo finish to 1/1000 s with times recorded to 1/100 s (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-b). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

The ten hurdles stand 0.838 m high for senior women, 13 m from the start, 8.5 m apart and 10.5 m from the finish; knocking a hurdle down is not a foul and does not disqualify the athlete, but leaving the lane to impede another competitor does (World Athletics, n.d.-b). A wind reading above +2.0 m/s in the running direction invalidates a record; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-b). At under-20 level the hurdles are lower (0.762 m for U20 women), and para athletics contests the event in classification classes; men race over 110 m hurdles instead, with 1.067 m hurdles (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

History

Sprint hurdling grew out of 19th-century flat sprinting and steeplechase racing in Britain and the USA; the women's 80 m hurdles was an Olympic event from 1932 to 1968 and was replaced by the 100 m hurdles at Munich 1972 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Yordanka Donkova of Bulgaria set the world record of 12.21 s in 1988, a mark that stood for almost 28 years until Kendra Harrison of the USA ran 12.20 in 2016; at the 2016 Rio Olympics the USA swept the medals with Brianna Rollins, Nia Ali and Kristi Castlin (Wikipedia, n.d.). Sally Pearson of Australia, pictured, won the 2012 Olympic gold in an Olympic record 12.35 s and added world titles in 2011 and 2017 (Olympics, n.d.). Tobi Amusan made history at the 2022 World Championships in Eugene by winning Nigeria's first world title in athletics and setting the world record of 12.12 s in the semi-final (World Athletics, n.d.-e).
