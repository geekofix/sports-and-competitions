Sitting Volleyball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sitting volleyball is the Paralympic version of volleyball, played by athletes with physical impairments on a smaller court with a lower net, in which players must keep contact between their torso and the floor when playing the ball. It has been part of the Paralympic Games since Arnhem 1980 and is governed worldwide by World ParaVolley, with more than 10,000 athletes competing in over 75 countries.

Rules

The court measures 10 by 6 metres — smaller than the 18\u00d79 m standing court — divided by a net 1.15 m high in the men's game and 1.05 m in the women's. Each team fields six players (with up to five reserves and one libero in a different-coloured shirt), largely following FIVB rules: three team touches plus the block, with the signature attacking move of serve, set and spike. Two sitting-volleyball adaptations stand out: the torso — some part of the body between the buttocks and the shoulders — must be in contact with the floor whenever a shot or attempt at a shot is made (a brief loss of contact is tolerated only for extreme defensive manoeuvres in the back court below net height), and players slide around the court on their arms without standing up; a player may also penetrate the opponent's court less fully than in standing volleyball, and blocks of the serve are permitted. Teams must field a mix of impairment classes, and eligibility requires a physical impairment; matches are best of five sets to 25 points.

History

Sitting volleyball originated in the Netherlands in 1956 as a rehabilitation and recreational activity for injured soldiers and athletes with disabilities, combining elements of volleyball and sitzball, and spread through European clubs in the following decades. The game reached the Paralympic Games at Arnhem in 1980, when a men's competition was held, and a women's event was added at Athens 2004; before that, standing volleyball appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1976 Toronto Games — the sport's Paralympic roots lie with Second World War veterans' sport programmes. World ParaVolley (formerly the World Organisation Volleyball for the Disabled) governs the sport today and stages world championships and zone championships; the historical powers in the men's game include Iran, long the sport's dominant Paralympic champion, with Bosnia and Herzegovina, Germany and Russia also successful, while China and the United States have led the women's event, in which athletes of varying statures — from very short stature to recently the tallest Paralympian, Mehrzadselakjani of Iran — compete under the classification framework.
