Haggis Hurling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Haggis hurling is a Scottish novelty sport in which competitors throw a haggis (the traditional sheep's pluck pudding, or a weighted replica) for distance, with medals awarded for the longest throw. World championships are held in Scotland, and the world record is ratified by Guinness World Records.

Rules

Competitors throw a haggis — in official competitions a standard weighted haggis of about 1 kg (2.2 lb) — from behind a marked line using a full throwing motion, similar to a hammer or weight throw. The distance is measured from the throwing line to the first point of impact (or where the haggis comes to rest, per the event's rules), and the longest valid throw wins. Categories include men's, women's and junior events at world championships, with multiple attempts allowed and the best counting. Safety rules require a clear field and spectator distance.

History

Haggis hurling emerged as a comic event at Scottish Highland games and Burns Night celebrations in the late 20th century, with modern world championships held at events such as the Ceres Highland Games. Guinness World Records ratifies the longest-distance record, which stands at over 50 m. The sport is featured at Highland games across Scotland and has been covered by the BBC and international media.
