Powerlifting Deadlift

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The deadlift is the third and final lift in competitive powerlifting: the barbell is pulled from the floor to a standing position with hips and shoulders locked in one continuous motion — the simplest of the three lifts, with no referee "start" command, just the bar, the floor and the clock (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is also contested as a standalone single-lift event and as a test of strength beyond organized sport in strongman and gym culture. Among the current leading women, the American April Mathis holds the IPF classic open deadlift world record with 290 kg from 2023, and women's classic deadlift records are contested across all bodyweight classes; among the men, the classic open record stands above 400 kg (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the lift now retired from record contention include Britain's Eddie Hall, who pulled 500 kg in 2016, and Iceland's Hafthor Björnsson, who pulled 501 kg in 2020 in strongman competition (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under IPF technical rules the bar is loaded horizontally on the platform and gripped with a conventional or alternate grip (hook grip permitted); the lifter pulls the bar upward in one continuous motion with no downward movement, and the lift is complete only when knees are locked, shoulders back and the body motionless and upright — the referee then gives the "down" command (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). Faults include resting the bar on the thighs (hitching), any downward bar movement, foot movement, releasing the bar before the command, or dropping it at the finish. Equipment is limited to a belt, wrist wraps and mandatory shin-length socks in classic lifting; equipped lifters may wear a deadlift suit (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). Each lifter gets three attempts, single-lift deadlift world championships run on the same technical basis, and men's and women's classes exist across all bodyweight categories plus junior and masters age groups (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The loaded barbell pull from the floor is as old as weightlifting itself, and the term "dead lift" — lifting "dead" weight without momentum — was established in 19th-century strongman performances, making the lift a staple of the odd-lift contests from which powerlifting was standardized; it became powerlifting's closing discipline when the IPF was founded in 1972 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Beyond the IPF, the lift lives in strongman's silver-dollar and car deadlifts and in CrossFit and gym culture; the heaviest confirmed pulls with straps exceed 500 kg — Eddie Hall's 500 kg in 2016 and Hafthor Björnsson's 501 kg world record in 2020, with Latvia's Martins Licis among the strongest deadlifters of the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
