Field Lacrosse

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Field lacrosse is the outdoor, full-contact version of lacrosse, played by two teams of ten (men) or twelve (women) who use netted sticks (crosses) to carry, pass and shoot a rubber ball into the opponent's goal. It is governed by World Lacrosse (formerly the Federation of International Lacrosse), which runs the World Lacrosse Men's and Women's Field World Championships, and is the national summer sport of Canada.

Rules

At World Lacrosse men's field championships, teams play four 15-minute quarters on a 110 m x 60 m field with a 1.8 m x 1.8 m goal. Each team fields 10 players: 3 attackmen, 3 midfielders, 3 defensemen and a goalkeeper. After a goal or at the start of a quarter, play restarts with a "face-off" — the ball is clamped between two players' sticks and they "face" each other. Players may cradle, pass, shoot and check (body-check within 5 yards of the ball carrier with a closed-head stick check to the body/arms). Offsides, holding the ball too long (20-second count in the offensive half) and fouls result in turnovers or penalty time (30 s to 3 min in the penalty box). Women's field lacrosse uses 12 players, a slightly different offside rule and no full body checking (stick checks to the body allowed in limited zones); boundaries are smaller (women's field 110 x 60 m with curved ends). Sudden-death overtime decides tied knockout games.

History

Lacrosse originates with Indigenous peoples of North America — the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) game "Tewaarathon" — documented by French missionaries in the 17th century; the name "lacrosse" comes from the French "la crosse" (the stick). Modern men's field lacrosse was codified in Canada in the 1860s, with the first organized club in Montreal (1856) and the first international match Canada vs. England in 1867. The National Lacrosse Association of Canada was founded in 1867. The Federation of International Lacrosse was founded in 1972, and the first men's World Championship was held in 1967 (Canada won); the women's field world championship began in 1982. The US and Canada dominate the men's game (Paul Rabil, Gary Gait, Lyle Thompson), while the US dominates women's field lacrosse. Lacrosse was an Olympic sport in 1904 and 1908 and returns at Los Angeles 2028 in a six-a-side format.
