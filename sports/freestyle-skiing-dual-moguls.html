Freestyle Skiing Dual Moguls

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dual moguls is the head-to-head version of moguls: two skiers race simultaneously down parallel red and blue courses — each with the same bumps and two jumps — and the winner of each duel is decided by judges' majority vote rather than by clock time (FIS, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed by FIS in the FIS Freestyle Ski World Cup and debuted as an Olympic event at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics in Livigno — the 15th freestyle skiing event on the Olympic program (FIS, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.). At the 2026 Games, Mikaël Kingsbury of Canada won the men's gold ahead of Ikuma Horishima (JPN) and Matt Graham (AUS), and Jakara Anthony of Australia the women's gold ahead of Jaelin Kauf (USA) and Elizabeth Lemley (USA) (Olympics, n.d.). In the 2025–26 FIS Freestyle Ski World Cup (November 20, 2025 – March 29, 2026), Ikuma Horishima of Japan swept the men's moguls and dual moguls Crystal Globes (FIS, n.d.-a).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Jakara Anthony (AUS), the first Olympic dual moguls champion, Jaelin Kauf (USA) and Elizabeth Lemley (USA), the 2026 moguls Olympic champion, while on the men's side Mikaël Kingsbury (CAN), the 2026 dual moguls Olympic champion who retired after the 2025–26 season as Canada's national champion, heads the historical records ahead of Ikuma Horishima (JPN), the 2025–26 dual moguls globe winner (Olympics, n.d.; FIS, n.d.-a).

Rules

Under the FIS freestyle competition rules, the dual moguls course mirrors the single moguls course — roughly 250 m long with a field of closely spaced bumps and two jumps (upper and lower) — but in two parallel lanes (red and blue) with simultaneous starts from a dual start gate (FIS, n.d.-b). Each skier performs one run; instead of a numeric score, a panel of judges (usually three to five) watches both runs and votes for the winner of the duel based on turns, aerials and speed, with the majority deciding; the loser is eliminated and the bracket continues to a final (FIS, n.d.-b). Because the two runs are judged comparatively, the format emphasizes race craft: passing, line choice and clean landings. Helmets are mandatory; men and women compete separately with identical courses, and junior classes exist at World Cup level; the discipline has been part of the FIS World Cup since 2010 and of the Olympics since Milano Cortina 2026 (FIS, n.d.-a; FIS, n.d.-b).

History

Dual moguls was introduced to the FIS Freestyle Ski World Cup in 2010, when the parallel-course format was added alongside classic moguls, and it reached the Olympic program only at Milano Cortina 2026, where it made its debut with Mikaël Kingsbury's victory (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.). The format's history is intertwined with moguls itself, which became an Olympic medal sport at Albertville 1992: the early icons were France's Edgar Grospiron (1992 Olympic champion), Canada's Jean-Luc Brassard (1994) and the USA's Jonny Moseley, whose 1998 Nagano victory with an innovative grab changed the sport, and on the women's side the USA's Donna Weinbrecht (1992) and Canada's Jenn Heil, who won Olympic silver in 2010 in the same Games where dual moguls debuted at World Cup level (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Kingsbury, with more than 80 World Cup wins and the 2026 dual moguls Olympic title, is the discipline's record holder (FIS, n.d.-a).
