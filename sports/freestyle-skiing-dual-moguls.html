Freestyle Skiing Dual Moguls

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dual moguls is a freestyle skiing event in which two skiers race head-to-head down the same mogul (bumps) course, each on their own line, and the winner is decided by time and judges' scores for turns, aerial maneuvers and speed. It is governed by FIS and has been an Olympic event since Albertville 1992 (as moguls) with the dual format added at the 2010 Vancouver Games.

Rules

At FIS dual moguls events, two competitors start simultaneously from a gate at the top of a moguls course about 250 m long, each on their own line, with aerial jumps at designated points. Each skier is scored by judges on turns (technical quality through the bumps), aerials (difficulty and execution of the tricks off the jumps) and speed (the time differential between the two skiers is converted into points). The higher total score advances in the bracket; at the Olympics, the format is knockout heats to a final. See Freestyle Skiing Moguls for the individual moguls event on this site.

History

Moguls became a FIS discipline in the 1970s and an Olympic medal sport in 1992; the dual format was introduced at the 2010 Vancouver Olympics, won by Canada's Alexandre Bilodeau (men) and Canada's Jenn Heil (women). The discipline is dominated by Canada, France, the US, Australia and Japan. Stars include Alexandre Bilodeau (2010 and 2014 gold), Mikaël Kingsbury (Canada, record 82 World Cup wins and 2018 Olympic gold), Perrine Laffont (France, 2018 Olympic gold) and Justine Dufour-Lapointe (Canada, 2014 gold).
