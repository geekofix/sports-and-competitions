Mixed Doubles Curling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Mixed doubles curling is a two-player variant of curling in which each team consists of one man and one woman. It is faster and more tactical than the four-player game, and it became an Olympic winter sport at the PyeongChang 2018 Winter Olympics. The format is governed by the World Curling Federation (WCF), and the World Mixed Doubles Curling Championship has been held annually since 2008.

Rules

Each team delivers six stones per end, with the two players alternating throws, and games are scheduled for eight ends. Before each end one stone is positioned in play (the power play), and the free-guard zone protects stones in front of the house early in the end. The team with the higher score after eight ends wins, with an extra end played in case of a tie. Sweeping is allowed, and mixed doubles rules were formalized by the WCF in 2008 and revised in 2015, when the power play and new tie-break rules were introduced. Qualification for the Olympics is based on results at the World Mixed Doubles Championships.

History

The idea was developed in 2001 by Warren Hansen of Curling Canada as one of the discipline variations for the inaugural Continental Cup of Curling in 2002. The first World Mixed Doubles Championship took place in 2008, and Switzerland won six of the first ten titles. Olympic scholar Bob Barney advocated adding mixed doubles to the Winter Olympics because it is one of the few sports in which men and women compete directly against each other; the IOC approved it for PyeongChang 2018. Canada's John Morris and Kaitlyn Lawes won the first Olympic mixed doubles gold medal, defeating Switzerland in the final. At the 2019 World Championship 48 of the WCF's member countries were represented, underlining how quickly the format spread to smaller curling nations.
