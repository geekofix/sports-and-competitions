Roller Speed Skating

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roller speed skating — also called inline speed skating — is the racing discipline of roller sport, in which skaters race on inline skates (or, historically, quad skates) on banked tracks, flat tracks and closed road circuits. Its world championships, staged since 1937, are one of roller sport's oldest international competitions, and the discipline is governed today by World Skate, which holds the World Roller Speed Skating Championships annually — the 2024 edition took place in Montesilvano and Sulmona–Pescara, Italy, as part of the World Skate Games. Powerhouse nations include Italy, Colombia, Belgium and the United States.

Rules

World championship racing is held in two venues: the track, a banked oval of 200 metres, and the road, a closed circuit, with men's and women's events across sprint, middle and long distances — from the 100 m and 200 m dual time trial sprints through 500 m and 1,000 m sprints, 5,000 m and 10,000 m points races and elimination races, to relays and the marathon of 42,195 metres. Racing is six-athlete, all-out competition in heats and finals, with tactics of drafting and attacks central; a race of the elimination type removes the last skater across the line on each designated bell. Equipment is regulated: inline skates may have no more than five wheels and wheels may not exceed 110 mm in diameter (125 mm in marathons only); helmets are mandatory; and skinsuits, aero helmets and clap-style frames are part of the elite equipment culture. Age categories run from youth, junior (under 19) and senior skaters age 19 or older, per the World Skate Speed rulebook; races often carry world-record status on standard courses.

History

Speed skating on quad roller skates dates to the 19th century, and international competition began early: the first Roller Speed Skating World Championship took place in Monza, Italy, in 1937, with a separate track championship in London the following year, and European nations, above all Italy, dominated the title races for the first four decades through 1970s and 1980s stars and champions. The World Championships were held unofficially until 1966, when they became official under the International Roller Sports Federation, and the United States enjoyed a strong period of dominance in the 1980s through 2000s. The introduction of inline skates in the early 1990s — from 1992 onwards — revolutionised the sport, rendering quads largely obsolete within a few seasons, and Colombia then rose as the dominant nation of the 2010s, winning overall podiums throughout the decade, while New Zealand, Belgium and Italy also collected world titles under the new World Skate structure. Since 2017 the world championships have been organised as part of the World Skate Games, and the sport continues as a feeder for ice speed skating — Olympic champions such as Chad Hedrick and Derek Parra moved from inline world titles to Olympic gold on ice — while pressing for Olympic inclusion of its own; roller speed skating appeared at the 2018 Youth Olympic Games as a showcase.
