Excel World Championship

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The Microsoft Excel World Championship is the world championship of Excel esports: competitors solve spreadsheet-modeling challenges head-to-head in Microsoft Excel, from financial modeling and data analysis to creative problem-solving, under time pressure in a knockout bracket (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.). It is organized by the Financial Modeling World Cup (FMWC), which has run the annual championship since 2021 — the early editions were held in Las Vegas — with the 2026 edition continuing the format of live head-to-head heats judged on speed, accuracy and model quality (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.). Excel esports sits inside the wider competitive-gaming scene: spreadsheet and data competitions share the stage with video-game esports at multi-title events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current competitors are the finalists of the 2026 Microsoft Excel World Championship, with men and women competing together in the same bracket (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.).

Rules

Competitors receive an identical Excel workbook with a modeling task and a fixed time limit — typically 30 minutes per heat — and are scored on the correctness of the model, the accuracy of the outputs and the elegance and speed of the solution; head-to-head winners advance through a bracket to the final (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.). Rules prohibit external data sources beyond those provided, require work to be done live in Excel, and use standardized laptops and software versions to keep the playing field level (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.). Divisions include individual and team categories, and youth and student categories run alongside the open championship; prize money is awarded to the top finishers (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.). There are no separate men's and women's brackets — all competitors meet in the same open competition (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.).

History

Spreadsheet modeling competitions grew out of university finance and accounting contests in the 2000s, and the Financial Modeling World Cup formalized them as an esport: the first Microsoft Excel World Championship was held in 2021 in Las Vegas, with subsequent editions drawing competitors from more than 100 countries and being streamed like traditional esports (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.). The FMWC also runs the Modelmania-style team events and collegiate qualifiers, and Excel esports has been covered by mainstream esports media as a non-violent, skill-based alternative within the Olympic-style esports discussion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women have competed since the first editions and are regular semifinalists in the open bracket (Financial Modeling World Cup, n.d.).
