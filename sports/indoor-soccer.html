Indoor Soccer

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Indoor soccer (also called arena soccer or, internationally, minifootball) is a form of small-sided football played inside a walled arena on artificial turf, usually the size of an ice hockey rink (about 61 m x 26 m). It differs from FIFA-sanctioned futsal: the ball is played off the perimeter walls, which keeps the game continuous, and restarts are kick-ins rather than throw-ins. Teams field six players including the goalkeeper, and the game is especially popular in the United States, Canada and Mexico, with professional leagues such as the Major Arena Soccer League (MASL). At international level the World Minifootball Federation (WMF) governs the sport.

Rules

A professional indoor soccer match is typically divided into four 15-minute quarters with a half-time interval, while amateur leagues often play two halves. Each team has six players on the field, substitutions are unlimited and rolling, and there is no offside. The ball may be played off the perimeter walls without penalty; if it goes over the walls or hits the ceiling, the opposing team restarts with a kick-in from where the ball left play. Goals are smaller than outdoor goals and set into the end wall, free kicks, penalty kicks, corner kicks and kick-ins are used, and many leagues ban sliding tackles because of the risk of burns on turf. Fouls are penalized with free kicks or penalties and time in a penalty box.

History

Indoor soccer was developed in the United States and Canada in the late 20th century as a way to play football during winter, when snow made outdoor play impossible, using adapted gymnasiums and later purpose-built arenas. The Major Indoor Soccer League (MISL), founded in 1978, was the first major professional indoor league and made stars of players such as Gordon Naylor and indoor specialists from around the world; later incarnations included the National Professional Soccer League (1984-2001), and today the Major Arena Soccer League (MASL) is the premier professional league. Versions of walled indoor football are also known as futbol rápido in Mexico, showbol in Brazil and futebol society, and the World Minifootball Federation organizes world championships for national teams.
