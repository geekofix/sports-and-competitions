Indoor Soccer

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Indoor soccer is the umbrella term for small-sided football played indoors. Its most widespread form is futsal, governed by FIFA under the Laws of the Game and played by more than 30 million registered players in over 130 countries, with the FIFA Futsal World Cup, the UEFA Futsal EURO (the 2026 edition's qualifiers drew 48 national teams) and the first FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup in 2025 in the Philippines (FIFA, n.d.-a; UEFA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A rival rulebook is used by the Asociación Mundial de Futsal (AMF, the successor of FIFUSA), which runs its own Futsal World Championships, and wall-based "keep the ball alive" indoor soccer is played professionally in leagues such as the Major Arena Soccer League in North America (Asociación Mundial de Futsal, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Futsal is played 5-a-side including the goalkeeper on a 40 × 20 m court (minimum 25 × 16 m) with a size-4 low-bounce ball, two 20-minute halves of effective time, no offside, kick-ins instead of throw-ins and unlimited rolling substitutions (FIFA, n.d.-a). Indoor soccer is the umbrella for the small-sided indoor formats Futsal and Five-a-side Football, within the wider football family that also includes Football, Beach Soccer, Seven-a-side Football, Six-man Football, Swamp Soccer, Walking Football, Fire-ball Soccer, the disability formats Amputee Football, Blind Football, Cerebral Palsy Football and Powerchair Football, and the historic games Royal Shrovetide Football, Calcio Fiorentino and Cuju.

On the women's side, the leading national teams at the inaugural 2025 FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup and in the 2026 UEFA women's qualifiers were Spain, Portugal and Brazil, and women's futsal was again showcased at the 2025 Jogos da Juventude in Brazil (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the men's side, the 2026 AFC Futsal Asian Cup in Jakarta and the UEFA Futsal EURO 2026 are the season highlights, with Brazil, Argentina, Spain and Iran the traditional powers (FIFA, n.d.-a; UEFA, n.d.).

Rules

Under the FIFA Futsal Laws of the Game 2025/26, each team fields five players including the goalkeeper on a hard, flat court of 40 × 20 m (25 × 16 m minimum) with goals 3 × 2 m (FIFA, n.d.-a). The ball is a size-4 low-bounce ball, 62–64 cm in circumference and 400–440 g (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match consists of two 20-minute halves of effective time — the clock stops whenever the ball is out of play — plus a 15-minute halftime (FIFA, n.d.-a). There is no offside; out-of-play balls are restarted with kick-ins, corner kicks or goal clearances, and a goalkeeper may not receive a deliberate pass from a teammate with the foot (FIFA, n.d.-a). Substitutions are unlimited and rolling, and a player sent off may be replaced after two minutes or immediately if the opposing team scores (FIFA, n.d.-a). After a team commits its sixth accumulated foul in a half, every further foul is punished with a direct free kick from the 10 m mark without a wall (FIFA, n.d.-a). Most restarts must be taken within four seconds, and the goalkeeper may not hold the ball for more than four seconds in his own half (FIFA, n.d.-a). Sliding tackles from behind and charging the goalkeeper are fouls; a second yellow card or a serious foul means a red card (FIFA, n.d.-a). Men and women play under the same Laws of the Game in separate competitions, with identical pitch, ball and timing rules; youth competitions may use a smaller pitch and lighter ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The AMF rulebook differs in details such as the use of a felt ball, a different court surface and its own disciplinary system with blue cards, while wall-based indoor soccer variants use the walls actively to keep the ball in play (Asociación Mundial de Futsal, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Futsal was invented in the 1930s in Montevideo, Uruguay, by the Uruguayan physical-education teacher Juan Carlos Ceriani Gravier, who combined rules of football, handball, water polo and basketball so that boys at the YMCA could play indoors on a basketball-sized court (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The game spread through South America and Europe, and the Federación Internacional de Fútbol de Salón (FIFUSA) was founded in 1971 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The name futsal was coined at the 1985 FIFUSA World Championship in Madrid, from the Spanish fútbol sala and Portuguese futebol de salão, and FIFA took over international governance, holding the first FIFA Futsal World Championship in the Netherlands in 1989 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The AMF was founded in 2002/2003 as the successor of FIFUSA and continues to run its own world championships (Asociación Mundial de Futsal, n.d.). Among the icons of the sport are the Brazilian Falcão (Manuel Tobias Pereira, born 1959 in São Paulo), voted best player at four FIFA Futsal World Cups, and Ricardinho (Ricardo Lucas Figueredo, born 1976), another Brazilian legend of the indoor game; on the women's side, the 2025 FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup in the Philippines marked a new era with Spain, Portugal and Brazil leading (FIFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
