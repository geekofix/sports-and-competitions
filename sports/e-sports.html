E-Sports

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Esports (electronic sports) are video games played competitively at a professional level, from real-time strategy and first-person shooters to sports simulations and mobile titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There is no single world federation: the International Esports Federation (IESF), founded in 2008 and representing around 60 national member federations, runs the World Esports Championship — the 2026 edition was held in Indonesia with Counter-Strike 2, Dota 2, eFootball, PUBG Mobile and Mobile Legends: Bang Bang on the program — while the Riyadh-based Esports World Cup Foundation organizes the annual Esports World Cup in Saudi Arabia, which announced a record prize pool of 70 million dollars across 24 games (International Esports Federation [IESF], n.d.-a; n.d.-b; Esports World Cup Foundation, n.d.). The IOC ran the Olympic Esports Series, inaugurated at the Olympic Esports Week in Singapore in 2023, and announced Olympic Esports Games for Riyadh in 2027 under a 12-year partnership with Saudi Arabia; that partnership ended by mutual agreement in October 2025, and as of May 2026 planning for the Games is paused (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Esports have been a full medal event at the Asian Games since Hangzhou 2022 and at the Southeast Asian Games since 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in women's competitive play — Norway's Julia "juliano" Kaasvik, one of the leading current Counter-Strike players, and — in men's play — Korea's Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok of T1, the record five-time League of Legends world champion (2013, 2015, 2016, 2023 and 2024) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Esports competitions are run under the rules of each game's publisher and the tournament organizer's rulebook; there is no universal set of rules (IESF, n.d.-a). Standard formats are best-of-one to best-of-five series, double elimination, Swiss-system groups and round-robin leagues, with a grand final; matches are played on standardized tournament PCs or consoles with locked settings, and anti-cheat software, referees and replay review are standard at tier-one events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Player contracts, transfer windows, franchise systems and salary caps govern professional leagues such as the League of Legends World Championship (first held in 2011) and The International for Dota 2 (first held in 2011), and many leagues set minimum ages — commonly 16–18 years — for competitors (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Prize pools are funded by publishers, sponsors and crowdfunding, and team organizations field rosters of five or more players plus substitutes and coaches (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women generally compete in the same open tournaments, and a number of titles additionally run women's-only circuits and collegiate leagues (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Competitive gaming began with high-score contests — a Space Invaders tournament in 1980 is often cited as the first — and the Twin Galaxies scoreboard tracked records worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 1990s brought online play and organized PC-bang culture in South Korea, where the Korea e-Sports Association (KeSPA) was founded in 2000 around StarCraft; the World Cyber Games ran from 2000 to 2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The streaming era began with Twitch in 2011, and publishers built professional circuits: Riot Games, founded in 2006, launched League of Legends in 2009 and its World Championship in 2011, won by Fnatic (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The IOC first discussed esports at the 2017 Olympic Summit, ran the Olympic Virtual Series in 2021, held the inaugural Olympic Esports Week in Singapore in 2023, and in July 2024 voted to create the Olympic Esports Games; the planned 2027 Riyadh edition under the 12-year Saudi partnership was abandoned in October 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons include Korea's Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok (born 17 May 1996 in Seoul), whose five world titles make him the most decorated player in League of Legends history, and Japan's Daigo "The Beast" Umehara (born 1981), the legendary Street Fighter player (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
