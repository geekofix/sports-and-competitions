Underwater Orienteering

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Underwater orienteering is CMAS's precision diving discipline: competitors in scuba gear navigate a mapped course at the bottom of a lake or calm sea using a compass and depth gauge, finding and rounding marker controls without surfacing, against the clock. A sport born in the diving clubs of Eastern Europe in the 1960s, it combines orienteering's map-and-compass craft with diving's three-dimensional navigation and is contested at world championships by CMAS.

Rules

Events are staged on a marked underwater course in a natural water body (typically 2 to 15 metres deep): competitors dive with a compass and depth gauge and navigate a course set with control points (marker buoys or anchored markers) defined by compass bearings and distance in the course map disclosed before the start. The standard events include the M-course (an individual course), a star course with five legs radiating from a center point, a route with buoys, and a straight 100-metre compass run, with scoring by time plus accuracy (missed or wrongly rounded controls add penalties, surfacing disqualifies), and rules govern buoyancy (no surfacing, drifting), breathing-gas use (air consumption scoring in some formats) and equipment standards.

History

Underwater orienteering was born in the Eastern European diving clubs of the 1960s (principally in Czechoslovakia, East Germany and the Soviet Union), whose diving federations formalized competitive precision navigation as a sport in its own right; the first international championships followed quickly, and CMAS adopted the discipline as a world-championship underwater sport in 1967, an honor extended to the modern championship circuit held at intervals since. The sport's modern heartlands remain Central and Eastern Europe (Czechia and Hungary above all), with world championships contested there regularly, and its training method keeps serving divers worldwide as the core open-water navigation skill.
