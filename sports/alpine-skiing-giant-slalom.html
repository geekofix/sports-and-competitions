Alpine Skiing Giant Slalom

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Giant Slalom (GS) is an alpine skiing discipline governed by the FIS: skiers race down a long course with wide-spaced gates, making two runs on different courses. It combines speed and technical turning and is a core Olympic event.

Rules

Under FIS rules each athlete completes two runs on different courses (the second course is set by a different coach), and the two times are added; the lowest combined time wins. Gates are spaced wider than in slalom, and the course has a set vertical drop range. Missing a gate results in disqualification. Equipment follows the same FIS specifications as the other speed disciplines.

History

Giant slalom joined the Olympic program in 1936 (Garmisch-Partenkirchen) and the FIS World Championships in 1931. It is considered the most balanced alpine discipline. Legends include Alberto Tomba (ITA), Ingemar Stenmark (SWE) and Mikaela Shiffrin (USA).
