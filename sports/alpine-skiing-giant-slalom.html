Alpine Skiing Giant Slalom

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Giant slalom (GS) is the classic alpine skiing discipline: athletes make two runs on different courses set on a long slope with wide-spaced gates, and the two times are added — combining speed with clean, rhythmic turning. It is governed by the FIS and has been an Olympic event since Garmisch-Partenkirchen 1936 (International Ski and Snowboard Federation [FIS], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c).

Among the athletes who shape the discipline today, the leading women come first: Italy's Federica Brignone won Olympic giant slalom gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 in 2:13.50, and the United States' Mikaela Shiffrin — the most successful World Cup racer of all time — also stands at the top of the standings. Among the men, Brazil's Lucas Pinheiro Braathen won Olympic gold in 2:25.00 — the first Olympic alpine skiing gold medal for Brazil — and Switzerland's Marco Odermatt is the reigning World Cup overall champion (FIS, n.d.-a; FIS, n.d.-b).

Rules

Under FIS rules, each athlete completes two runs on different courses — the second course is set by a different course setter — and the two times are added; the lowest combined time wins. In the second run, the top 30 starters begin in reverse order of their first-run results. Gates are spaced wider than in Alpine Skiing Slalom, the course must meet fixed vertical-drop ranges per category, and missing a gate — both ski tips must cross the gate line — means disqualification. Equipment follows the same FIS specifications as the other disciplines: prescribed minimum ski lengths and radii, and a mandatory helmet (FIS, n.d.-c). Men and women race under the identical rule set.

History

Giant slalom joined the Olympic program at Garmisch-Partenkirchen 1936 and the FIS World Ski Championships in 1931, and it is widely considered the most balanced of the alpine disciplines. Its icons include Sweden's Ingemar Stenmark, with 86 World Cup victories, and Italy's Alberto Tomba — "La Bomba", a three-time Olympic champion. On the women's side, the USA's Mikaela Shiffrin won Olympic GS gold at PyeongChang 2018, and Italy's Federica Brignone followed with gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 (FIS, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
