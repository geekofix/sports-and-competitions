Monster Truck Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Monster truck racing is motorsport in which heavily modified pickup trucks with huge off-road tires (originally 48-inch, today often 66-inch tires) compete on dirt courses in stadiums and arenas, in head-to-head races over jumps and obstacles and in judged freestyle stunt competitions. The trucks weigh around 10,000 to 12,000 pounds and carry supercharged V8 engines. The largest series is Monster Jam in the United States, sanctioned by the United States Hot Rod Association (USHRA) with strict safety standards and now owned by Feld Motorsports.

Rules

At a typical event, trucks first race head-to-head on a prescribed course; the first truck across the finish line advances, with the format culminating at the annual World Finals where racing and freestyle world champions are crowned. In freestyle, each driver gets a set time (usually two minutes) to perform jumps, wheelies, donuts and other stunts over ramps and crushed cars, and is scored by judges or, at some events, by fan voting via an app. All trucks must pass a USHRA safety inspection covering roll cages, fuel cells, harnesses and fire suppression, and every truck carries a remote ignition interrupt device so officials can shut down an unsafe run. Separate skill competitions include two-wheel skills (wheelies), donuts and high jump.

History

The sport began in the late 1970s when trucks such as Bigfoot, built by Bob Chandler in 1974, performed at truck-pulling and mud-bogging events, and the first side-by-side race took place in August 1983 between Bigfoot and USA-1, filmed for the TV show That's Incredible. By 1985 promoters such as the USHRA and TNT Motorsports were racing trucks regularly; the USHRA bought TNT in 1991 and the first Monster Jam-branded event took place in October 1992 in Pontiac, Michigan. Freestyle became a judged competition in 2000, and the numbered Monster Jam World Finals began in 2000 in Las Vegas, with earlier USHRA world finals dating to 1987. Famous trucks and drivers include Grave Digger (Dennis and Adam Anderson), Bigfoot, Maximum Destruction and El Toro Loco.
