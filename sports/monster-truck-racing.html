Monster Truck Racing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Monster truck racing is the motorsport in which custom-built monster trucks — machines of at least 10,000 lb (4,536 kg), roughly 12 ft (3.7 m) tall on 66-inch (1.68 m) terra tires with supercharged V8 engines of around 1,500 hp — compete head-to-head in racing, freestyle, skills and high jump competitions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is sanctioned in the United States by the United States Hot Rod Association (USHRA), and its premier touring show is Monster Jam, operated by Feld Motor Sports — part of Feld Entertainment since 2008 — which stages arena shows and the annual World Finals (Monster Jam, n.d.-a). At World Finals XXV, held 3–4 July 2026 at Rice-Eccles Stadium in Salt Lake City, the 2026 champions were Tristan England (JCB DIGatron, skills), Todd LeDuc (Megalodon, racing), Ryan Anderson (Son-uva Digger, high jump) and Blake Granger (Monster Mutt Dalmatian, freestyle), while Tyler Menninga (Grave Digger) won the 2026 JCB Racing Championship; Tom Meents and Lupe Soza were inducted into the Monster Jam Hall of Fame in 2026 (Monster Jam, n.d.-b). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women drivers include Krysten Anderson (Grave Digger), the first female Monster Jam series champion (2022 Arena Series Central), as well as Linsey Weenk (Max-D), Brianna Mahon, Ashley Sanford and Angelina Nock (Monster Jam, n.d.-a).

Rules

Racing is a heads-up, single-elimination contest: two trucks run side by side over a course of ramps and obstacles, and the first across the finish line advances (USHRA, n.d.). Freestyle is a timed routine of about two minutes in which six judges each award 0–10 points; the highest and lowest scores are dropped and the rest summed, for a maximum of 40 (Monster Jam, n.d.-a). Skills competitions are judged obstacle courses, and high jump is measured by the greatest height cleared. Trucks must pass USHRA safety inspection — roll cage, fuel cell, kill switch, five-point harnesses for the driver — and drivers wear fire suits, helmets and neck braces; unsafe driving or rule violations bring disqualification (USHRA, n.d.). Women and men compete in all disciplines on equal terms, and the Monster Jam roster includes a growing number of female drivers (Monster Jam, n.d.-a).

History

The first monster truck was Bob Chandler's Bigfoot, a 1974 Ford F-250 pickup fitted with oversized tires, which Chandler used to crush cars for promotion; the term "monster truck" was coined by event promoter Bob George in the early 1980s in Pontiac, Michigan (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first side-by-side race between two monster trucks took place in August 1983 at the Pontiac Silverdome, and monster truck shows were broadcast on television by TNT Motorsports from 1988 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Monster Jam name was first used on 17 October 1992 at the Pontiac Silverdome, and Feld Entertainment acquired the property in 2008, turning it into the world's largest touring motorsports show family with the annual World Finals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
