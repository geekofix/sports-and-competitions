Salburun

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Salburun (Kyrgyz salburun; also salbuurun, “hunting complex”) is the traditional mounted hunting sport of Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan, combining a trained golden eagle (burkut), the Kyrgyz taigan sighthound and mounted archery. In the burkut saluu discipline of the World Nomad Games, the hunter gallops on horseback while a lure — a fox skin or a model fox or hare — is dragged behind on a rope, and the eagle must strike and hold it; points are scored for the catch, measured partly by how far down the field the bird takes the lure, and for the speed and skill of the eagle. At the Games no live prey is used: the eagle catches the dragged lure, which is recovered and reused. Salburun has been a World Nomad Games discipline since 2016 (20 athletes in the burkut saluu event in 2026), alongside the related kush saluu (falconry), dalba and taigan zharysh (taigan dog racing) events. Women take part in modern eagle-hunting festivals and competitions in Kazakhstan and Mongolia. Within the Central Asian nomad games on this site, its closest relatives are Shagai, Kunan Chabysh, Naadam; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In the burkut saluu competition the hunter rides out with the eagle on the glove; on the signal a lure (fox fur or a dummy) is dragged behind a galloping horse, and the eagle must launch, strike and hold the lure — in the “undok” and “chyrga” sub-events the bird catches the moving lure in flight, and in dalba-style events the catch is made at speed along the course. Judges score the take: a clean catch of the flying lure scores highest, with deductions for a late launch or a lure recovered without a hold. The taigan zharysh races pair a horse and rider with the taigan dog over a set course. Equipment: a trained golden eagle or falcon, a horse, a padded glove, and the lure; birds are bred and trained for years, and falconry is recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage (inscribed multinationally in 2010).

History

Eagle hunting (berkutchilik) is a millennia-old tradition of the Kazakh and Kyrgyz steppe hunters of the Altai and Tian Shan: hunters ride out in winter with a golden eagle on the fist to take foxes and hares. The Kazakh eagle hunters of Bayan-Ölgii province are famous worldwide, and Mongolia's Golden Eagle Festival at Ölgii has drawn some 400 hunters on horseback since the late 1990s. The sport entered the international arena as a demonstration sport at the first World Nomad Games in 2014 and became a medal discipline at the 2016 Games in Cholpon-Ata, where the program included burkut saluu (undok, chyrga), dalba oynotuu and taigan zharysh. The tradition is documented on Soviet-era stamps and in the photography of Kyrgyz huntsmen.
