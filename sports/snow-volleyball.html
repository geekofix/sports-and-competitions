Snow Volleyball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snow volleyball is the winter counterpart of beach volleyball: two teams of three players contest a ball over a net on a snow-covered court, aiming to ground it on the opponent's side. Born in Austria in the late 2000s, the sport is governed internationally by the FIVB (with the European Volleyball Confederation, CEV, as its development driver), which since 2019 has staged a Snow Volleyball World Tour and openly targets a future place on the Winter Olympic programme.

Rules

Snow volleyball follows the beach-volleyball model with winter adaptations: two teams of up to three players on court (plus substitutes), a ball sent over the net with a maximum of three team touches, no consecutive touches by one player, and rally scoring with sets played under FIVB snow rules. The court is dug into packed snow with lines marked, the net is set at beach-volleyball height, and players wear thermal clothing, gloves and traction footwear (soccer-style cleats were used at the PyeongChang showcase) over a compacted snow surface that makes sliding saves part of the game. Matches are best of three sets under FIVB Snow Volleyball Rules (2021\u20132024), with smaller courts' endless rallies a feature; the 3x3 format is the standard international game (see our dedicated 3x3 page).

History

Informal snow volleyball has been played for decades in winter-sport countries such as Russia, Austria and Switzerland without codified rules, and unofficial tournaments popped up in Austria and Switzerland in the late 1990s. The modern sport crystallised around the Austrian promoter Martin Kaswurm, whose 2008 initiatives in Wagrain led to a local Snow Volleyball Tour in 2009 and recognition by the Austrian Volleyball Association in 2011; the tour expanded across Europe by 2013. The CEV officially adopted snow volleyball in October 2015, launching the CEV Snow Volleyball European Tour in 2016 and the first European championships in Austria in March 2018 (with 17 national federations staging qualifying championships); the FIVB then launched the inaugural Snow Volleyball World Tour in 2019 with stops in Wagrain and Plan de Corones and, first outside Europe, Bariloche, Argentina \u2014 where nations such as Brazil and Argentina claimed their first international medals in any ice or snow sport. Snow volleyball was showcased during the PyeongChang 2018 Winter Olympics (with beach and indoor stars playing at the Austria House), at the Lausanne 2020 Winter Youth Olympic Games festival programme, and at further World Tour events, while the FIVB pursues its long-term goal of adding volleyball to the Winter Games \u2014 which would make it the first sport on both summer and winter programmes.
