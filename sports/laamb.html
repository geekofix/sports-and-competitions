Laamb

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Laamb (Wolof; French: lutte sénégalaise, lutte avec frappe) is Senegal's national sport: folk wrestling in a sandy circular arena where — uniquely among West African traditions — hand blows (frappe) are allowed in addition to grappling; a bout ends when a wrestler is knocked down, touches the ground with hand, knee or back, or is ejected from the ring (CNGL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed by the Comité National de Gestion de la Lutte (CNGL), with the Arène Nationale in Pikine (20,000 seats, opened 2018) as its temple and the Roi des Arènes (King of the Arenas) title since 1984; on 5 April 2026 Sa Thiès defeated Modou Lô at the Arène Nationale to take the crown (Senegal Activities, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). Women's laamb exists but faces social resistance in the predominantly Muslim country, per the Oxford Research Encyclopedia (Spear, 2024). Within the combat sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Kokawa, Evala, Oil Wrestling, Belt Wrestling, Wrestling, Beach Wrestling; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Bouts are preceded by ritual (griot drumming, marabout blessings, women's chants) and take place in a sand circle, formally bounded by sandbags in modern arenas. Victory comes by throwing the opponent, forcing any part but the feet to touch the ground, or pushing him out of the ring; with frappe, punches to the body are allowed but not to the head. There is no weight class — fighters are matched by size — and the professional season runs roughly October to July with televised galas (Wikipedia, n.d.; CNGL, n.d.). Men's and women's competitions are organised separately, women's laamb being a small but growing scene (Spear, 2024).

History

Laamb roots in the Serer people's warrior training and initiation rites (the word derives from the Serer 'Fara-Lamb Siin'); the 14th-century wrestler Boukar Djilak Faye of the Kingdom of Sine is among the earliest recorded. It became a mass spectator sport in Dakar in the 20th century and is now Senegal's second most popular sport after football, with megastars such as Yékini (Yakhya Diop), Balla Gaye 2, Bombardier and Modou Lô (Wikipedia, n.d.; Spear, 2024).
