Tipcat

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Tipcat (tip-cat, also knurr and spell) is the old English flicking game and a documented precursor of baseball: a small piece of wood tapered at both ends (the “cat”) is balanced on a mark, flipped into the air with a bat, and struck as far as possible while the fielders try to catch it or hit the bat. The same game family includes gilli danda in South Asia. It was played by children — boys and girls — in England and New England from at least the 17th century and survives in schoolyards and heritage festivals. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are Gilli Danda, Chindro, Ekka Dokka; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

A “cat” about 5–10 cm long is set on a base line; the batter taps one end with the bat to flip the cat into the air and then strikes it. Fielders try to catch the cat before it lands (batter out) or, if it lands, throw it to hit the bat (a “run out”). Missing three strikes is an out; points are scored for distance and extra hits, and play rotates. In the indoor variant (as played in Gonabad, Iran, from 2014) a softer ball and a smaller court are used.

History

Tip-cat is documented in English children's game books from the 1700s (for example in “What to do at recess,” 1910) and was carried to North America, where it influenced early bat-and-ball games and baseball. The related South Asian game gilli danda (from ghaṭikā, “tip-cat”) shows the same game family on the other side of Asia. Heritage and school programs keep tip-cat alive in England today.
