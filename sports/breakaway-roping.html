Breakaway Roping

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Breakaway roping (also called calf roping with a breakaway) is a timed rodeo event: a cowboy on horseback ropes a running calf, dismounts, runs to it, throws it down and ties any three of its legs together with a piggin' string; when the rope is tied, the cowboy signals "breakaway" and the clock stops. It is governed in professional rodeo by the PRCA and the WPRA in the United States.

Rules

Under PRCA/WPRA rules, the horse starts behind a barrier that releases when the calf crosses the start line, giving the calf a head start. The rider ropes the calf's horns or neck, turns the horse to stop it, dismounts, and flanks the calf (throws it by hand onto its side), then ties three legs together with a soft piggin' string that must hold until the official checks it (a 6-second "tie" requirement). The clock stops when the rider raises both hands to signal the tie is complete ("breakaway"). The fastest clean time wins; penalties include breaking the barrier early (10 seconds) or failing to tie.

History

Breakaway roping grew out of ranch work in the American West, where calves were roped for branding or doctoring. It became a women's professional rodeo event in the 1940s-50s, since women were excluded from timed roughstock events, and the Women's Professional Rodeo Association (founded 1948) made it a championship event. Today it is one of the most popular events at PRCA rodeos and the WPRA World Championship, with stars like Jackie Crawford and Mary Burger dominating the record books.
