Laser Tag

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Laser tag is a recreational and competitive skirmish sport in which players use infrared-emitting phasers to "tag" sensors on opponents' vests and arena targets, with electronic scoring deciding the winner (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Despite the name, no laser is fired: the phaser emits a modulated infrared beam around 850–950 nm that is eye-safe and invisible, decoded by sensors on the vest and headband (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Equipment consists of a phaser (gun) and a sensor vest — sometimes a headband — plus arena elements such as respawn points, control zones and bases depending on the game mode: elimination, capture the flag, domination, free-for-all and mission-based modes are all common (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Competitive laser tag exists at club, national and international level — the Zone Laser Tag World Championships (ZLTWC) pitches national teams against each other in several competitive game modes — and teams are routinely mixed-gender, with men's, women's and youth divisions at major events (ZLTWC, n.d.). Military forces use the same principle in MILES-type training systems, as the Ukrainian army photo shows (АрміяInform, 2023).

Rules

A typical game lasts 10–30 minutes. Each hit on an opponent's sensors scores points (usually 10–100 depending on hit zone) and often triggers a short "stun" during which the victim's phaser is disabled; hits on teammates can cost points. Players respawn at designated points after a few seconds, and game modes define winning conditions — highest score, most eliminations, or most control points captured (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Equipment rules in competitive play are standardized by the arena system (e.g. Zone/Delta Strike): fixed phaser power and fire rate, sensor sensitivity settings, and safety rules such as keeping both hands on the phaser. Arenas use blacklight, fog and themed obstacles for atmosphere; the infrared signal itself is what counts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

There are no age restrictions beyond venue rules (typically 7+), and men's, women's, mixed and youth divisions are standard at tournaments (ZLTWC, n.d.).

History

The idea predates the arenas: in the late 1970s the US Army deployed MILES laser-based combat-training systems, and in 1979 the Star Trek Electronic Phaser Guns toy became the first infrared toy set. In 1982 George Carter III began designing a scored arena system — inspired in 1977 by Star Wars — and opened the first Photon center in Dallas, Texas, on 28 March 1984; the International Laser Tag Association honored him in 2005 as "Inventor and Founder of the laser tag industry" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

In 1986 Worlds of Wonder sold Lazer Tag toy sets alongside Photon; both fads faded by the early 1990s, but dedicated arena chains (Laser Quest, founded 1989 in Manchester, UK) and competitive play kept the sport alive. In March 2009 Germany briefly announced a ban on laser tag after the Winnenden shooting, claiming such games trivialized violence — later retracted (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
