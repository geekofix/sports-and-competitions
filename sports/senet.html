Senet

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Senet is the most famous ancient Egyptian board game (c. 3100 BC, played until the Roman era): a 3x10 grid of 30 squares; players move pieces according to dice/throw-sticks, with special squares (e.g. the 'House of Rebirth'); the goal is to move all your pieces off the board first. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are Mehen, Latrunculi, Nine Men's Morris; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Walters Art Museum, n.d.).

Figure: Interfase. (2013-04-27). A senet game set in the Egyptian Museum, Berlin [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Senet_game_in_Berlin.jpg

Rules

3x10 board; 5–10 pieces per player (later sets); moves by four throw-sticks or knucklebones; landing on certain squares has effects (e.g. must retreat); blocking rules apply; first to clear all pieces wins (Walters Art Museum, n.d.).

History

Senet appears in tomb paintings from c. 3100 BC (e.g. Nefertari's tomb, photographed); the Walters Art Museum and British Museum hold original boards and pieces; the Royal Ontario Museum displays a senet board; Berlin's Egyptian Museum has a playing set (2013) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
