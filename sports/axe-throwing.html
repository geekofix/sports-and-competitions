Axe Throwing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Axe throwing is a target sport in which competitors throw small hatchets at a wooden "bullseye" target from a fixed distance, usually 20 ft (6 m). It is governed internationally by the World Axe Throwing League (WATL), which runs the World Axe Throwing Championship, and in North America by the International Axe Throwing Federation (IATF) and its national member organizations. Matches are popular spectator events and have been broadcast on ESPN.

Rules

Under WATL rules, each competitor throws a set number of axes (typically 10 in professional divisions) at a target divided into scoring rings, with the innermost bullseye ("clutch") worth the most points. The throwing line is 20 ft from the target face, and axes must stick blade-first into the wood; a "clutch" throw is the tie-breaker. In league and championship play, competitors alternate throws and the highest total score advances. Different federations use small variations (number of throws, target size), but the 20-ft distance and stick-scoring system are standard.

History

Throwing axes at targets is an old lumberjack pastime in North America. Modern competitive axe throwing took off in Canada in the mid-2000s with urban axe-throwing bars and leagues. The World Axe Throwing League was founded in 2017 and held its first World Axe Throwing Championship, and televised league play brought the sport to a mass audience on ESPN.
