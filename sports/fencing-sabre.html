Fencing Sabre

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Sabre is the cutting-and-thrusting weapon of fencing, in which the valid target is the entire body above the waist (including arms and head), and the fencer who attacks first with right of way scores. See Fencing for the shared rules; this entry covers sabre-specific rules at the FIE World Championships and the Olympics.

Rules

In sabre, any touch with the point or the edge of the blade on the opponent's upper body (head, arms, torso) counts, provided the attacker has right of way: the fencer who initiates the attack, or the defender who parries and ripostes. If both fencers attack simultaneously and both land a hit, both score a touch. The bout is to 15 touches in three 3-minute periods; sabre is the fastest weapon, with bouts often decided by explosive attacks and counter-attacks. Corps-à-corps is penalized; covering the target or turning the back is a foul. Sabre is the only weapon where the rear foot crossing behind the en-garde line is a warning. Team events use relay bouts to 45.

History

The sabre evolved from the cavalry saber of the 19th century and was included in the Olympic program from Athens 1896 (it was the weapon of choice for military officers). The FIE standardized its rules, and the electric scoring was adopted in the 1950s-80s (sabre was the last weapon to get electronic scoring, in the 1980s). Hungary has historically dominated sabre, with golds from Aladár Gerevich, Pál Kovács and Áron Szilágyi (three consecutive individual Olympic golds 2012-2020), and Italy and the United States are also strong. Women's sabre was added to the Olympics in 2004 (Athens), won by Mariel Zagunis.
