Juego del Palo

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Juego del palo (Canarian: banot, "game of the stick") is the traditional stick-fighting sport of the Canary Islands: two players fence with a slender wooden stick (palo medio, 1.2–1.8 m, wielded two-handed) or a long staff (palo grande/garrote, up to 4 m), attacking and defending without protective gear — safety comes from controlled, non-full-force technique. Nine primary modern styles exist (Deniz, Morales, Verga, Acosta, Quintero, Vidal, Conejero, garrote, lata), named after islands and founders; FEJUPAL (founded 1997, Las Palmas) governs it. Men and women compete. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are Eskrima, Tahtib, Nguni Stick Fighting, Fencing; the related pages are listed under Important Links (JWMA — Journal of Western Martial Arts, 2000).

Rules

Traditional juego del palo has no points — a bout is won by demonstrating dominance/control or by agreement after exchanges; modern competitive juego del palo (FEJUPAL rules) uses protective gear (fencing mask, padded vest), timed rounds, and scoring for clean touches with the stick, plus separate competitions for palo medio and palo grande styles. Attacks are controlled and not made at full force on the body in traditional play (JWMA — Journal of Western Martial Arts, 2000).

History

The art is attributed to the Guanches, the indigenous Canarians, before the 15th-century Spanish conquest: Gaspar Frutuoso (1478 anecdote) and Leonardo Torriani's 1590 history describe ritual stick combat (magodo/amodeghe staves) between warriors on stone platforms. It survived as a shepherds' and herders' art and was revived from the 1970s; FEJUPAL was founded in 1997 and academic conferences study it. Resemblances to Portuguese jogo do pau and Venezuelan juego del garrote are debated (Wikipedia, n.d.).
