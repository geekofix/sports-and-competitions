Stock Car Racing

≈ 5 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Stock car racing is oval- and road-course auto racing with production-based cars, dominated in the United States by NASCAR's Cup Series — the top tier of the National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing, founded by Bill France Sr. in 1948 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; NASCAR, n.d.). The 2026 season was the 78th of NASCAR professional stock car racing, opening with the 68th Daytona 500 on 15 February 2026 and ending at Homestead–Miami Speedway on 8 November 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

For the first time since 2013 NASCAR used "The Chase" championship format in 2026: the top 16 drivers qualified after 26 regular-season races, points were reset (top seed 2,100, with a 25-point premium for the regular-season leader) and the driver with the most points after the final 10 Chase races became champion — no eliminations (NASCAR, 2026). Kyle Larson won his third Cup title and second consecutively, clinching after stage 1 of the Homestead finale — the first back-to-back champion since Jimmie Johnson (2006–2010) and the first to clinch before the end of the finale since Matt Kenseth in 2003; Christopher Bell finished second and Denny Hamlin third (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Toyota won the manufacturers' championship for the first time since 2019, and Connor Zilisch was Rookie of the Year (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The season was overshadowed by the death of two-time champion Kyle Busch, who died of sepsis on 21 May 2026 — the first active full-time driver to die in a season since Dale Earnhardt in 2001 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The Cup Series runs 36 points races per season on ovals, road courses and — since 2023 — street circuits, all in the Next Gen car introduced in 2022: a spec chassis with a ~5.8 L pushrod V8, sequential gearbox, single-lug wheels and Goodyear tires, entered by Chevrolet, Ford and Toyota teams (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Races are staged (stage points), with the Daytona 500 — the sport's most prestigious race, held since 1959 and opening the season since 1982 — as the centerpiece (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Points are awarded for race and stage finishes; in 2026 the top 16 by points after 26 races entered the 10-race Chase with reset points (2,100 down by seed, 25-point premium for the regular-season champion) and the highest total after Homestead wins the title (NASCAR, 2026). The second tier is the O'Reilly Auto Parts Series (Xfinity until 2025) and the Craftsman Truck Series (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Men and women compete in the same races — women such as Hailie Deegan have raced full-time in the Truck and Xfinity series and in Cup starts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

NASCAR was founded in December 1948 by Bill France Sr.; the first Strictly Stock race ran at Charlotte Speedway on 19 February 1949 (won by Jim Roper), and the series became the Grand National in 1950, the Winston Cup in 1971 and the modern Cup Series in 2022 alongside the Next Gen car (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Daytona 500, first run in 1959 (Lee Petty), became the first stock car race televised live flag-to-flag (CBS, 1979), when Richard Petty famously inherited the win after Cale Yarborough and Donnie Allison crashed on the last lap (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Richard Petty ("The King") holds the records with 200 wins and seven championships (1964, 1967, 1971–1975, 1979); Dale Earnhardt and Jimmie Johnson also won seven titles each, Johnson's five straight from 2006 to 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In 2023 Shane van Gisbergen won the first Cup street race in Chicago in his debut (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
