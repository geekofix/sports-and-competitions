Snowboard Slopestyle

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Snowboard slopestyle is the freestyle discipline in which riders descend a course of rails, boxes, kickers and jumps \u2014 the terrain-park architecture of a modern ski resort \u2014 linking rail tricks with aerial spins and flips in a judged run from top to bottom. It was added to the Winter Olympic programme at Sochi 2014, where Jamie Anderson and Sage Kotsenburg took the inaugural golds, and it is contested on the FIS World Cup, world championships and X Games circuits alongside big air and halfpipe.

Rules

A slopestyle course contains a rail section typically followed by three to four jumps, and riders perform one timed descent \u2014 in Olympic finals two or three runs with the best counting \u2014 each scored 0\u2013100 on overall impression: the difficulty and variety of the trick selection, execution (grab quality, clean landings), amplitude on jumps, use of the course's features and creativity all judged, with falls or hand-touches resulting in heavy deductions because run scores are judged, not timed. Trick families include jibbing (rail slides, 270s on and off), spins and flips from the kickers (double corks, 1260s and beyond at elite level), and each rider must hit both the rail and jump features to score well; competition riders wear soft boots, twin boards and helmets, and courses are built to FIS and X Games specifications for safety and length.

History

Slopestyle grew organically from resort terrain parks in the 1990s and 2000s \u2014 the halfpipe's younger cousin, borrowing skateboarding's handrail culture and the park features built for everyday riders \u2014 and it was formalised through contests early in the 2000s, most famously the X Games slopestyle, which became snowboarding's proving ground before it had FIS sanction (with Torstein Horgmo, Mark McMorris and Jamie Anderson building their names there before the discipline had Olympic sanction). FIS adopted the discipline into its world championship programme from 2013 — the medal table at Stoneham that year showing Roope Tonteri winning men's slopestyle and Spencer O'Brien the women's crown, with Mark McMorris taking silver — and the IOC added slopestyle to the Games at Sochi 2014, where Jamie Anderson (USA) and Sage Kotsenburg (USA) won the inaugural golds; Anderson defended at PyeongChang 2018 (with Red Gerard winning the men's), Zoi Sadowski-Synnott took gold at Beijing 2022, and the modern field \u2014 Mia Brookes, the 2023 world champion, Kokomo Murase, Tess Coady, Chris Corning and Su Yiming among them \u2014 is the deepest in snowboarding's freestyle branch.
