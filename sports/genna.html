Genna

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Genna is Ethiopia's traditional field-hockey-like game played at Christmas (Genna, 7 January): teams of about ten players including a goalkeeper use hand-carved eucalyptus sticks with a curved end to roll, pass and shoot a small wooden ball ('rur') through the opponents' goal on an open field (Ethiopian Herald, 2017; Hockey Museum, n.d.). Ethiopian legend holds that shepherds invented the game on the night of Jesus's birth; the word derives from Greek genna (birth). The Ethiopian Cultural Sports Federation governs genna alongside other cultural sports, and it is played by men and women in villages and at urban Christmas festivals (allAfrica, 2017). Within the hockey family on this site, its closest relatives are Field Hockey, Indoor Field Hockey, Bandy, Hockey5s, Floorball, Sand Hockey; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Teams of about 10 per side play on a large unmarked field with two goal posts at opposite ends; the curved stick is used to roll, hit and shoot the wooden ball. There are no fixed boundaries — grazing cattle occasionally join in. Matches are timed informally at festivals; men's and women's games are both played (Ethiopian Herald, 2017; Hockey Museum, n.d.).

History

Genna is documented in Amhara manuscripts and 19th-century photographs as a Christmas-season sport; emperors Tewodros and Menelik II organised matches at court. The Hockey Museum in the UK documents genna as one of the oldest known hockey-like games (Hockey Museum, n.d.; allAfrica, 2017).
