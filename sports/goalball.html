Goalball

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Goalball is a team sport designed specifically for athletes with a vision impairment: two teams of three players throw a 1.25 kg ball containing bells toward the opponent's goal on an 18 × 9 m court, locating it by sound; everyone — including sighted players — wears eyeshades so all competitors are equally blindfolded (International Blind Sports Federation [IBSA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ball is thrown by hand and never kicked; players dive to block and use ear–hand coordination, and the court lines are raised so they can be felt (IBSA, n.d.-a). Goalball was devised in 1946 by the German Hans Lorenzen and the Austrian Sepp Reindle to rehabilitate visually impaired World War II veterans, was a demonstration sport at the 1972 Heidelberg Paralympics and a full Paralympic sport from Toronto 1976; the first world championship was held in Vöcklamarkt, Austria, in 1978 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by IBSA (founded 1981), with 99 competing nations for men and 61 for women as of July 2025 and over 220 international referees (IBSA, n.d.-b). The 2026 IBSA Goalball World Championships were held in Hangzhou, China, from 6–16 June 2026 (matches 10–15 June) with 16 men's and 16 women's teams — the hosts China completed a historic double, winning both the men's and women's world titles, with China and Germany taking the men's LA 2028 qualification places and China and Israel the women's (IBSA, n.d.-b; Inside the Games, n.d.); the 2026 Youth World Championships follow in Astana, Kazakhstan, 25 November – 5 December 2026 (IBSA, n.d.-b) (IBSA — International Blind Sports Federation, n.d.-a) (IBSA — International Blind Sports Federation, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are China's national team, 2026 world champions in Hangzhou and favourites for Paralympic gold at Los Angeles 2028 (Inside the Games, n.d.). On the men's side, China are also the reigning world champions, with Germany, Brazil, the United States — fourth at the 2026 worlds after losing the bronze match 5–2 to Türkiye — and Lithuania among the leading nations (Inside the Games, n.d.; Turnstone, n.d.).

Rules

Under the IBSA Goalball Rules and Regulations 2025, a game consists of two 12-minute halves of playing time (formerly 10-minute halves) with a 5-minute half-time, on a court 18 m long and 9 m wide — usually a volleyball court — with goals spanning the full 9 m width and 1.83 m high; the court is marked with tape over twine so the raised lines can be felt, and each team defends a 3 m wide team area plus a neutral area (IBSA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ball (1.25 kg, about 24 cm in diameter, with eight holes and internal bells) is thrown from the team area; a player may not throw more than three consecutive times — after the third throw a throw-in is required — and the ball must cross the neutral area into the opponent's half within 10 seconds of the team gaining control (IBSA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Defenders block with the body and must get up within 3 seconds (10 seconds for the goalkeeper after a save); a ball that is thrown out of bounds or over the end line results in a throw-in or corner (IBSA, n.d.-a). Offences — high ball, early throw, illegal defence, delaying, unsportsmanlike conduct — are penalized with penalty throws (one player against the goalkeeper from 6 m) or team penalties (10 seconds in the penalty area) (IBSA, n.d.-a). If the score is level at full time, a golden-goal overtime of one 6-minute period is played; if still tied, extra throws (paired shootout, then sudden death) decide the winner (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's tournaments use identical rules with separate competitions; all players wear inspected eyeshades (plus optional eyepatches) so that totally blind and partially sighted athletes compete on equal terms (IBSA, n.d.-a).

History

Goalball was invented in 1946 by Hans Lorenzen of Germany — later a professor at the German Sport University Cologne — and Sepp Reindle of Austria, as a rehabilitation exercise for blinded World War II veterans, using a ball with bells and raised court lines; it evolved into a competitive game in the 1950s and 1960s, was demonstrated at the 1972 Heidelberg Paralympics and became a Paralympic sport at the 1976 Toronto Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first world championship was held in Vöcklamarkt, Austria, in 1978, and the IBSA Goalball Subcommittee — now part of the IBSA founded in 1981 — has organized the quadrennial World Championships ever since (IBSA, n.d.-b). Goalball was also played at the 2025 World Games in Chengdu (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the icons of the sport are the long-dominant Northern European and North American teams — Finland, Sweden, Lithuania, Canada and the United States — with Brazil now the leading non-European power and China the 2026 world champion in both divisions (Inside the Games, n.d.; IBSA, n.d.-b).
