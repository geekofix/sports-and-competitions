Goalball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Goalball is a team sport designed for blind and visually impaired athletes, played by two teams of three on a court the size of a futsal pitch (18 m x 9 m) with a sonorous ball (with bells inside) and goals spanning the full width of each end line. It is governed by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA) through its Blind Football (Goalball) section at blindfootball.sport, and has been a Paralympic sport since Toronto 1976.

Rules

Under the IBSA Goalball Rules, each team fields three players who wear eyeshades (blindfolds) to equalize B1/B2/B3 vision classes, plus up to three substitutes. The ball must be thrown by hand within 10 seconds of gaining possession and must touch the floor in the team's own half or the neutral zone before crossing into the opponent's half. Players defend by lying or crouching to block the ball with their body; a player may defend the ball more than once consecutively only after a teammate has touched it. The ball is put back into play from a throw-in after a goal, block-out or penalty. Matches at the Paralympics and World Championships are two 12-minute halves (stopped clock for stoppages); overtime and golden goal decide knockout ties. Goals are 9 m wide and 1.3 m high, with 1 m high sideboards along the touchlines.

History

Goalball was invented in 1946 by Austrian Hanz Lorenzen and German Sepp Reindle as a rehabilitation sport for blinded WWII veterans, combining elements of handball and bowling. It entered the Paralympic program in Toronto 1976 and became a full medal sport in 1984 (Arnhem). The IBSA (founded 1981) governs it internationally through the IBSA Blind Football subcommittee, running World Championships and the Paralympic tournament. Brazil and Lithuania have dominated the men's game, while Canada, the US, China and Sweden lead the women's. National teams from over 50 countries compete at the World Championships, with growing professionalism and video review at elite events.
