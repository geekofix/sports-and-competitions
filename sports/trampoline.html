Trampoline

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The trampoline is the apparatus of rebound gymnastics, and trampolining is the sport performed on it: athletes bounce on a tensioned webbed bed, launching to heights of up to 10 metres to perform somersaults and twists in set routines. From backyard equipment to a gymnastics Olympic discipline, the word covers both the device (invented in the 1930s) and the sport it created.

Rules

Competitive trampolining is performed on a standard trampoline (a steel frame with a 4-by-2-metre bed attached by springs) in routines of 10 elements: difficulty judges score each skill (somersaults and twists in prescribed shapes), execution judges deduct for form errors (legs apart, poor body position, travel toward the frame), and routines must start and finish upright on the feet with no pauses between skills. Competition rules govern time of flight in some formats (added as a scoring component at the Olympic Games), the maximum number of skills, permitted landing positions (feet, seat, front and back are all allowed) and safety standards (thickness of end decks and supervision requirements).

History

The trampoline was invented in the 1930s by the American gymnasts George Nissen and Larry Griswold, who built the first apparatus from angle iron, canvas and springs, and it spread through gymnastics clubs and military pilot training before competitive organizations formed in the postwar years. The International Trampoline Federation ran world championships from 1964, the sport joined the gymnastics federation structure in the late 1990s (with tumbling and double mini trampoline as companion disciplines), and trampoline made its Olympic debut at Sydney 2000. The apparatus itself entered worldwide recreation (backyard trampolines, parks and the newer fitness-trampoline formats), feeding gymnastics and freestyle sports from Trampoline gymnastics to Slamball, and its heartbeat remains the sport's pure form: ten soaring skills, judged to the tenth of a point.
