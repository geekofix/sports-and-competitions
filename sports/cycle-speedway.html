Cycle Speedway

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Cycle speedway is bicycle racing on short oval dirt tracks: up to four riders race four laps anticlockwise on brakeless, single-speed 26-inch bikes, leaning into the bends at speed. In Britain it is administered by British Cycling through its Cycle Speedway Commission, and internationally by the International Cycle Speedway Federation (ICSF), whose members include England, Scotland, Wales, Poland, Australia, the Netherlands and the US. The World Championships alternate biennially with European Championships.

Rules

Under the cycle speedway rules, races run over four laps of a 70-90 m oval track, starting from a gate or tape; up to four riders compete per race and the first across the line wins. Bikes are brakeless with a fixed gear, and riders wear helmets (mandatory since 1999), gloves and body protection. Riders may not touch the ground with a foot during the race, and dangerous riding is penalized. Points are awarded for placings across a meeting, with separate championships for adults, juniors and veterans.

History

Cycle speedway began in Britain around the 1920s, and the National Amateur Cycle Speedway Association (NACSA) was founded in 1950. The first international match was England versus the Netherlands at Earls Court on 26 October 1950, and the first world championship was held in Hilversum in 1958. Since then world and European championships have alternated every two years. British riders such as Jim Varnish (1985 world champion) and Brett Aitken became national heroes.
