Skyscraper Running

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Skyscraper running (tower running, vertical running) is the sport of racing up the internal stairwells of the world's tallest buildings, from the Empire State Building and Eiffel Tower to Taipei 101 and the Shanghai Tower. It is contested in time-trial and mass-start formats, attracts elite athletes from distance running, mountain and skyrunning backgrounds, and is ranked through the Towerrunning World Cup of the Towerrunning World Association (TWA) and the ISF's Vertical World Circuit — over 160 races on all continents are evaluated each year.

Rules

Races are point-to-point stairwell sprints from the lobby (or an assigned floor) to an observation deck, rooftop or landing, over anywhere from a few hundred to some 3,398 steps (the Shanghai Tower): the Empire State Building Run-Up climbs 1,576 steps up 86 floors to the observation deck, covering about 1,050 vertical feet, while Taipei 101's race sweeps 2,046 steps to the 91st floor. Formats differ by venue — most races send runners off in staggered individual starts against the clock, while mass-start races like the Empire State Building Run-Up launch waves of runners into a single narrow stairwell (women started five minutes ahead of the men in the ESBRU format) — and results are decided on elapsed time, with elite men completing the Empire State course in 10\u201312 minutes. Elite training emphasises stair intervals, quadriceps strength and the tactics of passing on tight flights, and athletes often take steps two at a time on buildings' unusually steep staircases; rankings are awarded by points per the World Cup's weighted system (Masters races carry higher factors).

History

Racing up stairs is an old pastime — the first recorded London stair challenge saw a barman run up the 311 steps of the Monument in 1730 — a competitive stair race was first staged on Bastille Day 1903 on the Rue Foyatier steps in Paris, and the first tower race proper was held at the Eiffel Tower in 1905, where 283 runners, cyclists and footballers contested what its organisers billed as the ultimate fitness test (it was repeated in 1906, returned in 1946 and 1995, and continues today as La Verticale de la Tour Eiffel). Tower racing reached Britain in university races at London's BT Tower (1968\u20131970), and the modern era began in the United States in 1978, when New York Road Runners founder Fred Lebow organised the first Empire State Building Run-Up — the oldest continuously running stair race in the world, won that year by Gary Muhrcke in 12:33. Further races spread through the 1980s at iconic towers in Singapore, Sydney and Europe; the sport's governing era arrived with the Towerrunning World Association's ranking and World Cup system (with weighted points since 2009) and the Vertical World Circuit of skyscraper races (founded 2009 under skyrunning's International Skyrunning Federation), while world championships were inaugurated in Doha in 2015 with champions Andrea Mayr and Piotr \u0141obodzi\u0144ski, and continued at Taipei 101 in 2018. Legends of the stairs include Germany's Thomas Dold — multiple ESBRU and Taipei 101 winner — and the dominant multi-year ESBRU women's champion Suzy Walsham of Australia.
