Roundnet

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Roundnet — best known by the brand name Spikeball — is a net sport played by two teams, usually of two players each, around a small circular net placed on the ground that resembles a mini trampoline. The game was invented in 1989 by the American toymaker and cartoonist Jeff Knurek and revived in 2008 by Chris Ruder's company Spikeball Inc., whose marketing made "spikeball" the common name for the sport; competitive roundnet is governed today by the International Roundnet Federation and national federations in Europe, the Americas and beyond.

Rules

Play begins with a serve from one team to the other across the net; after the serve, rallies proceed with teams alternating possession, each team having up to three touches (and no player hitting the ball twice in succession) to bounce the ball off the net so the opponent cannot return it. There are no court halves: after the serve, all players may move freely 360 degrees around the net. A point is lost by failing to return the ball to the net within three touches, letting it touch the ground, hitting the net's metal rim rather than the mesh surface, or letting the ball bounce twice on the net before the opponent plays it. Under official International Roundnet Federation rules, a non-hitting zone immediately around the net may be entered only to bump or set (never for the final hit of a possession), and matches are typically played to 15, 18 or 21 points, with rally scoring, win-by-two margins, and serve rotation rules derived from volleyball; sloppy play such as carrying the ball or double hits is a fault.

History

The game was created in 1989 by Jeff Knurek, who designed and marketed the original "Spikeball" set through the Japanese toy company Tomy; it enjoyed brief success in early-1990s American toy stores before fading away, as Knurek had not patented the game. In 2003 Chris Ruder rediscovered the game on a Hawaii beach trip, found the trademark had lapsed, and in Chicago founded Spikeball Inc. in 2007 (selling from 2008), redesigning the equipment for durability; his 2014 appearance on ABC's Shark Tank ended without a deal but gave the game mass exposure. The competitive infrastructure followed quickly: Spikeball sponsored its first tournament in 2013 and founded the Spikeball Roundnet Association, a ranking system was introduced in 2016, the first College National Championship was held at Clemson University on 29 April 2017, and tournaments began airing on ESPN2 in May 2018. The sport spread internationally with federations and national championships across Europe — Belgium, Germany, Spain, Sweden (whose championships run since 2020) and the United Kingdom among others — and the first Roundnet World Championship, initially scheduled for Belgium in September 2020, was held in September 2021 after a pandemic delay.
