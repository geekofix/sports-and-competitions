Aeromodelling

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Aeromodelling (model aircraft flying) is the sport of designing, building and flying unmanned scale and non-scale model aircraft. It is governed internationally by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its Commission Internationale d'Aéromodélisme (CIAM) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale [FAI], n.d.-a). The main competition classes are F1 free flight, F2 control line, F3 radio control, F4 scale and space models (FAI, n.d.-b). Competitions are open to women and men alike, who fly in the same classes. See also Air Racing on this site (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-b) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-c).

Among the athletes shaping the sport today are Japan's Tetsuo Onda, winner of the 2026 F3A (radio control aerobatics) World Cup with 80 points, followed by Italy's Andrea Cervi and France's Christophe Paysant-Le Roux (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale [FAI], n.d.-c).

Within the air sports family on this site, its closest relatives are Air Racing, Paragliding, Powered Paragliding, Hang Gliding, Skydiving; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

In the FAI classes, F1 free flight models are launched and fly without any physical connection to the pilot, with flight times measured over several rounds; F2 control line models are flown on two lines (typically around 15.92–20 m long) that control the elevator, and both speed and aerobatic patterns are judged; F3 radio control covers aerobatics (F3A), helicopters, gliders and pylon racing, judged on prescribed maneuver schedules; F4 scale is judged on the fidelity of the model to its full-size prototype and its flight realism; space models cover model rocketry (FAI, n.d.-b). At championships, competitors fly several rounds and the best scores count; world rankings are published by the FAI. Men and women compete together in the same classes (FAI, n.d.-c).

History

Aeromodelling began with the pioneers of flight: Samuel Pierpont Langley built steam-powered model aircraft in the 1890s, and the Wright brothers famously tested their ideas with model gliders before building their full-size airplane in 1903. Organized competition grew in the 1920s and 1930s, and the FAI — founded in Paris in 1905 — created its aeromodelling commission (CIAM) to standardize classes and records. After the Second World War, control line and free flight dominated until radio control became reliable in the 1960s and 1970s, opening the sport to the broad range of classes flown today (FAI, n.d.-a; Academy of Model Aeronautics, n.d.).
