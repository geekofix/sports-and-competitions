Aeromodelling

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Aeromodelling (model aviation) is the sport of designing, building and flying scale or non-scale model aircraft. The Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) governs international competition through its aeromodelling commission (CIAM), with classes covering free flight (F1), control line (F2), radio control (F3), scale models and even model spacecraft.

Rules

Under the FAI Sporting Code, model aircraft compete in class-specific events: free-flight models are judged on flight duration and consistency, control-line models on speed around a pylon course, radio-controlled aerobatic models on a judged pattern of maneuvers, and scale models on realism of appearance and flight. National and world championships are held regularly in each class.

History

Model aviation dates to the late 19th century — pioneers like Samuel Langley and the Wright brothers experimented with models before full-size flight. The FAI, founded in 1905, has governed air sports including aeromodelling since its earliest days.
