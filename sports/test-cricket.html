Test Cricket

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Test cricket is the longest and most traditional format of cricket: international matches between full member nations played over up to five days with two innings per side, a red ball, and no limits on overs. The format's name reflects the ultimate test of skill and endurance, and a Test match is the highest honor the sport awards, contested by a small group of nations under the International Cricket Council.

Rules

Each team bats twice across four innings spread over five scheduled days: innings alternate until ten wickets fall, a captain declares, or the chasing team reaches its target, with the four-innings match decided on total runs. Play runs in six-ball overs with no limit on overs or on each bowler, two scheduled intervals per day, a minimum over requirement per day and playing conditions governing new-ball timing, follow-on declaration (the leading team may enforce a follow-on with a big lead), reviews, and lights, with a draw (time expiring before a result) still one of cricket's unique results. Varying pitches degrading over five days and a ball aging through 80 overs create the format's tactical depth: spin bowling, reverse swing and declaration timing become decisive in ways no shorter format demands.

History

Test cricket began in 1877, when an England side met a combined Australian XI at the Melbourne Cricket Ground in the first match later recognized as a Test; the Ashes (born of a mock obituary after Australia's first win in England in 1882) remains the format's most historic contest, and the ICC's present Test championship was organized over a century later in 2019 with a two-year cycle and a world final. The classic powerhouse nations (Australia, England and South Africa) grew to nine and then twelve Test nations across the twentieth century, with the West Indies' era of domination, the Asian giants' rise and Australia's and modern-era dynasties shaping the format's record. Twenty20 cricket's rise has produced the strongest debate over the format's future, but a World Test Championship title, a five-day Ashes series and the format's iconic figures from Bradman to today's all-format stars keep Test cricket the sport's ultimate examination.
