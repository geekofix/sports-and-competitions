Freestyle Scootering

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Freestyle scootering is trick riding on a pro kick scooter — bars, tailwhips, flips and grinds in skateparks — with world-level competition at the World Scooter Championship (FSS/WS) and scootering as a World Skate Games discipline (2022), where riders are judged on difficulty, execution and style (World Skate, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Both men and women compete, with junior categories; safety gear (helmet, pads) is mandatory at events.

Figure: GCBA. (2022-11-02). Freestyle scootering at the World Skate Games 2022 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 2.5. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scootering_-_World_Skate_Games_2022.jpg

Within the action-sports family on this site its closest relatives are Kick Scooter Racing, Street Skateboarding, Vert Skateboarding, BMX Freestyle, Aggressive Inline Skating, Parkour and Skateboarding; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

Competition rules: runs of 45–60 seconds in park courses, judged by panels on difficulty, execution, variety and style; multiple runs, best counts; helmet mandatory, pads recommended; scooters must pass equipment checks (bars, fork, wheels, brake); categories by age and gender (World Skate, n.d.).

History

Freestyle scootering exploded in the 2000s–2010s via X Games and YouTube culture; the World Scooter Championship has run since the 2010s, and scootering was contested at the World Skate Games 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
