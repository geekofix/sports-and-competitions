Lead Climbing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Lead climbing is the classic roped discipline of sport climbing: the climber, tied to a rope clipped into quickdraws on a wall, tries to reach the highest hold possible within a time limit, while a belayer manages the rope from the ground. Lead walls at international level are around 15 m high and set with bolted holds on steep, overhanging terrain. The International Federation of Sport Climbing (IFSC) governs the discipline, and climbing made its Olympic debut at Tokyo 2020 in a combined format; lead is one of the three Olympic disciplines alongside speed and boulder.

Rules

In an IFSC lead competition, each climber gets one attempt on the route within a six-minute time limit. The score is the height reached, measured in holds: reaching a hold with control scores it, with a "plus" (+) for reaching the next hold without topping it. Falling ends the attempt. Routes are graded on the French scale, and the route setter designs a path that gets progressively harder. In the Olympic combined format, athletes compete in speed, boulder and lead, and the three rankings are multiplied to give a combined score — the lowest wins. In lead-only world cups, climbers advance from qualification to semifinal and final rounds.

History

Roped climbing on natural rock developed in the late 19th century in the Alps and the Dolomites, but competitive sport climbing on artificial walls began in the 1980s, with the first indoor lead competitions in France and Italy. The UIAA (International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation) organized early world championships from 1991, and the IFSC was founded in 2007 as the dedicated international federation. Climbing debuted at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics with speed, boulder and lead, won by Alberto Ginés López (men) and Janja Garnbret (women); lead remains the discipline that best rewards endurance, technique and route reading. Stars such as Adam Ondra, Jakob Schubert and Janja Garnbret set the standards of the sport.
