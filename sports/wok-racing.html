Wok Racing

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Wok racing is a German novelty winter sport in which modified woks are ridden down an Olympic bobsleigh track in timed runs — in one-person woks ("Einer-Wok") or four-person wok sleds ("Vierer-Wok") (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the folk/festival novelty family with soapbox racing and belongs to the sliding-sports world of bobsleigh tracks.

Figure: Andreas Cappell from Erlangen, Germany. (2006-03-11 22:50). SEAT-Hackl four-person wok team at the finish line, Wok-WM 2006 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wok_WM_2006_SEAT-Hackl-4er-Wok-Team_im_Zieleinlauf.jpg

The public event is the annual "Wok-WM" (World Wok Racing Championship), broadcast since 2003 by ProSieben as a special of Stefan Raab’s TV total — first held at Winterberg on 6 November 2003, with venues including Innsbruck (bobsleigh track, 1,270 m), Altenberg, Oberhof and Königssee (Wikipedia, n.d.). Champions of the one-person wok include Georg Hackl (three-time Olympic luge champion; record 8 titles), Joey Kelly (2022, 2023) and Stefan Raab (2003) (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Rules: a standard wok is reinforced at the bottom with epoxy and heated with a blowtorch; riders wear heavy protective gear (ice-hockey kit) and ladles on their feet to reduce friction; the 1,270 m Innsbruck track is raced in timed runs, with the fastest time winning (Wikipedia, n.d.). Four-person sleds couple two pairs of woks (photo: 2006 SEAT-Hackl four-wok team at the finish). Men and women compete in separate events.

History

Wok racing was invented by German TV host Stefan Raab after a bet on the show Wetten, dass..? — he wagered to ride a bobsleigh track in a wok, won the bet, rode anyway, and the fun became the first Wok-WM months later in November 2003 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Since 2009 the broadcast is classified as an infomercial under German advertising law because the event is staged for TV (Wikipedia, n.d.).
