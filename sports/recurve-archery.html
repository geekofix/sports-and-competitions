Recurve Archery

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Recurve archery is the standard Olympic form of archery, shot with a recurve bow — limbs that curve away from the archer at the tips, storing more energy than a straight limb — a sight, and no release aids. Competition at the elite level is shot at 70 meters at a 122 cm five-color target with ten scoring zones, governed internationally by World Archery (known as FITA before 2011). Recurve archery is one of the oldest sports on the modern Olympic program, having appeared in the games of 1900-1920 and continuously since 1972.

Rules

Olympic-format competition is a double 70 m round: a qualification round of 72 arrows, ranked into head-to-head match play with set scoring — each set of three arrows, the set winner taking 2 points (1 point for a draw), the match to 6 set points, with a one-arrow shoot-off on a tie. Ties within the match go to the archer whose arrow is closest to the center. At world championships and World Cup events, recurve competition includes individual, team (three archers) and mixed team formats, all shot on the same 122 cm faces at 70 m outdoor; indoor and field rounds exist alongside under separate rulesets. Bow limits are strict: sights must be unpowered and attached to the bow, and the string must be released with fingers using a glove or tab — mechanical release aids are banned. The compound division of archery, by contrast, is not on the Olympic program but is a World Games discipline.

History

The recurve bow's design descends from Asian horse archery, refined in Korea and throughout the 20th century into today's take-down bows of laminated wood, fiberglass and carbon with aluminum-and-carbon arrows; technology has made the competitive bow a precision instrument, with clickers, stabilizers and sights. Modern target archery — as organized by FITA, founded in 1931 — defined the Olympic format, and the recurve discipline formed the Olympic program in the early games of 1900, 1904, 1908 and 1920 before a 52-year hiatus and a continuous return since Munich 1972, with team events added in 1988. The women's field has drawn world-class depth, with South Korea an unmatched dynasty led by multiple Olympic champions such as Kim Soo-nyung and Park Sung-hyun; in the men's ranks, Brady Ellison of the United States has been the defining archer of the modern era. The World Archery Championships have been staged since 1931, and the World Cup circuit since 2006.
