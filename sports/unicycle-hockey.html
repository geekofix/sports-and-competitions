Unicycle Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Unicycle hockey is ice hockey's floor cousin played on one wheel: two teams of five riders on unicycles use ordinary ice-hockey sticks and a tennis-sized ball (or a dead-ball), dribbling, passing and shooting at goals while pedaling and idling on a single wheel. Organized internationally with league play in Europe and world championships contested by the International Unicycling Federation, it is one of the most skill-dense of all team sports.

Rules

Standard play follows simplified ice-hockey logic on a court (typically 20 by 36 metres, basketball-court-scale): five-a-side rider-players sit on unicycles of competition size (usually 24-inch wheels) and use ice-hockey sticks to play a low-bounce ball into hockey goals, with a face-off at the center after goals; rules prohibit riding with the stick in the wheel, body contact, using the non-stick hand or resting on walls or goals, and require that players ride the unicycle (a judge may stop play when a rider dismounts and the rider must remount quickly). Matches typically run two halves of 10 to 20 minutes under the IUF rulebook (the same body that sanctions world championship play in four-year cycles alongside other unicycling disciplines).

History

Unicycle hockey emerged from unicycling club culture in the twentieth century (early casual games reported in the United States, Germany and Japan from the 1970s, with Germany structuring the first recognized leagues in the 1990s), and the International Unicycling Federation added hockey to its world championship convention programme, where national teams have contested world titles since 1994 at each UNICON gathering. The sport's heartland is Germany's league system (a multi-division nationwide league), with active club scenes in Switzerland, Britain, France, Australia and North America; its top players transition seamlessly between unicycle hockey and other unicycle disciplines, and the modern game's combination of stick skill and riding artistry continues to draw new club members worldwide.
