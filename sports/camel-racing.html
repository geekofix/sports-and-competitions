Camel Racing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Camel racing is a traditional sport of the Arabian Peninsula with deep roots in Bedouin culture. In the United Arab Emirates it has developed into a professionally administered sport with dedicated dirt racetracks, most famously Al Marmoom Camel Racetrack in Dubai, home of the Dubai Camel Racing Club (DCRC). Races are run over distances that vary by the age and class of the camel, typically between 5 and 10 km, with top speeds around 40 km/h and up to 60 camels per race. Racing is governed in the UAE by the UAE Camel Racing Federation, and major prize-money events include the Al Marmoom Heritage Festival and the Dubai Crown Prince Camel Festival.

Rules

Since 2005, professional races in the UAE use lightweight robot jockeys strapped to the camel's back instead of human riders; owners and trainers follow in 4x4 vehicles along the track, operating the robot's whip and rein by remote control and urging the camel on through a loudspeaker. This replaced child jockeys after the UAE banned jockeys under 15 with effect from 1 September 2002, under directives of Sheikh Zayed. Races are run in age and class categories on oval dirt tracks with electronic timing and chipping, and doping controls plus veterinary checks protect the animals. The robot jockey must be mounted before the camels leave the farm, not at the racetrack.

History

Camel racing has been part of Bedouin gatherings, weddings and celebrations on the Arabian Peninsula for centuries. Organized racing on dedicated tracks developed in the second half of the 20th century with the support of the Gulf rulers, and the UAE now has around 14,000 racing camels. The decisive break with the child-jockey era came in 2002, and robot jockeys were introduced in 2005, making Gulf camel racing one of the most technologically distinctive traditional sports in the world. Today the sport is a multi-million-dirham industry with breeding programs, veterinary science and festival meetings that are free and open to the public.
