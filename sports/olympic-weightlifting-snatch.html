Olympic Weightlifting Snatch

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The snatch is one of the two lifts contested in Olympic weightlifting, alongside the clean and jerk: the barbell is lifted from the ground to full arm extension overhead in one continuous movement, with a wide grip and a deep squat under the bar. Because it is the faster, more explosive of the two lifts and is performed first in competition, the snatch often sets the tone for the contest. The lift is governed by the International Weightlifting Federation (IWF), and weightlifting has been an Olympic sport for men since Athens 1896 and for women since Sydney 2000.

Rules

In the snatch the lifter grips the barbell wide, pulls it from the platform in one continuous motion and catches it overhead in a deep squat, arms fully locked, then stands up with the bar held motionless until the referees' down signal; any pause during the pull, any contact of the bar with the head, or a downward movement of the bar during the catch invalidates the attempt. Each lifter receives three attempts at the snatch before the clean and jerk, and the heaviest successful snatch counts toward the two-lift total; the athlete with the highest combined total wins, with ties broken by the lower bodyweight. At the World Championships and Olympic Games the competition is divided into bodyweight categories, revised for 2025, and the best lifter across all categories is decided with the Sinclair coefficient.

History

The snatch has been contested since the first modern Olympic Games in Athens 1896, when the one-hand and two-hand snatch events were held, and it became a permanent part of the Olympic program from 1920 onward. In the early decades lifters such as the American Charles Vinci and later the Bulgarian Naim Süleymanov, the "Pocket Hercules", set the standard; Süleymanov lifted three times his bodyweight in the snatch at the 1988 Seoul Olympics. Women first contested the snatch at the Sydney 2000 Olympics. The lift remains the opening event of every major weightlifting competition, and at Los Angeles 2028 the Olympic program will use twelve new bodyweight categories with equal numbers for men and women.
