Boxing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Boxing is a combat sport in which two fighters exchange punches with gloved hands, aiming for knockouts or a points decision. Amateur boxing is governed by the International Boxing Association (IBA), which runs the IBA World Boxing Championships and oversees Olympic boxing; professional boxing is sanctioned by bodies such as the World Boxing Council (WBC). Boxing has been an Olympic sport since the ancient Games and the modern Olympics since St. Louis 1904.

Rules

Under IBA rules, elite men's amateur bouts last three 3-minute rounds and women's three 3-minute rounds (junior and youth formats differ), with a 1-minute rest between rounds. Scoring uses the 10-point-must system: judges award 10 points to the round winner and 9 or fewer to the loser, so a knockdown or dominant round wins the bout on points. A bout ends by knockout (10-count), technical knockout, referee stoppage, or retirement. Headgear is mandatory in most amateur events; protective gear and weigh-ins are strictly regulated. In professional boxing, championship bouts are 12 rounds of 3 minutes under WBC/WBA/WBO/IBF rules.

History

Boxing was introduced at the ancient Olympic Games in 688 BC, with leather hand straps; modern Queensberry rules (gloves, 3-minute rounds, 10-count) were codified in 1867 by the Marquess of Queensberry. It entered the modern Olympics in 1904. The 20th century produced icons such as Joe Louis, Muhammad Ali, Sugar Ray Robinson and Mike Tyson; women's boxing joined the Olympics at London 2012. The IBA (founded 1946 as the International Amateur Boxing Association) governs the amateur and Olympic side.
