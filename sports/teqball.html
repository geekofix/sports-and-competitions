Teqball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Teqball is the football-based table sport played on a curved table that returns the ball to the players' feet: two or four players hit a football back and forth over the curve with any part of the body except hands and arms, mixing the touch of football with the rally structure of table tennis. Invented in Hungary in 2014, it is the world's youngest internationally recognized sport with a world tour and world championships governed by the International Teqball Federation (FITEQ).

Rules

Players alternate striking the ball over the curved Teq table in the manner of table tennis's serve-and-rally: the server kicks the ball onto the opponent's side, every return must touch the table once and each side may take one touch before returning it (two touches setting up a partner in doubles), with hands and arms strictly forbidden and a point ending when the ball bounces twice on one side, is returned off the table, or is touched illegally. Matches are best-of-three sets to 12 points, with standardized rules on edge balls, serve execution and double-bounce calls governed by the FITEQ rulebook for singles, doubles and mixed doubles, and world rankings seed the international calendar.

History

Teqball was invented in Hungary in 2014 by three football enthusiasts (the former professional footballer Gabor Borsanyi, Viktor Huszar and Gyorgy Gattyan), who bent the tabletop after years of experimenting on a flat table tennis surface so the ball would bounce back to the players' feet. The sport launched internationally in Budapest in 2016 with the great Brazilian footballer Ronaldinho among its ambassadors, and after becoming the fastest internationally recognized sport (with recognition from the Olympic Council of Asia in 2018) it was added to the Asian Indoor and Beach Games programmes and the European Games of 2023. Its world tour reaches every continent with professional players from football's broader world; its profile at the Olympic movement's continental games keeps it on the recognized path toward eventual Olympic inclusion.
