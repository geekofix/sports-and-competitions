Practical Shooting IPSC

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Practical shooting under IPSC rules is a dynamic shooting sport in which competitors engage multiple targets from varying positions while moving through courses of fire, scored on the triad of accuracy, power and speed — the Latin motto Diligentia, Vis, Celeritas (DVC) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Practical Shooting Confederation (IPSC), founded in 1976, is the world's largest governing body in practical shooting, affiliating more than 100 regions, with the United States Practical Shooting Association (USPSA) administering the sport in the US (International Practical Shooting Confederation, n.d.). Women compete in dedicated Lady categories at every world shoot — the American Ashley Rheuark finished second in the Open division Lady category at the 2017 IPSC Rifle World Shoot — alongside men in the Open, Standard, Production, Classic and Revolver divisions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

IPSC matches are built from courses of fire with no set stage design: shooters may face multiple targets, partial and penalty targets, moving and reactive targets, barriers and mandatory movement, firing in minimum time for maximum points (International Practical Shooting Confederation, n.d.). Handgun matches balance short (up to 12 shots), medium (up to 24) and long courses (up to 32) in a 3:2:1 ratio, and no course may be shot twice at the same match. Scoring uses Comstock-style hit factors: A, C and D zones on the cardboard IPSC target score hit points, steel targets must fall, and the stage score is hit points divided by time, with penalties for misses, procedural errors and no-shoots; power factor divides major from minor scoring. Divisions range from Open (optics, compensators, large magazines) to Production, Standard, Classic and Revolver; rifle and shotgun have standalone rulebooks. Safety is paramount, enforced by range officers trained through the International Range Officers Association (IROA), and all competitors — women and men — shoot the same courses (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

History

Practical shooting grew out of informal handgun competitions in 1950s California that sought to develop defensive shooting skills; on 24 May 1976 enthusiasts from around the world met at the International Pistol Conference in Columbia, Missouri, and founded the confederation, electing Colonel Jeff Cooper, the influential shooting instructor, as first IPSC President — remembered as the founding father of the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first Handgun World Shoot followed in 1976, won by Jan Foss of Norway; handgun, rifle and shotgun World Shoots now run at three-year intervals, and the Production division arrived in the late 1990s with electronic scoring in the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
