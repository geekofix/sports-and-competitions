Practical Shooting IPSC

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Practical shooting under IPSC rules is a dynamic shooting sport in which competitors engage multiple targets from varying positions while moving through courses of fire, scored on the triad of accuracy, power and speed — the Latin motto Diligentia, Vis, Celeritas (DVC). The International Practical Shooting Confederation (IPSC), founded in 1976, is the world's largest and oldest governing body in practical shooting, affiliating more than 100 regions across all continents, with national bodies such as the United States Practical Shooting Association (USPSA) administering the sport in member countries.

Rules

IPSC matches are built from courses of fire with no set stage design: shooters may face multiple targets, partial targets, penalty targets, moving and reactive targets, barriers and mandatory movement, firing in the minimum time with maximum points. Handgun matches balance short courses (up to 12 shots), medium courses (up to 24) and long courses (up to 32) in a 3:2:1 ratio, and a course may never be shot twice at the same match. Scoring is by Comstock-style "count-back": hits on the octagonal cardboard IPSC target are scored A, C or D by zone, steel targets must fall to score, and the final stage score is hit points divided by time, with penalties for misses, procedural errors and hits on penalty targets. Competitors shoot in divisions — from Open, with optic-sighted pistols, compensators and large magazines, to Production, Standard, Classic and Revolver — under power factor rules that divide major from minor scoring, and competition principles demand safe gun handling above all, enforced by range officers trained through the International Range Officers Association (IROA). Rifle and shotgun are governed by standalone IPSC rulebooks.

History

Practical shooting grew out of informal handgun competitions in 1950s California, which sought to develop defensive shooting skills and quickly evolved into a pure athletic shooting sport. On 24 May 1976, enthusiasts from around the world met at the International Pistol Conference in Columbia, Missouri, adopting a confederation with a constitution and rules; Colonel Jeff Cooper, the influential shooting instructor, was unanimously elected the first IPSC President and is remembered as the founding father of the sport. The first Handgun World Shoot followed in 1976, won by Jan Foss of Norway, and the handgun, rifle and shotgun World Shoots are now each held at three-year intervals, hosted across the globe. The sport professionalized steadily: IROA was created to train international range officers, regional organizations such as the United States Practical Shooting Association grew into bodies with more than 40,000 members and 500 affiliated clubs, and divisions and scoring evolved — Power factor limits, Production division's arrival in the late 1990s and electronic scoring systems modernized match administration in the 2010s.
