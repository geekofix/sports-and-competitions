Carjitsu

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Carjitsu (car jitsu) is a combat sport in which two fighters grapple and exchange submission holds inside the confined interior of a parked car, usually a sedan. It was invented in 2020 by Russian judo and Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belt Vik Mikheev as a realistic self-defense scenario for carjacking, and the Pro League Network (PLN) acquired the rights and stages official Car Jitsu events, including a US Car Jitsu Championship. Because of the tight space, the fight is pure grappling: punches and kicks have little room to work.

Rules

In official Car Jitsu bouts, two competitors fight inside a stationary car with a referee supervising; the fight is decided by submission, knockout or judges' points under modified MMA-style rules adapted to the confined space. Everything in the car can be used to the fighter's advantage, including the seatbelt as a choking tool, which is why the sport is also promoted as practical self-defense for drivers (for example rideshare drivers). Fighters wear standard grappling gear, the car is stripped of loose objects, and medical staff are present at events.

History

Vik Mikheev began posting car-jitsu fights on his YouTube channel in 2020, and the videos went viral, leading to organized competitions in Russia and then internationally. In 2022 the Pro League Network, co-founded by Mike Salvaris and Kyron Bowen, took over the format and broadcast Car Jitsu events in the United States, with fighters and commentators pointing out the real-world self-defense lessons for carjacking situations. The sport remains a niche spectacle that combines submission grappling with motorsport showmanship.
