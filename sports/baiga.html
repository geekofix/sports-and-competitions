Baiga

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Baiga is the traditional Kazakh horse race, held at hippodromes — such as the Kazanat hippodrome in Astana — and at festivals such as Nauryz, and celebrated among the Turkic peoples of the steppe; it is also a discipline of the World Nomad Games (E-history.kz, n.d.-b; E-history.kz, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The races are classified by distance and by the age of the horse: kunan baiga over 1.2–2 km for horses up to three years old, donen over 2.5–4.8 km for horses up to four, the classic baiga over 5–8 km for horses five and older, and the alaman baiga — “sky race” — a cross-country run of 30, 50 or 100 km (E-history.kz, n.d.-a).

Prizes traditionally go to the first three to five riders, and while the classic jockeys are boys aged 7–14, professional jockeys also ride the longer distances; there is no codified gender split, so men and women alike take part at modern festivals, with the youngest riders competing in the kunan class (E-history.kz, n.d.-a; E-history.kz, n.d.-b).

Rules

The rules are simple and age-graded: horses are entered in their age class (kunan up to 3 years, donen up to 4 years, baiga 5+ years), the alaman baiga is run cross-country over a marked 30, 50 or 100 km course, and the first horse home wins, with placings paid to the leading three to five finishers depending on the race (E-history.kz, n.d.-a; E-history.kz, n.d.-b).

Races are run on the flat at hippodromes or across open steppe at festivals, with a single jockey per horse and no weight handicapping; fouls such as interference are penalised by the race judges, and the programme is identical for male and female riders, who compete in the same races (E-history.kz, n.d.-b; E-history.kz, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Horse racing is at the heart of Kazakh nomadic culture — horses were the measure of a family's wealth and a warrior's mobility — and baiga races were held to test the best mounts, to celebrate Nauryz and other holidays, and to mark great gatherings (E-history.kz, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

In the modern era baiga was included in the programme of the World Nomad Games, first held in 2014 at Issyk-Kul in Kyrgyzstan and repeated every two years, where the Kazakh delegation regularly dominates the horse-racing events; the steppe long-distance races pictured at Mongolia's Naadam festival show the shared Central Asian tradition out of which the Kazakh baiga developed (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; E-history.kz, n.d.-c).
