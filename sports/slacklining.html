Slacklining

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Slacklining is the balance sport of walking, jumping and performing tricks on a flat ribbon of nylon or polyester webbing tensioned between two anchors \u2014 unlike a tightrope, the line stretches and oscillates, making balance dynamic and expressive. From its birth among Yosemite rock climbers it has grown into a worldwide sport with distinct disciplines \u2014 tricklining, longlining, waterlining, highlining and yoga-style practice \u2014 organised internationally under the International Slackline Association (ISA).

Rules

The main disciplines are defined by line type and height. Tricklining uses short (15\u201330 m), very taut, wide (5 cm) webbing that behaves like a trampoline, on which athletes are judged in competitions for flips, spins, chest and butt bounces and somersaults \u2014 judged routines on technical difficulty, amplitude and landing. Longlining stretches the challenge to lines of many tens to hundreds of metres requiring sustained concentration; waterlining suspends the line over water; and highlining rigs the webbing high off the ground \u2014 traditionally walked with a leash attached to the line, with anchors, backups and rigging standards set by the ISA's safety framework (the ISA began tracking incidents in 2009, and both highline and trickline fatalities have been recorded, making disciplined rigging a core part of the sport). Speedline and freestyle highline formats are newer arrivals, while the basic game \u2014 walking the line without falling \u2014 defines "sending" a line in every discipline.

History

Rope-walking traditions stretch back thousands of years, but modern slacklining is traced to 1976\u20131980s American rock climbers \u2014 most consistently to Adam Grosowsky and Jeff Ellington \u2014 walking flat climbing webbing strung in Yosemite's parking lots on rest days, so the "slackers" could practise balance while relaxing. Highlining's early chapters were written by the discipline's first legends: Scott Balcom and Chris Carpenter made the first documented nylon highline crossing ("The Arches", about 9 m long at 35 m height, near Pasadena, California), Balcom crossed the Lost Arrow Spire highline in 1985, Darrin Carter followed with unprotected Lost Arrow crossings in 1995, Libby Sauter became the first woman to cross Lost Arrow in 2007, and Dean Potter BASE-jumped from a highline in 2008. Commercially, Robert and Jan Kaeding's Gibbon Slacklines (founded 2007) popularised the pastime with simple kits and later the Gibbon Slackline World Cup (first held in the USA in 2011), while the Humboldt Classic trickline events ran from 2008; today the sport counts world-class competitions (including the Red Bull Airlines and VOY community events) and global superstars like world highline and trickline figures Lukas Irmler and Friedi K\u00fchne, whose 975 m blindfolded walk in 2019 set records, while Giovanna Petrucci's 2016 Ipanema beach performance at the Rio Olympics brought the sport mass attention.
