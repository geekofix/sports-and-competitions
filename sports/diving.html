Diving

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive diving is an aquatic sport in which athletes dive from springboards or platforms into a pool, judged on the difficulty, execution, height and entry of each dive. It is governed by World Aquatics and has been part of the Olympic program since 1904 (men) and 1912 (women). The main Olympic disciplines are 3 m springboard, 10 m platform and synchronized diving (pairs, mixed at the Olympics since 2000); World Aquatics also runs high diving (20 m women, 27 m men) at the World Championships and the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series.

Rules

At Olympic and World Championship level, divers perform a set number of dives from both springboard and platform. Each dive has a DD (degree of difficulty) multiplier; judges award points for execution and the final score is the sum of all dives multiplied by their DD values. In individual events, divers advance from preliminaries to semifinals and finals, where the best scores count. In synchronized diving, two divers perform the same dive simultaneously from the same apparatus and are additionally judged on synchronization; at the Olympics mixed pairs compete. A dive must be announced in advance with its dive number, and failed dives score zero. Equipment rules (board and platform specifications, suit regulations) are set in the World Aquatics Competition Regulations.

History

Diving as a judged sport developed in 19th-century Sweden and Germany, where gymnasts dived into water, and spread to Britain and the United States. It entered the Olympic program in St. Louis 1904 for men and Stockholm 1912 for women, with platform diving first contested in 1904. The sport professionalized around the FINA (founded 1908 in London, now World Aquatics), which standardized judging and added springboard and synchronized events to the World Championships (from 1973 and 1995 respectively). China has dominated since the 1980s, with legends such as Fu Mingxia, Guo Jingjing and Quan Hongchan, while the United States produced long-time stars Greg Louganis and David Boudia. High diving (27 m) was added to the World Championships in 2013.
