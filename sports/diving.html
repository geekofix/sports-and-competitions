Diving

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Competitive diving is an aquatic sport in which athletes dive from springboards or platforms into a pool and are judged on takeoff, difficulty, execution, synchronization and entry. It is governed worldwide by World Aquatics, which runs the Olympic diving tournament, the World Aquatics Championships and the Diving World Cup, and it has been part of the Olympic program since St. Louis 1904 for men and Stockholm 1912 for women (Olympics.com, n.d.; World Aquatics, n.d.-a). The Olympic program comprises the 3 m springboard, the 10 m platform and synchronized events for pairs and, since Tokyo 2020, mixed pairs, while World Aquatics also sanctions high diving from 27 m for men and 20 m for women at the World Championships and the High Diving World Cup (World Aquatics, n.d.-a; Red Bull, n.d.). The 2026 Diving World Cup concluded with its Super Final at the National Aquatics Center in Beijing, 1\u20133 May 2026, where China swept all nine gold medals; Wang Zongyuan was named best male diver of the World Cup and 15-year-old Jiang Linjing best female diver (World Aquatics, n.d.-b; Xinhua News Agency, 2026). In high diving, the 2026 Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series crowned Rhiannan Iffland of Australia as women's champion for a record tenth time and Aidan Heslop of Great Britain as men's champion for the second time (Red Bull, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current athletes are \u2014 in women's diving \u2014 world champion and Olympic medalist Rhiannan Iffland (high diving) and Jiang Linjing (platform), and \u2014 in men's diving \u2014 Olympic champion and multiple world champion Wang Zongyuan (springboard) and two-time Red Bull Cliff Diving world champion Aidan Heslop (Red Bull, n.d.; Xinhua News Agency, 2026).

Rules

Diving is conducted under the World Aquatics Competition Regulations (World Aquatics, n.d.-a). Individual events are contested on the 1 m and 3 m springboards and the 10 m platform; each dive is identified by a four-digit number that encodes position, direction, somersaults and twists, and each dive carries a degree of difficulty (DD) multiplier between roughly 1.2 and 4.8. A panel of seven judges awards marks from 0 to 10 in half-point steps for execution; the two highest and two lowest awards are dropped, and the remaining three are added and multiplied by the DD, and the diver with the highest total after all dives wins (World Aquatics, n.d.-a). In synchronized diving, two divers perform the identical dive simultaneously from the same apparatus and are additionally judged on synchronization; mixed pairs compete at the Olympics since 2020 (Olympics.com, n.d.). The format runs from preliminaries to semifinals and a final of twelve divers at major championships, while World Cup Super Finals use a head-to-head pairing format among the twelve best divers of the season (World Aquatics, n.d.-b). A failed dive scores zero, and divers must announce dives in advance and wear regulation competition attire (World Aquatics, n.d.-a). High diving at the World Championships uses a 27 m platform for men and 20 m for women, with preliminary rounds and a final; the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series uses 27 m for men and 21 m for women, four dives per athlete, five judges awarding 0\u201310 in half points with the highest and lowest awards dropped, and the season title goes to the diver with the highest cumulative score (Red Bull, n.d.). Men and women compete under the same apparatus and judging rules at each height class; junior age groups (A and B) and a mixed team event complete the program at World Championships (World Aquatics, n.d.-a).

History

Diving as a judged sport grew out of gymnastics in nineteenth-century Sweden and Germany, where athletes practiced dives into water from boards, and it spread to Britain and the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.). It entered the Olympic program in St. Louis 1904 for men and Stockholm 1912 for women, and the international federation \u2014 founded as FINA in London in 1908 and renamed World Aquatics in 2022 \u2014 standardized judging and added springboard and synchronized events to the World Championships, which began in 1973 (World Aquatics, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). High diving from natural cliffs has a long tradition, most famously at the Stari Most bridge in Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina, where divers have jumped since 1566, and the World Championships introduced high diving in 2013, followed by the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series in the late 2000s (Red Bull, n.d.). Icons of the sport include the American Greg Louganis, pictured below, who won back-to-back Olympic gold medals on both boards in 1984 and 1988 and five Olympic medals overall, and on the women's side China's Fu Mingxia, who won Olympic gold at 14 in 1992 and added platform gold in 1996 and 2000, together with Guo Jingjing (four Olympic golds in synchronized events) and Quan Hongchan (Olympic champion on the 10 m platform in 2020) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
