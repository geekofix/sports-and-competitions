Jorkyball

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Jorkyball is a fast-paced small-sided football game played two versus two inside a small four-walled cage of about 10 m x 5 m on artificial turf: players may use the walls to pass, dribble and score, combining the technique of football with the rebound play of squash. It was invented in 1987 by the Frenchman Gilles Paniez in his garage in Lyon and was shown to a large audience as an exhibition at the 1990 FIFA World Cup in Italy. The Jorkyball International Federation (JIF), founded in 2014 and based in Rome, organizes the JIF World Cup.

Rules

A match is played inside the cage between two teams of two players, one striker and one defender; the roles are swapped at the end of each set, the striker may not play in the kick-off areas and the defender may not enter the opponent's half. A match consists of the best of three sets, and a set is won by the first team to score 7 goals. The cage measures about 9.8 m x 4.8 m with walls 2.7 m high, and the goals are two square openings of 110 cm x 110 cm at each end. The ball can be played off any wall, which makes the game continuous and favors quick combinations; there is no physical contact allowed.

History

Gilles Paniez, a petrol station manager from Lyon, devised jorkyball in his garage in 1987, inspired by small-sided football and rebound games; for three years he and his friends were the only players. The game was exported to Italy when it was included as an exhibition sport at the 1990 FIFA World Cup, and by the end of 1999 more than 100 jorkyball cages had been built in Italy, while France developed dozens of clubs. The Jorkyball International Federation was founded in 2014 with Gilles Paniez as president, and has since grown to 13 member nations, staging the JIF World Cup (Canada 2018, Japan 2021, Poland 2023), European championships and a league. The cage can be fitted into gyms, hotels and city-centre spaces, which has driven its growth as a commercial and club sport.
