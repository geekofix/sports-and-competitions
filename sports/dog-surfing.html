Dog Surfing

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dog surfing is a competitive water sport in which dogs ride ocean waves on surfboards, judged on ride length, wave size, confidence and maneuvers. The premier event is the World Dog Surfing Championships, held annually since 2016 at Linda Mar Beach in Pacifica, California, organized by the Northern California Dog Surfing organization. Other major events include the Surf City Surf Dog in Huntington Beach (since 2009), the Loews Coronado Surf Dog Competition (since 2006) and the Noosa Festival of Surfing dog division in Australia (since 2012).

Rules

Dogs compete in heats of about 10 minutes, divided into weight classes (e.g., small under 25 lb, medium, large, tandem and "dog and human" categories at the World Championships). Judges award points for the number of waves caught, ride length, the dog's position and balance on the board, and maneuvers; the top scorers from each heat advance to the final. Dogs must wear life jackets at some events, leashes are prohibited during the ride, and handlers enter the water with the dog then release it to catch a wave. At the World Dog Surfing Championships the competition also includes costume and best-wave awards alongside the main heats.

History

Dog surfing began as a novelty at California beaches in the early 2000s, when surfers started bringing their dogs into the shallows. The Loews Coronado Resort in San Diego formalized the first recurring competition in 2006, and Huntington Beach launched the Surf City Surf Dog in 2009. The World Dog Surfing Championships began in 2016 at Linda Mar Beach in Pacifica and have grown into the sport's world title event, with qualifying contests across the United States and entries from Australia, Japan and Europe. The sport has been featured on ESPN, Good Morning America and international news, and it now supports animal-welfare charities through its events.
