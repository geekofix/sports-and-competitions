Dog Surfing

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Dog surfing is a competitive sport in which dogs ride ocean waves on surfboards, usually pushed into the wave by their handler, and are judged on confidence, length of ride and overall ability (Wikipedia, n.d.). The best-known events are the World Dog Surfing Championships in Pacifica, California — the 2026 edition took place on 1 August 2026 — the Surf City Surf Dog competition at Huntington Beach, California, held every September, and the Noosa Festival of Surfing in Australia, which hosts one of the largest dog-surfing contests outside the United States (World Dog Surfing Championships, n.d.; Surf City Surf Dog, n.d.). Competitions are divided into size classes such as small, medium and large dogs, and the 2026 Purina Pro Plan Incredible Dog Challenge Western Regional in Huntington Beach in June 2026 was won by Rippin' Rosie, a five-year-old Labrador retriever handled by Stephen Drottar, and by Iza, a six-year-old French bulldog handled by David Fasoli (Los Angeles Times, 2026; Associated Press, 2026). At the end of 2026, the leading current surf dogs are the winners of the 2026 World Dog Surfing Championships in Pacifica pictured below and the top-ranked dogs of the Surf City Surf Dog series in Huntington Beach (World Dog Surfing Championships, n.d.).

Rules

In a heat, each dog is pushed into a wave by its handler and rides toward the shore on a board sized for the dog; judges award points for confidence on the board, length of the ride and overall wave-riding ability, and the highest-scoring dog in each heat advances (Surf City Surf Dog, n.d.). Events are organized in size classes (small, medium, large and sometimes tandem or "dog and human" divisions), with heats of a few minutes and a championship final; dogs wear life vests in most competitions, and handlers may push the dog into the wave but must stay in the water (World Dog Surfing Championships, n.d.). There are no separate competitions by sex of dog or handler — male and female dogs and handlers compete together in the same classes (Surf City Surf Dog, n.d.). Major events such as the World Dog Surfing Championships in Pacifica and Surf City Surf Dog in Huntington Beach also raise money for animal charities (Associated Press, 2026).

History

The first dog surfing competition is credited to Imperial Beach, California, in 2006, and the Surf City Surf Dog event was created in Huntington Beach in 2008, quickly becoming the largest annual dog-surfing contest (Wikipedia, n.d.). The World Dog Surfing Championships were established in Pacifica, Northern California, and have run annually since, with the 2026 edition held on 1 August 2026 (Associated Press, 2026). In Australia, the Noosa Festival of Surfing added a dog-surfing division that grew into the Surfing Dog Spectacular, where champion dogs such as Jonesy and Hugsley have ridden in front of large crowds since 2013 (World Dog Surfing Championships, n.d.). The sport spread through social media and charity events, and competitions are now held on both coasts of the United States as well as in Australia, the United Kingdom and elsewhere (Surf City Surf Dog, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
