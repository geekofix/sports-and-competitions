Chinlone

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Chinlone (Burmese for "cane ball") is the traditional national sport of Myanmar. Six players stand in a circle and keep a hand-woven rattan ball in the air using only their feet, knees, chest, head and hands' opposite — never the hands — combining athleticism with dance-like, acrobatic moves. It is non-competitive in its original form: the goal is cooperation, not beating an opponent. The Myanmar Chinlone Federation is working to have chinlone inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2026, and a competitive form has been part of the SEA Games since 2013.

Rules

In the traditional game, a team of about six players forms a circle about 6 m across; the ball is kept aloft by striking it with any body part except the hands, and the aim is to keep it airborne while performing creative combinations, with one player often in the center. Because the game is cooperative, there are no points and no winner; a drop ends the rally. In the competitive form introduced in 1953, teams perform set routines and are judged on difficulty, execution and style; the 2013 SEA Games in Naypyidaw included men's and women's non-repetition events.

History

Chinlone is believed to have been played in Myanmar for about 1,500 years, originally as entertainment for royalty. In 1953 the Burma Athletic Association's U Ah Yein was commissioned to write an official rulebook, and the first official competition was held in Yangon that year, turning the game into a judged sport. After independence in 1948, chinlone was promoted in schools as part of national cultural pride. The Myanmar Chinlone Federation announced in 2025 that it will submit chinlone to UNESCO for intangible cultural heritage recognition in March 2026.
