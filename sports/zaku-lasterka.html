Zaku Lasterka

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Zaku lasterka (sack race) is the Basque sack race: competitors hop a marked course inside heavy grain sacks — classically 81 kg sacks — individually or in relays; the first to the finish wins. Sack racing is a staple of Basque and European village fêtes (the same tradition as the Indonesian balap karung and the British village-show sack race). Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are Untziketariak, Segala, Lasto Altxatzea; the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

Figure: Irsam Soetarto. (2015-11-06). Balap karung — Indonesian sack race, the same festival tradition [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Balap_Karung.jpg

Rules

Runners must keep both hands on or inside the sack, hop on both feet and stay in their lane; falling is allowed, rejoining the sack is required. Distances are typically 50–100 m with heats and a final; relays pass the sack. Men's, women's and children's categories are run at festivals (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

History

Sack racing appears in Basque festa programmes from the late 19th century and remains one of the most popular novelty races; historic photographs (e.g. a 1921 university contest) show the same format a century ago (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
