Parkour Freestyle

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Parkour freestyle is the acrobatic, judged discipline of competitive parkour: instead of racing for time, athletes perform a choreographed routine of flips, spins, vaults and creative movements on an obstacle course, blending parkour with influences from gymnastics, tricking, martial arts and breakdancing. The term freerunning was popularized by Sébastien Foucan to describe this expressive style. Freestyle is one of the two FIG parkour disciplines alongside parkour speed, contested at the FIG Parkour World Cup (since 2018) and the World Championships, and was part of parkour's debut at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham.

Rules

In a freestyle competition, each athlete performs a routine of a set length on a standardized course and is scored by judges under the FIG code of points, which evaluates difficulty, execution, variety, creativity and flow of the movements, with deductions for falls or touches. The course typically includes bars, boxes, walls and rails on which athletes can run, vault, spin and flip. Athletes compete in men's and women's categories, and the highest total score wins. Safety requirements apply to the course construction and to protective equipment recommendations.

History

Freestyle grew out of the same Parisian roots as parkour in the 1990s, when the Yamakasi group's more acrobatic members, especially Sébastien Foucan, emphasized creative expression over pure efficiency; Foucan later coined "freerunning" in the 2003 BBC documentary Jump London. The style spread worldwide through films, commercials and early internet videos, with stars such as Foucan appearing in Madonna's 2006 "Jump" video. When the FIG formalized competitive parkour in 2017, it split the discipline into speedrun and freestyle, and freestyle became a medal event at the 2022 World Games in Birmingham and at the FIG Parkour World Championships.
