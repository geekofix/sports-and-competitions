Parkour Freestyle

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Parkour freestyle is the acrobatic, judged discipline of competitive parkour: instead of racing for time, athletes perform a choreographed routine on an obstacle course, blending vaults, flips, spins and creative movements with influences from gymnastics, tricking, martial arts, breaking and freerunning. The term freerunning was popularized by Sébastien Foucan to describe this expressive style. Freestyle is one of the two disciplines of the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) parkour program alongside parkour speed, contested at the FIG Parkour World Cup (since 2018) and the FIG Parkour World Championships, and it was part of parkour's debut at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham, Alabama (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate categories at all FIG events, and women's freestyle has grown rapidly since the discipline was added to the World Cup program (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Among the athletes who shaped the style are the Yamakasi's Sébastien Foucan, whose acrobatic flips made freerunning famous worldwide; today's leading women include competitors such as the FIG World Cup medalists in the women's freestyle category, pictured below in a mid-flip freestyle movement (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

In a freestyle competition under the FIG Parkour World Cup and World Championships rules, each athlete performs a single routine of a set length — typically 45 to 60 seconds at World Cup level — on a standardized course of bars, boxes, walls and rails, and the routine is scored by a panel of judges under the FIG code of points (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). The judges evaluate difficulty, execution, variety, creativity and flow of the movements, with deductions for falls, incomplete landings or touching the ground between elements; the highest total score wins. Athletes may use the course freely, including running on walls and using any vault, spin or flip, and there is no prescribed path as in speedrun. The course must meet FIG safety specifications, and protective equipment such as helmets is recommended in qualification stages. Competitions are held in men's and women's categories, usually with qualification routines followed by a final, and the same rules and scoring apply identically to both genders; age-group categories (junior and senior) are also offered at many events (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Fouls and unsafe conduct can lead to score deductions or disqualification under the FIG disciplinary regulations.

History

Freestyle grew out of the same Parisian roots as parkour in the late 1980s and 1990s, when the founding Yamakasi group in Lisses — including David Belle, Sébastien Foucan and Charles Perrière — trained together and some members emphasized acrobatic, expressive movement over pure efficiency (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Foucan later coined the term "freerunning" in the 2003 BBC documentary Jump London, and the style spread worldwide through films, commercials and early internet videos, with Foucan appearing in Madonna's 2006 "Jump" music video (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). When the FIG formalized competitive parkour in 2017, it defined two disciplines — speedrun and freestyle — and freestyle became a medal event at the FIG Parkour World Cup from 2018 and at the 2022 World Games in Birmingham, where the first world-level medals in the discipline were awarded (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). The World Freerunning & Parkour Federation, founded earlier by the freerunning community, continues to document the style's history and organize community events (World Freerunning & Parkour Federation, n.d.).
