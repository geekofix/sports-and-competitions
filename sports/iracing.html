iRacing

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

iRacing is iRacing.com Motorsport Simulations' subscription sim-racing platform (PC, laser-scanned tracks, realistic tire model), used for official series such as the eNASCAR Coca-Cola iRacing Series and its own world-level events: the iRacing World Championship and headline races like the esports Indianapolis 500 (Wikipedia, n.d.) (iRacing.com Motorsport Simulations, n.d.). Las Vegas hosted an iRacing Trak Racer event in fall 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Figure: TaurusEmerald. (2024-10-20 08:56:05). Toyota iRacing Trak Racer event in Las Vegas, fall 2024 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Toyota_iRacing_Trak_Racer_Las_Vegas_Fall_2024.jpg

Within the esports family on this site its closest relatives are Gran Turismo, Forza, Assetto Corsa, F1 (video game series), MotoGP (video game series), WRC (video game series), Zwift and E-Sports; the related pages are listed under Important Links.

Rules

iRacing competition rules: official series use fixed setups or open setup by series rules, with sporting code (no intentional wrecking, right of way rules); races by license class and safety rating; world championship events invite top qualifiers; prize money in the six figures for headline events; open to all genders (Wikipedia, n.d.).

History

iRacing launched in 2008 by Dave Kaemmer and colleagues and became the reference sim for official series: the eNASCAR Coca-Cola iRacing Series since 2020, the virtual 24 Hours of Le Mans, and the esports Indianapolis 500; the platform hosted the real-world 2020 Indy 500 broadcast race during the COVID pandemic (Wikipedia, n.d.).
