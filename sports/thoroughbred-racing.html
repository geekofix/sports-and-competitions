Thoroughbred Racing

≈ 6 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Thoroughbred racing is the sport of racing Thoroughbred horses at the gallop — on the flat or over jumps (steeplechasing) — and it is one of the oldest organized sports, with the breed itself descending from three foundation stallions imported to England in the 17th and 18th centuries (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). There is no single world championship; instead, national racing authorities coordinate through the International Federation of Horseracing Authorities (IFHA), which publishes the annual World's Best Racehorse Rankings, and the most prestigious races are the Kentucky Derby (first run 1875), the English Triple Crown at Royal Ascot and Epsom, the Melbourne Cup (1861), the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe (1920), the Breeders' Cup (1984) and the Dubai World Cup (1996) (International Federation of Horseracing Authorities, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Fillies and mares race against colts and geldings on the same terms in the vast majority of races — only classics such as the Kentucky Oaks or the 1,000 Guineas are restricted to fillies — and female jockeys compete at the highest level: the current leading woman rider in Britain is champion jockey Hollie Doyle, while top mares such as the 2009 Breeders' Cup Classic winner Zenyatta remain among the most celebrated horses of all time (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Rules

Races are run on turf, dirt or synthetic tracks, over distances from about 5 furlongs (1 km) on the flat up to 4.5 miles (7.2 km) over jumps; steeplechases use hurdles or fences (The Jockey Club, n.d.). In handicap races every horse carries a set weight so that all chances are equalized, while in weights-for-age and set-weight stakes the weights are fixed by age and sex; pattern races are graded Group 1–3 (Europe) or Grade I–III (North America) plus Listed races, alongside maiden and claiming races for lesser horses (International Federation of Horseracing Authorities, n.d.). Flat races start from stalls (starting gates); the first horse across the finish line wins, with photo finishes and dead heats under the rules, and interference or careless riding can lead to relegation or disqualification after a stewards' inquiry (The Jockey Club, n.d.). Jockeys must meet minimum weights — roughly 50–60 kg (110–133 lb) on the flat in Britain, more over jumps — and medication is governed by the IFHA International Agreement on Breeding, Racing and Wagering, with the U.S. additionally enforcing the federal Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority (HISA) since 2022 (International Federation of Horseracing Authorities, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Classification is by age and sex rather than by gender categories: two-year-old and three-year-old races form a separate ladder culminating in the classics, fillies have their own classics (Oaks, 1,000 Guineas), and mares, fillies, colts, geldings and female jockeys all compete openly against one another (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Horse racing under royal patronage flourished in 17th-century England, and the modern Thoroughbred traces to three foundation sires: the Darley Arabian (bought in Aleppo in 1704 by Thomas Darley), the Byerley Turk (captured at the 1687 Siege of Buda by Captain Richard Byerley) and the Godolphin Arabian (1724–1753) — about 95% of modern Thoroughbreds carry the Darley Arabian's Y chromosome (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Jockey Club was founded in 1750 at Newmarket, and its secretary James Weatherby published the Introduction to a General Stud Book in 1791 followed by volume one in 1793, the register from which every Thoroughbred pedigree is traced (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Epsom Derby was first run in 1780 (won by Diomed), the Kentucky Derby in 1875 at Churchill Downs (won by Aristides), the Melbourne Cup in 1861 (Archer) and the Grand National in 1839 (Lottery); the Breeders' Cup began in 1984 and the Dubai World Cup in 1996 (Churchill Downs, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the sport include Eclipse (foaled 1764, unbeaten and the ancestor of most modern Thoroughbreds), Man o' War (1919–20, 20 wins from 21 starts), Seabiscuit of the 1930s, Phar Lap in Australia, and Secretariat, whose 1973 U.S. Triple Crown with a record 2:24 Kentucky Derby still stands (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, the mare Zenyatta won the 2009 Breeders' Cup Classic — the first mare ever to win that race — and jockey Julie Krone (b. 1956) became the first woman to win a Triple Crown race when she took the 1993 Belmont Stakes on Colonial Affair, later adding five Breeders' Cup wins before retiring (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
