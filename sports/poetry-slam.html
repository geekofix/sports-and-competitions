Poetry Slam

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

A poetry slam is a competitive event in which poets perform original spoken-word poetry before a live audience and a panel of judges, typically five audience members chosen at random who score each performance on a scale from 0.0 to 10.0 (Britannica, n.d.). Poets may compete as individuals or in teams, and the format emphasizes both written content and delivery. Slam poetry is today practiced in many countries, with national championships, festivals and school programs, and women poets are prominent at every level — among the best-known slam poets is Germany's Nora Gomringer, pictured below with fellow poets at the Poetry Slam Festival Zurich 2010. The format is open to all genders, and dedicated women's slams such as the Women of the World Poetry Slam were historically staged alongside the open National Poetry Slam (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

The core rules of the format, standardized over the years by organizations such as Poetry Slam, Inc. but adapted by local organizers, are: poems must be the poet's own original work; performances are limited in time (commonly three minutes per poem, with score penalties for going over); no props, costumes or musical instruments are allowed; and the poet's performance is scored from 0.0 to 10.0 by a panel of judges selected from the audience, with the highest and lowest scores commonly dropped (Britannica, n.d.). Audience reaction — applause, snapping, stomping, and vocal response — is part of the event; heckling a poor performance and cheering a strong one are integral to the slam ethos of democratizing the stage. Rounds progress from open slams to finals, with cumulative scores determining who advances. Individual and team formats are both used, and men's, women's and mixed competitions are all common; the same scoring rules apply to every division (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

The format was created by the Chicago construction worker and poet Marc Kelly Smith, who wanted to make the poetry reading more like a direct competition between poets and audience, and the first slam series began in November 1984 at the Get Me High Lounge in Chicago, moving in July 1986 to the Green Mill jazz club in Uptown Chicago, where the Uptown Poetry Slam still runs weekly (Poetry Foundation, n.d.). The format spread rapidly: the Ann Arbor Poetry Slam was founded in 1987, Bob Holman began the slams at the Nuyorican Poets Café in New York in 1988, and the first National Poetry Slam was held in San Francisco in 1990 with teams from Chicago and San Francisco (Britannica, n.d.). Poetry Slam, Inc. was established in 1997 to oversee the National Poetry Slam, the Individual World Poetry Slam and the Women of the World Poetry Slam; the organization effectively wound down after 2018, but the movement continues in independent national and international championships, school and youth leagues, and on television, reaching Germany, France, the UK, Australia and beyond — and, in Germany, inclusion in the national UNESCO intangible heritage inventory (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
