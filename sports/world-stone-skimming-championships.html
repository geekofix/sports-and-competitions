World Stone Skimming Championships

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Stone Skimming Championships are held each year on the last Sunday of September on Easdale Island in the Inner Hebrides of Scotland, where competitors hurl flat slate stones across the flooded quarry and the longest measured throw wins (World Stone Skimming Championships, n.d.). Stones must be natural Easdale slate no more than three inches (7.6 cm) across, each competitor gets three throws, and a valid throw must bounce off the water at least twice before its distance is measured from the throwing platform (Wikipedia, n.d.). The championship runs separate men's, women's and junior categories, so women and men compete head-to-head for their own titles, and the Guinness-recognised absolute records stand at 121.8 m for men (Dougie Isaacs) and 52.5 m for women (Nina Luginbuhl, set at Abernant Lake in Wales on 28 May 2018) (Guinness World Records, n.d.). The most decorated champions are Dougie Isaacs with eight men's titles and Lucy Wood with five women's titles (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

At the Easdale championship, competitors throw from a wooden platform into the quarry, aiming for the "ring of truth" while the stone skims; each throw that bounces at least twice is measured from the platform to where the stone finally sinks, and the longest of a competitor's three throws counts (World Stone Skimming Championships, n.d.). Only natural Easdale slate stones up to three inches (7.6 cm) in diameter are allowed, and stones that have been "doctored" — shaped, weighted or treated — are grounds for disqualification, as several competitors discovered at the 2025 event (Wikipedia, n.d.). The quarry's back wall lies 63 m from the platform, so the arena naturally limits throws. Categories are men's, women's and junior, each with its own title; the traditional "Bertie" award (introduced 2002) and the "Old Tosser" award for over-50s (2008) add side prizes (World Stone Skimming Championships, n.d.).

History

Easdale's slate quarries, worked from the 18th century, were flooded by a great storm in 1881, creating the water arena used today (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first championship was started in 1983 by Albert Baker after a debate in the local pub about who could throw a stone furthest, and after a fallow period it was revived in 1997 by the Eilean Eisdeal association as a fundraiser for the island (World Stone Skimming Championships, n.d.). The event was cancelled in 2020–2022 during the COVID-19 pandemic and resumed in 2023. Record holders Dougie Isaacs (men's distance record and most titles) and Nina Luginbuhl (women's Guinness distance record of 52.5 m in 2018) are the sport's defining icons (Guinness World Records, n.d.).
