World Stone Skimming Championships

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

The World Stone Skimming Championships is an annual stone-skimming contest held on the island of Easdale in Argyll, Scotland, in which competitors skim slate stones across a flooded quarry pool, with the longest valid skim deciding the winner. Easdale is the smallest permanently inhabited island of the Inner Hebrides and lies at the heart of the historic Scottish slate industry, whose abandoned quarry provides a perfect natural arena.

Because it takes place on a flooded slate quarry, the championship has a distinctive local rule: all stones used must be natural Easdale slate, faith to the island's heritage.

Rules

Competitors throw stones from the bank of the flooded quarry, and a throw only counts if the stone skips across the surface; the distance it achieves is measured, and the longest valid skim wins. Stones must be natural Easdale slate and fit within the palm of the hand, and stones may not be treated with artificial aids. Championship rules require three skips (or for the counting distance, a minimum skim count) for a throw to be scored fully, with separate classes for men, women, juniors and veterans, and overseas entrants welcome.

History

Easdale's slate quarries, worked intensively in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and flooded after a great storm in the 1880s, left the island with a large water-filled pit that villagers later used for recreational stone skimming. The first World Stone Skimming Championships were held in 1997 as a local fundraiser, and the event has grown into a weekend that fills the car-free island with competitors from around the world each September, drawing the largest crowd Easdale hosts all year. The championships keep island heritage alive — profits supporting the island community — and hold the sport's most famous title.
