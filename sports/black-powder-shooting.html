Black Powder Shooting

≈ 2 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Black powder shooting is the competitive use of muzzleloading and black-powder cartridge firearms — flintlock, percussion and early cartridge rifles, pistols and shotguns — in accuracy and timed events (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the historical firearms side of the target-shooting family.

Figure: Jacek Halicki. (2019-09-15 12:05). Shooter firing a black powder firearm at a 2019 competition in Poland [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/2019_Strza%C5%82_z_broni_czarnoprochowej_1.jpg

Internationally the discipline is coordinated by the Muzzle Loaders Associations International Committee (MLAIC), which runs world championships, while in the United States the National Muzzle Loading Rifle Association (NMLRA) and the NSSA’s black-powder events are the major organizers (Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches include accuracy events at 50–200 yards with patched round ball or conical bullets, and timed "skirmish" and gong shoots (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rules

Muzzleloaders are loaded from the muzzle with measured black powder (or approved substitutes), patch and ball; safety rules require the firearm to be pointed downrange while loading, and eye and ear protection are mandatory (NMLRA, n.d.). Original and reproduction arms are both allowed, often in separate classes; calibres range from .32 to .54 and larger (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete together in most events, with separate ladies’ categories at major championships; youth shooters compete with reduced charges (NMLRA, n.d.).

History

Shooting with black powder arms was universal before smokeless powder appeared in the 1880s; competitive muzzleloading revived in the United States in the 1930s–50s with the founding of the NMLRA in 1933 at Friendship, Indiana, which still hosts the National Matches (Wikipedia, n.d.). In Europe the MLAIC world championships, first held in the 1970s, now rotate between countries and include team events (Wikipedia, n.d.). Icons include the NMLRA’s long-time champions at Friendship and the European world champions in the flintlock and percussion classes.
