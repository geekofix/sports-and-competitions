Bike Trial

≈ 4 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Bike trial (trials cycling) is an obstacle sport in which riders on special 20-inch or 26-inch bicycles without saddles manoeuvre over rocks, logs, boxes and ramps in marked sections, collecting penalty points for every "dab" (foot touch); it is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (Union Cycliste Internationale [UCI], n.d.-a). The 2026 UCI Trials World Cup comprised three rounds — Dole, France; Rzeszów, Poland (July 31–August 2); and Claußnitz, Germany (August 21–23, 2026) — with overall titles going to Spain's Alba Riera in Women Elite, Spain's Alejandro Montalvo in Men Elite 20″ and Britain's Charlie Rolls in Men Elite 26″, his third consecutive overall title; Spain's Vera Barón had won four consecutive world titles from 2022 to 2025, and the 2026 UCI Urban Cycling World Championships, including the Trials World Championships, were held November 3–7, 2026 in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (UCI, n.d.-b). Women compete on equal terms in their own Elite category — Alba Riera's 2026 World Cup overall title underlines the strength of the women's field — and junior categories ride 20″ bikes (UCI, n.d.-a) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-a) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-b).

This is part of the technical cycle disciplines — MTB Trials, Artistic Cycling, Cyr Wheel — within the cycling family (parent Cycling); the full list is under Important Links.

Rules

In a UCI trials competition the course contains a set number of sections (typically five) filled with natural and artificial obstacles; riders get a limited time per section and the lowest total penalty score wins the rainbow jersey (UCI, n.d.-a). The basic penalty is the dab: touching the ground with a foot costs one point, and five dabs in a section mean zero points for that section; additional penalties apply for other infringements, and touching an obstacle with the bike or body also costs points. Riders use rigid-frame bikes without saddles — 20″ for the 20-inch class and juniors, 26″ for the 26-inch class — with powerful brakes, and they may not put a foot down except as a penalised dab. Men's and women's elites ride the same course with the same scoring, and world champions wear the UCI rainbow jersey for a year (UCI, n.d.-b).

History

Trials evolved from motorcycle observed trials in Britain in the early 20th century, adapted to bicycles; the first UCI Trials World Championships were held in 1996, and Spain's Benito Ros became the most successful rider with six world titles, while Scotland's Danny MacAskill — photographed at the 2013 Sportnacht Davos — became the face of the modern freestyle trials scene and Britain's Jack Carthy is another multiple world medallist (UCI, n.d.-b). The sport split into the 20″ and 26″ classes, and urban trials events such as the UCI Urban Cycling World Championships (first held in 2017, including trials from 2026 in Riyadh) brought the discipline into city centres. The women's elite category has been contested at world championships since the 2000s, and Spain's Vera Barón dominated with four consecutive world titles from 2022 to 2025 before Alba Riera's 2026 World Cup success (UCI, n.d.-a).
