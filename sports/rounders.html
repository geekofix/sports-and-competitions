Rounders

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Rounders is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams who alternate between batting and fielding, scoring by completing a circuit of four bases or posts without being put out. It is one of the oldest organised bat-and-ball sports, first described in print in England in the 18th and early 19th centuries and formally codified in Ireland by the Gaelic Athletic Association in 1884; today the game is governed by the GAA in Ireland (GAA Rounders is one of its four official sports) and by Rounders England, whose National Rounders Association was founded in 1943. Rounders is a recognised ancestor of baseball.

Rules

The two codes differ in detail but share one essential game. Under Rounders England rules, up to nine players field (teams are limited to nine) and games are played on a diamond of posts up to a maximum 12 m apart, with the batting square between them; the bowler delivers underarm and a "good" ball must pass between the batter's knees and shoulders, and the batter must run on a good ball whether they hit it or not. One rounder is scored for a full circuit after a hit, with half-rounders awarded for reaching fourth post without hitting, or for obstruction or consecutive no-balls; batters are out if struck by a ball between posts or caught, and fielders stump posts to dismiss runners. Under GAA rules, the pitch is larger — a 70 m square with bases 25 m apart — the bat and ball are larger, and games consist of five innings (seven in senior championship finals) in which each team bats until three batters are put out; the pitcher must deliver underarm over home base between the batter's knees and shoulders, batters have three good pitches to hit, and runs are scored per base reached, with runners tagged out or forced as in baseball. Both codes permit mixed and single-sex teams, and GAA Rounders organises All-Ireland championships at junior, intermediate and senior level.

History

Rounders' ancestry lies in older English bat-and-ball games such as stoolball: a 1744 English children's publication (A Little Pretty Pocket-Book) already describes a recognisable predecessor, and William Clarke's The Boy's Own Book of 1828–29 printed the first detailed rules of rounders, alongside the first published description of baseball. The Gaelic Athletic Association included rounders in its original charter of 1884 alongside Gaelic football, hurling and handball — the first nationally formalised rules of the game — though it lay largely dormant for decades until a revival that began with the first formal GAA-rules match by Erin's Own of Antrim in 1958 and grew under organisers such as Peadar Ó Tuatáin. In England, the National Rounders Association of Liverpool and the Scottish Rounders Association were formed in 1889, and the modern National Rounders Association (Rounders England) was founded in 1943; the English game became a schoolyard staple for generations of children while remaining a competitive adult sport with national leagues. Both codes coexist and contest international fixtures of a kind, and the sport in Ireland continues to grow its All-Ireland championship structure under the GAA, which counts rounders among its four official sports to this day.
