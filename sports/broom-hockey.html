Broom Hockey

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Broom hockey is the dry-land, gym-class version of broomball: two teams play on a court with brooms (or broom-shaped sticks) and a ball, trying to score goals — essentially the indoor counterpart of broomball on ice. It is popular in school PE lessons and recreational leagues, especially in North America and Scandinavia; the organized ice variant is governed by the International Federation of Broomball Associations (IFBA).

Rules

In a typical broom hockey game, two teams of five to six players (including a goalkeeper) play on a marked court or gym floor with small goals at each end, using brooms to hit a soft ball into the opponent's goal. Games are usually two halves of 10-20 minutes with running substitutions. Key safety rules: the broom may never be swung above waist height, and checking is not allowed. Scoring is one point per goal; the team with the most goals wins. Because it is mostly recreational, local rules vary — some versions add offside or a crease around the goal.

History

Broom hockey emerged as a school and community game in Canada and the United States in the mid-20th century, using actual corn brooms with the bristles taped down — the origin of the name "broomball". It is the forerunner of organized broomball, which moved onto ice rinks and was codified by the IFBA (founded 1998 in Canada). Today broom hockey remains a staple of physical education classes and winter carnival games, while IFBA governs competitive broomball on ice and snow.
