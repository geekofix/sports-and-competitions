Russian Pyramid

≈ 3 min read

Basic Information Rules History Sources Important Links

Basic Information

Russian pyramid — also known as Russian billiards (russky bilyard) — is a pocket billiards game played on a large 12-by-6-foot (370 × 180 cm) table, the same size as a professional snooker table, with pockets only millimetres wider than the balls (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is the dominant cue sport across Russia and the former Soviet and Eastern Bloc countries, governed internationally by the International Pyramid Confederation (IPC), which joined the WPA family in 1998 and runs WPA World Pyramid Championships (WPA Pool, n.d.). Sixteen balls are used: fifteen numbered white object balls and a red, maroon or yellow cue ball, all 67–68 mm in diameter and about 255 g, far larger than Western pool balls (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's players compete in the same open events at world championships, with the women's game growing fast in Russia and Ukraine (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Rules

All pyramid games begin with the fifteen numbered white balls racked in a triangle at the foot of the table and the cue ball just in front of the baulk line; any object ball may be pocketed regardless of number, shots are not called, and the first player to pocket eight balls wins the frame (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Corner pockets are only about 3–4 mm wider than the 67 mm ball and side pockets 12–18 mm wider, demanding extreme precision (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The main variants are free pyramid (any ball may serve as cue ball; pocketing the struck ball without a carom off another ball is a foul), dynamic/Siberian pyramid (one cue ball; it may be pocketed by a carom, after which the scorer removes an object ball and gets ball-in-hand), combined/Moscow pyramid (as dynamic, but after pocketing the cue ball it is spotted in the "house" behind the baulk line where only side and far-corner pockets count until the next stroke), and classical pyramid (points-based, first to 71 of 130 points, the 1-ball worth 11 and the last ball 10) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Massé and jump shots are banned in official tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

History

Billiards reached Russia when Tsar Peter the Great brought a billiard table home from the Netherlands in 1705, and the game became a fixture of Russian aristocratic and merchant life (WPA Pool, n.d.). Russian pyramid in its modern form crystallized in the 19th and 20th centuries across the Russian Empire and the USSR, where it became the most popular cue sport, played in every city park and "culture house" (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Pyramid Confederation brought pyramid under the WPA umbrella in 1998, and WPA World Pyramid Championships have been held since the late 1990s (WPA Pool, n.d.). Retired icons include Evgeny Stalev of Russia (born 1979 in Lytkarino), WPA world pyramid champion in 1999 and 2000, and Yaroslav Vynokur of Ukraine, WPA world champion in 2003 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
