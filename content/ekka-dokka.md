## Basic Information

Ekka-dokka (also kutkut) is a Bengali hopscotch-style game traditionally played by girls: a large rectangle divided into six boxes is drawn on the ground, and the player rotates a small piece of broken pottery or clay (the guti) with the toes from box to box to the fifth box and back, singing the ekka-dokka melody while hopping on one foot. It belongs to the South Asian family of court games with chindro (stapoo) and is still played in rural Bengal and Bangladesh. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [Chindro](chindro.html), [Tipcat](tipcat.html), [Gutte](gutte.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

A large box is drawn and divided into six smaller boxes. The player places the guti in the first box, then rotates it with the toes into the second, third, fourth and fifth box, then back to the start, hopping on one foot and singing the ekka-dokka song; after a full round the guti is rolled again starting from the second box. Missing a box, touching a line or losing balance passes the turn to the next player; the first to complete the prescribed rounds wins.

## History

Ekka-dokka is one of the traditional games of rural Bengal documented in folk-game surveys of North Bengal (alongside gollachut, hamri, agdum-bagdum and others) and reflects older ideas of land division and ownership; the same game family includes chindro/stapoo across the subcontinent. It remains a girls' schoolyard game in West Bengal and Bangladesh.
