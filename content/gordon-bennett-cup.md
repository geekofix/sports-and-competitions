## Basic Information

The Coupe Aéronautique Gordon Bennett is the world’s oldest and most prestigious balloon race: gas balloons (hydrogen or coal gas, not hot air) launch together and the winner is the team that flies the farthest non-stop distance from the launch site (FAI, n.d.). It is the premier event of gas ballooning and part of the FAI ballooning family.

The 68th edition started from Metz, France, on 5 September 2025: the Swiss team SUI-1 of Kurt Frieden and Pascal Witprächtiger won their fifth title after 67 h 34 min in the air, flying 1,358 km non-stop to Gogosu, Romania (Gordon Bennett 2025, n.d.). Each national aero club may enter up to three teams of same-nationality pilots, with at least 50 hours of pilot-in-command experience and English-language ATC capability required (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Racing balloons are limited to about 1,000 m³ of gas — far smaller than passenger hot-air balloons — and can stay aloft up to about 100 hours; pilots manage ballast and gas to maximize distance, and the winner’s national aero club hosts the race two years later (Wikipedia, n.d.). The all-time distance record is 3,400 km, flown by Belgians Bob Berben and Benoît Siméons from Albuquerque to Quebec in 2005; the longest time aloft was over 92 hours by Germans Wilhelm Eimers and Bernd Landsmann in 1995 (Wikipedia, n.d.). There are no gender classes — mixed crews are the norm, and women have won the cup as pilots.

## History

The race was founded by James Gordon Bennett Jr., owner of the New York Herald: the first 16 balloons launched from the Tuileries Gardens in Paris on 30 September 1906 before a crowd of 200,000 (FAI, n.d.). It ran until 1938, was suspended through the wars, and was revived in 1979 by American atmospheric physicist Tom Heinsheimer; the FAI officially reinstated it in 1983 (Wikipedia, n.d.). France’s Vincent Leys won it nine times between 1997 and 2017, and American teams hold the most victories overall (twelve) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
