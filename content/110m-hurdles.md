## Basic Information

The 110 metres hurdles is the men's sprint hurdles event of athletics and the classic Olympic hurdle race: ten hurdles at 1.067 m are sprinted over 110 m, the first 13.72 m from the start, the rest 9.14 m apart and 14.02 m before the finish. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics; the world record is 12.75 s, set by Ja'Kobe Tharp of the USA in the semi-finals of the 2026 NCAA Outdoor Championships in Eugene on 10 June 2026, breaking the 12.80 s record of Aries Merritt (Brussels 2012) that had stood for 14 years (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the men's event was led by world record holder Ja'Kobe Tharp (pictured), who won the 2026 Diamond League Final in Brussels (13.03 s) and the inaugural 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship ahead of Cordell Tinch — the 2025 world champion — and Bradley Franklin, with Grant Holloway, the 2024 Olympic champion, Kendry Méndez and Jamal Britt among the other leading hurdlers (World Athletics, n.d.-d). Because the Olympic programme stages the women's sprint hurdles over 100 m, the women's equivalent of this event is the 100m Hurdles on this site.

## Rules

The 110 m hurdles is run in separate lanes from a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks; since 2010 a single false start disqualifies, and the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line by photo finish to 1/1000 s, recorded to 1/100 s (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-b). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

The ten hurdles stand 1.067 m high, 13.72 m from the start, 9.14 m apart and 14.02 m from the finish; knocking a hurdle down is not a foul, but leaving the lane to impede another competitor is a disqualification (World Athletics, n.d.-b). A wind reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-b). At under-20 level the hurdles are 0.99 m high, and para athletics contests the event in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

## History

The hurdles trace back to the 120-yard high hurdles of 19th-century British and American athletics; the metric 110 m event has been on the Olympic programme since Athens 1896 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Early icons include Forrest Smithson, the 1908 Olympic champion, and Martin Lauer; Renaldo Nehemiah became the first man under 13 seconds with 12.93 in 1981. Roger Kingdom won Olympic gold in 1984 and 1988, Colin Jackson set a world record of 12.91 in 1993, and Dayron Robles of Cuba won the 2008 Olympic title with a then world record of 12.87 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Liu Xiang of China, pictured, won the 2004 Olympic gold and set the world record of 12.88 in 2006, while Aries Merritt, pictured, capped his 2012 season with Olympic gold in London and a world record of 12.80 s at the Memorial Van Damme in Brussels — the mark that stood until Tharp's 12.75 in 2026 (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-e).
