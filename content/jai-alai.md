## Basic Information

Jai alai — Basque: cesta punta, "basket point" — is the fastest ball game in the world: a hard leather pelota is hurled against the front wall of a 54–60 m fronton with a curved wicker basket (cesta) strapped to the right hand, reaching speeds above 300 km/h (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport belongs to the family of Basque pelota, played in the Basque Country since at least the 13th century, and is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque (FIPV), founded in 1929; the Basque Pelota World Championships have been held since 1952, and the 2026 edition — the first ever in Argentina, jointly with Uruguay — adds frontball and adapted pelota (Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque, n.d.). Professional jai alai in the United States began with the first pro fronton in Miami in 1926; the Dania Beach fronton near Fort Lauderdale opened on 23 December 1953 and its 2025/26 season runs from 4 December 2025 to 28 February 2026, while Casino Miami reopened as the JAM Arena on 13 February 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women's events have been part of the world championships since 1990, and Becky Smith became the first woman to sign a professional jai alai contract in the late 1980s and the first woman to play on a 33 m fronton on 22 May 2019 — she heads the roll of honor of the women's game in 2026 alongside the leading pelotaris of Spain and France (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In cesta punta, only right-handed players compete: the cesta is worn on the right arm, and the pelota may only be caught and thrown with the basket — bare-hand play is a different variant (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A helmet is mandatory (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The serve must bounce behind the short line (line 11), strike the front wall and land between lines 4 and 7; a faulted serve is retaken, and two consecutive faults lose the point (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Rallies then continue until the ball bounces twice, goes out, or a player fails to return it — one bounce only is allowed (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Traditional games are played to 7 or 9 points; in the World Jai Alai League format, sets are played to 6 points, best of three sets, with rally scoring — every rally wins a point (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Points are lost for letting the ball bounce twice ("falta"), hitting it out of the court or into the gallery beyond the boundary lines, and a "charla" (ball hitting the front wall without bouncing properly on the return) ends the rally (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play on identical frontons under identical rules; women's singles, doubles and mixed events have been contested at world championships since 1990 (Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque, n.d.). Other fronton variants include 30 m and 36 m courts, and the 2026 world championship program adds frontball and adapted pelota for para athletes (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Pelota vasca has been played in the Basque Country since the 13th century, originally with the palm of the hand (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The cesta — a curved wicker basket — was invented in Guipúzcoa in the 1850s, and the name jai alai ("merry festival") was coined by the writer Serafín Baroja in 1875 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first enclosed fronton with the modern dimensions was built in the late 19th century, and the game spread to Cuba, the Philippines and the United States, where the first professional fronton opened in Miami in 1926 and the great Dania Beach fronton in Florida on 23 December 1953 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The FIPV was founded in 1929 and has organized the Basque Pelota World Championships since 1952 (Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque, n.d.). Women entered the professional game in the late 1980s with Becky Smith, the first female professional pelotari, who played her first match on a 33 m fronton on 22 May 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the icons of the sport are the legendary cesta punta champions of the 20th century from the Basque Country and Florida, whose names fill the fronton halls of fame (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
