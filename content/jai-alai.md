## Basic Information

Jai alai (Basque for "merry festival", also called cesta punta, "basket tip") is a spectacularly fast court game from the Basque pelota family: players use a curved wicker basket glove called a cesta, strapped to the hand, to catch a hard leather ball (pelota) and hurl it against the walls of a three-walled court called a fronton at speeds exceeding 300 km/h, earning it the nickname "the fastest sport on Earth". It is played professionally in Florida, where the World Jai Alai League operates frontons in the Miami area, and remains central to Basque culture in Spain and France.

## Rules

A fronton court is about 54 m long with a high front wall and side walls marked with numbered lines. The server bounces the pelota and serves it so that it lands beyond the service line and strikes the front wall; the receiving team may let the ball bounce once before catching it in the cesta and throwing it back in a single fluid motion, because holding the ball is a foul. A point is won when the ball cannot be returned, is hit out of bounds, or lands short of the required lines; matches are played as singles or doubles, typically to 7 or 9 points, with pari-mutuel betting traditionally attached at professional frontons. The ball weighs about 100-115 g and is replaced regularly because it wears and softens with play.

## History

The pelota games of the Basque Country date to at least the 13th century, and the modern cesta was developed in Guipúzcoa in the 1850s; the term jai alai was coined by the writer Serafín Baroja in 1875. The game crossed to Cuba in 1901 and reached the United States at the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair. The first Florida fronton opened at Hialeah on February 2, 1924, and Miami's first professional fronton opened in 1926; at its peak in the late 1970s there were fourteen professional frontons in the US with more than 300 pelotaris, and on Fridays the Miami fronton drew bigger crowds than the Dolphins. Dania Beach's Casino fronton, opened December 23, 1953, was the last traditional fronton and closed in 2021, but the sport is alive again through the World Jai Alai League, which relaunched the Miami fronton as JAM Arena in February 2026.
