## Basic Information

Winter triathlon is the cold-season version of triathlon, raced over snow rather than open water and pavement — its standard form consisting of running on snow, mountain biking on snow, and cross-country skiing, all on packed snow courses. Distances vary by event, with typical races built around legs of several kilometres each, and the whole field racing in running shoes, studded mountain bikes, and skate skiing technique.

It has an international governing platform and world championships through World Triathlon's winter triathlon structure, with strong participation in Alpine and Scandinavian countries.

## Rules

Standard world championship formats run the three legs in continuous succession — run, mountain bike, cross-country ski — over marked snow courses, with transition areas for changing footwear and equipment as in summer triathlon. Mountain bikes must conform to regulations on tyre width and studs, cross-country legs follow groomed tracks allowed in free technique, and drafting rules mirror triathlon's by being legal or staged per event format. Age-group and elite waves compete separately, with snowshoe-running variants in some events replacing one leg, and national federations set specific local rules for winter conditions including cold-weather clothing and course safety.

## History

Winter triathlon emerged in the late 1980s and 1990s in snowy countries of central Europe, with early championship events staged in the Alpine regions of Italy and Austria, blending cross-country skiing culture with triathlon's rising boom. The International Triathlon Union (now World Triathlon) adopted the discipline formally, holding winter triathlon world championships from 1997 onwards, with Italian and Scandinavian champions and host towns in Italy, Norway, Germany and Czechia featuring among medallists. Events continue with a dedicated world tour, and experimental format updates through the 2010s and 2020s keep the sport alive at amateur levels as well as well as elite.
