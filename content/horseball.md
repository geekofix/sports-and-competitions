## Basic Information

Horseball is an equestrian team sport described as "basketball and rugby on horseback": four riders per team (plus two substitutes) pass a ball fitted with handles and try to throw it through the opponent's hoop (1 m diameter, 3.5 m high) after at least three passes between three different players. It is governed by the Fédération Internationale de Horseball (FIHB, founded 1999), which holds the international rules and rankings and has a memorandum of understanding with the FEI. It originated from the Argentine game pato and was codified in France in the late 1970s.

## Rules

Under the FIHB International Rules of Horseball, each team fields four players on the pitch plus two substitutes, and a match is played in two halves of about 8 minutes plus a break. A goal is scored by throwing the ball (fitted with six leather handles) through the opponent's hoop mounted 3.5 m high at each end; before shooting, the attacking team must complete at least three passes between three different players. Contact between horses and riders is allowed within rugby-like limits, dangerous play is penalized, and the ball remains live when it bounces. Penalties include free throws and yellow/red cards. The FIHB runs the European Championships (senior, U16/U21) and maintains the World Horseball Ranking.

## History

Horseball descends from the Argentine national sport pato (played with a ball with handles, thrown into goals), brought to France in the 1970s; rules were codified in France in 1978-79, and the French Equestrian Federation accepted it as a discipline. The International Commission of Horseball was set up in 1992, the first European Cup was held in Paris that year, and the FIHB was founded in 1999 (Barcelona). The FIHB signed a memorandum of understanding with the FEI (International Federation for Equestrian Sports), and horseball is played in over 20 countries, strongest in France, Belgium, Germany, Great Britain, Portugal, Italy, Spain and Argentina.
