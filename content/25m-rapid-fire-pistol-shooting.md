## Basic Information

25m Rapid Fire Pistol is an ISSF shooting discipline: fast, timed strings of five shots at five turning targets 25 meters away. It is the only shooting event on the current Olympic program that dates back to the first modern Games in Athens 1896, and it is contested today as a men's Olympic event — women shoot the 25m pistol event with precision and rapid-fire stages instead. See also [10m Air Pistol Shooting](10m-air-pistol-shooting.html) and [Shooting](shooting.html) on this site (International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.-a) (International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.-b).

At the Paris 2024 Olympics the men's gold went to Li Yuehong of China; the ISSF world ranking in November 2026 is led by Clément Bessaguet of France. In the women's counterpart event, the 25m pistol, the Paris 2024 title went to Yang Ji-in of South Korea and the current world ranking leader is Erica Sessa of Italy (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.; ISSF, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under ISSF rules shooters use a .22 Long Rifle (5.6 mm) rimfire semi-automatic pistol, fired one-handed and unsupported from a 45-degree ready position (International Shooting Sport Federation [ISSF], 2023). Five target sets stand side by side with their centers exactly 75 cm apart, and one shot must be fired at each target within every series; the targets turn to face the shooter exactly when the time begins, and shots fired too late score as misses. The course of fire is 60 shots: two stages of 30, each with two five-shot series at 8 seconds, then two at 6 seconds, then two at 4 seconds, scored in whole rings to a maximum of 600 points (ISSF, 2023).

At major championships, the six best shooters qualify for the final. The final is shot as additional 4-second five-shot series scored as hit-or-miss: any shot scoring 9.7 or above counts as one hit, which corresponds to a circle of 124 mm diameter on the target, and everything below scores as a miss; the lowest shooter is eliminated after each series from the fourth series onward, which builds the decisive drama of the event (IOC, n.d.).

The historical gender rule is the defining classification difference of this discipline: rapid fire pistol is a men's event on the Olympic and world championship program, with a men's junior event below it, while women and women juniors contest the 25m pistol event — a discipline combining a precision stage and a rapid-fire stage on the same target distance (ISSF, 2023; IOC, n.d.). Older rules editions used different string times in qualification, so national or historical competitions can differ from the Olympic format; the classic rules also permit only one claimed malfunction per 30-shot stage and penalize fired double shots per target exposure (ISSF, 2023).

## History

Rapid fire pistol has been on the Olympic program since Athens 1896, though its format changed repeatedly before World War II and was stabilized in the post-war era. Its early heroes include Hungary's Károly Takács, who lost his right hand in a grenade accident in 1936, relearned shooting with his left hand and won back-to-back Olympic titles in 1948 and 1952 — one of the most remarkable comebacks in Olympic history (IOC, n.d.).

Germany's Ralf Schumann is widely considered the greatest rapid fire shooter of all time, with three Olympic titles (1992, 1996, 2004) and a record haul of world championship and World Cup titles (ISSF, n.d.-a). Leuris Pupo of Cuba took the gold at London 2012, Christian Reitz of Germany won at Rio de Janeiro 2016 with 34 hits, equaling Pupo's final Olympic record, and Jean Quiquampoix of France followed at Tokyo 2020 before Li Yuehong of China won at Paris 2024 (ISSF, 2016; IOC, n.d.).
