## Basic Information

Cross-country mountain biking (XCO) is the Olympic mountain-bike racing discipline: riders start together in a mass-start race over several laps of a technical off-road circuit with climbs, descents, rocks and roots, and the first across the line wins (Union Cycliste Internationale [UCI], n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). Mountain biking is governed worldwide by the UCI, which sanctions the cross-country disciplines — XCO, the short-track XCC, the marathon XCM and e-mountain bike — at the Olympic Games, the World Championships and the World Cup (UCI, n.d.-b). Mountain biking has been an Olympic medal sport since Atlanta 1996, and at Paris 2024 Pauline Ferrand-Prévot of France won the women's gold on the Colline d'Élancourt course, with Tom Pidcock of Great Britain winning the men's event (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 UCI Mountain Bike World Championships were held in Val di Sole, Italy, from 26 to 30 August 2026: in the elite XCO races Tom Pidcock won his second world title (after Glentress 2023) and Sina Frei of Switzerland took the women's gold, with Martina Berta of Italy on silver; Adrien Boichis of France won the men's short-track XCC title (UCI, n.d.-a). XCO is raced by women and men over the same course at World Championships and Olympics — the elite women and men contest identical circuits — and age categories run from juniors (U18) through under-23 to the elite class, in both genders; para-cycling's mountain-bike events (PT5/PT4 handbikes and tandems) are governed by the UCI alongside (UCI, n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current riders are the Val di Sole world champions — Sina Frei (Switzerland) first among the women, with Martina Berta and Pauline Ferrand-Prévot — and Tom Pidcock (Great Britain) among the men, with Loana Lecomte and Adrien Boichis of France also at the top of the UCI rankings (UCI, n.d.-a). ## Rules

XCO races are governed by the UCI Mountain Bike Regulations: the circuit is typically 3.5–5 km per lap, and the race distance is set so that elite races last about 80–90 minutes (roughly 6–8 laps for men, slightly fewer for women), with the exact number of laps announced before the start; junior and U23 races are shorter (UCI, n.d.-b). All riders start together in a mass start, seeded by UCI ranking with the leaders on the front rows; overtaking is unrestricted, but a rider lapped at the 80% point of the winner's race must withdraw (UCI, n.d.-b). Bikes are mountain bikes — front suspension is allowed and full-suspension bikes are common; wheel size is free (29-inch is standard), and aerodynamic add-ons such as clip-on aero bars are prohibited; a number plate and helmet (mandatory) are required (UCI, n.d.-b). Repairs and wheel/bike changes are only allowed in the designated technical assistance zones; outside help is forbidden and can lead to relegation or disqualification, as is dangerous riding and doping under the UCI Anti-Doping Rules (UCI, n.d.-b). Formats: the XCO is the Olympic and World Championship mass-start event; XCC (cross-country eliminator/short track) is a short, fast race on a circuit of about 1–1.5 km lasting roughly 20–30 minutes; XCM is a point-to-point marathon of 60–100+ km; e-MTB races use pedal-assist bikes with a speed cut-off of 25 km/h (UCI, n.d.-b). Scoring at World Cups and Championships is by finish order — World Cup points run 250 for the winner down the scale, and nations are ranked by their riders' points (UCI, n.d.-a). The rules are identical for women and men; only the race distance differs slightly at some events, while World Championship and Olympic XCO courses are the same for both (UCI, n.d.-b).

## History

Mountain-bike racing grew out of the late-1970s California klunker scene, when riders raced modified cruiser bikes down Mount Tamalpais; the first organized races and the first mountain-bike world championships followed in the 1980s, and cross-country racing became an Olympic sport at Atlanta 1996, won by Bart Brentjens of the Netherlands and Paola Pezzo of Italy (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2000s and 2010s were defined by icons such as France's Julien Absalon (b. 1980), Olympic champion 2008 and five-time world champion, and Switzerland's Nino Schurter (b. 1986), ten-time world champion, who dominated the men's circuit; on the women's side, Norway's Gunn-Rita Dahle Flesjå (b. 1973), Olympic champion 2004 and twelve-time world champion, and France's Pauline Ferrand-Prévot (b. 1992), Olympic champion 2024 and multiple world champion, set the standard (Wikipedia, n.d.). The rainbow jersey picture changed at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, where Switzerland's Jolanda Neff and Tom Pidcock won gold, and the 2026 Val di Sole worlds confirmed the new generation with Pidcock's second title and Sina Frei's first (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.). The discipline has also embraced the short-track XCC format at the World Championships since 2024, won in 2026 by Adrien Boichis, adding a spectator-friendly sprint spectacle to the classic XCO (UCI, n.d.-a).
