## Basic Information

Cross-country mountain biking (XCO) is the Olympic mountain bike discipline: a mass-start race on a 4-6 km loop repeated for a set number of laps, covering technical climbs, descents, rock gardens and jumps, decided in about 80-100 minutes. The marathon format (XCM) races 60-100 km point to point. Both are governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), which runs the Mountain Bike World Cup and World Championships; the XCO has been Olympic since Atlanta 1996.

## Rules

Under the UCI Mountain Bike Rules, XCO races start with a mass start on a circuit of at least 4 km (for elites), with the number of laps set so the race lasts about 80-100 minutes; the first rider to cross the finish line wins. Riders may change bikes or wheels in the pit zone, and lapped riders may be pulled depending on the 80% rule. Helmets are mandatory. In the XCM marathon, the race is point-to-point over 60-100 km with mass start; time bonuses can apply at feed zones. Downhill and enduro are separate UCI disciplines with their own rules.

## History

Mountain biking emerged in California in the 1970s (Gary Fisher, Joe Breeze and the Repack Race in Marin County, 1976), and the first UCI Mountain Bike World Championships were held in 1990 in Il Ciocco, Italy. Cross-country became an Olympic sport at Atlanta 1996, won by Bart Brentjens (men) and Paola Pezzo (women). The UCI Mountain Bike World Cup circuit began in 1991. Stars include Julien Absalon and Pauline Ferrand-Prévot (France) and Nino Schurter (Switzerland).
