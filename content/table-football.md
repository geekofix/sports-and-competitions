## Basic Information

Table football (foosball, German *Tischfußball*) is the game of sliding rod-mounted figures on a miniature pitch to kick a small ball into the opponent's goal. It is governed internationally by the International Table Soccer Federation (ITSF), founded in 2002 and headquartered in Nantes, France, which sanctions five official table brands — Bonzini, Roberto Sport, Garlando, Tornado and Leonhart — and runs the ITSF World Cup and World Championships (since 2004) with singles, doubles, mixed and women's events; the US-based World Football Table Soccer Association (WFT) has staged world championships since 1974 (ITSF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's World Cup and World Championship events have been part of the ITSF calendar for years, with women competing on equal terms in singles and doubles (ITSF, n.d.). A new World Foosball Tournament (WFT) launched in 2025 with ESPN2 broadcasting planned for 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

A regulation table is about 120 × 68 cm (tournament tables 34–36 in high), with eight rods per side carrying 11 figures in the classic 5-5-3-5 arrangement (one goalkeeper, two defenders, five midfielders, three attackers) — European tables use a single goalkeeper rod and sloped corners, while some US tables have three goalkeepers and flat corners (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Players turn the rods to pass, shoot and defend; a "spin" — rotating a rod 360° without striking the ball — is penalized, with the opponent awarded the ball or a goal depending on the rule set, and a ball that stops dead is restarted by the last player to touch it (ITSF, n.d.). Games are played to 5 goals (some tables 7 or 10, win by two at deuce) within best-of-five-game matches at ITSF events; doubles rotate service and strike order between partners (ITSF, n.d.). Men's and women's singles and doubles, plus mixed doubles, are contested under identical rules (ITSF, n.d.).

## History

The game's ancestor is the 19th-century parlor pastime of pushing coins on a table; the first foosball patent was filed in 1922 by Harold Searles Thornton in England, and the name foosball comes from the German *Tischfußball* (table football) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game peaked in American bars in the 1970s after being brought to the US in the 1950s, and artist Maurizio Cattelan's 2001 work *Stadium* — a 7-metre foosball table with miniature players — became a famous art-world statement (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Organized play revived with the WFT/IFP US world championships from 1974 in Denver (Tornado tables, biennial since 2015) and the founding of the ITSF in 2002 in Nantes, which unified the international calendar (ITSF, n.d.).
