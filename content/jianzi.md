## Basic Information

Jianzi (ti jianzi, "kick shuttlecock") is a traditional Chinese sport in which players keep a heavily weighted feathered shuttlecock in the air using their feet and other body parts, but never their hands or arms. It can be played casually in a circle in a park, or competitively over a net on a badminton-sized court, where teams score points by making the shuttlecock land in the opponents' court. The sport is governed internationally by the International Shuttlecock Federation (ISF), founded in 1999, which stages the World Shuttlecock Championships.

## Rules

The official competition shuttlecock consists of four equal-length feathers joined at a rubber or plastic base, weighing about 15-25 g and measuring 15-21 cm. In the competitive net game, two teams play on a badminton court with a net, and a team may touch the shuttlecock at most three times per side before it must cross the net; players may use feet, knees, chest and head but not hands or arms. Rally scoring is used, with sets played to 21 points and a two-point advantage required. In the freestyle/circle form, players simply try to keep the shuttlecock aloft for as long as possible, with spectacular overhead and spinning kicks in competition routines.

## History

Jianzi has been played in China since the Han dynasty (206 BC-220 AD), and is believed to have evolved from cuju, the ancient football-like game used as military training. It was especially popular during the Tang dynasty, and formal competitions were held from the Ming dynasty onward. A Chinese athlete from Jiangsu demonstrated the game at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, introducing it to Europe, where it became known simply as "shuttlecock". The International Shuttlecock Federation was founded in 1999 and the first World Championship was held in Újszász, Hungary, in 2000; the Shuttlecock Federation of Europe was founded in 2003, and the sport has been part of the Southeast Asian Games since 2003 and of the Asian Indoor Games since 2009. Vietnam, where the game is called đá cầu, has won the world title many times.
