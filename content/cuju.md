## Basic Information

Cuju (Chinese: 蹴鞠, "kick ball") is an ancient Chinese ball game in which players keep a leather ball in the air with their feet, chest, back and head — hands are not allowed — and is recognized by FIFA as the earliest form of football. It was played from the Han dynasty (206 BC - 220 AD) onward, used as military training, and later became a court and street entertainment, including a women's version in the Tang dynasty.

## Rules

In the classical form, two teams faced each other across a rectangular field with two goal openings (jū) at each end, and players tried to kick the ball through the opponent's goal; points were scored per goal. In the non-competitive form, players simply kept the ball aloft as long as possible, performing acrobatic kicks. The ball was a leather sphere stuffed with feathers or hair. Later variants used a single high goal (like a hoop) and introduced goalkeepers. Rules varied by dynasty and region.

## History

Cuju is documented from the Warring States period and flourished in the Han dynasty, when Emperor Wu promoted it for military fitness. In the Tang dynasty it became a court spectacle with music and women players, and in the Song dynasty professional cuju clubs and the first rules associations appeared; the book "Qi Dong Ye Yu" records rules. It declined in the Ming and Qing dynasties as other games gained favor. In 2004 FIFA officially recognized cuju as the earliest form of football in its history of the game.
