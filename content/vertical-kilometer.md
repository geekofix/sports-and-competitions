## Basic Information

The vertical kilometer (VK, Kilomètre Vertical) is a skyrunning race with exactly 1,000 m of elevation gain over a course of less than 5 km — the purest uphill running format (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the mountain-running family with fell running, trail running and skyrunning.

The discipline is governed by the International Skyrunning Federation (ISF); the Vertical Kilometer World Circuit, launched in 2017 as a spin-off of the Skyrunner World Series, runs 15–20 races per season from May to October (Wikipedia, n.d.). Trekking poles are allowed as optional equipment (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

World records: men 27:21 by Switzerland’s Rémi Bonnet at the Kilomètre Vertical de Fully on 18 October 2025 (breaking Philip Götsch’s 28:53 from 2017); women 32:52 by France’s Axelle Gachet-Mollaret at Fully 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Fully course follows an old funicular line with gradients over 50% (Fast and Light, 2025). Variants include the Double VK (2,000 m) and Triple VK (3,000 m) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women race on the same courses in separate categories.

## History

The vertical kilometer as a formalized race emerged in the 1990s within the skyrunning movement pioneered by Italian mountaineer Bruno Brunod, who created uphill-only races with minimal horizontal distance (Fast and Light, 2025). The Kilomètre Vertical de Fully in the Rhône valley became the benchmark event from the 2010s; the ISF now runs both mountain and skyrunning world championships with the VK as a championship discipline (Wikipedia, n.d.).
