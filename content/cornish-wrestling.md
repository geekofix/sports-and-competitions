## Basic Information

Cornish wrestling (omdowl Kernewek, "wrasslin'") is the traditional jacket wrestling of Cornwall: a standing catch-hold of the jackets above the waist, tripping and hooking allowed, no groundwork, decided by a back fall (or by three fouls/"go downs"). It is similar to Breton gouren and is classed with Cumberland & Westmorland as Celtic wrestling by the Fédération Internationale des Luttes Celtiques. It survives at Cornish games, the Royal Cornwall Show and the Gorsedh Kernow; men and (increasingly) women compete. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Cumberland & Westmorland Wrestling](cumberland-westmorland-wrestling.html), [Lancashire Wrestling](lancashire-wrestling.html), [Dorset Wrestling](dorset-wrestling.html), [Wrestling](wrestling.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).

## Rules

West Country catch-hold rules: wrestlers grip each other's jackets above the waist; the bout starts from a chest-to-chest clinch; trips and leg hooks are allowed; the first wrestler whose back or any body part (but feet or hands) touches the ground loses — a back fall decides; three fouls ("foils") or go-downs substitute one back fall in some venues; matches are best of three falls in championship bouts. No ground wrestling, no strangling. Costume: shorts and vest; the heavyweight championship belt is a famous trophy (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).

## History

Cornish wrestling is ancient — Geoffrey of Monmouth's 1139 Historia Regum Britanniae has the giant Gogmagog wrestle Corineus at Plymouth Hoe, and the Book of Leinster links Celtic wrestling to the Tailteann Games (c. 1829 BC). It flourished in the 18th–19th centuries (London matches at Sadler's Wells drew huge crowds; champions like Jack Rowett, John Slade, Hali Adali). It declined in the 20th century but is kept alive at the Gorsedh Kernow and country shows; the 1866 Illustrated London News scene shows a London match (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
