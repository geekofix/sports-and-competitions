## Basic Information

Unicycle hockey is ice hockey's one-wheeled cousin: two teams of five riders on unicycles dribble, pass and shoot a tennis ball with ice-hockey sticks at goals set into the court, and every player must be mounted and riding to play the ball (International Unicycling Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Unicycling Federation (IUF) is the world governing body and publishes the official rulebook — most recently the 2019 edition — while international competition runs at the biennial Unicon world championships and at regional events such as Eurocycle and the Asia-Pacific Unicycle Competition (International Unicycling Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Women and men compete side by side in the same open league structure — German clubs field mixed and women's teams alike — and at Unicon 22 in Steyr, Austria, in July–August 2026 the Swiss Team defended its world championship title in a tournament with German women and men among the national rosters (Wikipedia, n.d.; Deutsche Einradhockeyliga, n.d.).

## Rules

The court measures 35–45 m by 20–25 m with beveled or rounded corners and barriers on all sides, and the goals are set back from the end walls so players can ride behind them, as in ice hockey; a team fields five players plus substitutes, with no dedicated goalkeeper position, although one player usually stays back (International Unicycling Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Any stick legal for ice hockey — except a goalkeeper's stick — may be used, the unicycle wheel may be at most 24 inches (61 cm, 618 mm) in diameter, and the ball is a tennis ball (International Unicycling Federation, n.d.). A player must be freely riding the unicycle to play the ball: using the stick as a support, touching walls, goals or other players with the hand, and body contact are prohibited, and play stops when a rider dismounts and resumes when the rider remounts (International Unicycling Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches under the IUF rulebook run in two halves, typically 2 × 15–20 minutes, with face-offs after goals and at the start of each half; men and women play under identical rules in mixed or single-sex teams across open and youth age groups (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The first known instance of hockey on a unicycle is a segment in the 1925 film *Variety*, showing two stage performers pushing a ball with a field-hockey stick (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Albuquerque Unicycle Club in New Mexico — founded in 1957 and for years the world's only unicycle club — listed hockey among its activities in 1960, and its president Theodore Jorgensen (1944–2015) is regarded as the pioneer of organized unicycle hockey; the game was also played in Japan by 1971 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Wheel People club was founded in California in 1976, LaHiMo became the first German club in Langenfeld in 1985, LUNIS formed in north London in 1987 and won the first British national competition at Covent Garden in 1988, and Jens Stemminger founded the Uniwheelers in Bremen in 1990 — clubs that led to the German league founded in 1995, still the world's largest (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first European championship was held at the European Juggling Convention in Birmingham in September 1993, and the first world championship at Unicon VII in Minneapolis in 1994, won by Germany; every Unicon since has crowned world champions, with the Swiss Team the reigning title holder (Wikipedia, n.d.).
