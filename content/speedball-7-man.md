## Basic Information

Speedball 7-man is the seven-a-side tournament format of speedball paintball: two teams of seven players race across a symmetric field of inflatable bunkers, trying to eliminate opponents and hang the opponent's flag in their own start box before the clock runs out. The format was popularized by the National Professional Paintball League (NPPL), a player-run league formed in Chicago whose Super 7 series ran from 2003 to 2013, and it remains the standard layout of major XBall events today (NPPL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The field measures about 180 ft × 100 ft (55 × 30 m) with mirrored bunkers, start boxes and a center flag station, and rosters may carry up to 19 players who substitute freely between points (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women's and mixed teams compete in the same divisions — the rules contain no gender split — and paintball overall counts millions of recreational players of all genders (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The speedball 7-man field is 180 ft × 100 ft (about 55 × 30 m), mirrored left-right, with a start box at each end and a flag station in the middle; teams field 7 players per point from rosters of up to 19, and the game clock runs a 7-minute countdown per point (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Equipment: .68-caliber paintball markers limited to about 280–300 fps muzzle velocity, a mandatory full-face mask, a hopper and air tank; a player is eliminated when a paintball breaks on their body or equipment, and the penalty is a timed stay in the penalty box before re-entry at the own start box (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A point ends when one team hangs the opponent's flag in its own start box, all opponents are eliminated, or time expires; scoring awards points for the flag hang, each elimination and each player still alive at the horn, so aggressive flag play and survival both pay (NPPL, n.d.). The same field and rules serve 5-man (Super 5) and 10-man formats, differing only in the number of players on the field; men's, women's and mixed teams play under identical rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The paintball marker was developed by the Nelson Paint Company in 1965, and the first recorded paintball game was played on 27 June 1981 at H's Ranch in Victoria, Texas, by Charles Gaines and friends; early tournament play used grass fields, and inflatable bunkers — introduced by the French company Sup'Air in 1994 — created the modern speedball look (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The NPPL was founded in Chicago as a player-run league in the early 2000s and staged the Super 7 from 2003 to 2013 before going bankrupt in 2008; its events reached television when ESPN2 broadcast the 2005 Commander's Cup in Miami in spring 2006 and the XBall/Smart Parts World Championship in fall 2006 (NPPL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Iconic teams of the era included Dynasty and the Russian Legion, who dominated the mid-2000s XBall circuit (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
