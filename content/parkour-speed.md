## Basic Information

Parkour speed (speedrun) is the timed discipline of competitive [parkour](parkour.html): the athlete races against the clock over a standardized obstacle course, using vaults, jumps and precise footwork to get from the start to the finish as fast as possible. It is one of the two FIG parkour disciplines alongside [parkour freestyle](parkour-freestyle.html), contested at the FIG Parkour World Cup since 2018 and at the World Championships, and it was part of parkour's debut at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham. The format is similar in spirit to [obstacle course racing](obstacle-course-racing.html) and [ninja sport](ninja-sport.html), but on a compact standardized course.

## Rules

In a speedrun competition, the athlete must complete the standardized course in the shortest possible time; the course consists of a sequence of obstacles such as walls, rails, boxes and bars set at fixed distances, built to FIG specifications so results are comparable worldwide. Timing is electronic from start to finish. Touching the ground between obstacles, missing an obstacle or using an illegal path brings time penalties or disqualification under the FIG rules. Athletes compete in men's and women's categories, usually in qualification runs followed by knockout or final heats, and the fastest time wins.

## History

Speedrunning as a competitive format emerged when the FIG formalized parkour in 2017 and defined two disciplines, speedrun and freestyle, with David Belle and Charles Perrière working with the federation toward recognition. The first FIG Parkour World Cup events were held in 2018 within the FISE World Action Sports Festival in Montpellier, and the FIG Parkour World Championships have been held since 2018, with speedrun among the medal events. Parkour's first appearance at a major multi-sport event came at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham, USA, where both speedrun and freestyle were contested.
