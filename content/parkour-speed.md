## Basic Information

Parkour speed (officially speedrun in FIG terminology) is the timed discipline of competitive [parkour](parkour.html): the athlete races against the clock over a standardized obstacle course, using vaults, jumps and precise footwork to travel from the start to the finish as fast as possible. It is one of the two disciplines of the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) parkour program alongside [parkour freestyle](parkour-freestyle.html), contested at the FIG Parkour World Cup since 2018 and at the FIG Parkour World Championships, and it was part of parkour's debut at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham, Alabama (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). The format is similar in spirit to [obstacle course racing](obstacle-course-racing.html) and [ninja sport](ninja-sport.html), but on a compact, standardized course designed for comparability. Men and women compete in separate speedrun categories with identical courses and rules, and women's speedrun — won at world level by athletes such as France's multiple World Cup medalists in the women's category — is fully established on the international calendar (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). ## Rules

In a speedrun competition governed by the FIG parkour rules, the athlete must complete a standardized course — a fixed sequence of obstacles such as walls, rails, boxes and bars set at defined distances and heights — in the shortest possible time, with electronic timing from the start line to the finish (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). The course is built to FIG specifications at every World Cup and World Championship venue so that times are comparable worldwide, and the same course layout is used for men and women. Touching the ground between obstacles, missing an obstacle or leaving the designated path brings time penalties or disqualification under the FIG rules; athletes typically get a set number of runs in qualification, and the best time advances to the final or to knockout heats. Safety requirements apply to course construction, landing zones and matting, and athletes compete in men's and women's categories as well as junior and senior age groups, with the fastest clean time winning (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Because the course is identical for all competitors, the discipline is often described as pure racing against the clock and against one's own best time.

## History

Speedrun as a competitive format was defined when the FIG formalized parkour in 2017, splitting competitive parkour into the two disciplines speedrun and freestyle, with founding traceurs David Belle and Charles Perrière working with the federation toward Olympic recognition (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first FIG Parkour World Cup events were held in 2018 at the FISE World Action Sports Festival in Montpellier, France, and the FIG Parkour World Championships have been staged since 2018 with speedrun among the medal events (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Parkour's first appearance at a major multi-sport event came at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham, USA, where both speedrun and freestyle were contested, and parkour is scheduled to debut at the Olympic Games Los Angeles 2028 with speedrun as the discipline (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The community organization Parkour UK has documented the sport's early history, from the Lisses group of the 1990s to the first competitions (Parkour UK, n.d.).
