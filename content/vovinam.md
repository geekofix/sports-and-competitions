## Basic Information

Vovinam — officially Viêt Võ Đạo, "the Vietnamese Way of Martial Arts" — is the Vietnamese martial art founded in 1938 by Master Nguyễn Lộc (1912–1960) in Hanoi: a comprehensive system of hard and soft techniques (punches, elbows, kicks, knees, jumping scissor kicks, throws, joint locks and grappling), forms (quyền), self-defence sequences and traditional weapons, organized today by the World Vovinam Federation (WVVF) with world championships and 74 member countries (World Vovinam Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Women and men train and compete side by side — women's divisions are contested at every Vovinam world championship — and Vietnam's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism declared Vovinam a "National Intangible Cultural Heritage" in November 2023 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Training follows the art's "hard and soft" principle: striking and blocking fundamentals, the trademark jumping and scissor kicks, throws and joint locks, weapon forms (staff, sword, machete) and paired self-defence sets, all practiced through the codified quyền forms used for grading (World Vovinam Federation, n.d.). Competition comprises fighting (đấu) bouts — points for controlled strikes, throws and takedowns under protective-gear rules with weight and age categories — and judged forms divisions, while belt progression runs from beginner grades through the blue belt and the yellow-and-red leadership grades (Wikipedia, n.d.). The WVVF rulebook governs legal techniques, scoring, timing, safety equipment and refereeing at world and continental championships, and men and women compete in parallel categories (World Vovinam Federation, n.d.).

## History

Nguyễn Lộc (born May 24, 1912, in Thạch Thất near Hanoi; died April 29, 1960, in Saigon) synthesized traditional Vietnamese wrestling and folk fighting arts with Chinese and Japanese systems into Vovinam in the fall of 1938, first teaching a small circle of friends; the first public demonstration followed in the fall of 1939 at the Hanoi Opera House, and the first public classes opened in spring 1940 at the École Normale (Vovinam Viet Vo Dao Federation of Western USA, n.d.). Banned at times under colonial and later regimes, the art survived and flourished in Saigon after 1954; after Nguyễn Lộc's death in 1960, his senior disciple Grandmaster Lê Sáng (1920–September 27, 2010) carried the development and international promotion until 2010, and the name Viêt Võ Đạo was coined in 1964 (Wikipedia, n.d.). After 1975 the Vietnamese diaspora spread Vovinam across Europe, the Americas, Australia and Asia; international conferences in California (1990) and Paris (1996) paved the way for the World Vovinam Federation, officially established in 2009 in Ho Chi Minh City, which now counts 74 member countries and runs the world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.).
