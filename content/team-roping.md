## Basic Information

Team roping is the rodeo event in which a pair of mounted ropers works against the clock: the header ropes a running steer around the horns and turns it, then the heeler ropes both hind legs, and the fastest legal catch under the set time limit wins. The classic two-person test of horsemanship and technique, it is the only true team event at most professional rodeos and the centerpiece of dedicated roping circuits with national standings and world champions crowned each year.

## Rules

The steer is released from the chute with a head start protected by a barrier rope; breaking the barrier adds a ten-second penalty. The header rides out and ropes the steer's horns (legal catches are around both horns, half a head, or around the neck), dallies the rope around the saddle horn and turns the steer across the arena. The heeler then rides behind the turned steer, throws a loop over both hind legs and dallies; the clock stops when the steer is secured between the two mounted ropers with all ropes taut and both horses facing each other. Catching only one hind leg adds a five-second penalty, legal catch definitions are strict, and rulebooks of the professional circuits govern barrier timing, field flagging and time limits.

## History

Team roping grew straight out of ranch work on the great cattle ranges of the American West, where two riders routinely roped and doctored a steer on the open range, one taking the head and one the heels so the animal could be treated without injury. Arena contests formalized through the early twentieth century alongside rodeo's rise, and the event matured into a professional circuit of its own: the United Team Roping Championships and similar bodies run national standings, jackpots and finals series, while team roping at the National Finals Rodeo under the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association remains one of the sport's headline attractions. Winning times that once sat in the double-digit seconds have fallen to the four-to-five-second range at the elite level, with rodeo families keeping the event at the heart of western heritage sport.
