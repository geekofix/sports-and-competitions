## Basic Information

Disc golf is a flying-disc sport in which players throw a disc from a tee area toward a target, completing each hole in the fewest number of throws, exactly like stroke play in golf but with discs and metal basket targets. It is governed internationally by the Professional Disc Golf Association (PDGA), which sanctions the PDGA World Championships, the Disc Golf Major Championship and a global tour, and by the World Flying Disc Federation (WFDF), which governs the amateur World Championships. Courses are usually set in public parks and typically have 9 or 18 holes with a par of 3 per hole.

## Rules

Each hole begins with a tee throw from a designated tee pad. The disc is then thrown from where it landed (the lie) until it comes to rest in the target, a chain-out basket defined by the PDGA Technical Standards. The hole is completed when the disc rests in the tray or chains below the chain support. The player with the lowest total number of throws over all holes wins; ties are broken by sudden-death playoffs. Out-of-bounds areas, water hazards, mandos (mandatory flight paths) and relief rules are defined by the PDGA Official Rules of Play. Different disc types (driver, mid-range, putter) are used for distance, control and putting, and players may carry as many discs as they wish. Amateur divisions (by age, gender and rating) are used at World Championships.

## History

The modern game is usually traced to "Steady" Ed Headrick, who in 1975 founded the PDGA and installed the first standardized basket target (the Pole Hole) at Oak Grove Park in California; earlier informal games with tin cans and trees date back to the 1910s and 1960s. Disc golf grew rapidly in the 1980s and 1990s as courses were added to public parks across the United States and Scandinavia. The PDGA was formally incorporated in 1976 and now counts hundreds of thousands of members in more than 100 countries. The WFDF, founded in 1985, runs the amateur World Championships, and disc golf was added to the World Games program in 2025. Major professionals such as Ken Climo (12 world titles), Paul McBeth and Paige Pierce have turned the Majors into televised events.
