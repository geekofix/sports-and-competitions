## Basic Information

Disc golf is a flying-disc sport in which players throw discs from a tee pad toward a basket target and complete each hole in the fewest number of throws; the player with the lowest total score wins (Professional Disc Golf Association [PDGA], n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed by the PDGA, founded in 1976 by "Steady" Ed Headrick, who also designed the modern chain basket ("Pole Hole") in 1975, and internationally by the World Flying Disc Federation (WFDF) (PDGA, n.d.-b; WFDF, n.d.). The 2026 PDGA Pro World Championships were held 26–30 August 2026 at Kensington Metropark in Milford, Michigan (Detroit area), with 300 players and a $300,005 purse: Silas Schultz of the USA won the MPO (men's) title and Eveliina Salonen of Finland the FPO (women's) title; the 2027 worlds go to Portland, Oregon (PDGA, n.d.-a). On the Disc Golf Pro Tour 2026, Gannon Buhr won the Tour Championship in Lynchburg and the season standings in the MPO division, with Silva Saarinen winning the FPO Tour Championship; the 2026 DGPT standings leaders were Buhr and Ohn Scoggins, and Buhr — the 2025 world champion from Nokia — is world No. 1 (PDGA, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in the women's game — world champion Eveliina Salonen and DGPT leader Ohn Scoggins, and — in the men's game — world No. 1 Gannon Buhr and 2026 world champion Silas Schultz (PDGA, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the PDGA Official Rules of Disc Golf, a course consists of 9 or 18 holes, each with a tee pad, fairway and a PDGA-approved basket target; par is set per hole, usually 3 (PDGA, n.d.-b). The disc is thrown from behind the tee pad, and each subsequent throw is made from where the previous throw landed; the hole is completed when the disc comes to rest in the basket tray or chains, and the player must then play from within the "circle of confusion" — the 10 m area around the basket for the next putt if outside it (PDGA, n.d.-b). Out-of-bounds areas — water, roads and areas marked OB on the course map — add one penalty stroke and require a re-throw or drop from the last lie (PDGA, n.d.-b). Mando (mandatory) holes force play through a designated gate (PDGA, n.d.-b). Competitions are divided into MPO/FPO (men's/women's) plus age and amateur divisions (e.g., MJ/FA juniors, MA1–MA4), all using the same rules; the PDGA maintains official world rankings (PDGA, n.d.-a). Women and men play identical courses and rules, with separate divisions in tournament play (PDGA, n.d.-b).

## History

Ed Headrick installed the first formal disc golf course with basket targets at Oak Grove Park in California in 1975 and founded the PDGA in 1976 to standardize the rules and the Pole Hole basket (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first PDGA World Championship was held in 1982, and the sport grew rapidly through the 1990s with the founding of the Disc Golf Pro Tour circuit; today more than 100,000 courses exist worldwide (PDGA, n.d.-a). Icons of the men's game include Ken Climo, a record 12-time world champion, and Paul McBeth, seven-time world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.). On the women's side, Paige Pierce is a multiple-time world champion, alongside Valerie Jenkins and Elaine King (Wikipedia, n.d.).
