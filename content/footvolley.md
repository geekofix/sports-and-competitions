## Basic Information

Footvolley (Brazilian Portuguese *futevôlei*) is a beach sport that combines the court and rotation rules of beach volleyball with the ball-control rules of football: two players per side hit a ball back and forth over a net without ever using hands or arms (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was created in 1965 on Copacabana Beach in Rio de Janeiro by Octavio de Moraes — first called *pèvolei* — when footballers, facing a ban on playing football on the sand, moved to the volleyball courts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There is no single world championship; the sport is organized by several bodies, including the Fédération Internationale de Footvolley (FIFV), the World Footvolley Federation, the Asian Footvolley Federation and the European Footvolley League (EFVL), founded in November 2003 with 16 member nations and the official European federation (EFVL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the Americas, the Pro Footvolley Tour has run events since 2008, notably on Hollywood Beach, Florida, under modified rules. Footvolley was a demonstration sport at the Rio 2016 Olympics. In 2026 the Footvolley World Tour stopped in Albufeira, Portugal, at Praia dos Pescadores on July 10–12, organized by the Portuguese federation FNFV with TAFC International and including a Women's Open, and the EFVL League of Champions 2026 featured the London 100 and Santa Luzia 500 events, with Juan Silva and Rodrigo Otero leading the standings (FNFV, n.d.; EFVL, n.d.).

At the end of 2026 the leading women's teams come from Brazil and Portugal, the countries that also dominate the 2026 World Tour and EFVL circuits, while on the men's side the Brazilian and Portuguese internationals — including EFVL leaders Juan Silva and Rodrigo Otero — set the standard (EFVL, n.d.; FNFV, n.d.).

## Rules

Footvolley is based on beach volleyball rules with football ball handling (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The court is 16 m × 8 m (8 m × 8 m per side), played on fine quartz sand at least 35 cm deep, with a net 2.20 m high for men and 2.10 m for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Teams are two per side (3v3, 4v4 and mini/1v1 variants exist), and the ball may be played with any part of the body except hands and arms — contact with an arm or hand is a fault — with a maximum of three contacts per side before the ball must cross the net, and touching the net is forbidden (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The serve is played from a sand mound no more than 1 m behind the baseline. A set is won at 18 points with a two-point lead, capped at 21 (rally-point scoring), and the match is the best of three sets (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The indoor ball is smaller, heavier (about 360 g) and bouncier than a volleyball. The Pro Footvolley Tour in the Americas uses a modified format: a lower net of 6 ft 8 in (2.03 m), a larger court of 29.5 ft × 58 ft (9 m × 17.7 m), and the "shark attack," a designated super point worth two points, with at most three per set (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate categories, with the lower net for women, and mixed events are also played (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Footvolley was created in 1965 in Rio de Janeiro by Octavio de Moraes, originally under the name *pèvolei* (from *pé*, foot, and *vôlei*, volley), when football was banned on the city's beaches but volleyball courts remained open; by the 1970s the game had spread to Recife, Salvador, Brasília, Santos and Florianópolis (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Teams originally played five a side, but as the athletes — almost all of them professional footballers — rarely let the ball drop, the numbers were reduced until 2 versus 2 became standard (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first international event outside Brazil was held in 2003 in Miami Beach by the United States Footvolley Association, which led to the formation of international federations, and footvolley was played as a demonstration sport at the 2016 Rio Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In Europe the game arrived in the 1990s, the European Footvolley League (EFVL) was founded in November 2003, and in Germany it has been organized by the Deutscher Beachsoccer-Verband and, since 2007, by Footvolley Germany (EFVL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Many of Brazil's greatest footballers — Romário, Bebeto, Zico, Ronaldo, Ronaldinho, Edmundo, Léo Júnior, Renato Gaúcho and Edinho — have played footvolley, which helped popularize the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
