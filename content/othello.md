## Basic Information

Othello (also known as Reversi) is a two-player abstract strategy board game played on an 8 by 8 board with 64 double-sided discs, black on one side and white on the other (World Othello Federation, n.d.-a). The underlying game, Reversi, was invented in England in 1883 by Lewis Waterman and John W. Mollett, while the modern tournament-standard ruleset marketed as Othello was developed in Japan in 1971 by Goro Hasegawa and launched commercially by Tsukuda in 1973 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The World Othello Federation (WOF) governs the game and runs the World Othello Championship, first held in Tokyo in 1977 and held annually since; women and men compete together in open events, with a separate Women's World Championship since 1990 (World Othello Federation, n.d.-b). Like [chess](chess.html) and [go](go.html), it is a mind sport of perfect information.

## Rules

The board starts with four discs in the center in a diagonal pattern, two black and two white (World Othello Federation, n.d.-a). Players take turns placing one disc on an empty square with their color facing up; a placement is legal only if it flanks a straight line of one or more opponent discs between the newly placed disc and another disc of the player's own color, in any of the eight directions, and all flanked discs are then flipped (World Othello Federation, n.d.-a). A player with no legal move must pass; the game ends when neither player can move or the board is full, and the player with more discs on the board wins (World Othello Federation, n.d.-a). In official WOF play, each player has 30 minutes on the clock, the starting color is drawn by lot, and games at the World Championship are played best-of-one in the round robin and single elimination in the knockout stage (World Othello Federation, n.d.-b). There are no separate rules for women and men — all competitors play under the same laws in the open events, with junior and women's titles organized as separate classifications (World Othello Federation, n.d.-b).

## History

Reversi was invented in England in 1883, one version by Lewis Waterman and another by John W. Mollett, and it spread quickly across Europe and America (Wikipedia, n.d.). The name Othello and the modern rules were established in Japan in 1971 when Goro Hasegawa, a schoolboy, had the idea of marketing the game as "Othello" — named after Shakespeare's play because of the flipping of allegiances — and the game was launched commercially in 1973 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first World Othello Championship was held in Tokyo in 1977 and has been held annually since, dominated at first by Japanese players such as Yoshio Ishiguro and later by Europeans; the WOF was founded to govern the international game (World Othello Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). Today top-level play is supported by computer analysis, and the WOF publishes the official world ranking list (World Othello Federation, n.d.-a).
