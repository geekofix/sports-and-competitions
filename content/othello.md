## Basic Information

Othello (also known as Reversi) is a two-player abstract strategy board game played on an 8 by 8 board with 64 double-sided discs, black on one side and white on the other. The modern, tournament-standard ruleset marketed as Othello was patented in Japan in 1971 by Goro Hasegawa and launched commercially in 1973; the underlying game, Reversi, was invented in England in 1883. The World Othello Federation (WOF) governs the game and runs the World Othello Championship, first held in Tokyo in 1977 and held annually since. Like [chess](chess.html) and [go](go.html), it is a mind sport of perfect information.

## Rules

The board starts with four discs in the center in a diagonal pattern, two black and two white. Players take turns placing one disc on an empty square with their color facing up; a placement is legal only if it flanks a straight line of one or more opponent discs between the newly placed disc and another disc of the player's own color, in any of the eight directions, and all flanked discs are then flipped. A player with no legal move must pass; the game ends when neither player can move or the board is full, and the player with more discs on the board wins. Tournament play under WOF rules uses the standard 8 by 8 setup, fixed time controls and the rule that a player must play if a legal move exists. In 2023, computers proved that with perfect play the game on the standard board ends in a draw.

## History

Reversi was invented in 1883, with the Englishmen Lewis Waterman and John W. Mollett each claiming the invention, and it became a popular Victorian parlor game; the German publisher Ravensburger produced it from 1893. The Japanese salesman Goro Hasegawa, born in Mito in 1932, devised the modern version around 1949 as a schoolboy, using Go stones and milk-bottle tops, fixed the opening diagonal and named it Othello after Shakespeare's play, whose black-and-white conflict mirrors the flipping discs. He patented it in 1971, founded the Japan Othello Association in March 1973, held the first national championship on 4 April 1973, and the toy company Tsukuda Original launched the game commercially in April 1973. It was launched in the United States by Gabriel Industries in 1975 and became one of the best-selling board games of all time, with more than 40 million sets sold in over 100 countries, under the slogan "A minute to learn, a lifetime to master." The first World Othello Championship took place in Tokyo in 1977.
