## Basic Information

Adventure racing is a multi-discipline endurance team sport in which teams navigate a course of 50 km to well over 500 km using a combination of disciplines — trail running, mountain biking, orienteering, paddling, climbing and rope sections — finding checkpoints with map and compass. Teams consist of four athletes and must be mixed-sex, with at least one woman on every team (Adventure Race World Series [ARWS], n.d.-b). The sport is organized internationally by the Adventure Race World Series (ARWS), whose flagship event is the Adventure Racing World Championship (ARWC). See also [Foot Orienteering](foot-orienteering.html), [Trail Orienteering](trail-orienteering.html) and [Skyrunning](skyrunning.html) on this site (Adventure Race World Series, n.d.-a) (Adventure Race World Series, n.d.-b).

Among the teams shaping the sport today are Estonia's ACE / La Sportiva — Rain Eensaar, Reeda Tuula-Fjodorov, Timmo Tammenae and Tauno Riiback, with two women on the team — who won the 2026 Adventure Racing World Championship, "Raid in France — Corsica is Magic," and the Swedish Armed Forces Adventure Team, winners of the 2025 ARWC at Expedition Canada (ARWS, n.d.-a; Raid in France 2026, n.d.). The ARWS world ranking is refreshed every four months and is based on each team's best four results over the previous two years (ARWS, n.d.-b).

 Within the orienteering and adventure family on this site, its closest relatives are [Foot Orienteering](foot-orienteering.html), [Trail Orienteering](trail-orienteering.html), [Orienteering](orienteering.html), [Ski Orienteering](ski-orienteering.html), [Mountain Bike Orienteering](mountain-bike-orienteering.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

A team of four — always mixed-sex, with at least one woman — races non-stop over a marked but unmarked-distance course, navigating from checkpoint to checkpoint with map, compass and GPS where allowed; the course combines disciplines such as trail running, mountain biking, trekking, paddling, caving and rope work, and teams may use any means of travel except motorized transport (ARWS, n.d.-b). Cut-off times apply at checkpoints, teams must carry mandatory gear and food, and all four members must cross the finish line together within a set time window. Transition areas are used to change equipment and discipline. The first team to finish wins; there are no separate gender categories because the mixed-team rule already guarantees women's participation on every team — in fact, teams with two women (as the 2026 world champions had) are common at the front (ARWS, n.d.-b; International Trail Running Association, n.d.).

## History

Adventure racing traces its origin to the Raid Gauloises, first held in 1989, which pioneered the multi-discipline team format and inspired a wave of similar events worldwide. The Adventure Race World Series was founded in the early 2000s and held the first Adventure Racing World Championship in 2001 in Switzerland, won by Team Nokia Adventure — Petri Forsman, Mikael Pinola, Markku Hirvinen and Anni Mäki-Rautila. Since then the ARWC has been staged annually on a different continent; the 2006 edition was held at Hemavan-Tärnaby in Sweden and won by the Swedish team of Ian Adamson, Andrew Merrell, Simon Ussher and Jon Wiens — pictured below (ARWS, n.d.-a).
