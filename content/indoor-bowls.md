## Basic Information

Indoor bowls is the indoor form of [lawn bowls](lawn-bowls.html), played on a narrow carpet or synthetic rink about 40 m long and less than 5 m wide, in which players deliver biased bowls toward a small white target ball called the jack, scoring one point for each bowl closer to the jack than the opponent's best (World Bowls, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The two premier annual events are the World Indoor Bowls Championship at Potters Resorts in Hopton-on-Sea, Great Yarmouth, England — the 2026 edition ran 9–25 January 2026 and was televised by the BBC — and the rival World Bowls Indoor Championships organized by World Bowls+ (merged with the International Indoor Bowls Council in 2019), held 11–16 May 2026 at Ocean Grove Bowls Club, Victoria, Australia (World Bowls, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). At the 2026 Potters event, England's Robert Paxton won the open singles title (beating Paul Foster on the third tie-break end) and Katherine Rednall of England claimed her seventh ladies singles title (beating Nicole Rogers); at the 2026 Ocean Grove event, Iain McLean of Scotland won the men's singles, Alison Merrien MBE of Guernsey the women's singles, and the Australian pair Wedlock/Cottrell the mixed pairs (World Bowls, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Leading current women players include Katherine Rednall and Alison Merrien, both multiple indoor world champions (World Bowls, n.d.-b). Together with [Crown Green Bowls](crown-green-bowls.html) it forms the bowls side of the wider boules-and-bowls family, which also includes the thrown-bole games [Pétanque](petanque.html), [Bocce](bocce.html) and [Boule Lyonnaise](boule-lyonnaise.html) — the full list is under Important Links.

## Rules

The rink is about 40 m long and under 5 m wide, laid in carpet or synthetic material with a mat at the delivery end; players deliver the biased bowl underarm from the mat, and the bowl curves toward the jack as it slows (World Bowls, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After all bowls of an end are played, the player whose bowl is closest to the jack scores one point for each of their bowls closer than the opponent's nearest bowl; a game is played to a target score (21 points in championship play), and if the scores are level at the end, tie-break ends are played until a winner emerges (World Bowls, n.d.-a). Events at the World Indoor Bowls Championship include men's and women's singles, pairs, triples and fours, plus mixed pairs; matches are often best-of-sets formats at the top events (World Bowls, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in parallel events under identical rules, and age-group and disability categories exist at national level (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Bowls has ancient roots — polished stone bowls have been found in Egyptian tombs dating to around 3000 BC — and the modern game of lawn bowls was codified in 19th-century England, with the first rules drawn up in the 1860s; indoor bowls developed in British bowls halls so the game could be played year-round, and the first World Indoor Bowls Championship was held in 1979 at Potters in Hopton-on-Sea (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the sport's icons are England's Tony Allcock and David Bryant — Bryant won the world singles title eight times — and Scotland's Alex Marshall MBE, also an eight-time world champion nicknamed the "Emperor of Bowls" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Bowls, n.d.-b).
