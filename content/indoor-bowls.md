## Basic Information

Indoor bowls — also called short-mat bowls or carpet bowls — is the indoor variant of lawn bowls. Instead of a grass rink, the game is played on a long rectangular carpet mat laid in a hall, using the same kind of biased bowls (woods) and a small white target ball called the jack. Players take turns delivering their bowl from a small mat at one end of the lane, and the bowl that stops closest to the jack scores. The sport is governed internationally by World Bowls, and its premier event is the World Indoor Bowls Championship, staged annually at the Guild Hall in Preston, England.

## Rules

In singles, two players alternate delivering one bowl each per end; in pairs and fours, each player delivers two bowls per end. The jack is placed at the far end of the mat at the start of each end, and players aim to finish their bowl nearer to the jack than any opposing bowl, using the curve (bias) of the bowl or by hitting ("driving") the jack or other bowls. Once all bowls are played, the player or team whose bowl is closest to the jack scores one point for every bowl closer to the jack than the opponent's best, and the scoring team leads off the next end. A match consists of a set number of ends, and the highest total wins. Bowls that leave the mat or the jack knocked out of play are dead and replayed.

## History

Lawn bowls has been played in England since at least the 16th century, and indoor variants developed in the 20th century so that the game could be enjoyed year-round in halls and leisure centres, especially in countries with cold winters. The World Indoor Bowls Championship was first staged in 1979 and has long been associated with the Guild Hall in Preston, where thousands of spectators watch the world's best indoor bowlers compete each January. Today short-mat and carpet bowls are played in clubs across the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Canada and many other countries, with national indoor championships feeding into the world event.
