## Basic Information

Para table tennis is the Paralympic version of [table tennis](table-tennis.html), played under the rules of the International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF) with slight modifications for athletes with impairments. Athletes are classified into eleven classes: classes 1 to 5 compete in wheelchairs, classes 6 to 10 compete standing, and class 11 is for players with an intellectual disability; within each group, a higher number means more function. The sport has been on the Paralympic program since the first Games in Rome 1960, governance moved from the IPC to the ITTF in 2007, and with more than 40 million players in over 100 countries it is the third most played Paralympic sport.

## Rules

Para table tennis follows the standard ITTF rules with a few adaptations, mainly around the serve when the receiver uses a wheelchair and the wider table legs that allow a wheelchair to pass. Matches are best of five sets, each set played to 11 points and won by two clear points. Wheelchair classes 1 to 5 play on the standard table, and standing classes 6 to 10 follow the ordinary laws of table tennis without exceptions. Classification is carried out by ITTF-trained classifiers, and competition at the Paralympics and World Para Table Tennis Championships includes singles, doubles and team events in each eligible class.

## History

Para table tennis debuted at the first Paralympic Games in Rome 1960 with 11 medal events for 35 wheelchair athletes from 10 countries, and it has been included at every Games since. Standing players were added in 1976, and athletes with cerebral palsy and intellectual impairments from 1980. The first Para Table Tennis World Championship was held in Assen, Netherlands, in 1990, and the championships have since been organized by the ITTF on a cycle with the Paralympics. China, South Korea and other Asian nations have traditionally dominated the medal tables.
