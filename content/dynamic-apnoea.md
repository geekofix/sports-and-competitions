## Basic Information

Dynamic apnoea (DYN) is the pool distance discipline of freediving: the athlete swims horizontally underwater on one breath, with fins (DYNB/DYN) or without fins (DNF), and the measured distance counts (AIDA, n.d.). It belongs to the freediving family with static apnoea and constant weight.

*Figure: jayhem from Antibes, France. (2009-05-31 07:18). Freediving competition — swimmer covering distance underwater (Mandy's record swim) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Freediving_Competition_Mandy%27s_Record_Swim_%283615916369%29.jpg*

AIDA and CMAS ratify separate world records; the men’s DYN monofin record stands at 326.5 m, swum by Poland’s Mateusz Malina under CMAS rules in Athens in May 2025 — more than six lengths of an Olympic pool (DeeperBlue, 2026). At the 2025 AIDA world championships Malina swept all three dynamic disciplines (DNF, DYNB, DYN), the first man since Natalia Molchanova to win triple gold at a pool world championship (Molchanovs, 2025).

## Rules

Rules: the dive must be in a certified pool with safety divers, the distance is measured along the surface line where the swimmer surfaces, and any blackout or loss of motor control (LMC) disqualifies the attempt (AIDA, n.d.). No pre-oxygenation is allowed (AIDA, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate events at world championships.

## History

Dynamic apnoea grew with competitive freediving from the 1990s onward; the 200 m barrier was broken in the early 2000s by pioneers such as Tom Sietas (212 m in 2004 with fins) and Natalia Molchanova (250 m women’s record in 2009–2013) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today top men swim beyond 300 m with bifins and women beyond 250 m (DeeperBlue, 2026).
