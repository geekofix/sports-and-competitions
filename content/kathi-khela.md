## Basic Information

Kathi khela (“stick game”) is a traditional two-team game of Bengal and Assam played with sticks (kathi) and a ball: the batting side tries to hit the ball and run between marks while the fielding side retrieves the ball and hits the runners or the wickets. It is one of the many regional stick-and-ball games of the subcontinent that influenced cricket, and it is played by boys and girls in villages and schools of West Bengal, Bangladesh and Assam. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [Gilli Danda](gilli-danda.html), [Ekka Dokka](ekka-dokka.html), [Atya Patya](atya-patya.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Two teams alternate batting and fielding. The batter strikes the ball with the kathi and runs between two marked points (or around bases); fielders retrieve the ball and may eliminate runners by hitting them directly or by hitting the wicket/stick target before the runner returns. Outs, catches and run-outs follow locally agreed rules; the team with the most runs after the agreed innings wins. Equipment is minimal: a wooden stick, a small ball (often a tennis or rubber ball) and drawn lines.

## History

Stick-and-ball games have been played in Bengal and Assam for centuries under many names (kathi khela, kati khel among the Santali, and cognates in Odisha); they form the folk background of modern cricket in the region, just as gilli danda did in the Punjab. Folk-game surveys of North Bengal document kati khel among the traditional games of the region, and heritage organizations promote it in schools today.
