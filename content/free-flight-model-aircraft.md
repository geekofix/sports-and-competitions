## Basic Information

Free flight model aircraft are uncontrolled models that, once launched, fly entirely on their own trim — gliders (F1A), rubber-powered models (F1B "Wakefield"), engine-powered models (F1C) and indoor models (F1D) (FAI, n.d.). They belong to the FAI aeromodelling family and represent the purest form of model aviation: no radio, no lines, just aerodynamics.

![F1A free flight glider on tow line](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Aeromodel_F1A_Towing.jpg?width=560)

*Figure: Ismail SARIOGLU. (2006). F1A free flight glider on tow line [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Aeromodel_F1A_Towing.jpg*

In competition, each flyer gets a set number of flights (typically 5–7, with a max of 3 minutes per flight in F1A/F1B/F1C, longer for F1D indoors up to 30–40 minutes); the best times count, and fly-offs with extended max times decide ties (FAI, n.d.). Models must meet class limits — F1B rubber models carry a maximum 40 g of rubber motor; F1C engines are 2.5 cc maximum with a limited fuel charge; F1D indoor models have a 55 cm span limit and weigh grams (FAI, n.d.).

## Rules

Launch techniques are part of the sport: F1A gliders are towed on a 50 m line and circle away on the tow; rubber models are wound with a winding stooge and launched with a steep climb (FAI, n.d.). The FAI ratifies duration, altitude and distance records for free flight classes, including indoor records set in huge halls (FAI, n.d.). World championships are held every two years (FAI, n.d.). Competitors range from juniors to seniors; men and women compete in the same events.

## History

Free flight modelling dates to the 1870s–1900s, when Alphonse Pénaud’s rubber-powered planophore (1871) inspired the first model aeroplane clubs; the Wakefield trophy (donated 1927/1928) made F1B rubber models the classic international class, and powered free flight and indoor classes were added in the 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FAI has run world championships for free flight since the 1950s, and the 2025 edition continued the tradition (FAI, n.d.).
