## Basic Information

Guresh (Azerbaijani: güleş, gyulesh) is Azerbaijan's national wrestling: two pekhlevans in wide trousers, a soft belt and shoes grapple standing — body, legs, belt and trousers grips, backheels and throws — and win by throwing the opponent onto both shoulder blades; some ground techniques are permitted. Bouts are accompanied by zurna and drums in the traditional zorkhana arenas of Baku, Ganja and Ordubad. Regular competitions at all levels are held across Azerbaijan, and gyulesh is a World Nomad Games discipline; the National Gulesh Federation governs the sport. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Kokh](kokh.html), [Kuresh](kuresh.html), [Wrestling](wrestling.html), [Kurash](kurash.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Advantour, n.d.).

## Rules

Standing start with grips on trousers, legs, belt or body; win by a clean throw onto both shoulder blades (some ground work allowed); throat clutches, punches and kicks below the stomach are forbidden. Historically pekhlevans wore mirrors on their knees to show they never touched the carpet with their knees. Modern bouts follow the National Gulesh Federation rules with referees, weight classes and medical checks; women's guresh is growing (Advantour, n.d.).

## History

Gyulesh is rooted in the Zurkhaneh (house of strength) tradition shared with Persian pahlevani (UNESCO intangible heritage 2010); it was a village pastime and a warrior training. The Azerbaijan Wrestling Federation (founded 1993) oversees it alongside Olympic styles; the Victory Sports Festival in Baku (2022) featured gulesh. It has been contested at the World Nomad Games since 2014 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
