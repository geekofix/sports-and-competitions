## Basic Information

Kumite is the sparring discipline of karate: two competitors face each other on a marked square mat and score points with controlled punches and kicks, refereed for safety and scored electronically. It is one of the two WKF competition disciplines, alongside kata, and was part of karate at the Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 Olympics. For the general sport, see [karate](karate.html).

## Rules

Under WKF rules, a senior kumite bout lasts three minutes (shorter for younger age classes) on a 8 m x 8 m mat. Legal techniques are punches and kicks; scoring is: yuko (1 point) for a punch to the midsection, waza-ari (2 points) for a kick to the midsection, and ippon (3 points) for a kick to the head, a throw or a sweep that downs the opponent. The match ends when one competitor leads by eight points, or the higher score at the buzzer wins; if tied, a judges' decision (hantei) or extra time decides. Four corner judges and electronic sensors (since 2013) register valid scores. Excessive contact, evasion or leaving the mat is penalized in categories up to hansoku-make, a direct disqualification. Competitors fight in weight classes (e.g. men: -60, -67, -75, -84, +84 kg) wearing a red or blue belt and protective gear (gloves, gum shield, and in many events a chest protector and shin guards for juniors).

## History

Kumite developed from Okinawan free-sparring practice (bunkai applications and dojo challenges) and was formalized with protective gear and rules in 20th-century Japan, notably in styles such as Shotokan and Goju-ryu. Electronic scoring was introduced at the WKF World Championships in the 2010s, making decisions more objective. Kumite has been contested at every WKF World Championship and at the Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 Olympic Games, where Steven Da Costa of France won gold in the men's -67 kg category.
