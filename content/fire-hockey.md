## Basic Information

Fire hockey can refer to two distinct games. The older and more serious one is pasárutakua (pelota purépecha), the "fire ball" game of the Purépecha people of Michoacán, Mexico, played at night with a ball of burning cloth or resin-soaked material, considered one of the oldest continuously played ball games in the Americas. The term also denotes an English pub/dinner-party game in which players hit a flaming ball with hockey sticks.

## Rules

In the Purépecha fire ball game, two teams play on a field with goals at each end, using curved wooden sticks (uárhaperi) to hit a ball traditionally wrapped in cloth and set on fire (recently often replaced by a less dangerous ball at festivals for safety), scoring by getting the ball into the opponent's goal; matches are played at night so the fireball is visible, and the game is accompanied by music and ceremony. In the English fire hockey party game, a tennis ball or similar is soaked in a flammable liquid and lit, and players in two teams use hockey sticks to hit it toward goals or simply keep it in play, with the game ending when the flame goes out; safety rules (played outdoors, water nearby) are essential.

## History

The Purépecha fire ball game is documented as being played for at least several centuries, with some accounts tracing it back around 3,500 years, and it remains part of festivals in towns such as Quiroga and Pátzcuaro, Michoacán, often played during Holy Week and patron saint festivals; it was featured in the documentary series Trans World Sport. The English fire hockey party game is a 20th-century novelty, popular at themed dinners and student events in Britain, with no formal governing body. Both versions are played for spectacle as much as competition.
