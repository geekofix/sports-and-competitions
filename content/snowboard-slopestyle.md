## Basic Information

Snowboard slopestyle is the judged discipline in which riders descend a course of rails, boxes and jumps, scoring on the tricks performed on each feature; it is governed by the FIS and has been an Olympic event since Sochi 2014 (FIS, n.d.-a). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, Su Yiming of China won the men's gold with 82.41 — China's first Olympic snowboarding gold, on his 22nd birthday — while Mari Fukada of Japan won the women's gold with 87.83 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the current leading women are 2026 Olympic champion Mari Fukada and Zoi Sadowski-Synnott of New Zealand, the 2022 Olympic champion; among the men, Su Yiming and 2025 world champion Liam Brearley of Great Britain (FIS, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

A slopestyle course consists of a sequence of jib features — rails, boxes and ledges — and two or three kickers, set on a steep slope; at the Olympics riders take three runs in the final with the best two counting (FIS, n.d.-a). Judges score each run out of 100 for overall impression, with sections for the rail-and-box section and each jump; scoring covers difficulty, execution, amplitude, variety and the landing of tricks such as spins, flips, grabs, presses and rail tricks (FIS, n.d.-b). Qualification runs advance the top riders to the final, and World Cup slopestyle events follow the same runs-and-judges format (FIS, n.d.-a). Women's and men's events use the same course and identical judging with separate classifications; juniors compete at the FIS Junior World Championships (FIS, n.d.-b).

## History

Slopestyle developed in the late 1990s and early 2000s as terrain parks with rails and jumps spread through resorts, and dedicated slopestyle contests became a fixture of the US Open and X Games; the FIS put slopestyle on the World Cup programme and added it to the Olympic programme at Sochi 2014, where Sage Kotsenburg and Jamie Anderson of the United States won the first gold medals (Wikipedia, n.d.). Retired icons include Kotsenburg and Anderson — Anderson, a four-time X Games champion and Olympic champion in 2014 and 2018, retired after the 2022 season (Wikipedia, n.d.).
