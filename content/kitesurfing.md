## Basic Information

Kitesurfing (kiteboarding) is a water sport in which the rider stands on a board and is propelled across the water by a large controllable kite harnessed to the body (International Kiteboarding Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Modern kitesurfing emerged in the late 1990s when the French Legaignoux brothers developed the bridled, inflatable, water-relaunchable kite (1997–98), building on Peter Lynn's kite-traction experiments of the 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). All kiteboarding classes are managed by the International Kiteboarding Association (IKA) under World Sailing, with five disciplines carrying world rankings and world championships: course racing (Formula Kite), freestyle, big air, wave riding and speed (International Kiteboarding Association, n.d.). Kiteboarding debuted at the Olympic Games at Rio 2016 as course racing and was contested as Formula Kite at Paris 2024; it is confirmed for Los Angeles 2028 (World Sailing, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the top of the women's sport stand Gisela Pulido of Spain — a ten-time world champion who won her first freestyle title at age 10 in 2005 and competed at Paris 2024 — and the Formula Kite stars Lauriane Nolot of France and Olympic champion Ellie Aldridge of Great Britain (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Kiteboarding competition is governed by the IKA class rules within the World Sailing racing rules (International Kiteboarding Association, n.d.; World Sailing, n.d.). In freestyle, riders are judged on the difficulty, execution and variety of tricks; in big air, on the height of jumps measured by officials; in wave riding, on wave selection and maneuvers scored like surfing; course racing (Formula Kite) is a fleet race around a windward-leeward course with low-points scoring; and speed is measured as the average over a fixed 500 m course (International Kiteboarding Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Safety rules require a quick-release harness, helmet and impact vest in competition, and right-of-way rules on the water follow adapted sailing rules — the rider with the kite overtaking gives way, and the rider on the starboard tack has right of way (World Sailing, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate rankings and events in every discipline under identical rules (International Kiteboarding Association, n.d.). Rule source: World Sailing / IKA, https://kiteclasses.org/ (International Kiteboarding Association, n.d.).

## History

Kite traction on water began with Peter Lynn's experiments in New Zealand in the 1990s, but kitesurfing as a sport took off when Dominique and Bruno Legaignoux of France patented the inflatable, water-relaunchable bridled kite in 1997–98 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first professional tours — the PKRA (from 2001) for freestyle, course racing and wave, and the KSP for wave — structured the sport in the early 2000s, and speed sailing records followed: Sebastien Cattelan broke 50 knots in 2008 and Rob Douglas set the outright wind-powered water speed record of 55.65 knots in 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Kiteboarding was included in the Olympic programme for Rio 2016 as course racing and replaced by the hydrofoil Formula Kite class at Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the sport include Gisela Pulido of Spain, ten-time world champion and the dominant female freestyle rider of the 2000s, Robby Naish, the windsurfing legend who became a kitesurfing champion, and the speed record holders Cattelan and Douglas (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
