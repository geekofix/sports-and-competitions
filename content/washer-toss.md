## Basic Information

Washer toss (also known as washers or washer pitching) is a lawn game in which players toss metal or plastic washers toward a target — typically a filled can, box or recessed cup embedded in the ground, or a vertical stake — scoring points for landing washers inside the target. It belongs to the same family of throwing games as horseshoes and cornhole and requires only simple, inexpensive equipment.

Games are commonly played casually at gatherings as well as in organised leagues and tournaments, particularly in the United States, where it is a staple of backyards, campsites and tailgates.

## Rules

Two teams (singles or doubles) throw washers at a cup, can or hole roughly 20 to 25 feet away, with players positioned beside one target throwing toward the other. Each round, players alternate throws of a set number of washers; a washer landing inside the target scores points (most commonly five for a ringer, with secondary scoring for nearest washers or leaners depending on local rules). Points cancel out when both teams score in the same round, and the first team to reach an agreed total such as 11 or 21 points wins. Exact distances, washer sizes and scoring conventions vary by region, so many tournaments publish their own rule sheets.

## History

Washer pitching grew out of informal rural American recreation, and oral tradition traces it back to players who lacked horseshoes or horseshoe stakes and instead threw hardware washers at cups or cans sunk into the ground. Over the twentieth century the game spread through the American South and Midwest, developing regional variants with names like Texas washers. In recent decades it has gained standardised kits, league play and championship tournaments run by organised bodies.
