## Basic Information

Areca Nut Tree Climbing is a traditional competition from South and Southeast Asia in which climbers race up tall, slender areca (betel nut) palms — over 20 m high and only 10 to 16 cm wide in trunk — to harvest the nut clusters or simply to reach the top first. It is both a harvest skill and a sport, with competitions in the areca-growing regions of India (Karnataka, Kerala) and beyond.

## Rules

In competitions, climbers ascend a marked palm as fast as possible, traditionally barefoot and using a rope loop or harness for safety, then descend; the fastest time wins. Some events require the climber to cut or harvest a bunch at the top. Safety rules (harness, helmet) vary by organizer; falls are the main risk on the thin, swaying trunks.

## History

Climbing areca palms has been a specialized skill of plantation workers in coastal Karnataka and Kerala for centuries; with a shortage of skilled climbers, universities and cooperatives now run training programs, and climbing contests are held at agricultural fairs. Mechanized "tree climbers" are a modern innovation changing the trade.
