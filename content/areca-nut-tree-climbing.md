## Basic Information

Areca nut tree climbing is a traditional competitive sport of coastal South India — especially Karnataka and Kerala — in which contestants race to climb a tall areca palm (*Areca catechu*), often more than 20 m high with a trunk only 10–16 cm in diameter; the fastest climber to the top wins, and in some events a bunch of nuts must be cut down at the summit. The same tradition exists in Indonesia as *panjat pinang* — greased-pole climbing at Independence Day celebrations on August 17 — and in the Philippines as *palo-sebo*. India is the world's largest areca nut producer — about 54% of world output according to FAO statistics for 2017 and around 60% in 2023 — with Karnataka alone producing about 63% of India's crop (2013–14) (Food and Agriculture Organization [FAO], n.d.). Modern competitive tree climbing is organized by the International Society of Arboriculture (ISA) through its International Tree Climbing Championship (ITCC) (International Society of Arboriculture [ISA], n.d.). The sport is traditionally male-dominated, but women's competitions are increasingly held in Kerala and Karnataka.

 Within the climbing and mountaineering family on this site, its closest relatives are [Rock Climbing](rock-climbing.html), [Sport Climbing](sport-climbing.html), [Lead Climbing](lead-climbing.html), [Speed Climbing](speed-climbing.html), [Bouldering](bouldering.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

In a typical race, several climbers start at the base of a palm — bare or greased — and the first to reach the top or touch a marker wins; timed heats and finals are common, and in harvest-style events the climber must cut a bunch of areca nuts at the summit before descending. Falling or losing grip ends the attempt; harnesses and helmets are mandatory at organized events and increasingly at traditional festivals, where safety rules vary by organizer. In the modern ITCC, climbers ascend a fixed rope on a measured tree wearing a harness, and events include the footlock climb, the speed climb and the throwline (throwbag) event, judged on time and technique (ISA, n.d.).

## History

Climbing areca palms is part of the harvest tradition of South and Southeast Asia — the areca nut has been chewed with betel leaf (*taamboola*) since pre-Vedic times and is central to Hindu rites. Competitive climbing at festivals in Karnataka and Kerala dates back generations, Indonesia's *panjat pinang* is held every August 17, and modern arborist competition is organized by the ISA through the ITCC. A notable modern innovation is the "areca bike"/tree scooter invented by Karnataka farmer Ganapathi Bhat, which climbs a palm in about 30 seconds (ISA, n.d.; FAO, n.d.).
