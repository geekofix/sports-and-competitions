## Basic Information

The parallel bars are the men's artistic gymnastics apparatus of swinging strength: two wooden rails 3.50 m long, set about 2 m above the floor and adjustable in width, on which the gymnast swings in handstand, performs release moves and finishes with a dismount (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is the fifth men's apparatus; at Paris 2024 the title went to Zou Jingyuan of China, ahead of Illia Kovtun of Ukraine and Shinnosuke Oka of Japan (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.).

Among the leading parallel bars workers of the 2026 season: Zou Jingyuan (pictured), the 2024 Olympic champion and multiple world champion, Carlos Yulo of the Philippines, who also won parallel bars medals at the 2025 World Championships in Jakarta, and the Japanese world team champions. This is a men's apparatus — the women's counterpart is the uneven bars (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

## Rules

The parallel bars rails are 3.50 m long and set about 2 m high, with the width adjustable between about 1.0 and 1.7 m; landing mats with an additional 10 cm soft mat are mandatory (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). The routine has no time limit and must include swings, at least one flight element and a dismount; eight elements count toward the D-score with connection bonuses, and the E-panel starts at 10.0 (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a).

A fall costs 0.5 plus deductions and the gymnast may continue within 30 s; the top eight from qualification advance to the final, where the highest D + E wins (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). Men compete on the parallel bars; women's counterpart is the uneven bars; junior categories use lower bars (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

## History

The parallel bars descend from the 19th-century German *Barren* and have been an Olympic apparatus since 1896, when the German team, pictured, won the first Olympic parallel bars competition in Athens (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Japan's Sawao Kato won parallel bars gold at Mexico City 1968 and Munich 1972 as part of his eight Olympic golds, the USSR's Vladimir Artemov and later Vitaly Scherbo dominated the 1980s, and the apparatus is known today for spectacular release moves such as the “Belyavsky” (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
