## Basic Information

Gillidanda — known regionally as *gilli-danda*, *tipcat*, *tip-cat*, *dandi-biyo* (Nepali), *viti-dandu* (Marathi), *çelik çomak* (Turkish) and *lippa* (Italian) among many other names — is a street game of South Asia in which a batter flicks a small tapered wooden peg (*gilli*) into the air with a longer stick (*danda*) and then strikes it as far as possible (Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game has been played in the Indian subcontinent for more than 2,500 years — references appear from the Mauryan era — and it is widely considered one of the ancestors of cricket and baseball, since a pitched ball is struck with a bat and fielders try to catch or return it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Typical equipment is a *danda* (long stick) of about 45–90 cm and a *gilli* (short peg) of 5–15 cm, both of wood, played on streets, courtyards and beaches (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Organized play today is coordinated by the Traditional Games Federation of India, the Pakistan Gilli Danda Federation and an India-based international federation whose members include Pakistan, Bangladesh, Iran, Brazil, South Africa and Italy (Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

At the end of 2026 the strongest organized scenes are in Pakistan and India, where national gilli-danda championships are held — the Pakistan Gilli Danda Federation runs regular national events — with growing scenes in Bangladesh, the UK Bangladeshi community and Brazil (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a). Men and women both play, and women's and junior categories are contested at national festivals (Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The batter balances the *gilli* on a small hole or stone at an angle, flicks it into the air with the *danda*, and then strikes it as far as possible; the distance is measured in *danda* lengths from the batting point and counts as the batter's score for that turn (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a). If a fielder catches the *gilli* in the air before it touches the ground, the batter is out; otherwise the fielding side retrieves the *gilli* and may throw it back at the *danda* (or at the batter's circle) — if the thrown *gilli* strikes the *danda*, the batter is out (a run-out), and the batter then runs to a pre-agreed base or point before the *gilli* is returned (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A batter who misses the *gilli* entirely three times is out (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Teams alternate innings, and the team with the most runs (distance points plus completed runs) wins; casual games are usually played to a fixed number of overs or until all batters are out (Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a). The rules are the same for men, women and juniors, with lighter sticks and shorter distances for children (Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a).

## History

Gillidanda is among the oldest known bat-and-ball games: it is depicted in Indian art and mentioned in texts from the Mauryan period (c. 322–185 BCE) and later in Sanskrit literature, and it spread through South Asia under many regional names — *gilli-danda* in Hindi/Urdu, *tipcat* in English, *dandi-biyo* in Nepal, *viti-dandu* in Maharashtra, *kitti* in Tamil country and *çelik çomak* in Turkey, where it arrived via trade routes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scholars of baseball history point to gillidanda (and similar games such as the English *stoolball*) as direct ancestors of cricket and baseball, because the core mechanic — a pitched or tossed object struck by a bat, with fielders attempting catches and run-outs — is identical (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the 20th century the game was played by children across India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, and from the 1990s it was organized into federations — the Pakistan Gilli Danda Federation, the Traditional Games Federation of India and an international body — which now run national and international championships, including in Brazil, South Africa and Italy (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Traditional Games Federation of India, n.d.-a).
