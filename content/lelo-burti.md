## Basic Information

Lelo Burti is Georgia's traditional mass ball game: two village halves each field a large team and struggle in a ruck-like scrummage to carry a heavy leather ball to the opposing side's goal, historically the far bank of the village stream. The full village form remains a festive Easter ritual in western Georgian villages, and the sport is often called Georgian folk rugby.

The game lives on in Georgian rugby culture too: the national team's nickname, the Lelos, honours the traditional game.

## Rules

The organised festival form is played on a marked field a few hundred metres long between teams of about fifteen to twenty a side, drawn from the halves of the village. The stuffed leather ball, traditionally sewn heavy and tough, is carried and passed through a dense pack; the game is won by grounding the ball over the opposing goal line, historically the stream bank. Festival tradition prizes the communal scrum more than individual dashes, and village elders bar punches and dirty play while the match runs until one side scores.

## History

Lelo burti is documented in Georgian tradition for centuries, tied in oral history to spring and Easter festivals between the youth of opposing village halves, with a win read as a good omen for the harvest. The best-known organised matches continue to be played at Easter in villages of western Georgia. The modern link to rugby came when Georgia took up the sport in the twentieth century and its national team adopted the Lelos nickname, crediting the folk game as the ancestor spirit of Georgian rugby.
