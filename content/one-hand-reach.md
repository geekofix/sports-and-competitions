## Basic Information

One-hand reach is an Arctic hanging strength event: the athlete hangs from a bar or frame with one fully extended arm and holds for as long as possible; longest time wins (AWG, n.d.). Contested at the Arctic Winter Games in men's and women's divisions. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Gripping a bar or rope loop with one hand, the arm must stay fully extended; the time stops when the athlete lets go or bends the arm beyond the allowed angle. Best of three attempts (AWG, n.d.).

## History

The one-hand reach tests grip and shoulder endurance, derived from climbing and hauling tasks; it has been an AWG Arctic sport since 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
