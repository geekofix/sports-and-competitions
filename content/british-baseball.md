## Basic Information

British baseball is a bat-and-ball team sport descended from rounders, played mainly in England and Wales with two teams of nine. It is governed by the British Baseball Federation (BBF), which fields Great Britain national teams at WBSC events such as the Baseball World Cup and the European Baseball Championship. Unlike American baseball, it uses a flat bat and a round ball is bowled underarm toward the batter.

## Rules

Under BBF rules, teams of nine alternate between batting and fielding for a set number of innings (typically five in senior games, fewer in junior play). The "bowler" delivers the ball underarm; the batter tries to hit it and run around four posts (bases). A batter is out if the hit is caught on the full; a runner is out if hit by the ball while not on a post, or if the ball reaches the base before the runner; three outs end the innings. There is no stealing in the classic form, and games are decided by the most runs after the final innings. GB teams play under WBSC rules internationally, with local adaptations.

## History

British baseball evolved from rounders in the 19th century and was codified in the early 20th century, peaking in the 1930s-50s when thousands of teams played in England and Wales, especially in Liverpool, Manchester and South Wales. The British Baseball Federation was founded in 1948; Great Britain has competed at European Championships since the 1950s and at the Baseball World Cup. The sport declined after the 1960s but survives in clubs and schools, with a revival in recent years.
