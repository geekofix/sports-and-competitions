## Basic Information

Dash-guti (“the game of ten”, dash = ten in Hindi) is an Indian war game of the Central Provinces, United Provinces and Bengal (in the Karwi subdivision it is called Kowwu Dunki), played on a board of 21 points — two triangle grids joined at a common vertex with two short “antenna” line segments. Each player commands ten pieces, all placed on the board at the start except the central point, and pieces are captured by leaping over them, as in draughts and alquerque. It belongs to the family of Indian war-games that includes lau kata kati, egara-guti, pretwa and gol-skuish. Within the mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Morabaraba](morabaraba.html), [Seega](seega.html), [Latrones](latrones.html), [Draughts](draughts.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

All pieces of both sides are laid out on every point except the center, so the first move must be onto the central point — and is immediately captured by the second player's piece. Players alternate moving one piece one space along a line to a vacant adjacent point. Captures are compulsory and made by the short leap over an adjacent enemy piece onto a vacant point directly behind it, along the lines of the board; a capturing piece must continue capturing while it can. A player wins by capturing all enemy pieces, by reducing them to five, or by stalemate (leaving the opponent unable to move); if neither side can capture, the player with more pieces wins, equal numbers being a draw.

## History

H. J. R. Murray described dash-guti in his A History of Board-Games Other Than Chess (1952), the standard reference for Indian war-games, noting its kinship with lau kata kati (Lower Bengal) and with the Mozambican game butterfly, which suggests an old Indian Ocean connection. The war-games share their all-points-filled opening with pretwa (Bihar) and gol-skuish, whose larger boards have 19 and 43 points respectively.
