## Basic Information

Snowboard cross (boardercross, SBX) is the racing discipline of snowboarding in which groups of riders \u2014 four or six at a time \u2014 race head-to-head down a course of jumps, berms, rollers, whoops and banked turns, the first across the line advancing. Invented by the Austrian riders behind the original "boardercross" events of the 1990s, it joined the Olympic programme at Turin 2006 and is today contested at the FIS World Cup, world championships and Games in individual and \u2014 since Beijing 2022 \u2014 mixed team formats.

## Rules

Riders first seed the field through timed qualification runs, after which heats are raced side by side: typically four riders (six in some formats) per heat, the top two (or three) advancing round by round to the final. The course features steep banked turns, gap jumps, rollers and flat sections demanding speed, tactics and contact \u2014 deliberate interference is forbidden, but the shoulder-to-shoulder jostling of close racing is intrinsic, and crashes common; the first rider's nose across the finish line wins the heat, with heats decided on finishing order rather than time. FIS rules mandate full protective equipment (helmets, back protectors are standard) and set course specs (vertical drop ~200 m, features banked at prescribed angles); the Olympic programme since Beijing 2022 also includes a mixed team snowboard cross event, in which a man and a woman from each nation race tag-team heats.

## History

Snowboard cross descends from the grass-roots racing formats of the 1990s \u2014 the boardercross events run by riders and resort organizers such as the Kanmass and the original Xtreme games of the era \u2014 in which mass-start racing on a motocross-style course brought elbow-to-elbow drama to snowboarding. The FIS adopted it into its snowboard world cup and championship programme early in the 2000s, staging world championships from 2005 (a year after it had raged through the World Cup circuit), and the Olympic debut came at Turin 2006, where Seth Wescott of the United States won the first men's gold and Tanja Frieden of Switzerland the women's. Its Olympic roll of honour defines the discipline — Wescott defended his title in 2010, Canada's Maëlle Ricker won the women's crown in 2010, France's Pierre Vaultier took the men's golds of 2014 and 2018, and Lindsey Jacobellis of the United States, a five-times Olympian, finally won individual gold at Beijing 2022 and then took the inaugural mixed team title with Nick Baumgartel — while the mixed team event debuted at Beijing 2022 and para snowboard cross (banked slalom formats) has grown in parallel through World Para Snow Sports. Today SBX remains crowd-television friendly at its purest: the sport's blend of speed, air time and contact keeps it a Winter X Games and Olympic staple.
