## Basic Information

Freestyle slalom skating is skating through a line of cones (typically 20 cones at 50/80/120 cm spacing) on inline skates, in two formats: a timed run, where knocked cones add time penalties that scale with the cone spacing, and a judged freestyle run of about two minutes where tricks, turns and style score points; the World Slalom Skating Championships crown champions in men's and women's classes (World Skate/IFSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Italy's Alice Ricossa is one of the most decorated slalom skaters in history.

## Rules

Slalom rules (World Skate/IFSA): timed slalom on lines of 20 cones at 50, 80 and 120 cm spacing with per-cone penalties; freestyle slalom: two-minute runs judged on difficulty and style with deductions for falls; separate men's and women's, junior and senior classes; knocked cones are reset by the skater while the clock runs (World Skate, n.d.).

## History

Slalom skating formalized in the 1990s in Europe (France, Italy, Germany) from street skating games; the first world championships ran in the 1990s–2000s under the International Freestyle Skaters Association (IFSA) and later World Skate; Alice Ricossa (ITA) is a multiple world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.).
