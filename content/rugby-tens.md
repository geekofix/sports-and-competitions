## Basic Information

Rugby tens (also called ten-a-side or Xs) is a variant of rugby union in which teams of ten players — typically five forwards and five backs — play shortened matches of two ten-minute halves. Uniquely among the major rugby union variants, it was developed in Malaysia, by the Combined Old Boys Rugby Association (COBRA) in 1967, with the aim of enabling smaller Asian players to compete with larger opponents while keeping much of the character of the fifteen-a-side game. Its law variations are published by World Rugby alongside those of sevens.

## Rules

Tens is played on a standard rugby union pitch under World Rugby's ten-a-side law variations. Matches consist of two halves of ten minutes (tournament organisers may adjust this; finals are often longer — the COBRA 10s final lasts 30 minutes), and scrums are formed from five players rather than the eight of fifteens or three of sevens, with no loose forwards. Common tournament rules require drop-kick conversions taken within 40 seconds of a try, bar penalty goals and drop goals in many competitions, shorten yellow-card suspensions to two minutes, and settle draws with five-minute sudden-death periods followed by a kicking competition. Squads typically allow up to five substitutions, and the scoring values mirror the fifteen-a-side game, with tries worth five points — keeping more of union's set-piece and positional play than sevens does.

## History

Tens was created in 1967 by COBRA in Malaysia, whose members — many of them local players of smaller stature facing touring sides from traditional rugby nations — conceived a faster, more open format on the "barstools of their favourite after-match haunt", and staged the first COBRA 10s that same year at the Astaka field in Petaling Jaya; it is now held each November at Petaling Jaya's MBPJ Stadium, is the sport's oldest and most prestigious tournament, and has grown from a local competition into an international event at which more than 40 nations have been represented. The format spread through Asian rugby — the Hong Kong Football Club Tens (since 1986), the Manila 10s (since 1989), the Stockholm 10s and Flanders Open (both since 1993) — and found a second heartland in South Africa, where the Cape Town Tens has run since 2009 and where the game has taken firm popular root. Professional era events included the World Club 10s (2014–2018, won by clubs such as the Blue Bulls) and the Brisbane Global Rugby Tens (2017–2018), and since 2021 the Rugby Tens Championship has franchised men's and women's tens teams internationally, promoting equal pay structures — the newest chapter in a format that retains a worldwide festival calendar.
