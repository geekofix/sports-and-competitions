## Basic Information

Rugby tens — also called ten-a-side or "10s" — is a variant of rugby union with ten players per side, typically five forwards and five backs, playing two ten-minute halves; it is governed by World Rugby law variations for 10s and, unlike sevens, was developed in Malaysia, a country that has never qualified for the Rugby World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The world's premier tens event is the COBRA Rugby Tens, organized annually since 1967 by the Combined Old Boys Rugby Association (COBRA) of Petaling Jaya at the MBPJ Stadium, where 16 invited teams contest the Hui Weng Chun Challenge Trophy over two days in November (COBRA Rugby Tens, n.d.). The format is popular across Asia, and also strong in South Africa, where the Cape Town Tens has been held since 2009 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's tens tournaments are both played, with women's tens growing especially in Asia (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

A tens match lasts 20 minutes in two halves of ten minutes, though the final at COBRA 10s lasts 30 minutes; the scrum has five players instead of eight as in rugby XV (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; COBRA Rugby Tens, n.d.). Otherwise the World Rugby laws of rugby union apply with 10s variations, published alongside the sevens and under-19 variations on the World Rugby site (World Rugby, n.d.). Scoring is standard union: try 5, conversion 2, penalty 3, drop goal 3 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).


## History

Rugby tens was introduced in 1967 by COBRA of Malaysia — founded in 1967 at Petaling Jaya — primarily so that Asian players could compete better against the larger players of traditional rugby nations; COBRA organized the first ten-a-side tournament, the COBRA 10s, the same year, and the event went international in 1992 with more than 40 nations represented to date (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; COBRA Rugby Club of Malaysia, n.d.). COBRA, which won the Malaysian National Sports Leadership Award in 1996 and an IOC Diploma in 2002 for its contribution to sport, conceived the tens game "on the barstools of their favourite after-match haunt" as a fast, open format retaining the character of the 15s game (COBRA Rugby Tens, n.d.). Other major tournaments include the Hong Kong Football Club Tens (since 1986), Manila 10s (since 1989), Flanders Open Rugby in Belgium (since 1993), Stockholm 10s (since 1993), Cape Town Tens (since 2009), the World Club 10s (2014–2018) and the Brisbane Global Rugby Tens (2017–2018), won by the Chiefs over the Crusaders 12–5 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
