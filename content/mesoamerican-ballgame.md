## Basic Information

The Mesoamerican ballgame, known in English as pok-ta-pok, in Nahuatl as ollamaliztli and in Mayan as pitz, is an ancient ballgame of pre-Columbian Mesoamerica played since at least 1650 BCE. Teams struck a solid natural-rubber ball weighing up to about 4 kg, most commonly with the hips, on long stone ballcourts with slanted side walls. A modern descendant called ulama is still played by communities in Mexico (especially Sinaloa) and in Belize and Guatemala, and FIFA has formally recognized the Mesoamerican ballgame as the oldest historical precursor of modern football.

## Rules

The exact rules of the ancient game are not known, but judging from its descendant they resembled a game in which the aim is to keep the ball in play, with points scored when the ball crossed an end line or left the court. Some versions allowed forearms, rackets or bats, but the classic form used only the hips, protected by leather hip pads. Modern ulama exists in three main forms: ulama de cadera (hip ball, teams of five or more with a 3-4 kg rubber ball), ulama de antebrazo (forearm ball, one to three players with a lighter ball and wrapped forearms) and ulama de mazo (paddle ball, with a heavy two-handed wooden paddle striking a light ball). A team scores a point when an opponent hits the ball out of turn, misses it, touches it with an illegal body part or lets it cross their own end line.

## History

Rubber balls dating to at least 1600 BCE have been found at El Manati in Veracruz, ballgame figurines to at least 1200 BCE, and archaeologists have uncovered nearly 1,500 ancient ballcourts from Chiapas to present-day Arizona, the oldest being Paso de la Amada in Mexico (around 1600 BCE). The game had deep ritual significance in Olmec, Maya and Aztec culture and was in some interpretations linked to cosmological battles, occasionally with human sacrifice. After the Spanish conquest the game was suppressed but survived in a few communities in Sinaloa, where Spanish influence was weaker. The revival gained momentum after an exhibition game at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, and the Pok-Ta-Pok World Cup has been held since 2015, starting at Chichen Itza, organized by the Central American and Caribbean Ancestral Mayan Ballgame Association (ACCDAPM); in Mexico and the United States the game is governed by AJUPEME, and in Belize by the Belize Hipball Association.
