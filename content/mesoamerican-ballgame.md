## Basic Information

The Mesoamerican ballgame — Classical Nahuatl ōllamaliztli, Mayan pitz, commonly called pok-ta-pok — is the ancient ritual ballgame of Mesoamerica, played since at least 1650 BCE and recognized by FIFA president Gianni Infantino as the oldest historical precursor of modern football (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Players struck a solid natural-rubber ball weighing up to 4.1 kg with their hips (some versions allowed forearms, rackets or handstones) on long, narrow stone ballcourts with slanted side walls; nearly 1,500 ancient ballcourts have been found from Mexico to Nicaragua and as far north as Arizona (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The modern descendant ulama is still played in a few communities in Sinaloa, Mexico — one of the oldest continuously played sports in the world and the oldest known game using a rubber ball — and since 2015 an international Pok-Ta-Pok World Cup has been organized by the Central American and Caribbean Ancestral Mayan Ballgame Association (ACCDAPM), with the sixth edition held in Honduras in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Associated Press, n.d.). Women play the forearm variant of ulama (ulama de antebrazo) in many communities, alongside the men's hip-ball game (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

The original rules are not fully known, but the surviving descendant ulama shows the pattern: two teams keep the ball in play and score points (rayas) when opponents hit the ball out of bounds, miss it, touch it with anything but the hip, or fail to cross the center line (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Modern ulama is played on a temporary chalked court called a tastei, about 50 m long and 3.5 m wide, divided by a center line (analco); a ball crossing the end line (chichi or chivo) scores for the opposing team (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Three main forms exist: ulama de cadera (hip ball) with teams of five to twelve in loincloths and leather hip pads against a 3–4 kg rubber ball; ulama de antebrazo (forearm ball) on a smaller field with one to three players and a lighter ball, wrapped forearms only — this is the form women often play; and ulama de mazo, in which a 6–7 kg two-handed wooden paddle strikes a 500 g ball, usually in teams of three or four (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In hip ulama, touching the ball with leg, thigh, knee, foot, hand, head or above the iliac crest is an illegal hit and costs the team a point (Journal of Sport for Development, n.d.). Ulama de cadera is governed in Mexico and the US by AJUPEME (Asociación de Juego de Pelota Mesoamericano, founded by Armando Uscanga and Reyna Puc), and in Belize by the Belize Hipball Association (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The ballgame was played across Mesoamerica from at least the second millennium BCE: archaeologists have found rubber balls dating to about 1600 BCE, ballplayer figurines from about 1200 BCE and nearly 1,500 stone ballcourts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Aztecs called it ōllamaliztli and the Maya pitz; the modern term pok-ta-pok was coined in a 1932 article by Danish archaeologist Frans Blom from the Yucatec Maya pokolpok (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game carried deep ritual meaning, and in some late-period cultures major matches were associated with human sacrifice — depicted on the reliefs of the Great Ballcourt at Chichen Itza — though casual play by children was also common (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A famous 1528 drawing by Christoph Weiditz shows Aztec players performing before Emperor Charles V in Spain (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). After the Spanish conquest the game was suppressed but survived in Sinaloa, where Spanish influence was weaker; an exhibition match at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics sparked the modern revival, which spread in the 1990s when resort staff in the Mexican Caribbean recruited Sinaloan families to demonstrate the game in the Riviera Maya (Associated Press, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Since 2015 the Pok-Ta-Pok World Cup has been held in Chichen Itza (2015), Guatemala City (2017), Orange Walk (2018, 2021, 2023), San Salvador (2019), Xcaret (2022) and Honduras (2025), and the Xipe Totec 2026 tournament took place in Mexico City on 18 April 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
