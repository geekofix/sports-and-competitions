## Basic Information

Hex is a connection game on an 11x11 rhombus of hexagonal cells: two players (red/blue) alternately place stones and try to connect their two opposite sides; a chain cannot be blocked. It was independently invented by Piet Hein (1942, Denmark) and John Nash (1948, USA). Nash proved with a strategy-stealing argument that the first player can force a win; a draw is impossible. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Havannah](havannah.html), [Go](go.html), [Connect6](connect6.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Hex Wiki / International Hex Federation, n.d.).

## Rules

Each player owns two opposite sides; placing a stone on a cell claims it; the first to form an unbroken chain between their sides wins. 11x11 is the standard size (world championship); the Laos photo shows the street version ('honeycomb game') (Hex Wiki / International Hex Federation, n.d.).

## History

Piet Hein published Hex in Politiken (1942); John Nash at Princeton co-invented it (1948) and proved no draw exists. The World Hex Championship is held annually (since the 1990s) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
