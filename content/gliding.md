## Basic Information

Gliding (sailplane flying) is the sport of flying unpowered aircraft — sailplanes — using rising air (thermals, ridge lift, wave) to stay aloft and complete racing or distance tasks; it is governed worldwide by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its International Gliding Commission (IGC) (FAI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competition classes are defined by wingspan and mass: the Open Class (maximum all-up mass 850 kg), the 18 Metre Class (18 m wingspan, 600 kg) and the 20 Metre Two-Seat Class (20 m, 750 kg, flown by a crew of two) (FAI, n.d.-a). The 40th FAI World Gliding Championships were held at Częstochowa-Rudniki airfield in southern Poland from 16–30 May 2026 (10 scoring tasks, up to 120 competitors): Felipe Levin of France won the Open Class with 8,945 points — his second title — just seven points ahead of Sebastian Kawa of Poland (8,938), with Michael Sommer third; Victor Mallick of France won the 18 Metre Class on 8,440 points ahead of Christophe Abadie (8,165) and Stefan Langer (8,051) (FAI, n.d.-b). The 27th FAI World Glider Aerobatic Championships and the 15th World Advanced Glider Aerobatic Championships were held in Fayence, France, from 3–11 September 2026, and the 13th FAI Sailplane Grand Prix series began in 2026 with 20 finalists (FAI, n.d.-c) (FAI — Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a) (FAI — Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-b) (FAI — Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-c).

At the end of 2026 the leading women include the top female competitors in the 18 Metre and Open classes at the 2026 Worlds in Poland, competing in the same classes as the men — gliding has no separate women's classes at world championships, though women's records and rankings are kept separately by the IGC (FAI, n.d.-b). On the men's side, the 2026 world champions are Levin (Open) and Mallick (18 Metre) (FAI, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In FAI/IGC competitions, pilots launch by winch, aerotow or self-launching engine, then complete a task — an assigned area task, a racing task through turnpoints, or a distance task — and are scored on speed (racing tasks, with a minimum speed and maximum 1,000 points for the day winner) or distance-and-time (area tasks); handicaps based on the IGC handicap list equalize different glider types (FAI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the World Gliding Championships, each of the three classes flies its own tasks — in 2026, up to ten scoring days with fixed-route races exceeding 800 km on the best days — and the class champion is the pilot with the highest total points (FAI, n.d.-b). Safety rules require parachutes, FLARM collision-warning devices and radio contact on many tasks, and pilots may use water ballast (Open and 18 Metre classes) to adjust wing loading (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Gliders are certified with airworthiness reviews, and pilots need a sailplane pilot licence; competition classes are Open (≤850 kg all-up mass), 18 Metre (≤600 kg), 20 Metre Two-Seat (≤750 kg) and, at other events, Standard, Club, World and 15 Metre classes (FAI, n.d.-a). Men and women compete together in the same classes with the same tasks; separate women's world records and an IGC women's ranking exist (FAI, n.d.-a).

## History

Gliding began with Otto Lilienthal's controlled flights in Germany in the 1890s, and the first gliding competitions were held in the 1920s at the Wasserkuppe in the Rhön; the FAI's International Gliding Commission has organized world championships since the first edition in 1938 at the Wasserkuppe, with European championships since 1982 (FAI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Gliding was a demonstration sport at the 1936 Berlin Olympics and was planned for Helsinki 1940, but it has never been an Olympic medal sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The post-war era saw the rise of laminar-flow sailplanes in the 1960s–70s, composite gliders from the 1980s, and today's high-performance 18 m designs such as the JS3 and AS 33; the 38th World Championships in Uvalde, Texas, in 2024 were won by Stefan Langer (18 Metre) (FAI, n.d.-c). Among the sport's icons are the multi-time world champions such as the German pilot Hans-Werner Grosse, holder of long-standing distance records, and Sebastian Kawa of Poland, 18 Metre world champion in 2017, 2019 and 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
