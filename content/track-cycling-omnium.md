## Basic Information

The omnium is track cycling's all-round event: a multi-race contest (the modern Olympic format of four events run over one day, historically six over two days) in which riders accumulate points race by race toward a single overall title. Combining bunch-race craft, sprint speed and endurance consistency, it crowns the best all-round track racer and has been an Olympic event since London 2012.

## Rules

The modern championship format (used at the Olympic Games) runs four events in sequence: a scratch race, a tempo race, an elimination race and a points race, with riders scoring points per placement through the first three events carrying into the concluding points race, where gained and lost points can swing the final standings. Earlier versions ran six events including a flying lap, individual pursuit and kilo or 500-metre time trial, with cumulative placing points; current international rules fix race distances (for example, a 10-kilometre scratch for men at the Olympic distance), count-back rules and conduct standards, and the event demands recovery between races within a compressed single-day programme.

## History

The omnium (an old English racing word for an event open to all) was standardized as cycling's track decathlon in the 2000s, joining the world championship programme and the Olympic Games (2012), with each subsequent olympiad refining the format (from six events to the current four-event day). The discipline rewards classic all-rounders: its champions have been among track cycling's biggest stars, and the format's compressed drama (a whole event decided in one day) has made the modern omnium one of the programme's most watchable events.
