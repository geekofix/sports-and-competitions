## Basic Information

The omnium — Latin for "of all" — is track cycling's all-round event, a multi-race competition in which riders accumulate points over four mass-start races (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). Since the 2016 reform it consists of a scratch race, tempo race, elimination race and points race held in a single day, and it has been an Olympic event since London 2012 and a World Championship event since 2007 (men) and 2009 (women) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the current leading riders — women first — are Jennifer Valente of the United States, who won the Olympic omnium gold at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 plus the world titles in 2022 and 2023, and the men's stars Benjamin Thomas of France (Paris 2024 Olympic champion) and Albert Torres Barceló of Spain, the 2025 world champion (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The modern omnium comprises four races in fixed order — scratch race, tempo race, elimination race and points race — all mass start, all on one day (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). In the first three races the winner scores 40 points, second 38, third 36 and so on down to 1 point for 21st and below; the points race decides the overall ranking, with sprints every 10 laps on tracks under 333.33 m (5 laps on longer tracks) worth 5-3-2-1 points, doubled in the final sprint, plus 20 points for lapping the field and −20 for being lapped (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). Elite distances are 10 km for men and 7.5 km for women in the scratch and tempo races, and 25 km (men) / 20 km (women) in the points race; junior riders race shorter distances (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). The rider with the most total points wins the omnium; a tie is broken by the placing in the final sprint of the points race, and a rider must complete every event or is ranked last (Wikipedia, n.d.). Between 2014 and 2016 the omnium was a two-day, six-race event that also included an individual pursuit, a flying lap and a 1 km/500 m time trial; those timed events were removed after Rio 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women contest identical formats with the shorter women's distances listed above (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.).

## History

The omnium was reintroduced to the UCI Track World Championships in 2007 for men and 2009 for women after the Belgian six-day star Patrick Sercu lobbied the UCI for its return after a 40-year absence, and it replaced the individual pursuit and points race in the Olympic programme at London 2012 — a change criticized by pursuer Rebecca Romero, who could not defend her Olympic title (Wikipedia, n.d.). Olympic champions include Lasse Norman Hansen (2012) and Elia Viviani (2016) in the men's event and Laura Kenny (2012, 2016, 2020) and Jennifer Valente (2020, 2024) in the women's (Wikipedia, n.d.; Olympics, n.d.). Laura Kenny, Britain's most decorated female Olympian with five golds, retired in 2021; Matt Walls won the men's omnium gold at Tokyo 2020 and Benjamin Thomas at Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
