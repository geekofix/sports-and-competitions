## Basic Information

The Supercars Championship (known through its history as the Australian Touring Car Championship, Shell Championship Series and V8 Supercars) is Australia's premier touring car category and one of the world's most followed national racing series. Heavier, saloon-shaped racers built around Ford and Holden-derived silhouette cars contest sprint and endurance rounds across every Australian state, headlined by the Bathurst 1000.

## Rules

Supercars are purpose-built front-engine, rear-wheel-drive racers cloaked in production car bodies: a control chassis with a 5.0-litre (later V8) engine specification, control Dunlop tires, transaxle gearboxes and a strict parity-focused rulebook that historically locked the grid to Ford and Holden V8 sedans. Race weekends use practice sessions, multi-part qualifying, top-ten shootouts, and sprint races of roughly 120 to 200 kilometres plus marquee endurance events, including the Bathurst 1000 held each October on the Mount Panorama Circuit in New South Wales. Championship points accumulate across races; teams run two-car entries with driver pairings for endurance rounds, and regulations (through control parts and Balance of Performance philosophy) keep a wide grid within a second in qualifying trim.

## History

The category descends from the Australian Touring Car Championship, first staged as a multi-race series in 1969 and won across eras by the sport's great names, from Ian Geoghegan and Allan Moffat to Peter Brock, Dick Johnson, Craig Lowndes and modern champions such as Jamie Whincup and Shane van Gisbergen. The modern commercial formula arrived in the 1990s: AVESCO was formed in 1996 to run the series, the era of the V8-only rules produced record crowds with the Bathurst 1000 and Adelaide 500 as landmarks, and the championship spread beyond Australia to rounds in New Zealand and, across the years, to China, Bahrain, the United Arab Emirates and the United States. Renamed Supercars in the 2010s and racing today with the Gen3 rulebook (Mustang and Camaro-bodied cars), the championship remains one of the closest-fought touring car series in the world.
