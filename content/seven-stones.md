## Basic Information

Seven stones (lagori, sitoliya, pittu, dabba kali, ezhu kallu — depending on region) is a traditional team game of the Indian subcontinent: one team scatters a pile of seven flat stones with a ball, then rebuilds the pile while the opposing team throws the ball to hit them. It is played by boys and girls in streets, schoolyards and parks across India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka, and it has organized leagues and world cups (Lagori World Cup since 2015, Indian Lagori Premier League 2017). Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [Pithoo](pithoo.html), [Gutte](gutte.html), [Kancha](kancha.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Each team has equal numbers of players (12 per side, six on court per set, in the International Lagori Federation format; three 3-minute sets per match). A seeker throws the ball at the pile of seven stones from behind the throwing line; failing to scatter at least one stone in three tries ends the turn. The seekers then rebuild the pile while hitters throw the ball to eliminate them — a hitter may not run with the ball, and a ball caught after one bounce eliminates the thrower. The seeker announces the completed pile with the game's name. Points are awarded to the seekers for a rebuilt pile and to the hitters for each elimination; the team with the most points after the sets wins. Modern play uses fibre discs instead of stones and a soft ball on synthetic turf.

## History

The game is ancient — the Bhagavata Purana mentions Krishna playing it — and it spread through cultural exchange across Asia (haft sang in Iran, sat chara in Bangladesh, game ball in Nepal, cantracon in Afghanistan) and via the British Empire to Africa, the Caribbean and Southeast Asia, where it is now played in more than 30 countries. Revival efforts include the Amateur Lagori Federation of India's Indian Lagori Premier League (2017) and the Lagori World Cup (2015, 2018).
