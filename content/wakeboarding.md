## Basic Information

Wakeboarding is the towed board sport in which a rider, strapped into a wakeboard, is pulled by a motorboat — or by a cable system at a wake park — at roughly 18–24 mph, carving across the wake and launching aerial tricks that mix water skiing, surfing, snowboarding and gymnastics (International Waterski and Wakeboard Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The world governing body is the International Waterski and Wakeboard Federation (IWWF), founded in Geneva in 1946 with 91 member federations, IOC-recognized for all towed water sports, running World Championships for boat and cable wakeboarding; the World Wake Association (WWA) runs the boat and cable tour series (International Waterski and Wakeboard Federation, n.d.; World Wake Association, n.d.). Women stand at the top of the sport in their own divisions: at the 2017 IWWF European Championships in Italy, France's Aurélie Godet won the women's event, and women's boat and cable titles are contested at every world championship (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Competition splits into boat and cable wakeboarding; boat events are judged on a run of tricks across the wake — spins, inverts, grabs and mobes — scored for execution, intensity, variety and difficulty, with two passes per rider; cable events run riders around a lake on an overhead cable past kickers and rails, scored on the tricks performed on each feature, with falls ending a pass (International Waterski and Wakeboard Federation, n.d.). Rules fix boat speed, rope length and wake shape for competition, and events are divided by age group and gender — separate men's and women's divisions — under the IWWF and WWA rulebooks (International Waterski and Wakeboard Federation, n.d.; World Wake Association, n.d.). Equipment is a twin-tip or directional board with bindings, a tow rope with handle, a life vest and helmet in competition (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The sport grew from mono water skiing in 1950s America and from surfboards towed behind boats (documented from the 1960s); in 1985 Tony Finn of San Diego began mass-producing the "Skurfer", a surf–water-ski hybrid, and the first Skurfer Skiboard World Championship was held in 1990 on the Wailua River in Hawaii, won by Eric Perez and televised on ESPN (Wikipedia, n.d.). In 1989–1990 Jimmy Redmon founded the World Wakeboard Association in Winter Park, Florida, and in 1991 Herb O'Brien's HO Sports introduced the Hyperlite, the first compression-molded neutral-buoyancy board, which made deep-water starts easy; the name "wakeboarding" replaced "skiboarding" in the early 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport reached the X Games and exploded in the 1990s; cable wake parks boomed from the late 1990s onward, making the sport accessible far from the coast, and the IWWF — the IOC-recognized federation since 1946 — took wakeboard under its umbrella with world championships for boat and cable disciplines (International Waterski and Wakeboard Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
