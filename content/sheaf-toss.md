## Basic Information

The sheaf toss (Scottish Gaelic *sguab*) is a traditional Scottish and Irish agricultural sport in which competitors hurl a hessian bag stuffed with straw — the sheaf — with a pitchfork over a horizontal bar that is raised after every round until only one thrower remains (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is a staple event at the Scottish Highland Games, although technically it is not one of the "heavy" athletics events, and it is also contested in the Basque Country, at England's Sykehouse Show in South Yorkshire and in Ireland, where a tightly bound rush sheaf is used instead of a bag (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; NASGA, n.d.). The world records stand at 11.30 m (37 ft 1 in) with the 20 lb (9.1 kg) men's sheaf, set by Spencer Tyler at the 2019 Chicago Highland Games, and 12.80 m (42 ft 0 in) with the 16 lb (7.3 kg) sheaf, set by Zach Riley in 2017, while the women's records are 36 ft 0 in (10.97 m) with the 10 lb sheaf by Nikita Marzano (2019, Pleasanton) and 32 ft 6 in (9.91 m) with the 12 lb sheaf by Victoria Bunchek (2017, Glasgow, Kentucky) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; WomensHighlandGames, n.d.).

Among today's leading throwers, the women's elite is headed by Nikita Marzano, holder of the 10 lb world record since 2019, and Victoria Bunchek, holder of the 12 lb record since 2017, while the men's standard-bearers are Spencer Tyler, whose 11.30 m in 2019 remains the 20 lb world record, and Zach Riley, whose 12.80 m in 2017 stands as the 16 lb record (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; WomensHighlandGames, n.d.).

## Rules

Competitors use a pitchfork to hurl the sheaf over a horizontal bar suspended above their heads; the standard weights are 20 lb (9.1 kg) for men — 16 lb (7.3 kg) in masters' classes — and 12 lb (5.4 kg) for women, with a 10 lb class in North American women's competition (ScottishAthletics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each thrower gets three attempts to clear the bar cleanly at each height; after all competitors have thrown, the bar is raised and everyone who cleared it moves on, continuing until all but one athlete are eliminated — the highest clean clearance wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The same pitchfork is usually provided for all competitors so that no one gains an advantage with a custom fork, and a custom fork brought to the games must be made available to the other competitors (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Irish variant uses a rush sheaf bound tightly with baling twine rather than a bag, with otherwise identical rules, and the event follows the same elimination format as weight over bar (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women compete in their own weight classes at the same games, with records tracked separately (WomensHighlandGames, n.d.).

## History

Sheaf tossing originated at country fairs and agricultural shows in Scotland and Ireland, where farm workers bet on who could throw a bundle of straw highest, and it became a fixture of the Highland Games movement from the 19th century onward; it also survives as a Basque tradition and at England's Sykehouse Show (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The men's 20 lb world record progressed from Harry MacDonald's 9.50 m in 1997 at Alexandria, Virginia, through Karl Dodge (10.06 m in 1998, 10.34 m and 10.36 m in 1999), Ryan Vierra (10.67 m in 2001), Mike Smith (10.75 m in 2004) and Eric Frasure (11.00 m and 11.03 m in 2010) to Spencer Tyler's 11.12 m in 2018 and his current 11.15 m and 11.30 m marks of 2019 at Long Beach and Chicago (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 16 lb record of 12.80 m was set by Zach Riley at the 2017 Bartholomew Scottish Festival (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
