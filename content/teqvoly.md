## Basic Information

Teqvoly is a teqball variant played like volleyball over the curved teqball table: two-player teams rally a ball around a central table fitted with a net, combining teqball's table with volleyball's rally logic (Teqvoly, n.d.). The game was invented in Hungary as an offshoot of teqball and is organized internationally under the FITEQ umbrella, with its own official site and annual world championships — the first was held in Budapest on 7–8 September 2019 with 16 nations and a $25,000 prize fund, the final won by the Polish pair Włostowski/Choromański over the Austrians Bachler/Fellner; the 2025 edition took place in Romania on 3 December 2025 (Teqvoly, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Women's teams compete on equal terms with men's, and the rules even give women and children a special allowance: they may enter the playing field when attacking (Teqvoly, n.d.).

## Rules

Teqvoly is played on a hexagonal court 18 × 16 m around the teqball table in the center, which carries a plexiglass net; two players form a team (Teqvoly, n.d.). The ball weighs 260–300 g with a circumference of 65–67 cm and a pressure of 0.24–0.26 bar (Teqvoly, n.d.). Each side may touch the ball at most three times and must play it to the partner at least once before attacking (Teqvoly, n.d.). Each player serves at most two serves in a row — one to each opponent (Teqvoly, n.d.). A set consists of two halves of 8 points, the match is best of two sets with a decider set to 12 points, and from 7–7 in the decider the serve changes every point (Teqvoly, n.d.). Attacks are made from outside the boundary after the internal play, and women and children may enter the field when attacking (Teqvoly, n.d.). The official rule source is the teqvoly regulations (Teqvoly, n.d.).

## History

Teqvoly was invented in Hungary shortly after teqball (2014) as a volleyball-style variant on the teq table; the first world championship in Budapest on 7–8 September 2019 drew 16 nations and offered $25,000 in prize money, with Poland's Włostowski/Choromański defeating Austria's Bachler/Fellner in the final (Teqvoly, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). World championships have been held annually since, including the 2025 edition in Romania on 3 December 2025, and the game is played in numerous countries with mixed and women's teams (Teqvoly, n.d.).
