## Basic Information

Drifting is a motorsport in which drivers deliberately oversteer through corners, sliding the rear wheels while keeping the car under control, with competitions judging speed, drift angle, line, style and — in head-to-head battles — proximity to the rival car (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). There is no single world federation; the sport is run by major series promoters, above all the D1 Grand Prix in Japan, Formula DRIFT in the United States and Drift Masters in Europe (D1 Grand Prix, n.d.-a; Formula DRIFT, n.d.). In 2026, Japan's Koudai Sobagiri won the D1GP championship, with compatriot Masashi Yokoi taking five round wins, while the 2026 Formula DRIFT PRO Championship — the 23rd season, running from 10 April to 24 October 2026 and bookended by the Streets of Long Beach — was won by Ireland's Conor Shanahan, with five-time champion James Deane second (Formula DRIFT, n.d.; D1 Grand Prix, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current drivers are — in women's drifting — Japan's Shiina Tamaki, the leading female competitor who took the best women's result in the 2026 D1GP season, and Malaysia's Minvern K, pictured below, and — in men's drifting — 2026 D1GP champion Koudai Sobagiri and 2026 Formula DRIFT champion Conor Shanahan (D1 Grand Prix, n.d.-a; Formula DRIFT, n.d.).

## Rules

Competitive drifting is run under the regulations of the respective series, principally the D1GP rules in Japan and the Formula DRIFT PRO Championship rules in the United States (D1 Grand Prix, n.d.-a; Formula DRIFT, n.d.). Cars are production-based, rear-wheel-drive machines fitted with a full roll cage, racing seat and harnesses, a hydraulic handbrake and modified suspension; tires are regulated, and after the inaugural D1GP season in 2001 semi-slick competition tires were banned as not road-legal (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competition begins with solo qualifying runs (tansou) that are scored by judges for speed, drift angle, line and style — Formula DRIFT divides the track into zones and awards points per zone, and the 2026 season returned to a classic two-run qualifying format feeding a Top 32 bracket (Formula DRIFT, n.d.). The best drivers then advance to head-to-head elimination battles (tsuiso, "twin runs"), in which the leading car starts first and the chasing car is judged on how closely it follows — contact, spinning out or dropping too far behind costs points or the battle (D1 Grand Prix, n.d.-a). Penalties include off-course excursions, insufficient angle and collisions; results combine qualifying position and battle wins into a championship table (Formula DRIFT, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same events and classes at D1GP, Formula DRIFT and Drift Masters — there are no separate women's championships at the top level — so the leading female drivers race directly against the men (D1 Grand Prix, n.d.-a).

## History

Drifting as a driving technique grew out of 1970s Japanese touring-car racing and the hashiriya street racers who slid their cars on the mountain passes (touge) of Japan; Keiichi Tsuchiya (born 30 January 1956 in Tōmi, Gunma Prefecture), the "Drift King", made the slide famous with his Toyota Corolla Levin AE86 and later became a commentator and team owner (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport became a championship when Tsuchiya and entrepreneur Daijiro Inada organized the first professional drift event at Ebisu Circuit in October 2000, which was folded into the first full D1 Grand Prix season in 2001; from the second round onward the series adopted the one-to-one tsuiso battle format that is now standard worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first D1GP and first professional drifting champion was Nobuteru Taniguchi (born 18 May 1971 in Hiroshima), who won the 2001 title in his HKS Nissan Silvia S15 and retired from professional drifting in 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the United States, Formula DRIFT was co-founded in 2003 by Jim Liaw and Ryan Sage of Slipstream Global Marketing, which had also brought D1GP to America, and held its first season at Long Beach in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Women have taken part in competition drifting since the 2000s and now race regularly at the highest level of D1GP, Formula DRIFT and Drift Masters (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
