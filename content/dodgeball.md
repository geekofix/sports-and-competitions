## Basic Information

Dodgeball is a team elimination ball game in which players throw balls at opponents while dodging throws from the other team; the team that eliminates all opposing players wins the round (WDBF, n.d.-a). Competitive dodgeball is governed internationally by the World Dodgeball Federation (WDBF), founded in Edmonton, Canada, in 2011, which standardizes the rules and runs the biennial WDBF World Championships — the 11th edition took place in Bangkok, Thailand, 5–13 December 2026 with more than 1,400 athletes from 36 countries in six divisions, the gold-medal finals on 12 December — and in Europe by the European Dodgeball Federation (EDF), which stages the European Championships in odd years since 2021 (WDBF, n.d.-b; EDF, n.d.). At the 2024 World Championships in Graz, Austria, the United States men defended their title for a third time and Canada won its fourth women's title (WDBF, n.d.-b). The leading current players at the end of 2026 are the members of the 2026 world champion squads from Bangkok and, in Europe, the reigning European champions of the 2025 EDF European Championships (WDBF, n.d.-b; EDF, n.d.) — the sections below cover rules, history and more.

## Rules

Under the WDBF Dodgeball Rules, a team fields six players on a court of about 18 m × 9 m (60 × 30 ft) with an attack line roughly one third of the court from each back line and no neutral zone (WDBF, 2024). At the start of each point, all six balls lie along the center line and, on the referee's call, both teams rush to grab them — the opening rush pictured above (WDBF, 2024). A player hit by a live ball is out; a thrown ball caught before touching the ground, another player or a wall eliminates the thrower; and eliminated players leave the court (WDBF, 2024). A team wins the point when all opponents are out or, when time expires, when it has more players remaining (WDBF, 2024). Matches are played in two 20-minute halves within a set-based format, with defined substitution, timeout and overtime procedures, and the WDBF sanctions both cloth and foam ball formats across its divisions (WDBF, 2024; WDBF, n.d.-b). The EDF applies the same core rules at the European Championships with regional variants such as no-sting balls and modified catch rules (EDF, n.d.). Men's, women's and mixed divisions are contested at world and continental level with identical court dimensions and ball counts (WDBF, n.d.-b; EDF, n.d.).

## History

Dodgeball's modern form was codified in early twentieth-century North American schools and YMCAs, but the competitive game with formal rules developed mainly from the 1990s onward, influenced by playground games and popularized by the 2004 film Dodgeball: A True Underdog Story (Wikipedia, n.d.). The WDBF was founded in Edmonton, Canada, in 2011, held its first World Championship in Kuala Lumpur in 2012 and grew into continental championships on every continent; since 2022 the World Championships have been held biennially (WDBF, n.d.-b). The EDF organizes the European Championships, and national federations run leagues across the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Europe and Asia (EDF, n.d.). The 2024 World Championships in Graz drew about 1,400 athletes from 36 countries, and the 11th edition followed in Bangkok in December 2026 (WDBF, n.d.-b).
