## Basic Information

Canicross is a dryland sled-dog sport in which a runner is pulled by one or two dogs harnessed to a waist belt with an elastic line. The dog wears a pulling harness and runs in front, and the pair race cross-country against the clock. Canicross is governed internationally by the International Federation of Sleddog Sports (IFSS), a GAISF-recognized federation founded in 1987, which also runs the World Dryland Championships covering canicross, bikejor and scooter.

## Rules

The IFSS Race Rules (2026-2028 edition) contain a dedicated canicross chapter. The dog must be harnessed and attached by a line to the runner's belt and must stay in front of the runner; the runner may push from behind but may only be pulled by the line. Races are run as mass starts or time trials over courses of typically 3 to 10 km depending on category, with men's and women's divisions and a minimum age for the dogs of 18 months. Above temperature limits, races are shortened or cancelled to protect the dogs, and veterinary checks apply. National federations organize qualifying races for the IFSS World Dryland Championships.

## History

Canicross developed in Europe in the 1990s, mainly in France, as off-season cross-country training for sled-dog mushers, and quickly became a sport in its own right. The IFSS created dryland disciplines and rules alongside its sleddog disciplines, and its World Dryland Championships bring together canicross, bikejor and scooter athletes from many countries. The sport is now practiced across Europe, North America, Australia and Asia, with national championships feeding into the world championships.
