## Basic Information

Draughts (checkers) is a two-player board game played on an 8x8 or 10x10 chequered board, in which each player moves diagonal-forward men to capture opponent pieces by jumping over them; a player wins by capturing all opponent pieces or blocking all their moves. The 100-square variant is known internationally as international draughts and is governed by the World Draughts Federation (FMJD); the 64-square English/American variant is called checkers in the US. Both are played at the World Mind Games and by national federations worldwide.

## Rules

In international draughts (FMJD rules, 10x10 board, 20 pieces per player), men move diagonally forward one square and capture by jumping over an adjacent opponent piece onto the empty square beyond, with mandatory capture and majority-capture rules; captured pieces are removed only at the end of the move (in international draughts, unlike English draughts). Kings (flying kings) move and capture any distance along diagonals. A game is drawn by agreement, by the 25-move rule for kings without progress, or when neither player can force a win. In English draughts (8x8), men capture forward and backward but move only forward, multiple jumps are mandatory, and a man reaching the far row is crowned king. At FMJD World Championships, matches are played with time controls in Swiss or round-robin formats, in men's, women's and youth categories.

## History

Draughts-like games are among the oldest known board games, with predecessors in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia around 3000 BC; the modern 100-square game with flying kings was formalized in France in the 16th-17th centuries. The World Draughts Federation (FMJD, Fédération Mondiale du Jeu de Dames) was founded in 1947 in Amsterdam and organizes the World Championship (men's since 1948, women's since 1973). English draughts/checkers spread in the American colonies and became a staple of American culture, with the first American championship in 1840 won by William Payne. Computer checkers reached a milestone in 1994 when Marion Tinsley's program Chinook became effectively unbeatable, leading to the game being "solved" in 2007. Champions such as Tinsley, Alexander Moiseyev and Guntis Valneris are celebrated in the draughts world.
