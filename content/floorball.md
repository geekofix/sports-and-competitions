## Basic Information

Floorball is a fast indoor team sport played with a lightweight plastic stick (with a basket-like blade) and a perforated plastic ball, on a 40 m x 20 m court surrounded by a rink board, with goals 1.6 m x 1.15 m. It is governed by the International Floorball Federation (IFF), which runs the Floorball World Championships (men's since 1996, women's since 1997) and is an Olympic-recognized federation; floorball will debut at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics.

## Rules

At IFF World Championship and Olympic level, teams field six players (five outfield plus a goalkeeper) on a 40 x 20 m court, and matches are three 20-minute periods. Players use sticks to dribble, pass and shoot the 23 g plastic ball into the opponent's goal; the goalkeeper may use hands and body inside the goal crease but cannot cover the ball for more than 3 seconds, and may not receive a pass from a teammate with hands/arms (throw-in rules differ from ice hockey). Body contact is limited (no checking, holding or tripping); fouls are penalized with 2-minute minors, 5-minute majors, match penalties and penalty shots. Face-offs restart play after goals and fouls, and a team shorthanded plays 5v4 or 4v3 etc. Substitutions are unlimited and on the fly. The goalkeeper wears a mask and protective gear. Matches tied after regulation go to overtime (10 min at Worlds; golden goal at the Olympics) and then penalty shots.

## History

Floorball developed in Sweden in the 1960s-70s (as "innebandy," codified around 1968-1970, inspired by ice hockey played indoors), spreading to Finland, Switzerland and the Czech Republic. The IFF was founded in 1986 in Helsinki, and the first men's World Championship was held in 1996 in Sweden (won by Sweden); the women's followed in 1997. Sweden, Finland, Switzerland and the Czech Republic dominate, with Sweden and Finland having won most of the world titles; Finnish star Mika Kohonen is among the sport's best-known players. The IOC recognized the IFF in 2021, and floorball makes its Olympic debut at Milano Cortina 2026 with six men's and six women's teams.
