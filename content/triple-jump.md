## Basic Information

The triple jump — historically the 'hop, step and jump' — is the second horizontal jump of athletics: from a 1.22 m take-off board set 11–13 m from the pit, the athlete hops off one foot, lands on the same foot, steps onto the other foot and jumps into the sand pit; the total distance of the three phases is measured. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic programme since 1896 for men and since 1996 for women (first at a World Championships in 1993) (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world records are 18.29 m for men, set by Jonathan Edwards of Great Britain at the 1995 World Championships in Gothenburg, and 15.74 m for women, set by Yulimar Rojas of Venezuela (indoor, Belgrade 2022; outdoor best 15.67 m, Tokyo 2021) (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was headlined by reigning world champion Leyanis Pérez of Cuba (pictured), who jumped 15.06 m — her first legal mark over 15 m — in Monaco on 10 July 2026, with Saly Sarr of Senegal (2026 Diamond League Final and Ultimate Championship winner) and Davisleydi Velazco of Cuba; world record holder Yulimar Rojas (pictured at Rio 2016) returned to competition in 2025 and won bronze at the Tokyo World Championships (World Athletics, n.d.-c). The men's event was led by 2025 world champion Pedro Pichardo of Portugal (17.91 m, world lead, Tokyo 2025) and Jordan Díaz of Spain, the 2024 Olympic champion (17.86 m, Paris; personal best 18.18 m) (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

## Rules

The triple jump uses the same board, pit and measuring rules as the long jump, with the board set 11–13 m from the pit at elite level; the hop must be landed on the take-off foot, the step on the other foot, and the jump into the pit (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Field events at major championships begin with a qualification round; in the final each athlete has one attempt at each height, and after three attempts the best eight receive three further attempts in reverse order of placings, with a one-minute time limit per attempt (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Fouls include over-stepping, wrong foot sequence and landing outside the pit; wind above +2.0 m/s invalidates records; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete separately under identical rules; junior and para competitions use adapted distances and classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

The triple jump has been contested at every modern Olympics for men since 1896; Nelson Évora of Portugal won the 2007 world title and Pedro Pichardo the 2020 Olympic title in Tokyo (Wikipedia, n.d.). Jonathan Edwards, pictured, produced one of the greatest competitions in athletics history at the 1995 World Championships in Gothenburg, breaking his own world record twice in the same final — 18.16 m and then 18.29 m — a mark still standing in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e). The women's event debuted at the World Championships in 1993 and at the Olympics in 1996, where Inessa Kravets of Ukraine won gold and set the world record of 15.50 m in Gothenburg 1995; it stood for 26 years until Yulimar Rojas jumped 15.67 m for Olympic gold in Tokyo 2021 and 15.74 m indoors in Belgrade 2022 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).
