## Basic Information

Pencak silat is an umbrella term for the traditional martial arts of the Indonesian archipelago and the Malay world, encompassing hundreds of regional styles (aliran) with empty-hand techniques, grappling, throws and traditional weapons such as the stick (toya), machete (golok) and dagger (keris) (UNESCO, 2019). Since 1980 the sport has been organized internationally by the International Pencak Silat Federation (IPSF-PERSILAT), founded in Jakarta on 11 March 1980, which has more than five million members across dozens of national federations (International Pencak Silat Federation, n.d.). Pencak silat has been a regular sport at the Southeast Asian (SEA) Games since 1987 and was included in the Asian Games for the first time in 2018 in Jakarta and Palembang, where host Indonesia won 14 of 16 gold medals (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). PERSILAT distinguishes four aspects of the art: self-defense (bela diri), sport (olah raga), artistic forms (seni) and mental-spiritual development (UNESCO, 2019). The sport is contested by men and women in separate classes at every international championship — the women's final at the 2011 SEA Games is pictured below — and in December 2019 the traditions of pencak silat were inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (UNESCO, 2019).

## Rules

In international competition, PERSILAT sanctions four categories: tanding (full-contact sparring, covered on this site as [Pencak Silat Tanding](pencak-silat-tanding.html)) and three artistic categories — tunggal (solo forms), ganda (doubles) and regu (group), covered as [Pencak Silat Seni](pencak-silat-seni.html) (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Competition is governed by the International Pencak Silat Competition Rules and Regulations issued by PERSILAT. Tanding bouts are fought by athletes in protective gear, weighted in classes of roughly 5 kg increments, with points awarded for clean strikes, sweeps and takedowns; attacks to the spine, throat and groin are prohibited. Artistic competitors perform jurus (formal movement sequences) before a jury that scores precision, power, flow and fidelity to tradition. International events such as the SEA Games apply the same PERSILAT rulebook, with one athlete per weight category and per seni event per federation (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Men's and women's competitions use identical rules and weight-class structures, with a breast protector additionally required for women in tanding (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Structured pencak silat traces its earliest organized roots to the Minangkabau region of West Sumatra in the 6th century, and over the centuries regional schools developed distinctive styles tied to local traditions (UNESCO, 2019). In 1948 the Indonesian Pencak Silat Association (IPSI) was founded in Solo and the unified term "pencak silat" was adopted for the national art. The international federation PERSILAT followed in Jakarta on 11 March 1980, founded jointly with the Malaysian, Singaporean and Bruneian federations. Pencak silat entered the SEA Games in 1987 in Jakarta and the Asian Games in 2018, and in December 2019 the traditions of pencak silat were inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (UNESCO, 2019). Indonesia has dominated the sport's international championships, which PERSILAT stages every two years for juniors and seniors, and the 2011 SEA Games in Jakarta — whose women's and men's tanding finals were photographed at the Padepokan arena — marked the sport's continued growth in the region (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
