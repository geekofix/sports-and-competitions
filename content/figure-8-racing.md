## Basic Information

Figure-8 racing is stock-car racing on a track shaped like a figure eight, so the cars cross paths at the intersection and must time their crossing — deliberate contact at the crossover is a signature of the show (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the oval-track stock-car family with short-track and demolition derby.

![Figure 8 action on the track (historical)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Figure_8_Action.jpg?width=420)

*Figure: Rigdon at English Wikipedia. (17 August 2006 (original upload date)). Figure 8 action on the track (historical) [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Figure_8_Action.jpg*

Tracks are typically short ovals (about 1/5 to 1/2 mile) with the crossover paved; races run heats plus a main event, and the figure-8 format awards points for both loops of the figure (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety rules require roll cages, racing seats, harnesses and helmets; the crossover is marshalled to avoid head-on contact where possible (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Cars are usually inexpensive stock or "claimer" cars — the Eastern Michigan State Fair event in the photo shows trailer-class machines — keeping entry costs low (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same classes; ladies’ divisions exist at some tracks.

## History

Figure-8 racing began in the United States in the 1930s–40s at county fairs and short dirt ovals, when promoters realized that a crossover added drama; it peaked in popularity in the 1950s–70s and survives today at fairgrounds and small ovals, especially in the US Midwest and at events like the Eastern Michigan State Fair (Wikipedia, n.d.).
