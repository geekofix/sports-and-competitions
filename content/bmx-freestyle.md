## Basic Information

BMX freestyle is BMX cycling performed as an acrobatic judged sport: riders do tricks on ramps, rails, boxes and in skateparks, and in the Olympic "park" format on a course of bowls and jumps. It is governed by the UCI and has been an Olympic discipline since Beijing 2008, with men's and women's park events.

## Rules

Under UCI and Olympic rules, each rider gets two runs of 60 seconds in the park discipline; judges score difficulty, execution, height, variety and flow on a 100-point scale, and the better run counts. At the UCI BMX Freestyle World Championships there are also flatland events, judged on tricks performed while balancing on the ground. Riders must wear helmets; full-finger gloves and pads are recommended. The top scorers from qualifications advance to the final.

## History

BMX freestyle began in the 1970s in Southern California, where kids raced and did tricks on 20-inch bikes in empty pools and on dirt jumps. Skatepark culture and halfpipes drove the sport in the 1980s-90s; the X Games (from 1995) made stars of riders like Dave Mirra. The UCI integrated BMX freestyle in the 2010s, and park became an Olympic medal event at Beijing 2008. Stars today include Hannah Roberts (USA) and Logan Martin (AUS).
