## Basic Information

The combined is alpine skiing's two-discipline test pairing a speed leg with a technical leg: traditionally one skier raced both a downhill and a slalom run and the combined times decided, and from Milano Cortina 2026 the Olympic event is a team combined for pairs — each nation's two skiers split the legs, one racing the downhill, the other the slalom — with the pair's combined time counting (FIS, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The individual combined was dropped from the Olympic programme after Beijing 2022 and had already left the World Cup after the 2020 season (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At Milano Cortina 2026 the inaugural team combined events were held at the Stelvio Ski Centre in Bormio for the men (9 February) and at the Olimpia delle Tofane in Cortina d'Ampezzo for the women (10 February) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the champions, the women lead: Austria's Ariane Rädler and Katharina Huber won the women's title in 2:21.66, ahead of Germany's Kira Weidle-Winkelmann and Emma Aicher (2:21.71) and the United States' Jacqueline Wiles and Paula Moltzan (2:21.91); on the men's side Switzerland's Franjo von Allmen and Tanguy Nef took gold in 2:44.04, with two silver medals shared by Switzerland (Marco Odermatt and Loïc Meillard) and Austria (Vincent Kriechmayr and Manuel Feller) in 2:45.03 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.).

## Rules

Under FIS alpine rules, the speed leg (downhill or super-G) and the slalom leg are set as separate courses with their own gate settings; in the team combined each pair's downhill and slalom times are added, and the pair with the lowest combined time wins; ties share the medals (FIS, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the classic individual combined the same athlete raced one downhill (or super-G) run and one slalom run, with the two times added (FIS, n.d.-a).

Men and women compete in separate events on their own courses at the same venues; equipment (skis, boots, helmets) follows the FIS equipment regulations, with minimum ski lengths and FIS-marked helmets required; at the World Championships the combined (individual or team) is one of the five alpine disciplines alongside downhill, super-G, giant slalom and slalom (FIS, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The combined has been a staple of the Olympic and World Championship programme for decades, first contested at the Olympic Winter Games in the 1930s; France's Jean-Claude Killy, pictured, became its most famous icon at Grenoble 1968, where he won three gold medals — downhill, slalom and combined — the only man ever to win three alpine golds at one Winter Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

On the women's side, Liechtenstein's Hanni Wenzel won the Olympic combined gold in 1980, and Austria's Petra Kronberger won combined and slalom gold at Albertville 1992; the event has been dropped and reintroduced several times, and from 2026 it is contested as the team combined at the Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).
