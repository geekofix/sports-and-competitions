## Basic Information

Latigo y Cadena ("whip and chain") is the flexible-weapon division of Filipino martial arts: the bullwhip-like latigo and the chain/bola (sagad? — chain with weight), plus scarves and ropes, used for striking, wrapping, disarming and entangling at range before closing to stick or blade. It is one of the three classical FMA divisions alongside larnis (blade) and arnis de mano (hand). Practiced in the Philippines and by FMA schools worldwide; flexible-weapon events appear at modern tournaments with safety rules. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Larnis](larnis.html), [Eskrima](eskrima.html), [Arnis de Mano](arnis-de-mano.html), [Pekiti-Tirsia](pekiti-tirsia.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Traditional Filipino Weapons, n.d.).

## Rules

Latigo y cadena has no standalone sport rules; in competition flexible weapons are demonstrated or used in controlled sparring under FMA tournament safety rules (no full-contact whip strikes to the body in sport events; padded targets). The combat application — wrapping the opponent's weapon arm, disarming, striking — is assessed in forms and scenario drills. Modern FMA rule sets (WEKAF) govern any competitive use (Traditional Filipino Weapons, n.d.).

## History

Flexible weapons grew from rural and maritime tools (herding whips, fishing lines with weights) and from encounters with Spanish sword-and-rapier fencing; the most famous whip system is Momoy Cañete's San Miguel Eskrima. Spanish rule suppressed weapons practice, pushing flexible weapons underground with the rest of FMA. The art survives in Visayan and Cebuano lineages and in modern combatives curricula (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
