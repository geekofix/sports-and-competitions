## Basic Information

Lawn bowls — or simply bowls — is a precision ball sport played on a flat green: players roll biased (asymmetric) bowls so that they curve toward a small white target ball, the jack, and score one point for every bowl lying closer to the jack than the opponent's best bowl (World Bowls, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by World Bowls (formerly the International Bowling Board, founded 1905), which publishes the Laws of the Sport of Bowls and runs the World Outdoor Bowls Championship every four years — the next edition in July 2027 at Leamington Spa, England — with men's (Taylor Trophy) and women's (Leonard Trophy) titles contested together since 2008 (World Bowls, n.d.-b). Bowls has been on the Commonwealth Games programme since 1930, with women added in 1982; at Glasgow 2026 (24 July – 2 August 2026) the indoor programme featured singles, pairs and Para Bowls (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the top of the women's game stand India's fours team — Lovely Choubey, Pinki, Nayanmoni Saikia and Rupa Rani Tirkey — who won Commonwealth Games gold in Birmingham 2022, alongside the current world indoor champions — the 2026 season's leaders are listed on the World Bowls rankings (World Bowls, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Bowls is the rolling member of the wider boules-and-bowls family: its thrown-bole cousins are [Pétanque](petanque.html), [Bocce](bocce.html), [Bocce Punto](bocce-punto.html), [Bocce Raffa](bocce-raffa.html), [Bocce Volo](bocce-volo.html) and [Boule Lyonnaise](boule-lyonnaise.html), its bowls variants are [Indoor Bowls](indoor-bowls.html) and [Crown Green Bowls](crown-green-bowls.html), and the family also covers [Irish Road Bowling](irish-road-bowling.html), [Varpa](varpa.html), [Ice Stock Sport](ice-stock-sport.html), [Shuffleboard](shuffleboard.html), [Table Shuffleboard](table-shuffleboard.html) and Paralympic [Boccia](boccia.html).

## Rules

Bowls is played on a flat grass or artificial green divided into up to six rinks, each 4.3–5.5 m wide; each rink has a ditch at both ends and a starting mat (600 × 360 mm, placed no more than 100 mm from the ditch) from which the jack is delivered (World Bowls, n.d.-a). The bowls are biased: weighing at most 1.59 kg and measuring about 11–13 cm in diameter, they curve as they slow down; the jack is a small white ball of about 6–7 cm diameter (World Bowls, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Play proceeds in ends: the lead of each team delivers the jack, then players alternate rolling their bowls; after all bowls are played, the rink measures and scores one point per bowl closer to the jack than the opponent's closest bowl (World Bowls, n.d.-a). Formats are singles (first to 21 points), pairs (18), triples (15) and fours (12); the World Indoor Bowls Championship (Yarmouth, 9–25 January 2026) uses the short-mat indoor game, and Para Bowls uses sport classes (e.g. LA7) with delivery aids (World Bowls, n.d.-b). Fouls include moving the jack with a bowl, delivering from off the mat and touching a bowl at rest; men and women compete in separate events at world level but share club and mixed competition (World Bowls, n.d.-b). Rule source: World Bowls, Laws of the Sport of Bowls (Crystal Mark, fourth edition), https://www.worldbowls.com/ (World Bowls, n.d.-a). See also [petanque](petanque.html), [bocce](bocce.html), [croquet](croquet.html) and [curling](curling.html) on this site.

## History

Bowls-like games are ancient — a ball game is depicted in an Egyptian tomb of about 5000 BC, and lawn bowling was played in medieval Europe, with the oldest surviving bowling green at Southampton, England, dating to 1299 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The modern sport was codified in 17th–19th century England, where the bias of the bowl was refined, and the International Bowling Board was founded in 1905 to standardize the laws internationally (World Bowls, n.d.-b). Bowls entered the Commonwealth Games in 1930 (Lawn Bowls at Hamilton, Ontario) and the women's event followed in 1982; the first World Outdoor Bowls Championship was held in 1966 in Sydney, Australia, and has been held every four years since (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The international body was rebranded World Bowls in recent years and continues to govern outdoor, indoor and para bowls worldwide (World Bowls, n.d.-a).
