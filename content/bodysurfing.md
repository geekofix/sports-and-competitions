## Basic Information

Bodysurfing is riding a wave without any board: the rider uses their body, swimfins and often a handplane to catch and ride breaking waves head-first toward the shore. The International Bodysurfing Association (IBSA) is the global governing body and runs the Bodysurfing World Tour; the biggest traditional contests are the Pipeline Bodysurfing Classic (Hawaii) and the Oceanside World Bodysurfing Championship (California).

## Rules

Bodysurfing contests are judged events: riders catch waves in heats and are scored on difficulty, speed, power, flow and style on each wave (out of 10), with the best waves counting. The classic contests at Banzai Pipeline (Oahu, Hawaii) and the Wedge in Newport Beach (California) have run since the 1970s. The IBSA Bodysurfing World Tour uses a qualifying-series plus finals format with Open Men and Open Women divisions. There is no equipment beyond fins and optionally a handplane.

## History

Bodysurfing predates board surfing; recorded history begins in 1899 in Australia, when Fred Williams was taught to bodysurf by Tommy Tanna. It was popularized in the United States in the 1920s by Olympic swimmer Wally O'Conner, and the Churchill swim fin (1940) revolutionized the sport. The Pipeline Bodysurfing Classic first ran in 1971, and the Oceanside World Bodysurfing Championship in 1977. Legendary riders include Mark Cunningham and nine-time bodyboarding/bodyboarding crossover champion Mike Stewart, who won the Pipeline Classic a record 13 times.
