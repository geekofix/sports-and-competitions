## Basic Information

Barebow archery is the World Archery division in which archers shoot a recurve bow or longbow without any sight, stabilizers, or other aiming aids — aiming "instinctively" or by instinctive/sight-picture methods. It is governed by World Archery (WA), founded in 1931, which runs barebow events at the World Archery Championships, the World Field Championships and the World Games. The 2026 World Archery Field Championships at the NFAA Easton Yankton Archery Center in South Dakota (September 22–27, 2026, followed by the World 3D Championships September 28–October 3) were dominated by Italy: Giuseppe Seimandi won the men's barebow field title over Sweden's Erik Jonsson, Eleonora Strobbe won the women's barebow title, and the mixed team title went to Cinzia Noziglia and Matteo Uggeri — Noziglia being widely dubbed the "Queen of Barebow" — while the USA won both full team titles (World Archery, n.d.-a).

Among the archers who shape the division today, the women come first: Italy's Cinzia Noziglia, the dominant barebow field shooter of the 2020s, and Eleonora Strobbe, the 2026 world field champion; on the men's side, Giuseppe Seimandi (2026 world field champion), Sweden's Erik Jonsson and Britain's Oliver Hicks (2024 world field finalist) lead the rankings (World Archery, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the World Archery Book of Rules, barebow archers use a recurve bow or longbow with no sight, no stabilizers, no clicker and no string-walking reference aids beyond the barebow rules: string-walking (moving the fingers down the string for gap aiming) and face-walking are permitted in WA barebow, while in some national federations (e.g. NFAA instinctive) they are not. The bow must be shot over the hand or with a simple arrow rest; no mechanical release aids are allowed in barebow at the Olympics and World Championships (World Archery, n.d.-a).

Target archery is shot at 50 m on an 80 cm target face for Recurve and Barebow at World Championships and World Cups, in a set system: best of five sets, each set best of three arrows (or one arrow per archer alternating in the shoot-off), 10-ring scoring, highest set total wins; ties are broken by a single-arrow shoot-off. Field archery is shot on unmarked 3D courses: 40 targets per round over two days, distances 5–60 m unmarked, with a maximum of three arrows per target and 11-10-8-5 scoring on the animal/3D target faces. 3D archery (NFAA/WA 3D) uses marked distances with similar scoring (World Archery, n.d.-b).

Men's and women's barebow use identical distances and target faces at World Championships — 50 m and the 80 cm face for both — while at the Olympic Games barebow is not on the program (recurve only); the World Games include barebow. Age classes: U18 (cadet) and U21 (junior) shoot shorter distances and smaller targets (40 cm face at 50 m for juniors in target, marked 3D distances for juniors). Para archery has its own open and W1 classes with some barebow-style setups (World Archery, n.d.-a).

## History

Archery is one of the oldest sports, with the first archery competitions dating to 17th-century England (the Scorton Silver Arrow contest of 1673, still held). Modern target archery was codified with the founding of the Grand National Archery Society in Britain in 1861 and the National Archery Association of the USA in 1879; World Archery (originally FITA) was founded in 1931 in Lwów (then Poland) (World Archery, n.d.-c).

Barebow as a formal division emerged in the mid-20th century, particularly in the United States (NFAA instinctive shooting) and Europe; longbow shooting has an even longer tradition, revived by the English Longbow Society founded in 1951. Archery became an Olympic sport in 1900, was dropped, and returned permanently in 1972 (Munich) with recurve; barebow and compound were added as World Championship divisions later, and compound joined the Youth Olympics. On the women's side, the first women's world archery championship was held in 1931, and legendary barebow/instinctive women include the USA's Lisa Unruh and Italy's Cinzia Noziglia, whose run of world field titles in the 2020s has made her the most decorated female barebow archer in history (World Archery, n.d.-b).
