## Basic Information

The World Nomad Games are a biennial international multi-sport event dedicated to the traditional and ethnic sports of nomadic peoples, often described as the “Olympics of the nomads.” The first three editions were held in Cholpon-Ata on the northern shore of Lake Issyk-Kul in Kyrgyzstan (2014, 2016, 2018); the fourth took place in Iznik, Turkey (2022), and the fifth in Astana, Kazakhstan (2024). The sixth edition (VI World Nomad Games) was held from 31 August to 6 September 2026 in Bishkek and Cholpon-Ata, Kyrgyzstan, with around 3,000 athletes from more than 100 countries competing in 43 sports; the next Games are scheduled for Kazan, Russia, in 2028. The program spans equestrian games, traditional wrestling styles, traditional archery, hunting with birds of prey and dogs, intellectual games such as toguz korgool, oware and ordo, and strength contests such as mass wrestling, arm wrestling and tug of war. In 2021 UNESCO included the World Nomad Games in its Register of Best Safeguarding Practices under the 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of Intangible Cultural Heritage. Women compete in many disciplines, including the intellectual games, archery and horse racing.

## Rules

The Games follow a shared international rulebook for each discipline, published on the official site worldnomadgames.org, so that national variants — for example the Kyrgyz, Turkish and Hungarian archery styles or the Kyrgyz-style and horseback archery — compete under standardized conditions. Editions are held every two years; the 2020 edition was postponed to 2022 because of the COVID-19 pandemic. The 2026 program counted 43 sports in categories such as equestrian games (10 sports, 17 disciplines, including flat racing, kunan chabysh, zhorgo salysh, alaman chabysh and er enish), wrestling (18 sports), archery (3 sports, 6 disciplines, including Kyrgyz-style archery with 193 athletes), intellectual games (mangala, ordo, oware, toguz korgool), hunting games (burkut saluu, kush saluu, dalba, taigan zharysh) and strength games (omurtka bone breaking, strongman, tug of war), plus demonstration sports. Medals are awarded per discipline, and a combined medal table ranks the participating nations.

## History

The Games were conceived by the Kyrgyz philanthropist and sports administrator Askhat Akibaev, who wanted a festival showcasing not only Kyrgyz heritage but the traditions of nomadic peoples worldwide; he launched the first edition in 2014 at the highest state level with the support of Kyrgyz President Almazbek Atambayev, who personally led the Kyrgyz delegation at the opening ceremonies of 2014 and 2016. The inaugural Games in Cholpon-Ata (9–14 September 2014) gathered 583 athletes from 19 countries in 10 competitive sports plus demonstration sports; the 2016 edition involved athletes from 40–80 countries in 23 sports, and the 2018 edition more than 1,500 athletes from 66 countries in 37 sports. After the 2022 Games in Iznik, Turkey, and the 2024 Games in Astana, Kazakhstan (more than 2,500 athletes from 89 countries), the sixth edition returned to Kyrgyzstan in 2026. UNESCO inscribed the Games in its Register of Best Safeguarding Practices in 2021.
