## Basic Information

Riichi mahjong is the Japanese ruleset of the four-player tile game mahjong, played with 136 tiles and distinguished by the riichi declaration — a 1,000-point stake announcing that the hand needs only one more tile to win — dora indicator tiles revealed during play, and the yaku system, which requires at least one scoring pattern to win a hand (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is the standard competitive variant in Japan, where professional bodies such as the Japan Professional Mahjong League (JPML) run ranked leagues, and in Europe it is governed by the European Mahjong Association (EMA), which has staged the European Riichi Championship since 2008 in Hannover and whose EMA Riichi Rulebook 2025 takes effect on 1 January 2026 (EMA, n.d.). The World Riichi Championship, held every three years since 2014, was won at its 2025 edition in Tokyo (1–6 July 2025, Nihonbashi Mitsui Hall) by Japan's Kotaro Uchikawa, with a team event added for the first time; the next edition follows in 2028 in New York (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The game is open to women and men on equal terms, and the 2024 European Championship in Valencia crowned women's champions including Maude Grimberg of France and Anastasiia Veremenko of Ukraine (EMA, n.d.).

## Rules

Riichi mahjong uses 136 tiles: three suits (circles, bamboo, characters) numbered 1–9 with four copies each (108 tiles) plus four copies of each of the seven honour tiles — winds and dragons (28 tiles) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Four players sit around the table, the dealer (East) rotates after each round, and each player holds a hand of 13 tiles, drawing and discarding in turn; a hand wins with a 14th tile (tsumo from the wall or ron from a discard) when it forms four melds plus a pair and contains at least one yaku (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Declaring riichi — possible when the hand is one tile from winning (tenpai) — costs a 1,000-point stake, after which the hand may no longer be changed; dora and ura-dora indicators add bonus han, and the WRC rules apply the head-bump (atamahane) rule for simultaneous winning claims (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring works in han (pattern value) and fu (hand composition), with limit hands mangan (5 han), haneman (6–7), baiman (8–10), sanbaiman (11–12) and yakuman (13 or more), plus special yakuman like kokushi (thirteen orphans) and four concealed pungs; furiten (a waiting tile previously discarded) blocks a ron win (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There are no gender or age classes at professional level — women and men compete in the same open tournaments under the EMA and WRC rulebooks (EMA, n.d.).

## History

Mahjong developed in 19th-century China during the Qing dynasty and reached Japan around 1924, where the riichi declaration and dora mechanics were popularized in the early 20th century; professional league play in Japan dates to the 1920s and the JPML organizes today's top professionals (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The World Riichi Championship was founded in 2014 by the French brothers Quentin and Valérian Thomas through their company Tri Nitro Tiles: the first edition was held in Puteaux and won by Japan's Hiroshi Yamai, followed by Las Vegas 2017 (Masaharu Tomotake), Vienna 2022 (Keijun Nara) and Tokyo 2025 (Kotaro Uchikawa), with the 2028 edition announced for New York (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In Europe, the EMA has run the European Riichi Championship since 2008, the inaugural event in Hannover, with the 2024 edition in Valencia, Spain, on 5–8 December 2024 won by China's Junjie Sun (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; EMA, n.d.).
