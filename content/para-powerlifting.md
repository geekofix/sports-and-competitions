## Basic Information

Para powerlifting is the adapted form of powerlifting for athletes with eligible physical impairments (amputation, cerebral palsy, spinal cord injury, dwarfism and others); at the Paralympics only the bench press is contested, governed by World Para Powerlifting within the IPC (World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Athletes compete in ten bodyweight categories for each gender (men 49–107+ kg, women 41–86+ kg), take three attempts, and the best valid lift counts; since 2022 the sum of all good lifts is also awarded as a "total lift" medal, and since 2023 total medals and age-group events were added (World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The biennial World Championships were held in Cairo 2025, World Cups ran in Cairo (8 February 2026) and at the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games, and the 2026 Asia-Oceania Open in Bangkok (7–12 April) saw China's Wenjun Zhu lift 158 kg for an Asian record in +86 kg alongside Paralympic champions Zhe Cui (-41 kg) and Lingling Guo (-45 kg, equalling her 125 kg world record) (World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-a; 2026, April 13). Leading women today are therefore Lingling Guo and Zhe Cui of China; the picture shows Azerbaijan's national para powerlifting team in the gym (World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-c; 2026, April 13).

## Rules

Under World Para Powerlifting rules the lift is the bench press only: the lifter lies on the bench, receives the bar on extended arms, lowers it to the chest, holds it motionless ("pause"), then presses it back and racks it on the referee's commands; three attempts are allowed, announced in ascending order within the flight, and the heaviest successful lift wins the category (World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Eligibility requires one of the eight eligible impairment types verified by classification; men and women compete in separate weight categories; world and Paralympic records are recorded per category, and ties are broken by body weight (World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-c; World Para Powerlifting, n.d.-b).

## History

Weightlifting for athletes with disabilities has been part of the Paralympic programme since the 1980s and was rebranded Para powerlifting with the "World Para" name in 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The discipline's most famous athlete was Siamand Rahman of Iran (1988–2020), the "world's strongest Paralympian": he won gold at London 2012 and Rio 2016 and set the +107 kg world record at 310 kg in Rio before his death from cardiac arrest on 1 March 2020; he is pictured at Rio 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
