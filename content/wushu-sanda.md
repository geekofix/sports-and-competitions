## Basic Information

Wushu Sanda (Chinese kickboxing) is the full-contact combat sport of Chinese wushu in which two fighters on a raised platform (leitai) spar with punches, kicks, throws and knee techniques — combining kickboxing strikes with wrestling-style sweeping throws into a distinctive ruleset born from Chinese martial arts. Fighters wear protective gloves and, in amateur competition, body protectors and headgear.

Sanda is the fighting half of competitive wushu alongside taolu (forms), governed internationally by the International Wushu Federation with world championships contested by national teams alongside Asian Games presence.

## Rules

Bouts consist of two or three rounds of two minutes on an elevated platform in competition events, scoring points for clean striking technique (punches and kicks to legal targets), and higher points for throws that sweep or dump the opponent to the mat — throws earn the signature scoring emphasis of the sport. Prohibited techniques include elbow strikes in amateur rules, strikes to the back of the head and neck, and attacking a downed opponent; fighters can be penalised or disqualified for excessive contact, and pushing the opponent off the platform scores. Protective equipment requirements, weight categories and match structure follow IWUF competition rules across men's and women's divisions.

## History

Sanda developed in modern China as a sporting application of traditional martial arts, influenced by leitai challenge duels of earlier centuries; after 1949 the People's Republic formalised full-contact sparring into a sporting system, with wushu's national and then international structures introducing sanda competition. The International Wushu Federation (founded in 1990) staged world wushu championships with sanda categories from 1991, Asian Games included wushu in 1990 with sanda later added, and the sport now flourishes as China's combat staple, with crossover stars moving between sanda, kickboxing and mixed martial arts internationally.
