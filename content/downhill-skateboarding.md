## Basic Information

Downhill skateboarding is a gravity racing sport in which riders reach very high speeds (over 100 km/h, with world-class runs above 120 km/h) on longboards down closed mountain roads, wearing full leather suits, slide gloves and full-face helmets. It is governed by the International Downhill Federation (IDF) and also appears in the World Skate downhill program.

## Rules

Races are timed individual runs on a closed road course of several kilometers with an average gradient of about 6-8%, featuring hairpin turns. Riders start individually (typically 30-60 second intervals) in seeded order and the fastest time wins; the field is cut between heats to a final. Mandatory protective equipment at IDF and World Skate events includes a full-face helmet certified for downhill, a leather one-piece suit, slide gloves and a back protector. The board must pass a technical inspection (deck length, wheel size, trucks); pushing is limited to a maximum number of pushes after the start. Categories include open/men, women and junior; crashing or leaving the course results in no time for that run.

## History

Downhill skateboarding began in the 1970s in California and Hawaii, where skaters rode the steep roads of the islands at high speed; the first organized races date to the late 1970s. The sport evolved with urethane wheels, precision trucks and eventually full leather suits borrowed from motorcycle racing. The International Downhill Federation was founded to standardize rules and safety, and World Skate (the IOC-recognized roller sports federation) added downhill to its World Championships program. Iconic races include the Maryhill Festival of Speed in Washington State, the Kozakov Challenge in the Czech Republic and races in Brazil and Colombia; top riders such as Max Dubler and James Kelly have pushed speeds past 130 km/h.
