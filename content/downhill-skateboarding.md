## Basic Information

Downhill skateboarding is a timed racing discipline in which riders on longboards with large soft wheels descend paved mountain roads at speeds well above 100 km/h, wearing full-face helmets, leather suits and slide gloves (World Skate, n.d.). It is governed internationally by World Skate, the IOC-recognized federation for roller sports, which crowned the 2026 downhill skateboarding world champions at the World Skate Games in Asunción, Paraguay, 1\u20135 October 2026 on Cerro Lambaré, where time trials and knockout races in heats of four decided the titles (World Skate, n.d.-a). The parallel International Gravity Sports Association (IGSA) circuit \u2014 whose World Downhill Skateboarding Championship (WDSC) tour staged its 2026 season finale in Tamánico, El Salvador, with 125 riders from 21 nations \u2014 crowns its own world champions (IGSA, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current riders are the 2026 world champions of Asunción in the women's and men's categories (World Skate, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Downhill skateboarding is run under the World Skate downhill regulations and the IGSA rulebook (World Skate, n.d.-b; IGSA, n.d.). Riders complete a timed qualifying run, and the fastest qualifiers advance to knockout races in heats of four, with the top two of each heat progressing until the final (World Skate, n.d.-a). Courses are closed public roads of 2\u20135 km with an average gradient of 5\u20138 percent, lined with hay bales, tire walls and air fences (IGSA, n.d.). Mandatory protective gear includes a full-face helmet certified for downhill use, a leather or reinforced suit, slide gloves with plastic pucks, and closed-toe shoes; riders must pass a gear check before starting (IGSA, n.d.). The clock starts at the start gate and stops at the finish line; the fastest time wins, and ties are broken by the qualifying run (World Skate, n.d.-b). Men's and women's categories race the same courses with the same rules and equipment standards; junior categories exist for riders under 18 (World Skate, n.d.-b; IGSA, n.d.).

## History

Downhill skateboarding began in the 1960s and 1970s in Southern California, when surfers and skateboarders \u2014 the "sidewalk surfing" scene around Laguna Beach \u2014 strapped clay wheels to boards and raced down roads such as Gunsight Mountain, now Signal Hill (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first organized races ran in the mid-1970s, the International Gravity Sports Association (IGSA) was founded to govern the sport, and the discipline grew through the 1980s and 1990s with legendary riders such as Cliff Coleman and later Mischo Erban, who set the world speed record above 130 km/h in 2014 (IGSA, n.d.). Since 2019, World Skate has run official downhill world championships as part of the World Skate Games, adding street luge and inline downhill to the program; the 2026 edition in Asunción crowned the new world champions on Cerro Lambaré in October 2026 (World Skate, n.d.-a). The women's downhill scene has grown steadily, with dedicated women's titles at both World Skate and IGSA events since the 1990s (IGSA, n.d.).
