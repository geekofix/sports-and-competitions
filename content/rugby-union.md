## Basic Information

Rugby union is a close-contact team sport played fifteen-a-side between two teams of fifteen players, who compete for an oval ball on a rectangular pitch by running, passing backwards, kicking and contesting possession in scrums, lineouts, rucks and mauls. Its roots lie at Rugby School in Warwickshire, England — where pupils first codified their football rules in 1845 — and across the British Empire in the 19th century; today it is governed worldwide by World Rugby, whose premier event is the quadrennial Rugby World Cup, first staged in 1987. A try is worth five points, and games are played in two 40-minute halves.

## Rules

The fundamental laws, published as World Rugby's Laws of the Game, require the ball to be passed laterally or backwards and forbid blocking; the ball advances by carrying, kicking, or rolling it back from the tackle area. A try (grounding the ball in the opponent's in-goal area) scores five points and affords a conversion attempt worth two; penalty goals and drop goals are worth three. The set-piece scrum (eight forwards per side) and lineout (a throw-in contested in the air) restart play, while rucks (ball on the ground, contested by players on their feet) and mauls (ball-carrier held up) form the game's dynamic possession contests. Kicking directly into touch from inside one's own 22 retains the throw; from outside it, the lineout is taken where the kick was made. Matches last eighty minutes plus stoppage time, a match-day squad of twenty-three includes eight replacements, and cards — yellow (ten-minute suspension) and red (sending-off) — govern discipline.

## History

The game's origin legend credits pupil William Webb Ellis with picking up the ball and running during a football match at Rugby School in 1823 — a story World Rugby itself treats as cherished legend rather than fact — but the documented milestones are the school's first written rules of 1845, Blackheath FC's departure from the nascent Football Association in 1863 and the formation of the Rugby Football Union in 1871, the year of the first international between England and Scotland. The 1895 schism saw the northern clubs break away to form rugby league, leaving union strictly amateur for a century — the IRFB (founded 1886 by the home unions) opened the game to professionalism in August 1995. The first World Cup followed in 1987 in New Zealand, won by the host All Blacks; South Africa lead the roll of honour with four titles (1995, 2007, 2019 and 2023) against New Zealand's three, and the tournament expands to 24 teams for Australia 2027. The professional era produced trans-national club competitions such as the Heineken Cup/Champions Cup in Europe and Super Rugby in the southern hemisphere, while annual tests such as the Six Nations and the Rugby Championship structure the international calendar; union returned to the Olympics through sevens in 2016 and counts some ten million players across more than a hundred member unions.
