## Basic Information

Airsoft is a team tactical sport in which players eliminate each other with spherical 6 mm plastic BBs fired from replica firearms that shoot below 1 joule (or the local legal limit) at muzzle energy; hits are called by the hit player on the honour system in most game formats (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the recreational combat-simulation family alongside paintball and laser tag.

Game formats range from short skirmishes (team deathmatch, capture the flag, bomb/defuse scenarios) to multi-day military simulation (MILSim) events with realistic rules, medics and night play (Wikipedia, n.d.). Fields are dedicated airsoft sites or woodland areas with safety zones; full-seal goggles or mesh masks are mandatory, and barrel socks and minimum engagement distances are standard safety rules (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Replica guns (AEG electric, gas blowback, spring sniper rifles) must look like real firearms in many jurisdictions and are often required to be marked, e.g. with an orange tip in the US (Wikipedia, n.d.). Velocity limits are chronographed at the field: typically 350–500 fps for rifles, lower for pistols, with sniper rifles allowed higher FPS but much larger minimum engagement distances (Wikipedia, n.d.). There is no single world federation; national associations and event organizers (e.g. in the UK, EU and US) set field rules, and international tournaments are organized by leagues and event companies (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women play together in mixed teams.

## History

Airsoft was developed in Japan in the late 1970s — Tokyo Marui released the first practical gas-powered replica in 1986 — because real firearms were banned; the sport spread to East Asia, Europe and North America in the 1990s and 2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today it is played in more than 60 countries with millions of players, and competitive airsoft (speedsoft leagues, IPSC-style practical airsoft) has emerged alongside recreational play (Wikipedia, n.d.).
