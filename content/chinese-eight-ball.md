## Basic Information

Chinese eight-ball — played under the name *heyball* — is the Chinese variant of eight-ball pool governed as an official discipline by the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA), the world governing body of pool (WPA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The game is hugely popular in China, where more than one million heyball tables are in use, and the WPA has held an annual Heyball World Championship since 2015, the first edition in Yushan, Jiangxi (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2026 WPA Heyball World Championship took place from 12 to 15 August 2026 in Pune, India, at the Rajan Khinvasara National Academy / Jairaj Sports & Convention Center: England's Gareth Potts defended the men's title, defeating compatriot Jordan Shepherd, while England's Kelly Fisher defended the women's title, defeating Waratthanun Sukritthanes of Thailand; Potts and Fisher had also won in Brisbane 2025 (WPA, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026 the leading players are therefore, first on the women's side, Kelly Fisher, the reigning back-to-back women's world champion, followed by Waratthanun Sukritthanes; on the men's side, Gareth Potts, the 2025 and 2026 men's world champion, ahead of Jordan Shepherd (WPA, 2026). The 2026 season also included the Joy Cup World Heyball Masters Grand Finals in Qinhuangdao, China, in March 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Heyball is played under the WPA rules of eight-ball adapted to the Chinese table: the table is a 9 ft pool table with a playing surface of 2540 × 1260 mm and rounded, snooker-style pockets with a mouth of about 76–82 mm, and the balls are 57.2 mm in diameter (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). As in eight-ball, the object ball group is divided into solids (1–7) and stripes (9–15), the 8-ball is black and must be pocketed last; a player who pockets the 8-ball early loses the frame, and fouls — including failing to hit the correct group first, scratching, or pocketing the cue ball — give the opponent ball-in-hand behind the baulk line or anywhere, depending on the WPA rulebook version in force (WPA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Matches are played as best-of-frames contests at WPA-sanctioned events, with men's and women's divisions contested separately at the world championship — the gender difference is organizational only; the table, balls and rules are identical (WPA, 2026). Age categories follow the WPA junior and senior classifications (under 23 / 40+). The official rule source is the WPA rulebook published by the world governing body at wpa-pool.com, which lists heyball among its official disciplines (WPA, n.d.).


## History

Heyball emerged in China in the late 20th century as a local adaptation of American eight-ball played on snooker-sized tables with tighter pockets, and it grew into a mass-participation game played in bars and clubs across China — estimates run to more than a million tables (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The World Pool-Billiard Association, founded in 1965 and headquartered in the United States, recognized heyball as an official discipline and began sanctioning an annual world championship in 2015, first held in Yushan, Jiangxi province, China (WPA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Early editions were dominated by Chinese players; the 2018 world championship in China and the 2023 edition marked the rise of European professionals, and since 2023 England's Gareth Potts and Kelly Fisher have held the men's and women's titles — Potts winning in 2023, 2025 (Brisbane) and 2026 (Pune), Fisher winning the women's titles in 2025 and 2026 (WPA, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The commercial boom of Chinese cue sports in the 2010s — with televised Heyball Masters events such as the 2026 Joy Cup Grand Finals in Qinhuangdao — turned the game into a professional sport with ranking points, while its rules remained close to eight-ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
