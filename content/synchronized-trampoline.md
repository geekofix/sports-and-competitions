## Basic Information

Synchronized trampoline is the paired discipline of trampoline gymnastics: two athletes perform the same routine simultaneously on adjacent trampolines, judged on both execution and mirror-image timing. A spectacle discipline alongside individual trampoline at national and world championships, it rewards two gymnasts flying in perfect unison ten metres above the mat.

## Rules

Each pair performs a compulsory and a voluntary routine of ten elements "+E+" somersaults and twists in defined shapes and difficulty "+E+" bounced side by side on two identical trampolines, with two sets of judges at work: execution judges deduct for form faults (separation of legs, travel toward the frame, poor landing position) exactly as in individual trampoline, while additional synchronization judges score how closely the two athletes match, reflecting the moment their feet hit the bed at the same time on every skill. Difficulty is credited by the routine's elements, multiplied in a scoring system that mirrors the FIG's trampoline code; routines must end with an upright, held landing, and interrupting a routine by touching the frame or spotting the bed destroys the value of the remaining elements.

## History

Trampoline gymnastics was invented by George Nissen in the 1930s, developed as a training and show device before becoming a competitive sport after the Second World War and reaching world championships under the International Trampoline Federation (FIT) from 1964; synchronized competition appeared early on the world stage, giving the world code of the sport a dual test of skill and teamwork. When trampoline joined the FIG in 1999 and made its individual Olympic debut at Sydney 2000, synchro remained a world-championship discipline, and today FIG world championships crown men's, women's and mixed pairs, with the discipline's most famous stage the World Age Group and World Championship finals where pairs bounce within centimetres of identical timing.
