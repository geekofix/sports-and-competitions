## Basic Information

Beach cricket is a version of cricket played on sand or compacted turf with fewer players and simplified rules — usually a social game, but with occasional serious tournaments (TopEndSports, n.d.). Because the bounce on sand is inconsistent, bowling is often restricted to full tosses (TopEndSports, n.d.). Teams are typically smaller than eleven — six-a-side co-ed teams playing five-over matches are a common format, as at the Beach Cricket Blast 2026 at Carlisle Bay, Barbados (CrickWick, n.d.). The game is especially popular in the Caribbean and in Indian Ocean islands such as Mauritius, Réunion, Seychelles and Madagascar, where matches are played on beaches under palm trees (CrickWick, n.d.). A professional beach cricket tournament was organized in Australia in the 2006–07 season featuring Australia, England and the West Indies, and England won the final over Australia (CrickWick, n.d.).

Beach cricket keeps the core of the Laws of Cricket — batting, bowling, fielding, wickets and runs — while shrinking the field: boundaries are small, often marked by cones or ropes, and games last a few overs per side rather than a full day (TopEndSports, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Fielding in sand is slower and diving saves are rare, so running between wickets matters more, and full-toss bowling is the norm (TopEndSports, n.d.). Mixed-gender and family teams are the standard, and the game is played barefoot on the sand (CrickWick, n.d.).

## Rules

There is no single worldwide rulebook for beach cricket; tournaments adapt the Laws of Cricket to the beach (Wikipedia, n.d.). Common adaptations: teams of four to eight players (six-a-side is typical), four to ten overs per innings, smaller boundaries set by the organizer, full-toss bowling only, and relaxed fielding restrictions (TopEndSports, n.d.; CrickWick, n.d.). Batting, scoring and dismissals (bowled, caught, run out, LBW where applied) follow the Laws of Cricket (Wikipedia, n.d.). Co-ed teams with a minimum number of women per side are required at many events, and men’s, women’s and mixed divisions exist at larger tournaments (CrickWick, n.d.). Safety rules on the beach — clear space around the batter — are left to organizers (TopEndSports, n.d.).

## History

Cricket has been played informally on beaches for as long as the game has existed in coastal countries, but the first professional beach cricket tournament was staged in Australia in the 2006–07 season, with Australia, England and the West Indies competing and England defeating Australia in the final (CrickWick, n.d.). The game is deeply rooted in Caribbean and Indian Ocean island culture, where beach matches under palm trees are a staple of holidays (CrickWick, n.d.). More recently, branded events such as the Beach Cricket Blast at Carlisle Bay, Barbados (2026), with co-ed six-a-side teams in five-over matches, have brought stadium-style production — commentary, music and creator squads — to the sand (CrickWick, n.d.).
