## Basic Information

Weight pulling is a dog sport in which a harnessed dog pulls a loaded cart, rail trolley or sled along a short course — typically 16 feet (4.9 m) — within a time limit of usually 60 seconds, and is ranked by the percentage of its own body weight pulled (International Weight Pull Association, n.d.). The sport celebrates the freighting heritage of working dogs, from Arctic sled teams to farm dogs, and because scoring is percentage-based a small dog can out-pull a large one; dogs compete in weight classes, and titles are sanctioned by bodies such as the International Weight Pull Association (IWPA), founded in 1984 in the United States, and by breed and sled-dog clubs such as the Interior (Alaska) Freight Dog Association (Wikipedia, n.d.). Handlers of both sexes compete — women handlers and their dogs take part at every level of the sport.

## Rules

The dog wears an approved pulling harness clipped to a line attached to the load; on the handler's command it must pull the weight the full course so that the nose of the load crosses the finish line within 60 seconds (International Weight Pull Association, n.d.). The handler stands ahead of the dog and may encourage only with voice and gesture — touching the dog, or using bait such as food or toys, voids the pull, as do leaving the chute early or crossing the sidelines. Dogs are weighed before competing and placed in weight classes, and placements are awarded by the greatest percentage of body weight pulled, with titles for set percentage thresholds; formats exist on dirt, snow and rail systems, and wheeled carts are standard indoors (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Weight pulling grew out of the working heritage of freighting dogs in the Arctic and on North American farms, where teams hauled mail, supplies and firewood by sledge and cart. Organised weight-pull contests were held alongside sled-dog races from the mid-twentieth century, and in 1984 enthusiasts in the United States founded the International Weight Pull Association to standardise rules, sanction contests and keep official records (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport has since spread internationally — for example to the Tanana Valley State Fair in Fairbanks, Alaska, where freight-dog weight pulls are a fixture — with clubs in several countries running events on snow, dirt and rail under IWPA-style rules (International Weight Pull Association, n.d.).
