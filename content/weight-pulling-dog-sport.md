## Basic Information

Weight pulling (dog sport) is a canine sport in which dogs, fitted with a specially designed harness, pull a loaded cart or sled along a short course — typically 16 feet on natural ground or a rail system — against the clock or by percentage of body weight pulled. Handicapped by weight classes, the sport celebrates the pulling instinct shared by breeds from Siberian Huskies to American Pit Bull Terriers and lets dogs of every size compete on equal terms.

Events are run by clubs such as the International Weight Pull Association in North America and by sled-dog and breed-club organisations elsewhere, with titles awarded for dogs that meet percentage-based pulls.

## Rules

The dog wears an approved pulling harness attached by line to a wheeled cart, rail trolley or snow sled loaded with a set weight. On the handler's command the dog pulls the load the full course distance (usually 16 feet) within a time limit, typically 60 seconds, and a pull is valid once the nose of the load crosses the finish line. Handlers may not touch the dog or use bait such as food or toys, only voice and gesture encouragement from ahead. Dogs compete in weight classes so that achievement is measured by percentage of body weight pulled rather than absolute distance, and the heaviest successful percentage pulls earn placements; fouls, crossed lines or leaving the chute early lead to disqualification of the attempt.

## History

Weight pulling grew out of the working heritage of freighting dogs in the Arctic and on American farms, where teams hauled mail and supplies by sledge. In the twentieth century enthusiasts formalised the practice into a sport: organised weight pull contests were held alongside sled-dog races from the mid-1900s, and the International Weight Pull Association was founded in 1984 in the United States to standardise rules, sanction contests and keep records. The sport has since spread internationally, including European clubs running events in several countries under rules adapted to snow, dirt and rail formats.
