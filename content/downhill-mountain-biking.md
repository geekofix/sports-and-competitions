## Basic Information

Downhill mountain biking (DH) is a timed gravity discipline in which riders race down steep, technical courses on full-suspension mountain bikes, navigating jumps, rock gardens and berms. It is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) and is the flagship event of the UCI Mountain Bike World Cup and World Championships, and has been in the Olympic program since Atlanta 1996.

## Rules

At UCI World Cup and World Championship events, each rider gets a single timed run down the course (two runs at some World Cups, best time counts). The fastest time wins; ties are broken by the rider's qualifying time. Courses are typically 2.5-4 minutes long, with a mix of pedaling sections and bike-park-style jumps, and must be ridden within the taped course. Riders must wear full-face helmets, body armor, goggles and a full-finger glove; bikes must pass a technical inspection (minimum weight 15 kg at World Cups, working brakes). The field starts in seeded order (reversed from qualifying) with at least 30-second intervals, and missed gates or leaving the course result in disqualification. Elite categories are divided by age and gender, with separate junior and U23 classes.

## History

Downhill racing emerged in the 1990s in the US and France as mountain bikers rode lift-served trails at speed; the first UCI Mountain Bike World Championships with downhill were held in 1990 in Il Ciocco, Italy. The sport grew rapidly with dedicated downhill bikes and full protective gear, becoming one of the most spectacular Olympic disciplines at Atlanta 1996. Legends include Nicolas Vouilloz and Anne-Caroline Chausson (first Olympic champions), Steve Peat, Greg Minnaar (three-time world champion) and Rachel Atherton (most successful female DH racer with multiple World Cup overall and World Championship titles). The UCI Mountain Bike World Championships also include enduro and cross-country as separate disciplines.
