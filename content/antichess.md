## Basic Information

Antichess (suicide chess) inverts the goal: you must lose all your pieces to win. Captures are compulsory whenever possible, which leads to wild, forced sequences. It is a standard variant on lichess and chess.com with its own ratings. Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Losing Chess](losing-chess.html), [Crazyhouse](crazyhouse.html), [Chess](chess.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Lichess, n.d.).

## Rules

Normal chess movement, but capturing is mandatory if any capture is available; the winner is the player who first has no pieces left (or is stalemated with no legal moves). Kings are ordinary pieces (they can be captured). Pawns promote to any piece including the king— no: to queen/rook/bishop/knight as usual (Lichess, n.d.).

## History

Suicide chess rules were codified in the 19th century (the 'giveaway' game); the variant gained its modern name and online popularity in the 2000s. World championships are held (e.g. by the World Antichess Championship organizers) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
