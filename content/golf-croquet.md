## Basic Information

Golf croquet is a variant of association croquet in which players hit balls through a course of hoops set on a lawn (typically two circuits of six hoops) in the fewest strokes, with bonus points for hitting the central peg (the "rover peg"). It is governed by the World Croquet Federation (WCF), founded in 1997 (successor to the Croquet Association), which runs the World Golf Croquet Championship, and by the Croquet Association of England (1887) and the US Croquet Association.

## Rules

Under the WCF Golf Croquet Rules, each side (singles or doubles) plays two balls through two circuits of six hoops; a player scores a hoop by passing through it in the correct direction, and earns a bonus when both balls of a side are "in sync" and a player hits the central peg with the correct ball, starting the next circuit. Turns pass between sides after each stroke (unlike association croquet's multiple-shot turns), making it a fast game. The game ends when a side completes the second circuit and hits the peg; the higher score wins; each circuit is worth 13 points (12 hoops plus one peg point), for a maximum of 26. Matches at championships are played under a time limit.

## History

Golf croquet was devised in Ireland in the 1850s, popularized in the 1860s-70s and standardized in the 1880s-90s, spreading to England and the British Empire. The Croquet Association of England was founded in 1897. The World Croquet Federation, founded in 1997, unites national associations from over 30 countries and runs the biennial World Golf Croquet Championship. Golf croquet is the faster, more attacking variant compared to the tactical association croquet, and is popular in the UK, Australia, New Zealand, the US, South Africa and Egypt.
