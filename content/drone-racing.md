## Basic Information

Drone racing (FPV racing) is a motorsport in which pilots fly small quadcopter drones through three-dimensional courses of gates, flags and tunnels at speeds well above 200 km/h, steering by the live video feed of an onboard camera shown in FPV goggles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The world governing body for air sports, the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI), sanctions FPV drone racing in its F9U class and runs the FAI Drone Racing World Cup and the FAI World Drone Racing Championship, while grassroots and professional leagues such as MultiGP, the Drone Racing League (DRL) and the Drone Champions League (DCL) organize events worldwide (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale [FAI], n.d.-a; MultiGP, n.d.). In 2026 the FAI Drone Racing World Cup stop in Macao ran 5–8 November 2026, the MultiGP Championship took place in Dallas, 15–17 October 2026, and the 2026 FAI World Drone Racing Championship is scheduled for Guangzhou, China, 9–12 December 2026 (FAI, n.d.-b; MultiGP, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current pilots are — in women's racing — Italy's Luisa Rizzo, the 2026 Italian FAI F9U PRO champion with a perfect five wins from five starts, pictured below, and — in men's racing — the elite F9U field headed toward the 2026 FAI world title in Guangzhou (FAI, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Drone racing is governed internationally by the FAI Sporting Code for the F9U FPV racing class, which sets aircraft specifications, video-transmitter power limits, weight and failsafe requirements, and runs the World Cup and World Championship (FAI, n.d.-a). Racing drones are typically 5-inch quadcopters built for speed and crash resistance; each league defines race classes by frame size and power, and MultiGP additionally publishes standardized courses so that times flown worldwide can be compared on a global leaderboard ("The List") (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; MultiGP, n.d.). Races are flown in heats of up to four drones, with electronic timing; the winner is the fastest around the course or the first past the post, and in bracket formats the heat winners advance through elimination rounds (FAI, n.d.-a). Safety rules require failsafe disarm (instant motor stop on signal loss), designated spotters and flight within the approved airspace (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Drone Racing League, by contrast, supplies all pilots with identical in-house-built spec drones and backup aircraft to create a level playing field, while MultiGP pilots fly their own machines in PRO and SPORT classes (Drone Racing League, n.d.; MultiGP, n.d.). Men and women compete together in the same classes; FAI championships have included women's and junior entries since the first edition in 2018 (FAI, n.d.-a).

## History

FPV (first-person-view) drone racing grew out of the hobbyist FPV community and began as an amateur competitive sport around 2014 in Australia (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first major United States event was the 2015 Fat Shark US National Drone Racing Championships at the California State Fair, won by Australia's Chad Nowak with more than 100 pilots entered (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two organizations then shaped the professional era: MultiGP was founded by Chris Thomas in February 2015 in Palm Bay, Florida, and grew into the world's largest chapter-based racing league with a national championship from 2015, while the Drone Racing League was founded in 2015 by Nicholas Horbaczewski, former chief revenue officer of Tough Mudder, launched publicly in January 2016 and began racing in 2016 (MultiGP, n.d.; Drone Racing League, n.d.). The FAI, founded in 1905 as the world air sports federation, integrated the sport and held the first FAI World Drone Racing Championships in Shenzhen, China, 1–4 November 2018, with 128 competitors — including 13 women — from 34 countries (FAI, n.d.-a). Women have been part of the sport since these first championships and now compete at every level, from local chapters to the FAI World Championship (FAI, n.d.-a).
