## Basic Information

Nine-pin bowling (also called kegeln or kegel) is a pin bowling game played mainly in Europe, in which a ball without finger holes is rolled at nine pins arranged in a diamond formation at the end of a lane. It is governed internationally by the World Ninepin Bowling Association (WNBA, world-ninepins.org), a disciplinary association within World Bowling, with about 250,000 members in 28 countries across Europe, South America and Asia. It differs from [ten-pin bowling](ten-pin-bowling.html) and general [bowling](bowling.html) in pin count, ball and lane types.

## Rules

The nine pins stand in a diamond (rows of 1-2-3-2-1) at the end of the lane, with the center pin usually marked in a different color. Players roll a wooden or synthetic ball (no finger holes in sanctioned play; novice balls may have two holes) and score one point per pin knocked down, with bonuses for clearing all nine (a "ringer"). A full game consists of 120 throws across four lane passes (30 throws per lane), and the player or team with the highest pin total wins; top players average 7-8 points per throw, with official world records (recognized only at WNBA world championship events) above 700. There are three lane systems with different surfaces — Classic (wide, flat), Bohle (narrow, concave) and Schere (scissors-shaped) — each administered by its own WNBA section (NBC, NBS, NBN) with slightly different rules and records.

## History

Nine-pin bowling has medieval roots in Germany and was depicted in art as early as 1834; German immigrants brought it to the United States, where Texas famously taxed ninepin alleys instead of banning them in 1837. In Europe it developed into a high-performance team sport, with over 90,000 registered league players in Germany alone playing on official Bundeskegelbahnen. The Fédération Internationale des Quilleurs (FIQ) was founded in Hamburg in 1952 to govern both tenpin and ninepin bowling; its ninepin section became the World Ninepin Bowling Association (WNBA), which merged with the tenpin body into World Bowling in 2015. World Championships for national teams and singles, European championships and Champions League events are held regularly, and nine-pin was a demonstration sport at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham.
