## Basic Information

Running target — renamed "moving target" in the ISSF rulebook from 2025 — is an ISSF shooting event in which a target is pulled across a 2-metre-wide aisle at the firing line and the shooter must hit it while it moves (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the rifle/pistol target-shooting family of the ISSF and was an Olympic event until 2004 (10 m) and 1992 (50 m) (Wikipedia, n.d.).

*Figure: pxfuel. (Unknown date). ISSF running target (moving target) being engaged at an indoor range [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Running_target_shooting.jpg*

In the 10 m event a single-shot .177 air rifle is used and the target is visible for 5 seconds (slow) or 2.5 seconds (fast) per run, with 20 shots in qualification and a final for the top shooters (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 50 m event uses .22 LR and is visible for 5 or 2.5 seconds over the 2 m aisle at 50 m (Wikipedia, n.d.). Scoring uses the target’s rings, with the centre worth 10.9 in decimals; mixed team events were added to the ISSF program in the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The discipline is governed by the ISSF and its 2026 rulebook; it is no longer on the Olympic program but remains a World Cup and World Championship event, with separate world records for men, women and mixed teams (ISSF, n.d.). The women’s 50 m world record is 573, set by Ukraine’s Valentyna Honcharova at the 2024 European Running Target Championships (Wikipedia, n.d.). Polish shooter Łukasz Czapla, a four-time world champion and former world-record holder in the mixed event, is one of the discipline’s best-known athletes (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate and mixed events; junior categories exist at World Cup level.

## History

Running target shooting developed from the "running deer" and "running boar" single-shot events of the late 19th century — Walter Winans won Olympic gold for the running deer at the 1908 London Games and again in 1912 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The ISSF standardized the moving target in the 1950s, the 50 m event entered the Olympic program in 1972 (Mexico) and the 10 m air-rifle event in 1984 (Los Angeles); both were dropped from the Olympic program after 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Großschießsportanlage in Suhl, Germany, shown in the historical photo, was one of the great running-target venues of the era.
