## Basic Information

Ear weight is a WEIO strength event: a heavy weight (traditionally up to several kilograms, modern standardized weights) is suspended from a loop behind the ear, and the athlete holds it for as long as possible; longest time wins (WEIO, n.d.). At the 2026 WEIO Frank M. Lane (Kotzebue) held 1,012 lb 4.5 oz equivalent record mark in the men's event (ICT News, 2026). Contested by men and women. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The weight hangs from an ear loop; the athlete stands upright and the time stops when the loop slips or the weight drops. Standard weights per category; best of attempts (WEIO, n.d.).

## History

Ear weight grew from traditional tests of endurance at WEIO, which began in 1961; it remains one of the most spectacular WEIO events (Wikipedia, n.d.).
