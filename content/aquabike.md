## Basic Information

Aquabike is the official term for jet ski racing: personal watercraft competing in closed-circuit and offshore races, in runabout (seated) and ski (stand-up) classes with sprint distances from 200 to 1500 metres plus freestyle. Governed by the UIM (Union Internationale Motonautique), the sport holds world and continental championships with national series beneath them.

The machines reach planing speeds within seconds and race close to shore, making aquabike one of the most spectacular water motorsports.

## Rules

UIM competition classes define runabout and ski craft by engine specification and rider posture, with events over marked courses combining buoys, starts and finishes under world championship racing rules: heats, finals and points toward titles. Closed-course events demand buoy rounding in order, with penalties for cutting; jumping and wake riding feature in offshore endurance events, while freestyle runs of about two minutes are judged for tricks such as bar rolls, backflips and superman moves. Riders must wear approved flotation and safety gear, with flags and medical boats officiating.

## History

Racing personal watercraft grew rapidly in the 1980s after mass-produced sit-down and stand-up machines changed water recreation, with manufacturer-backed racing series forming in the United States and Europe. The UIM brought the discipline into its world championship structure in the 1990s and today sanctions the Aquabike World Championship with rounds in Europe, the Middle East and beyond, runabout and ski classes each crowning world champions, and the sport remains a fixture of maritime festivals worldwide.
