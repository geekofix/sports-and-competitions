## Basic Information

Pelota Tarasca (Purépecha: uárhukua ch'anakua, "game of the burning ball") is the stick-ball game of the Tarascan/Purépecha people of Michoacán, Mexico: two teams use curved oak sticks (uárhukua) to hit a ball — traditionally a burning ball soaked in oil in the fire variant — around a marked field, a game possibly 3,000 years old and resembling field hockey. It is played at Purépecha festivals (e.g. in Tzintzuntzan) and was shown at Mexico's traditional-games revivals; the Mexican Federation of Indigenous and Traditional Games promotes it. Men and women play in mixed teams at festivals. Within the pelota, wall-ball and handball games on this site, its closest relatives are [Pelota Mixteca](pelota-mixteca.html), [Field Hockey](field-hockey.html), [Basque Pelota](basque-pelota.html), [Pitz](pitz.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Medium — Piercey, L, n.d.).

## Rules

Played on a marked field (pasarutakua) with two teams of 5–7; players use the curved oak stick to hit the ball (wooden or, in the fire variant, oil-soaked and burning) and score by getting it past the opponent's goal line or into a marked zone. Local rules vary by town; festival games are usually timed halves. Safety rules govern the burning-ball variant (water buckets, designated thrower). Modern tournaments use a wooden ball (Medium — Piercey, L, n.d.).

## History

The Purépecha (Tarascan) empire's warriors played the game; it may date back 3,000 years (Mexican Federation of Indigenous and Traditional Games). It survives in Michoacán villages, especially around Lake Pátzcuaro; the flaming-ball variant ("pelota de fuego") is a festival spectacle. It was demonstrated at Mexico City's revival of traditional games, which announced a $300,000 traditional-sports centre with courts for pelota mixteca and tarasca (Wikipedia, n.d.).
