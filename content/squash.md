## Basic Information

Squash is a fast indoor racket sport played by two players (singles) or four (doubles) in a walled court of 9.75 by 6.4 metres, hitting a small hollow rubber ball against the front wall so that it stays in play until it bounces twice; it is governed worldwide by the World Squash Federation (WSF, founded 1947 as the International Squash Rackets Federation) and professionally by the PSA World Tour, which runs the World Championships for men (since 1976) and women (since 1976) (World Squash Federation, n.d.; PSA World Tour, n.d.). Squash will make its Olympic debut at Los Angeles 2028 (World Squash Federation, n.d.). The 2025 Women's World Championship in Chicago, 9–18 May 2025, was won by Egypt's Nour El Sherbini, her eighth world title, equaling Nicol David's record, after she beat compatriot Hania El Hammamy in the final; the 2026 women's title went to 18-year-old Amina Orfi, who defeated El Sherbini, while Egypt's Mostafa Asal won the 2026 men's title, his second in a row (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Squash sits in the walled-court branch of the racket family — [Rackets](rackets.html), [Racquetball](racquetball.html), [Hardball Squash](hardball-squash.html), [Squash Tennis](squash-tennis.html) — next to the net games [Tennis](tennis.html), [Table Tennis](table-tennis.html), [Badminton](badminton.html) and the paddle games [Padel](padel.html), [Pickleball](pickleball.html); the full list is under Important Links.

## Rules

The court measures 9.75 m long by 6.4 m wide, with a front wall out line 4.57 m high, a tin 43 cm (17 in) high at the front, a service line 1.78 m from the front wall and a short line dividing the service boxes; the ball is about 40 mm in diameter and must be warmed up before play (World Squash Federation, n.d.). Singles is point-a-rally to 11, and a player must win by two points (at 10–10 the game continues until a two-point lead); doubles is played by four players on the same court with the same scoring. The server must strike the ball so it hits the front wall above the tin and below the out line and lands in the opposite service box, with one foot in the service box; a let is played when a shot is obstructed, and a stroke (awarded when the opponent's shot would have been good but for interference) is the standard remedy for hindrance (World Squash Federation, n.d.). Match formats are best-of-five games at world tour and world championship level, with the PSA World Tour ranking determining seedings and the year-end finals; men and women play under identical rules on the same courts, with separate professional tours and world championships, and junior (under-19) and masters age categories exist at national level (PSA World Tour, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Squash developed in the early 19th century at Harrow School in England, where boys hit a ball against the walls of confined courtyards, and the first dedicated squash courts were built at Harrow in 1864; the International Squash Rackets Federation was founded in 1947 and is today the World Squash Federation (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the sport now retired include Pakistan's Jahangir Khan, who won six world titles and ten British Open titles and went 555 competitive matches unbeaten between 1981 and 1986; his compatriot Jansher Khan, eight-time world champion; New Zealand's Susan Devoy, who dominated the women's game in the 1980s; Australia's Heather McKay, unbeaten in international competition from 1962 to 1981 with 16 British Open titles; and Malaysia's Nicol David, eight-time world champion between 2005 and 2014 who retired in 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
