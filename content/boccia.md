## Basic Information

Boccia is a Paralympic ball sport for athletes with severe locomotor disabilities (cerebral palsy and other conditions), played indoors on a 12.5 m × 6 m court in which players throw, kick or — in the BC3 class — use a ramp to deliver leather balls as close as possible to the white jack ball. It is governed by World Boccia (World Boccia, n.d.-a), which took over from BISFed on January 1, 2013. At the Paris 2024 Paralympics (Arena Paris Sud, August 29–September 5, 2024), 124 slots across 11 events produced eight gold-medal nations, with Hong Kong leading the medal table with three golds (World Boccia, n.d.-b).

Among the current stars, Kang Sun-hee of South Korea won BC2 gold in Paris and Aurélien Aubert of France took BC4 gold on home soil; Britain's David Smith, with five Paralympic medals including three golds (team 2008, individual BC1 2016 and 2020), finished fourth individually and fifth with the team in Paris 2024 (World Boccia, n.d.-b). The women's side is equally strong: Kang Sun-hee is the reigning BC2 Paralympic champion, and women hold gold in individual and team classes at every world championship (World Boccia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

A boccia match is played on a 12.5 m × 6 m court between two sides (individual, pairs or teams of three), each with six red or blue balls, plus the white jack; the jack is thrown into the valid service area first, then players alternate delivering their balls by throwing, kicking or rolling, or — in BC3 — sliding them down an inclined ramp operated by an assistant (World Boccia, n.d.-a). Each "end" is complete when all balls are played; the side with the ball closest to the jack scores one point per ball closer than the opponent's best, and matches are best of four ends (six in team/pairs ties at some levels). Classification determines how the ball is delivered: BC1 (throw/kick with hands or feet), BC2 (throw), BC3 (ramp, with assistant), BC4 (throw, for other neurological conditions); BC1–BC3 athletes may also use a ramp if needed. Men's and women's events use identical rules, equipment and court; mixed team events exist, and youth categories use shorter ends (World Boccia, n.d.-a).

## History

Boccia originated in ancient Greece and Rome as a precision ball game and was revived as a sport for people with cerebral palsy in Scandinavia in the 1970s; it joined the Paralympic programme at Seoul 1988 as an open event and has been medal sport since Barcelona 1992 (World Boccia, n.d.-b). BISFed (the International Sports Federation for Persons with a Disability) governed it until World Boccia was founded on January 1, 2013. Britain's David Smith became the most decorated British boccia player with three Paralympic golds; women's boccia grew rapidly from the 2000s, and Paris 2024 marked the first Games with women's individual gold in multiple classes, including Kang Sun-hee (BC2) and Aurélien Aubert (BC4) for France (World Boccia, n.d.-b).
