## Basic Information

Boccia is a precision ball sport similar to bocce, played by athletes with severe physical disabilities — originally designed for players with cerebral palsy. It is governed by World Boccia (formerly BISFed, the Boccia International Sports Federation), has been a Paralympic sport since 1984, and is one of only two Paralympic sports with no Olympic counterpart. Events are played in sport classes BC1-BC4.

## Rules

Under World Boccia (BISFed) International Boccia Rules, players throw, roll or kick their balls (leather balls) from a throwing box toward the white jack ball on a 12.5 x 6 m court. In individual events, each athlete has six balls per end; the player whose ball is closest to the jack scores one point per ball closer than the opponent's best. Matches at the Paralympics and World Championships are best of four ends (BC3 players use a ramp). In pairs and team events (BC1/BC2), assistants may help position ramps but must not see the court. Classification BC1-BC4 is based on functional ability; BC3 players use an assistive ramp.

## History

Boccia originated in ancient Greece and Rome and was revived as a sport for people with cerebral palsy in the 1960s-70s, spreading from Scandinavia and the UK. It joined the Paralympic program in 1984 (Arnhem) and was governed by CPISRA until BISFed took over on 1 January 2013; the brand "World Boccia" is now used at competitions. Great Britain, Portugal, Brazil and South Korea are among the leading nations, with stars like David Smith (GBR) and Evelyn de Oliveira (BRA).
