## Basic Information

Broomball is a team sport played on ice (or, in its dry-land form, on a gym floor) in which two teams of six — a goaltender and five skaters wearing rubber-soled shoes instead of skates — use short brooms to hit a ball into the opponent's goal. It is governed internationally by the International Federation of Broomball Associations (IFBA), founded in Canada in 1998 by Rick Przybysz after a 1988 visit to Australia and registered as a not-for-profit in 2013 (International Federation of Broomball Associations [IFBA], n.d.). The 2026 IFBA World Broomball Championships were held in Whitehorse, Yukon, Canada, in early November 2026 at the Canada Games Centre, with games starting November 3, 2026, more than 24 teams and divisions for men's (two full-contact divisions), women's (non-contact), mixed and masters, plus a Hall of Fame induction on November 4, 2026 (IFBA, n.d.). In the United States the sport is organized by the United States Broomball Association (USBA) and All Elite Broomball, while the former USA Broomball organization folded in 2020; in Canada, Broomball Canada counts around 19,000 registered players (United States Broomball Association [USBA], n.d.).

On the women's side, broomball has a long tradition: girls' broomball teams were already playing in Ontario in the early 1900s, and today the women's division at IFBA worlds is played non-contact, with women's and mixed teams competing at every world championship (IFBA, n.d.).

## Rules

Under IFBA rules, a team fields six players — one goaltender and five skaters — on an ice rink or court roughly 200 × 85 ft, with two halves of 20 to 30 minutes; play begins and resumes with a face-off, and goals count when the ball fully crosses the goal line. Players wear helmets with face masks (or a cage), gloves and light padding, but skate with ordinary rubber-soled shoes — no ice skates — and carry a broom whose head is a triangular block of rubber; the broom blade may never be raised above waist height, and checking and body contact are forbidden in most divisions (women's broomball is non-contact by rule; men's divisions may be full-contact at IFBA worlds) (IFBA, n.d.).

Equipment and field: the indoor ball weighs 270–280 g with a circumference of 18–20 in; goals are nets 5 × 7 ft (1.5 × 2.1 m) under IFBA rules, versus 6 × 8 ft in U.S. play; the centre red line is the only offside line (IFBA, n.d.) (USBA, n.d.). Fouls include high sticking (broom above the waist), slashing, tripping, holding and roughing; penalties are two minutes in the penalty box, and in full-contact divisions fighting draws a five-minute major (IFBA, n.d.). Formats and classifications: men's, women's, mixed and masters (over-30/over-40) divisions at world championships; league play in Canada and the U.S.; youth and school variants on gym floors are called broom hockey (see broom hockey). Men's and women's games use the same equipment and field dimensions, with contact rules differing by division (IFBA, n.d.).

## History

The first documented broomball game was played in Perdue, Saskatchewan, Canada, on March 5, 1909; girls' teams were photographed in Ontario in the early 1900s, and the sport also spread to Duluth, Minnesota, around 1910. A First Nations origin is claimed for the game. Broomball Canada, evolved from the Canadian Broomball Federation founded in 1976, is now the largest organization with around 19,000 registered players (IFBA, n.d.) (Topend Sports, n.d.). The IFBA was founded in Canada in 1998 after Rick Przybysz saw broomball in Australia in 1988 and became a registered not-for-profit in 2013; the first IFBA World Championship was held in 1991 in Victoria, British Columbia, with 44 teams and more than 800 players, and later editions took place in Bolzano (1998), Innsbruck/Telfs (2010), Ottawa (2012, the largest with 48+ teams and 1,100+ players), Tomakomai (2014), Regina (2016), Blaine, Minnesota (2018), Kingston (2022) and Mont-Blanc, France (2024), before Whitehorse 2026 (IFBA, n.d.).
