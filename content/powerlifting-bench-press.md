## Basic Information

The bench press is the second of the three lifts in competitive [powerlifting](powerlifting.html): the lifter lies on a flat bench, receives the barbell at arm's length, lowers it to the chest and presses it back to a locked-out finish under the referee's commands (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is contested both within full powerlifting meets and as a standalone single-lift event, and it is the basis of para powerlifting, the bench-press-only Paralympic discipline for athletes with lower-limb impairments (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the current leading women, the American Bonica Brown holds the IPF classic open bench press world record of 151.5 kg set in 2018 and has been a seven-time classic world champion, with Liubou Bialova of Belarus a ten-time world single bench press champion of the 1990s and 2000s; among the men, the equipped world record exceeds 350 kg in the heaviest classes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under IPF technical rules, the lifter lies with head, shoulders and buttocks on the bench and both feet flat on the platform; after the bar is unracked the chief referee gives the "start" command, the lifter lowers the bar to the chest or upper abdomen and holds it motionless, then presses it upward in one continuous motion until the elbows are locked, holding it motionless before the "rack" command (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). Faults that void the attempt include heaving or bouncing the bar off the chest, any downward movement during the press, uneven arm extension, lifting the head, shoulders or buttocks off the bench, or moving the feet; each lifter gets three attempts and the best valid one counts toward the total or the single-lift result (International Powerlifting Federation, 2026). In equipped lifting a supportive bench shirt is worn; in classic lifting only a belt, wrist wraps and singlet are allowed. Single-lift bench press world championships are sanctioned in both divisions, with men's and women's classes across all bodyweight categories and junior and masters age groups (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The floor press — pressing a barbell while lying on the ground — predates the bench press, but the modern lift became possible when benches and racks became standard gym equipment in the mid-20th century and quickly became the popular measure of upper-body strength (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). As odd-lift contests were standardized into powerlifting, the bench press took the middle position between squat and deadlift and has been part of IPF world championships since 1973 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Equipped bench shirts added tens of kilograms to records from the 1980s, and the classic division created a separate raw record path from 2012; icons of the lift include Scot Mendelson, who pressed over 1,000 lb (454 kg) in multi-ply competition, and Liubou Bialova, ten-time world single-lift champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
