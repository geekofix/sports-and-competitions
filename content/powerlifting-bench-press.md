## Basic Information

The bench press is the second of the three lifts in competitive [powerlifting](powerlifting.html). The lifter lies on a flat bench, receives the barbell at arm's length, lowers it to the chest or abdominal area and presses it back to full lockout, all under the referee's commands. The discipline is contested both as one lift within full powerlifting meets and as a standalone single-lift event, and it is the basis of the Paralympic sport of para powerlifting, where athletes with lower-limb and other impairments press from a dedicated bench.

## Rules

Under IPF technical rules, the lifter lies with head and shoulders on the bench, with the soles of both shoes flat on the platform; in equipped lifting a supportive bench shirt is worn. After the bar is unracked, the chief referee commands "start", the lifter lowers the bar to the chest or abdominal area and holds it motionless on the chest, then presses it upward. The bar must move upward in one continuous motion and finish with elbows locked and the bar held motionless before the referee's "rack" command. Faults causing disqualification include heaving or bouncing the bar off the chest, any downward movement of the bar during the press, uneven extension of the arms, lifting the head, shoulders or buttocks off the bench, or moving the feet. Each lifter receives three attempts, and the best successful attempt with correct commands counts toward the total or the single-lift result.

## History

The floor press — pressing a barbell while lying on the ground — predates it, but the modern bench press became possible when benches and racks became standard gym equipment in the mid-20th century, and it quickly became the most popular measure of upper-body strength. As "odd lift" contests were standardized into powerlifting's three lifts, the bench press took the middle position between squat and deadlift, and by the founding of the IPF in 1972 it was a fixture of world championships. Equipped bench shirts, which support the press like a stiff spring, added tens of kilograms to the record books from the 1980s onward, before the IPF's classic (raw) divisions created a separate record path. The IPF sanctions standalone single-lift bench press world championships in both equipped and classic lifting. The all-time greats of the lift include equipped specialists such as Scot Mendelson, who pressed over 1,000 pounds in multi-ply competition outside the IPF, and IPF champions such as Bonica Brown, an 84+ kg equipped world-record holder.
