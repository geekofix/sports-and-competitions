## Basic Information

The Alaskan high kick is a one-legged balance variant: the athlete balances on one foot while holding the other foot up with one hand, then kicks the suspended target straight overhead with the balancing foot before landing on both (WEIO, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is an Arctic Sport at the Arctic Winter Games and a WEIO event, with separate men's and women's competitions. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Standing on one foot, the free leg is held up by the hand; the athlete kicks upward with the standing foot to strike the target and lands on both feet. Heights are raised until only one athlete succeeds (WEIO, n.d.).

## History

The Alaskan high kick developed among Alaska Native communities as a balance and flexibility test related to the one-foot high kick; it joined the WEIO and AWG programmes in the 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.).
