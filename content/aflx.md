## Basic Information

AFLX was a six-a-side exhibition variant of Australian rules football created by the Australian Football League (AFL) and played from 2018 to 2019. It was designed as a faster, more spectacular version of the game for broadcast and live audiences, on a smaller oval with quicker restarts. The competition was discontinued after the 2019 season because of cost and scheduling conflicts, and the AFL's community focus has since shifted to formats such as [AFL 9s](afl-9s.html) and [Australian Rules Football](australian-rules-football.html) on this site (Australian Football League [AFL], n.d.; AFL Tables, n.d.).

AFLX is mixed-gender by design — women and men share the field in the same teams.

## Rules

AFLX was played six per side on a smaller oval with a slightly smaller ball. Matches consisted of four short quarters, and restarts after behinds and goals were quicker than in the full game, with play-on or a short kick-in instead of the traditional set restart, keeping the ball in motion. Otherwise the core laws of Australian rules applied: tackling, marking, free kicks, 50-metre penalties, and the standard scoring of 6 points for a goal and 1 point for a behind. Because the format was centrally organized by the AFL, the rules were uniform across all clubs — there were no regional variants (AFL, n.d.; AFL Tables, n.d.).


## History

The AFL launched AFLX in 2018 as a mid-season exhibition competition with shortened quarters on smaller grounds, hoping to attract new audiences with a faster game. After two seasons — 2018 and 2019 — the league announced in late 2019 that the competition would be discontinued, citing cost and fixture congestion, and the AFL redirected its innovation efforts toward participation formats like AFL 9s and women's competitions such as the AFL Women's (AFL, n.d.; AFL Tables, n.d.). AFLX remains a notable footnote in the league's history: a deliberately modified version of Australia's national game that lasted only two years.
