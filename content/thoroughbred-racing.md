## Basic Information

Thoroughbred racing is the sport of racing Thoroughbred horses at the gallop: the breed developed in England expressly for racing, mounted by professional jockeys over flat courses or obstacles (jumps racing), from sprints of 1,000 metres to staying tests beyond 3,000 metres. Known as the Sport of Kings and organized on every inhabited continent, it is one of the world's largest spectator sports and among its oldest regulated professions.

## Rules

Flat races are started from stalls or a flag, run weight-for-age (with handicaps allocating weights by past performance) over prescribed distances on turf or synthetic surfaces, with stewards enforcing interference rules, riding standards and drug bans, and photo finishes deciding margins measured in hundredths. Jump racing adds fences (hurdles of brush and birch or steeplechase fences), each horse clearing obstacles on the way to the line; jockeys must weigh in and out at their assigned weight, race riding is governed by a strict rulebook of interference penalties, and the world's racing authorities operate shared anti-doping and horse-welfare rules. The sport's biggest events are its conditions races: classic races for three-year-olds (the English 2,000 Guineas, Derby, Oaks and St Leger and their international counterparts), the Breeders' Cup, plus the great handicaps and jump races such as the Grand National.

## History

Racing is ancient, but the Thoroughbred breed was formalized in seventeenth- and eighteenth-century England by crossing native mares with imported Arabian, Barb and Turkoman stallions; race meetings at Newmarket and Ascot created the sport's classic structure, the Jockey Club (1750) codified its rules, and the General Stud Book (1791) sealed the breed. Racing spread worldwide with the empire and beyond (the Kentucky Derby, the Melbourne Cup, the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, Dubai's World Cup and Japan's top races became national institutions), while the twentieth and twenty-first centuries brought pari-mutuel wagering, international breeding empires, modern safety and medication reforms. Today the sport blends ancient pageantry with worldwide industry: billions in wagering, elite stallions and an international racing calendar that peaks with the Breeders' Cup, Arc and Dubai's spring carnival.
