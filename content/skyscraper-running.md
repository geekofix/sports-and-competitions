## Basic Information

Skyscraper running — also called tower running or stair climbing — is the timed race up the staircases of tall buildings and towers (TWA, n.d.-a). The sport has two international bodies: the Towerrunning World Association (TWA), which crowns world champions and maintains the Towerrunning 200 world ranking, and the World Federation of Great Towers (WFGT), founded in 1989, which runs the Towerrunning Challenge circuit (WFGT, n.d.). The 2026 Empire State Building Run Up (ESBRU), the 48th edition, was held on 6 October 2026 over 1,576 stairs to the 86th floor, with more than 200 runners from over 15 countries; Ryoji Watanabe of Japan won the men's race and Annemarie Wilhelm the women's, and it counted as the seventh stop of the 2026 WFGT Towerrunning Challenge (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Taipei 101 Run Up 2026, the 19th edition on 9 May 2026, drew 5,147 competitors from 32 countries racing 2,046 steps to the 88th-floor observatory (TWA, n.d.-b). At the top of the Towerrunning 200 world ranking stand Soh Wai Ching of Malaysia (men) and Tea Faber of Croatia (women) (TWA, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In a tower run, athletes race against the clock from a start line at the base to a finish line at the top landing; every step counts, and receiving assistance, using elevators or holding a rail for propulsion in a way that violates the event rules brings disqualification (TWA, n.d.-a). Races are individual time trials, with wave starts at major events such as the ESBRU; some iconic races are held on external staircases (the Rue Foyatier in Lyon, the Eiffel Tower's La Verticale) and most inside towers. Competitions are divided into elite and age-group categories, men's and women's, and handicap divisions for athletes with disabilities at selected events (TWA, n.d.-a). The TWA awards world and continental championship titles, while the WFGT Towerrunning Challenge aggregates selected races — including the ESBRU and Taipei 101 — into a season-long circuit ranking (WFGT, n.d.). The Towerrunning 200 ranks the world's best 200 tower runners by points from designated races (TWA, n.d.-b).

## History

The earliest recorded stair race is the climb of London's Monument to the Fires of London, 311 steps, whose best time of 2 minutes 32 seconds was set in 1730 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first competitive stair race was held in Paris on 14 July 1903 on the Rue Foyatier, organised by the Revue Sportive, and the first tower race took place at the Eiffel Tower on 26 November 1905 with 283 participants, organised by Les Sports (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Empire State Building Run Up was first held in 1978 by the marathon organiser Fred Lebow and has run annually since — except 2020 — becoming the 48th edition in 2026; La Verticale de la Tour Eiffel has been staged since 2015 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The inaugural world championship was held in Doha in 2015, won by Andrea Mayr of Austria and Piotr Łobodziński of Poland; at Taipei 101 in 2018 Łobodziński defended his title and Suzy Walsham of Australia won the women's race, and the 2024 world title went to Soh Wai Ching in Taipei (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Germany's Thomas Dold, ESBRU winner from 2006 to 2012 with a best of 10:06 in 2009, and Soh Wai Ching, the first Asian ESBRU winner (2021–2023) who set a Guinness-recognised 1.295 km of vertical ascent per hour in Kuala Lumpur in 2020, are the sport's best-known icons (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
