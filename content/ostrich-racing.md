## Basic Information

Ostrich racing is a novelty racing sport in which jockeys ride on the backs of ostriches over short tracks, a spectacle best known from the ostrich farms of Oudtshoorn in South Africa, the "ostrich capital of the world", and from the annual Chandler Ostrich Festival in Arizona, USA. Wild ostriches can reach speeds of up to 70 km/h, but racing birds typically run at 45 to 50 km/h, and the main task of the jockey is to stay on and reach the finish line. Riders must wear helmets and are usually limited to a maximum weight of about 75 to 80 kg.

## Rules

A race is run over a short straight or oval track, typically a few hundred meters, with several ostriches and jockeys starting together. The jockey sits on the bird's back, holds its wings and steers by pulling the neck in the desired direction, with special saddles, stirrups and reins used in faster races. Falls are common, and an ostrich whose rider falls off may simply continue running without him. Some events also feature two-person outfits in which the ostrich pulls a light wagon. Protective helmets are mandatory for all jockeys, weight limits apply to protect the birds, and the fastest jockey across the finish line wins.

## History

Ostrich racing began in South Africa in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when the ostrich feather boom made Oudtshoorn in the Little Karoo a center of ostrich farming and the birds became a local attraction; organized ostrich derbies are still held at farms there and at the Rand Show in Johannesburg. The sport spread to the United States, where an ostrich farm opened as a tourist attraction in Jacksonville, Florida, in 1892, and races are today held at venues such as the Chandler Ostrich Festival in Arizona, begun in 1989 and held annually since, the International Camel and Ostrich Races in Virginia City, Nevada, and racecourses such as Canterbury Park in Minnesota, Ellis Park in Kentucky and Prairie Meadows in Iowa. Ostrich racing is also practiced in parts of the Middle East and Australia.
