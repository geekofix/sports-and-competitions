## Basic Information

Bandy is a team sport on ice often described as "field hockey on ice": eleven players per side skate on a rink the size of a football pitch and hit a small ball with a stick into the opponent's goal. It is governed by the Federation of International Bandy (FIB) and contested at the Bandy World Championship, held for men since 1955 and for women since 2004. It was a demonstration sport at the 1952 Oslo Olympics.

## Rules

Under FIB rules, a bandy match lasts 90 minutes (two halves of 45 minutes, longer in finals), played on a rink about 110 x 65 m with goals 3.5 x 2.1 m. Each team has 11 players including a goalkeeper. The ball may be played with the stick, skates or body, but the stick may not be raised above shoulder height when playing the ball. Offside, corner strokes and free hits exist, similar to football. A goal counts when the whole ball crosses the goal line. Penalty shots are taken from 12 m.

## History

Bandy developed in England in the 19th century alongside the codification of association football and ice hockey, then spread to Scandinavia and Russia, where it became a mass winter sport. The first Bandy World Championship was held in 1955 in Finland; Sweden and Russia (later the Soviet Union) have dominated the medal table ever since. The FIB was founded in 1955 and today counts over 20 member federations.
