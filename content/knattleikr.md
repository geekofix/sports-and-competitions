## Basic Information

Knattleikr (Old Norse for "ball game") is the ball game of the Viking Age, described in the Icelandic sagas of the 13th and 14th centuries: teams played with a hard ball that could be hit with a wooden bat (tré) or thrown and carried by hand, with body contact in the fight for the ball, on frozen ponds, fjords or open fields. The exact historical rules are lost, but the game has been revived by re-enactors and is played today at medieval festivals and at several US universities, including Brandeis, Clark, Providence College and Yale.

## Rules

Because the original rules are unknown, modern revivals use reconstructed rules: two teams on a lined field, each player armed with a wooden bat; the hard ball may be hit with the bat, thrown or carried, and players may tackle and wrestle for possession; goals are scored by hitting or carrying the ball past the opponents' goal line or onto a target shield. Matches are fast and physical, with substitutions when a player loses his bat, and safety rules (helmets in some versions) are applied. The Hurstwic Viking reenactment group in Massachusetts has played reconstructed knattleikr at its annual feast since the early 2000s, and the first New England intercollegiate tournament was held in 2007 between Clark University and Brandeis.

## History

Knattleikr is mentioned in several Sagas of Icelanders — Grettis saga (ch. 15), Gísla saga (ch. 15 and 18), Egils saga (ch. 40), Eyrbyggja saga (ch. 43) and Vápnfirðinga saga (ch. 4) — where matches could last from morning to night and drew crowds from whole districts; the game is depicted in the 2022 film The Northman. No bat or ball has survived, and scholars note the sagas give no goals or points. The game was probably similar to early hurling and to other ancient bat-and-ball games. Its revival began with Norse culture enthusiasts in the late 20th century, and today it is played at Viking festivals in Iceland, North America and Europe.
