## Basic Information

Knattleikr (Old Norse for "ball game") is an ancient Norse ball game recorded from the Viking Age in Iceland beginning in the 9th century: a hard ball was struck with a stick, hands were also allowed, and full body contact was permitted in the fight for the ball (Hurstwic, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The most complete descriptions survive in the Sagas of Icelanders — Grettis saga chapter 15, where Grettir Ásmundarson struck Auðun so hard with the ball that his nose was broken and his face bloodied, as well as Gísla saga chapters 15 and 18, Egils saga chapter 40, Eyrbyggja saga chapter 43 and Vápnfirðinga saga chapter 4 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Matches were played from morning to night on frozen ponds and fjords (*svell*), drew huge crowds, and featured team captains, penalties and even a penalty box; the exact rules are lost (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A modern revival began in the United States: the first annual New England intercollegiate knattleikr competition was played in April 2007 at Clark University between Clark and Brandeis, and teams now exist at Clark University, Brandeis University, Providence College and Yale University; the game was also depicted in the 2022 film *The Northman* (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the modern revival, men's and women's teams both play under the same reconstructed rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

The original rules of knattleikr are lost; the sagas record that teams had captains, that a hard ball was hit with a stick while players could also use their hands, that body contact was allowed and that penalties — including a penalty box — existed (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Hurstwic, n.d.). Games were played on flat, ice-covered surfaces such as frozen ponds, with players sometimes using tar and sand under their boots for traction, and could last from morning to night (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Icelandic Grágás law book allowed a player to leave the game at any time, reflecting how brutal the game could be (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the modern revival, reenactment groups and college teams at Clark, Brandeis, Providence College and Yale play a reconstructed version on grass or ice: teams with captains, a hard ball struck with a stick, hands allowed and body contact permitted, following the saga descriptions (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Hurstwic, n.d.). Modern players are cautioned that the game is dangerous, and the college competitions since 2007 use the reconstructed rules rather than an official codified set (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's teams both play in the modern revival under the same reconstructed rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Ball games of the knattleikr kind are recorded across the North Atlantic — Swedish *väderboll* and *isboll*, English medieval football, hockey — and the Icelandic sagas describe knattleikr from the 9th century onward as a spectacular, full-contact game on the ice (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The best-known saga episode is in Grettis saga chapter 15, where Grettir Ásmundarson's ball-strike breaks Auðun's nose during a match on the ice of Lake Miðfjarðarvatn (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After centuries of decline, the game was revived by Norse reenactors — the Hurstwic group among them — and by American college students: the first annual New England intercollegiate competition was held at Clark University in April 2007 between Clark and Brandeis (Hurstwic, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2022 film *The Northman* depicted a game of knattleikr, bringing the Viking ball game to a wide audience (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
