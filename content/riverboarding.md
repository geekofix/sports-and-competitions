## Basic Information

Riverboarding is prone river surfing on a bodyboard-style board: the rider lies on the board, paddles with the arms and steers with fins, running rapids or surfing standing waves on rivers (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is closely related to hydrospeed but uses a larger, buoyant board with a leash, and has its own competitive scene in river-running countries (IRF, n.d.). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are [Hydrospeed](hydrospeed.html), [Whitewater Rafting Race](whitewater-rafting-race.html), [Playboating](playboating.html), [Freestyle Kayak](freestyle-kayak.html), [Canoe Slalom](canoe-slalom.html), [Rafting](rafting.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Competitive riverboarding uses timed descents of marked rapid sections or wave-jam formats on a river feature; riders wear helmet, PFD, wetsuit and a leashed board. Safety rules require scouting rapids and carrying a whistle and knife. Events are open to men and women in separate or mixed categories depending on the organiser (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Riverboarding grew out of surfing and river-running culture in the 1970s in California and Europe; it remains largely an amateur competitive scene with events in the US, France and New Zealand, and overlaps with hydrospeed competitions (Wikipedia, n.d.).
