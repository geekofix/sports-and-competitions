## Basic Information

Jeu provençal (also called longue provençale or "la longue") is a French boules game played on a long pitch of 15 to 20 m — twice the length of a pétanque terrain — and is the direct predecessor of modern pétanque. Two teams throw metal boules toward a small wooden target ball (the but or jack), and the team whose boules finish closest to the but scores. Unlike pétanque, where players throw from a fixed circle, jeu provençal is played "on the move": the thrower may take a run-up. The game is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Pétanque et Jeu Provençal (FIPJP) and is played above all in the Provence and the Lyon region.

## Rules

The terrain is a long pitch of 15 to 20 m. The but is thrown from a throwing circle at one end and must land within the playing area; players then take turns throwing their boules, aiming to finish closer to the but than the opponent's best boule or to knock opposing boules away. The formulas of play are the same as in pétanque — triplette (3 vs 3, two or three boules per player), doublette and tête-à-tête — and a game is normally played to 13 points: after each end, the team with the boule closest to the but scores one point for every boule nearer to the but than the opponent's best. Boules must be officially approved by the FIPJP.

## History

Boules games have been played in Provence for centuries, and the jeu provençal as a codified "longue" form developed in the 18th century around Lyon, where the Fédération Lyonnaise et Régionale was formed in 1906. It remained the dominant boules game in southern France until the shorter pétanque, played from a standing circle, spread from the 1910s onward, largely because pétanque could be played on smaller, uneven ground. Today jeu provençal survives as a codified discipline of the FIPJP with national championships in France, and its longer distances and run-up throws make it a test of power and precision distinct from pétanque.
