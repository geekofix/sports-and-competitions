## Basic Information

CROSSNET is a four-player racket sport combining volleyball and four square: played on a court divided into four quadrants around a central net. It was invented in 2017 in Woodstock, Connecticut, by brothers Chris and Matt Meade together with their friend Mike Delpapa. It is played recreationally worldwide and competitively under USA Volleyball's partnership (since 2021), with professional prize events such as the $10,000 ESPN8 tournament in Chula Vista, California.

## Rules

Under the official CROSSNET rules, each player owns one quadrant. The serve is hit from square 4 diagonally into square 2, and play continues with each player allowed one hit per return over the net into any quadrant; the ball may not be caught or held. If a player is eliminated (ball hits their quadrant or they fault), the remaining players rotate one quadrant clockwise. Only the player in square 4 can score points, by making the ball land in an opponent's quadrant. Games are played to 11 points, win by two; matches are best of three games.

## History

The Meade brothers and Mike Delpapa created CROSSNET in 2017 in Woodstock, Connecticut, and founded the company to sell the nets. The game went viral on social media in the late 2010s. In 2021 USA Volleyball partnered with CROSSNET to sanction competitive play, and the first professional prize-money events followed, including the ESPN8 Chula Vista tournament. Today CROSSNET is played in parks, beaches and backyards across the US, Europe and Asia.
