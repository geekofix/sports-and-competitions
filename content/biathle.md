## Basic Information

Biathle is a combined endurance sport — a continuous sequence of running and swimming (run–swim–run) — governed by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne [UIPM], n.d.-a), which has staged Biathle World Championships every year since 1999, when the first edition was held in Monaco. The 2026 UIPM Biathle-Triathle-Laser Run World Championships were held October 26–November 1, 2026 in Funchal, Madeira, Portugal, at the Centro Náutico São Lázaro, with about 1,000 athletes from dozens of countries competing across 12 age groups, mixed relays and Para categories; Great Britain and South Africa each won five golds, Britain's Sam Cobb took the men's senior title, Ireland's Toby Horgan won the Boys U9 gold, and South Africa's Misha Meyer won the Girls U11 title, while Italy's Giorgio Malan — bronze medallist at Paris 2024 — also competed (UIPM, n.d.-b). The first combined Biathle-Triathle-Laser Run World Championships had been held in Mossel Bay, South Africa, in December 2025, and the 2022 edition took place in Machico, Madeira. Women's and girls' categories are an integral part of every edition — the 2026 girls' U11 title went to South Africa's Misha Meyer — and mixed relay teams are standard (UIPM, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In the standard senior biathle the athlete runs 2.5 km, swims 200 m and runs another 2.5 km, with the clock running continuously and the total time deciding the result; the swim may be in a pool or in open water depending on the venue, and transitions are part of the race (UIPM, n.d.-a). Distances are scaled down through the age groups — from short sprints for the under-9 children up to the full senior distances — and the championships offer boys' and girls' categories in every age group from U9 to senior, plus mixed relay teams and Para categories. The related UIPM disciplines triathle (swim–run–swim) and laser run (run–shoot) are often staged at the same championships. Men's and women's races use identical distances within each age group, and team classifications are awarded alongside the individual titles (UIPM, n.d.-a).

## History

Biathle was created as a summer, equipment-light cousin of modern pentathlon and was added to the UIPM programme in the 1990s; the first World Biathle Championships were held in Monaco in 1999, and the early Monte Carlo editions drew international attention — Prince Albert II of Monaco attended the 2007 championships, where American athlete Tracy Mattes was among the leading competitors (see figure). Since then the worlds have been held annually in venues including Madeira (Machico 2022) and Mossel Bay, South Africa (December 2025, the first combined Biathle-Triathle-Laser Run edition), before the 2026 championships in Funchal (UIPM, n.d.-b). The women's side has grown steadily from the first editions, with girls' categories from U9 upward now standard at every world championship (UIPM, n.d.-a).
