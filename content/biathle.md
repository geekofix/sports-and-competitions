## Basic Information

Biathle is a sub-sport of modern pentathlon: a continuous run-swim-run sequence raced against the clock. It is governed by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM), which holds the Biathle World Championships annually since 1999 and runs Biathle World Tours. It is an accessible entry point to multisport racing and is not an Olympic discipline.

## Rules

Under UIPM rules, a biathle race is a continuous Run-Swim-Run: at elite level roughly 2.5 km running, 200 m freestyle swimming, then another 2.5 km running (distances are shorter in youth age groups). Athletes start together, run to the swim course, swim a marked loop, and run to the finish; the clock runs throughout. Competitions include individual, team and mixed relay events. At the Biathle World Championships, about 500 athletes from around 50 countries compete each year.

## History

Biathle was created by the UIPM as a development sport and the first Biathle World Championships were held in 1999 in Monaco, which hosted the event regularly in the 2000s. It resembles aquathlon (swim-run from triathlon) but keeps the run-swim-run sequence and is shorter. The UIPM promotes biathle worldwide together with triathle (shoot-run-swim) and laser run as pathways into modern pentathlon.
