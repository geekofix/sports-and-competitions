## Basic Information

Ice speedway is motorcycle speedway raced on oval ice tracks of about 400 m, usually frozen lakes or specially prepared ice rinks. The bikes are single-cylinder machines of up to 500 cc with no brakes and long steel spikes (up to roughly 2.5 cm) studded into the tires, and riders race counterclockwise at speeds well above 100 km/h, dragging the left boot on the ice in the corners. The sport is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM), and its pinnacle event is the FIM Ice Speedway World Championship.

## Rules

Riders compete in heats of four over a set number of laps, with the fastest riders advancing to the final; the FIM Individual Ice Speedway World Championship is decided over a series of Grand Prix events, and the Team World Championship pits national teams against each other. Machines must be single-cylinder with a maximum displacement of 500 cc, run on methanol, and have no brakes; only the tires are studded, with a regulated maximum spike length. Riders may touch the ice with their left foot in corners but must not use it to propel the bike, and dangerous riding or leaving the track results in exclusion. Points are awarded for placings in each race and the rider with the most points over the season becomes world champion.

## History

Ice speedway emerged in Sweden in the early 1960s, when speedway riders fitted spikes to their bikes to race on frozen lakes during the winter months; it quickly spread across Scandinavia and to the Soviet Union. The first FIM World Championship was staged in the mid-1960s, and since then Sweden, Finland and Russia have traditionally dominated the sport. The season runs from December to March, with major meetings in Sweden, Finland, the Netherlands, Germany and Russia drawing large crowds on temporary ice ovals.
