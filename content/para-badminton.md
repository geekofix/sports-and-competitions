## Basic Information

Para badminton is the Paralympic version of [badminton](badminton.html), governed by the Badminton World Federation (BWF) since 2011 (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-a). Players are classified into six sport classes: WH1 and WH2 for wheelchair players, SL3 and SL4 for standing players with lower-limb impairments, SU5 for players with upper-limb impairments and SH6 (formerly SS6) for players of short stature (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-b). Badminton made its Paralympic debut at Tokyo 2020 with 14 medal events — men's and women's events in every class — and will also be contested at Paris 2024 and Los Angeles 2028 (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). Among the current stars is Egypt's Ahmed Eldakrory, pictured below, the SH6 singles champion at Tokyo 2020.

## Rules

Para badminton follows the standard BWF laws of badminton with court adaptations for the wheelchair classes, which play on a half court in singles (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-a). Events are offered as men's and women's singles, men's and women's doubles and mixed doubles within each sport class or a combined class, such as WH1-WH2 doubles and SL3-SU5 doubles (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-b). Serving, scoring to 21 points and the rally rules are those of ordinary badminton, with modifications only where the class requires them — for example wheelchair players serve from a stationary position (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-a). At the BWF Para Badminton World Championships and the Paralympics, classification panels examine athletes before competition and place them in the correct class (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-b). Men's and women's events use identical courts, nets and scoring within each class (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.).

## History

Badminton for players with disabilities developed in the 1990s, led by Germany and England, with the first European Para Badminton Championships in 1995; the BWF took over governance of para badminton in 2011, making badminton one of the first sports governed by one federation for both able-bodied and para athletes (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-a). The sport debuted at the Tokyo 2020 Paralympics with 14 medal events and 90 athletes from 41 countries, with Egypt's Ahmed Eldakrory winning the men's SH6 singles gold (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). The BWF Para Badminton World Championships have been held since 2011, and the Paris 2024 Games expanded the program (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-b).
