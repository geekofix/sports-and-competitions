## Basic Information

Pentathlon is an athletic contest of five disciplines. The term describes both the ancient Greek pentathlon — stadion running, wrestling, long jump, javelin throw and discus throw, introduced at the 18th Olympiad around 708 BC — and the modern pentathlon invented by Pierre de Coubertin for the Stockholm 1912 Olympic Games (Britannica, n.d.). The modern sport, governed by the International Modern Pentathlon Union (UIPM) since 1948, originally comprised pistol shooting, fencing, freestyle swimming, horse riding and cross-country running; since 2025, equestrian show jumping has been replaced by obstacle course racing at the senior level (UIPM, n.d.). Its own page covers the Olympic discipline of [modern pentathlon](modern-pentathlon.html). The modern competition is open to men and women on identical terms — the women's Olympic event was added at Sydney 2000 — and among today's stars are France's Élodie Clouvel, Olympic champion at Paris 2024, and the United States' Samantha Schultz, a Soldier-athlete of the Army's World Class Athlete Program pictured below (Olympics, n.d.).

## Rules

The ancient Greek pentathlon judged all five events at a single festival, with the wrestlers among the event winners meeting in a final wrestling bout for the overall victory (Britannica, n.d.). The modern pentathlon, as contested at the Olympics, scores five disciplines into a single points total (UIPM, n.d.). Fencing is a round-robin of one-touch épée bouts against every other competitor, followed by a direct-elimination bonus round; swimming is a 200 m freestyle race scored against a standard time; the riding element (until Paris 2024) was show jumping over 12 obstacles on an unfamiliar horse allocated by draw, replaced from 2025 by an obstacle course race over a standardized series of obstacles; the finale is the laser run, a cross-country run interspersed with rounds of laser pistol shooting at five targets. Points from the earlier events set the starting order of the laser run, so the first athlete across the finish line wins the gold medal. Men's and women's competitions use identical formats, distances and scoring tables at the Olympics and World Championships; junior and youth age groups use shortened runs and adapted equipment (UIPM, n.d.).

## History

The ancient pentathlon was part of the Olympic program from 708 BC and was regarded as the climax of the games, its winner considered the greatest all-round athlete; a famous red-figured kylix tondo by the Greek vase painter Onesimos, ca. 490 BC, shows the discus and javelin throwers and the jumping weights of the pentathlon (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Coubertin revived the idea for the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, modeling the contest on the skills of a 19th-century soldier: riding an unfamiliar horse, fighting with the épée, shooting, swimming and running across country (UIPM, n.d.). From 1912 to 1980 the Olympic competition was held over five days, in 1996 it was compressed to a single day, and at Paris 2024 it was staged in about two hours in one stadium. The modern competition was introduced alongside the UIPM's world championships in non-Olympic years, the women's Olympic event was added at Sydney 2000, and the team event was held from 1952 to 1992. In 2021, following criticism of the riding discipline at the Tokyo Games, the UIPM congress decided to replace riding with obstacle racing, which debuted at senior level at the 2025 World Cup in Cairo (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
