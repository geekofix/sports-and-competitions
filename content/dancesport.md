## Basic Information

Dancesport is competitive dancing as a sport: couples (or solo and formation dancers) are judged in disciplines ranging from International Standard (waltz, tango, Viennese waltz, slow foxtrot, quickstep) and International Latin (samba, cha-cha-cha, rumba, paso doble, jive) to breaking, hip hop, salsa, rock'n'roll, boogie woogie, discofox and acrobatic rock'n'roll, plus Para dancesport for wheelchair couples (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is governed worldwide by the World DanceSport Federation, founded on 12 May 1957 in Wiesbaden as the International Council of Amateur Dancers (ICAD), renamed IDSF in 1990 and WDSF in 2011, headquartered in Lausanne, IOC-recognized since 1997 with 99 member federations as of January 2026 (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Breaking made its Olympic debut at Paris 2024 — its sole Olympic appearance to date — with gold for Victor Montalvo of France (b-boys) and Ami Yuasa of Japan (b-girls) (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Among the disciplines in 2026, the WDSF stages World Championships in Standard, Latin and Breaking plus GrandSlam and World Series events; the 2022 World Games included breaking for the first time, and dancesport has been a World Games sport since 1997 (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Leading 2026 competitors include the Villach congress dancers pictured at a 2022 international dancesport competition (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In Standard and Latin disciplines, couples are judged in heats, quarterfinals, semifinals and finals using the marks system (places 1–6): panels of judges evaluate hold and frame, posture, balance, movement quality, rhythm, choreography and presentation; each dance lasts about 1:30–2 minutes in the final, and couples dance several dances in rotation (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In breaking, solo dancers battle head-to-head in rounds (top 16 to the final) performing toprock, downrock (footwork), power moves and freezes, judged on foundation, musicality, variety, execution and creativity (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Formation teams of eight couples perform choreographed routines in Standard and Latin; Para dancesport is contested by wheelchair couples with at least one wheelchair user and is IPC-recognized (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Men and women compete in the same couple events in leader/follower roles, and age categories (junior, senior, seniors) structure the calendar (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-a).

## History

Competitive ballroom dancing began with the first unofficial world championship in 1909 and the first formation team in 1932 at the Astoria Ballroom in London; the first televised competition aired in Germany in 1960 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ICAD was founded on 12 May 1957 in Wiesbaden by nine federations from seven nations, became the IDSF in 1990 and the WDSF in 2011, was IOC-recognized in 1997, and staged breaking's Olympic debut at Paris 2024 after the first World Breaking Championships in Paris in December 2021 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-b). Cha-cha competition dancing of the 1990s, pictured, shows the Latin tradition that feeds today's dancesport (World DanceSport Federation, n.d.-a).
