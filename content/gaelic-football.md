## Basic Information

Gaelic football is one of the four Gaelic games, played in Ireland by two teams of 15 on a large grass pitch (130-145 m x 80-90 m) with H-shaped goals (crossbar 2.44 m high, 6.5 m wide, posts 6.5 m apart). Players may carry the ball for four steps, then must bounce or solo it; they score by kicking or hand-passing the ball over the crossbar (a goal, 3 points) or under it between the posts (a point, 1 point). It is governed by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), founded 1884, which runs the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship.

## Rules

Under the GAA Official Guide, each team fields 15 players (goalkeeper plus 14 outfield) in championship matches, four 15-minute quarters (senior inter-county) or two 30-minute halves (club). The ball may be carried for a maximum of four steps or held for as long as needed while bouncing/soloing; punching the ball is allowed but not lifting it off the ground except by the goalkeeper. Tackling is shoulder-to-shoulder only; holding, pushing, tripping and dangerous play are fouls punished with free kicks, yellow/red/black cards (sin-binning for 10 minutes for certain fouls, introduced 2024/25 for cynical play). Kick-outs restart play after scores. The offside rule is limited (no player may be nearer the opponent's goal line than the ball and the second-last opponent when the ball is played forward, with exceptions). The All-Ireland Senior Football Championship final is played at Croke Park, Dublin, in front of 80,000+.

## History

Gaelic football has ancient Irish roots, with the first written rules in 1884 when the GAA was founded in Thurles, County Tipperary, to promote Irish sports and culture under British rule. The first All-Ireland championship was held in 1887 (won by Limerick). The sport became the most popular in Ireland, with clubs in every parish; the 1884 rules were revised over time, including the introduction of the four-step carrying limit in the 20th century. Legends include Kerry's Mikey Sheehy, Dublin's Stephen Cluxton (record 8 All-Ireland medals as goalkeeper), Mayo's Colm Cooper and Tipperary's Séamus Callanan. The GAA also governs hurling, camogie and ladies' Gaelic football; women's football (the Ladies' Gaelic Football Association amalgamated with the GAA in 2023).
