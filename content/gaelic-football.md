## Basic Information

Gaelic football is the most popular of the four Gaelic games, played by two teams of 15 players on a grass pitch of 130–145 m × 80–90 m with H-shaped goals; players may carry the ball for a maximum of four steps, solo it, hand-pass it or kick it, and score a goal (3 points, under the crossbar between the posts) or a point (1 point, over the crossbar) (Gaelic Athletic Association [GAA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed by the GAA, founded on 1 November 1884 in Thurles, County Tipperary, which organizes the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship — the final of the 140th edition was played at Croke Park, Dublin, on 26 July 2026, when Mayo beat Kerry to win the Sam Maguire Cup and end the 75-year "Curse of Mayo" (their first title since 1951), with Ryan O'Donoghue named man of the match (GAA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the 2026 TG4 All-Ireland Senior Ladies' Football Championship final at Croke Park on 2 August 2026 saw Galway beat Kerry 2–07 to 0–12 — Galway's first ladies' title since 2004 — with Leanne Coen scoring two goals and Eva Noone named player of the match (GAA, n.d.-c). The GAA's Football Review Committee package of rule enhancements, trialled in 2025, was ratified as permanent rule at Special Congress on 4 October 2025 and took effect from 1 January 2026, including the two-point arc beyond 40 m, the 4 v 3 rule, stricter 50 m advance on deliberate delay, solo-and-go and the kick-out mark (GAA, n.d.-d).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the Galway county team, 2026 All-Ireland senior ladies' champions, and stars such as Leanne Coen (Galway) and Ailish McLoughlin (Kerry) (GAA, n.d.-c). On the men's side, Kerry remain the most successful county with a record-extending 39th Sam Maguire title in 2025, and Mayo ended their 75-year wait in 2026; Dublin's Ciarán Kilkenny and Kerry's David Clifford are among the standout current players (GAA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the GAA Official Guide Part 2 (Playing Rules, edition effective from 2026), each team fields 15 players on a pitch 130–145 m long and 80–90 m wide, marked with goalposts (uprights 6.5 m apart, crossbar 2.5 m high), a 13 m square, a 20 m line, a 45 m line and, since 2026, a 40 m two-point arc; a match at senior inter-county level lasts two halves of 35 minutes (club 30 minutes) with 15 minutes at half-time (GAA, n.d.-a; GAA, n.d.-d). A player in possession may carry the ball for a maximum of four consecutive steps, must then play it, and may solo (bounce) or kick the ball; the ball may be played by hand (hand-pass, with the ball struck with the fist or open hand) or foot (kick, including a kick-pass), but not lifted from the ground with the hands, and not thrown (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; GAA, n.d.-a). A goal — ball under the crossbar between the posts — is worth 3 points, and a point over the crossbar is worth 1; since 2026 a point scored from beyond the 40 m arc is worth 2 points, and a team must have at least four players inside the 45 m line when the ball is played into the scoring zone (the 4 v 3 rule) (GAA, n.d.-d). Restart rules include the kick-out (goalkeeper from the 20 m line, with a clean catch past the 45 m line awarding an advanced mark), free kicks (with the solo-and-go allowing immediate play after a foul), sideline kicks and the one-on-one throw-in; fouls are penalized with free kicks, yellow/red/black cards, and a 50 m advance for deliberate delay (GAA, n.d.-a; GAA, n.d.-d). Men and women play identical rules; ladies' football is organized by the Ladies' Gaelic Football Association (LGFA) under the GAA, with the same pitch and scoring; age grades include U21, minor (U18) and juvenile (U16) (GAA, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Gaelic football in its modern form was codified when the Gaelic Athletic Association was founded on 1 November 1884 in the billiard room of the Hayes Hotel in Thurles, County Tipperary, by Michael Cusack, with Maurice Davin as first president; the GAA's patron was Archbishop Thomas Croke, and the first rules book was published in 1887 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; GAA, n.d.-b). The first All-Ireland Senior Football Championship was held in 1887 — won by Limerick — and the competition became the centerpiece of Irish sport, with Croke Park in Dublin (opened 1913 after the GAA bought the ground in 1913) as its final venue (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the iconic players are Kerry's Mick O'Connell (seven All-Ireland medals, 1977–1986), Dublin's Stephen Cluxton, and, on the women's side, Mayo's Cora Staunton, the record seven-time All-Ireland ladies' medallist, and Kerry's 2026 final stars (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; GAA, n.d.-c). The 2025–2026 rule overhaul by the Football Review Committee, chaired by former Dublin manager Jim Gavin and ratified at Special Congress on 4 October 2025, is the most significant change to the playing rules in the game's modern history (GAA, n.d.-d).
