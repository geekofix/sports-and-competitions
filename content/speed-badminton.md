## Basic Information

Speed badminton \u2014 officially renamed crossminton in 2016 \u2014 is the net-less racket sport combining elements of badminton, squash and tennis, played with specially designed rackets and a heavier wind-resistant "speeder" shuttlecock on two squares fixed 12.8 metres apart, on any surface: tennis court, street, beach or gym. Invented in Berlin in 2001, it is played today by some 2.5 million recreational players worldwide, with an international federation \u2014 the International Crossminton Organisation (ICO), founded 2011 \u2014 running world championships since 2011.

## Rules

The court consists of two 5.50 m squares set 12.8 m apart, with no net between them: points are won by landing the speeder in the opponent's square or forcing a fault \u2014 playable anywhere, since the lines, not a net, frame the game. Equipment is standardised: rackets 58\u201360 cm long, similar to squash rackets, and the speeder \u2014 a shuttlecock (shorter and heavier than a badminton bird, with a rubber weight) that flies straight even in wind up to force 4 and reaches speeds of nearly 300 km/h in elite play; different speeder models (Fun, Match, and others) set the game's pace. Scoring follows badminton logic: matches to 16 points with two-point margins in the ICO format, with serves alternating in sets, draws deciding who serves first (the serve is thrown, then struck), and crossminton played as singles or, with doubles courts, doubles.

## History

The game's shuttlecock was invented around 2000\u20132001 by the Berliner Bill Brandes, who wanted an outdoor, wind-stable badminton and shortened, weighted the bird, first calling his creation "shuttleball", soon "speed badminton", on Kreuzberg's playgrounds in 2001; the Speedminton company (Speedminton GmbH, founded 2002) developed the equipment and the organised sport, which counted 6,000 active players in Germany by 2003 and first clubs and tournaments by 2005. The International Speed Badminton Organisation (ISBO) was founded in Berlin on 25 August 2011, and one day later the first Speedminton World Championships were staged with over 380 participants from 29 countries; the 2015 General Assembly renamed the sport crossminton and the organisation the International Crossminton Organisation. Today the ICO counts national federations across Europe, the Americas, Asia and Africa (26 members by 2018), stages world championships biennially plus a world series and continental championships, and the sport's heartland \u2014 Germany, Switzerland, Poland, Czechia and Hungary in Europe \u2014 continues to grow alongside recreational play worldwide.
