## Basic Information

Speed skating is the race on long ice skates over set distances on a 400-metre double-lane oval, governed by the International Skating Union (ISU), founded in 1892 in Scheveningen, Netherlands, which has staged World Championships since 1893 and put the sport on the Olympic programme at Chamonix 1924 (ISU, n.d.-a). Olympic individual distances are 500, 1000, 1500, 3000 and 5000 m for women and 500, 1000, 1500, 5000 and 10000 m for men, plus the team pursuit, team sprint and — since PyeongChang 2018 — the mass start (ISU, n.d.-b). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, Jordan Stolz of the United States won gold in the men's 500 m and 1000 m, Femke Kok of the Netherlands won the women's 500 m, and Jutta Leerdam of the Netherlands won the women's 1000 m (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 2026 World Sprint Championships in Heerenveen, 5–6 March 2026, Jenning de Boo of the Netherlands set the sprint-combined world record of 134.670 and Femke Kok won the women's title in 146.670 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the current leading women are Femke Kok (500/1000 m), Miho Takagi of Japan (1500 m), Ragne Wiklund of Norway (long distances) and Mia Manganello of the United States (mass start); among the men, Jordan Stolz dominates the sprint distances and Metoděj Jílek of Czechia the long distances (ISU, n.d.-b).

## Rules

On a standard 400 m oval with two lanes, skaters race in pairs — one per lane — and change lanes at every crossing of the home straight so that both skate exactly the same distance; the skater starting in the inner lane wears a white armband (ISU, n.d.-b). Individual races over 500 to 10000 m are decided by total time within the lane pairing. The team pursuit races teams of three against the clock from opposite sides of the track, with the time taken when the third skater crosses; the team sprint is a three-lap relay per skater; the mass start is a single race of up to 24 skaters with intermediate sprint points and a final sprint for the medals (ISU, n.d.-b). Fouls — incorrect lane changing, obstruction, cutting the course or a false start — lead to relegation or disqualification, signalled by the referee (ISU, n.d.-b). Women's and men's events use the same rules on the same ovals, differing only in the distance programme (women skate 3000/5000 m where men skate 5000/10000 m at major championships); junior categories exist at the ISU Junior World Championships (ISU, n.d.-b). The ISU World Cup 2025–26 comprised five stops: Salt Lake City, Calgary, Heerenveen, Hamar and Inzell (ISU, n.d.-a).

## History

Organised speed skating matured in the Netherlands in the nineteenth century, and the International Skating Union was founded on 15 July 1892 in Scheveningen; the first European and World Allround Championships for men followed in 1893, women's world championships began in 1936, and the sport debuted at the Chamonix 1924 Winter Olympics for men and at Squaw Valley 1960 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Retired icons of the sport include the Netherlands' Ard Schenk (three gold medals at Innsbruck 1972), America's Eric Heiden (a record five golds at Lake Placid 1980), Norway's Johann Olav Koss (three golds at Lillehammer 1994), Germany's Gunda Niemann-Stirnemann, America's Bonnie Blair (five Olympic golds between 1988 and 1994), East Germany's Karin Enke and America's Dan Jansen, who won the 1000 m at Lillehammer 1994 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
