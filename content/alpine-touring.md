## Basic Information

Alpine touring (ski mountaineering racing, 'skimo') combines uphill travel on skis with removable climbing skins and a timed downhill on marked courses; the premier events are the ISMF (International Ski Mountaineering Federation) World Championships and the big citizen races such as the Patrouille des Glaciers, and ski mountaineering made its Olympic debut at Milano Cortina 2026 with an individual sprint and a mixed relay (ISMF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Races are timed end-to-end over roughly 1,500–2,000 m of vertical gain, with mandatory transitions where athletes switch from skins to skis; men and women compete in separate fields, and the women's World Cup is led by multiple world champions such as Laëtitia Roux (FRA).

## Rules

ISMF race rules: individual races (vertical race, individual, sprint, relay) with defined transition zones, mandatory equipment (skis with metal edges, skins, boots, poles, helmet, avalanche transceiver/shovel/blanket for some events), and safety checks; Olympic sprint format: qualification heats of ~3 minutes then knockout finals; the mixed relay pairs one woman and one man over four legs. Penalties for missing gates or transitions; men's and women's events in each discipline (ISMF, n.d.).

## History

Ski mountaineering racing grew from military ski-patrol races in the Alps in the 1920s–30s (the Patrouille des Glaciers began in 1943); the ISMF was founded in 1995 and has run World Championships since 1997, and the discipline joined the Olympic program at Milano Cortina 2026 (February 2026, Valtellina venues). Spain's Kilian Jornet and France's Laëtitia Roux are among the most decorated ski mountaineers in World Cup history (Wikipedia, n.d.).
