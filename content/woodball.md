## Basic Information

Woodball is a sport invented in Taiwan in the 1990s, played with a wooden mallet and a wooden ball through a course of gates — in effect a fairway game similar to golf but with bottle-shaped gates instead of holes, and courses typically laid out across parkland in 12 gates over several hundred metres. Games are played by individuals or doubles — counting strokes like golf, with the fewest swings to pass ball through all gates winning.

It has since spread internationally with continental federations and world championships, and featured at World Games programme events as a demonstration in the early 2000s.

## Rules

A standard woodball course consists of twelve fairways, each with one gate made of two bottle-shaped posts and a hanging crossbar; strokes are played with a wooden mallet off the ground, each shot advancing the ball toward and through the gate — the ball must pass through the gate cleanly in the direction of play to complete the fairway. Scores are counted per fairway like golf strokes, and totals decide individual and team placements; rules set stroke order, ball replacement procedures for out-of-bounds lie, penalties for striking the gate and for playing out of turn, and formats exist for singles, doubles and team competitions over one or more rounds of the twelve fairways.

## History

Woodball was created in 1990 by Taiwanese golf course entrepreneurs Weng Ming-hui and Hsu Ming-hui, with its distinctive bottle-shaped gates inspired by a champagne bottle, as a simple, low-cost lawn game suited to players of all ages. The sport spread from Taiwan through Asia and Europe, the International Woodball Federation was established in 1999, and world championships have been held under its auspices since 2000s editions. It featured as a demonstration sport at the 2001 Akita World Games and later editions, and national federations across Asia, Europe and the Americas now run international tours and youth and senior championships.
