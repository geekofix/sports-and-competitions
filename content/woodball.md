## Basic Information

Woodball (*Mùqiú*) is a fairway game invented in Taiwan in 1990: players use a wooden mallet to strike a wooden ball along fairways and through gates, and like golf the side with the fewest strokes wins — but the "holes" are bottle-shaped wooden gates rather than cups, making the game cheaper and playable on grass, sand or indoors (International Woodball Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). A standard course has 12 fairways, each around 6 m wide, laid out over parkland; the ball is 9.5 cm in diameter and the mallet head and gates are famously modelled on beer bottles (Taipei Times, n.d.).

The sport is governed by the International Woodball Federation (IWbF), founded in Taipei and now encompassing more than 40 countries and territories, with the Asian Woodball Association as its continental body; it has been on the Asian Beach Games programme since 2008 and made its SEA Games debut at the 2025 Games in Thailand, where women's events were contested and Thailand's Siriwan Kangkeeree won the women's singles stroke gold (Wikipedia, n.d.). Ten editions of the Woodball World Cup Championship have been held, the most recent in Malaysia in 2026, and the first World University Championship took place in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Players must complete the 12 fairways in order, striking the ball with the mallet from the ground toward and through each gate; a fairway is completed when the ball passes cleanly through the gate in the direction of play, and if the ball finishes still in contact with the gate, one penalty stroke is added immediately (International Woodball Federation, n.d.). Strokes are counted per fairway exactly as in golf, and the lowest total over the course wins; if the ball goes out of bounds, it is placed at least one woodball length outside the fairway before the next stroke, and rules govern stroke order, penalties for hitting the gate and for playing out of turn (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competition formats include singles, doubles, mixed doubles and team events, played as stroke play or as team fairway match play over one or more rounds of the 12 fairways, and women and men compete in separate singles and team events at world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Woodball was invented in 1990 by Weng Ming-hui (with Kuang-chu Young) in Shilin, Taipei, who wanted to convert a hillside property so his retired father could play golf leisurely; he spent two further years formalising the rules, and the ball, 9.5 cm across, was modelled on a spherical staircase railing decoration while the mallet head and the two-post gate with its crossbar took their shapes from a Taiwan Beer bottle and a wine glass (Taipei Times, n.d.). Weng founded the Chinese Taipei Woodball Association in 1993 and promoted the game in schools; the Asian Woodball Association and the International Woodball Federation were then established in Taipei, and the first World University Championship was held in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Olympic Council of Asia added woodball to the Asian Beach Games programme in 2008 (beach woodball was last played in 2016), the 2025 SEA Games in Thailand staged the sport for the first time at a regional games, and the World Cup Championship has now run ten editions, the latest in Malaysia in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
