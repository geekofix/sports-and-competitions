## Basic Information

Taekwondo ("the way of the foot and the fist") is a Korean martial art and combat sport that emphasizes kicking techniques; since Sydney 2000 its sparring discipline (kyorugi) has been an Olympic medal event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.). The sport has two main international branches — World Taekwondo (WT, formerly the World Taekwondo Federation), the Olympic style founded on 28 May 1973 at the Kukkiwon in Seoul with more than 210 member nations and president Choue Chung-won since 2004, and the traditional International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) founded by Gen. Choi Hong Hi in 1966 (World Taekwondo, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The IOC recognized the WTF on 17 July 1980; taekwondo was a demonstration sport at Seoul 1988 and Barcelona 1992 and was adopted as an official medal sport for Sydney 2000 on 4 September 1994 (World Taekwondo, n.d.). At Paris 2024, 128 athletes — equally men and women — contested eight weight classes (four per gender) at the Grand Palais from 7 to 10 August 2024 (Olympics, n.d.). Among the current top female athletes — named first — are the Paris 2024 champions Panipak Wongpattanakit of Thailand (–49 kg), Kim Yu-jin of South Korea (–57 kg), Viviana Márton of Hungary (–67 kg) and Althéa Laurin of France (+67 kg), plus Brazil's Maria Clara Pacheco, the 2025 world champion at –57 kg; leading men include Paris 2024 –58 kg champion Park Tae-joon and +80 kg Olympic champion Arian Salimi of Iran (Wikipedia, n.d.-c) (Olympics, n.d.).

## Rules

Kyorugi (sparring) is fought on a matted contest area with electronic scoring (PSS) in the hogu (trunk protector) and headgear: bouts run three rounds of two minutes with one-minute rests, a punch to the trunk scores 1 point, a kick to the trunk 2 points and a kick to the head 3 points, with turning or spinning techniques earning bonus points; an eight-point lead or a knockout ends the match immediately (World Taekwondo, n.d.). World and Olympic events are single-elimination with repechage, at eight weight classes per gender at the world championships (men –54 to +87 kg, women –46 to +73 kg) and four per gender at the Olympics (men –58, –68, –80, +80 kg; women –49, –57, –67, +67 kg); men and women compete under identical rules in separate divisions, and para taekwondo — with no head contact and no punches — has been on the Paralympic programme since Tokyo 2020 (World Taekwondo, n.d.; Olympics, n.d.). Poomsae (forms) competition is governed by the same World Taekwondo rules, and a mixed team event joined the Olympic programme at Paris 2024 (Olympics, n.d.). The official rule source is the World Taekwondo Competition Rules (World Taekwondo, n.d.).

## History

After Korea's liberation in 1945, masters of the nine kwans (schools) revived Korean fighting arts that had absorbed karate and Chinese methods; in 1955 General Choi Hong Hi proposed the name taekwondo to unify them, founded the ITF in 1966, and the Korea Taekwondo Association opened its Central Dojang in 1972, renamed the Kukkiwon (World Taekwondo headquarters) in 1973 — the same year the WTF held its inaugural meeting at the Kukkiwon with 35 national representatives and staged the first world championships in Seoul (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 1977 the nine kwans recognized the Kukkiwon and WTF as the promotional body, the IOC recognized the WTF on 17 July 1980, taekwondo appeared as a demonstration sport at Seoul 1988 and Barcelona 1992, and the 103rd IOC session on 4 September 1994 adopted it for Sydney 2000; the WTF was renamed World Taekwondo in June 2017, and a 2014 protocol reunited WT and ITF events (World Taekwondo, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons of the sport include founder Choi Hong Hi (1918–2002), Iran's Hadi Saei (b. 1976, Olympic gold 2004 and 2008, two world titles, retired 2012), the USA's Steven López (b. 1978, Olympic gold 2000, five world titles, retired) and Spain's Brigitte Yagüe (b. 1980, six world titles and Olympic silver 2012, retired 2013) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
