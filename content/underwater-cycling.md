## Basic Information

Underwater cycling is the novelty sport of riding a bicycle at the bottom of a swimming pool: divers in full gear pedal heavily weighted bicycles along the pool floor, racing each other or the clock in a sport that tests diving skill and leg strength alike. Its best-known showcase has been the world's underwater cycling championship staged at the bottom of a pool in France, a fixture of wild-sport reporting for two decades.

## Rules

The typical event runs on the floor of a pool (a few metres deep): competitors in scuba gear mount purpose-weighted bicycles (frames loaded with ballast so they stay on the bottom) and pedal a set distance along marked lanes, wearing mask, regulator and fins abandoned (fins are prohibited, and the bike's ordinary pedals do the work) under standard diving rules (buddy supervision and judging on the surface). Race distances run tens of metres, event categories include slalom courses around markers, and the whole contest turns on the odd physics of pedaling underwater: drag is enormous, traction is defined by wheel weight and the athlete's air supply, and stamina and buoyancy-correct breathing decide results as much as leg power.

## History

Underwater cycling is a creature of the novelty sport era: its organized heart is the Rencontre Subaquatique Antoine de Saint-Exupery and the world championship that French diving clubs have staged at the bottom of a swimming pool in the Pays de Caux since 1997 (the festival's cycling championship drawing dozens of divers and media coverage from the era of its first events to today's annual festivals). Diving clubs worldwide have imitated the idea, treating it as both a training challenge (dive fitness and buoyancy control) and a spectacle. The sport remains a niche festival event: contested annually by diving enthusiasts, covered as one of the world's most unusual championships, and beloved precisely for its absurdity.
