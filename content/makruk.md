## Basic Information

Makruk (Thai: หมากรุกไทย), or Thai chess, is the chess variant played in Thailand on an 8×8 board; it descends from chaturanga and shatranj and reached the kingdom of Ayutthaya in the 14th century, with records of play from the Sukhothai period (1238–1438) onward, while Cambodia knows the cognate game ouk chatrang (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-e). In Thailand the game is promoted by the national chess body and the Thailand Traditional Sports Association, which campaigns to internationalise it, and it became an official medal sport at the 33rd SEA Games in Bangkok, 9–20 December 2025, where eight events were staged at The Bazaar Hotel in Chatuchak from 10 to 19 December (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Among current competitors the women come first: Singapore's Sara-Ann Ang claimed silver in the women's makruk category at a Penang test event ahead of the Games, while her teammate Tin Jingyao took gold in the men's standard event; at the Games themselves host Thailand beat Vietnam 3–2 in the mixed team standard final to take gold (The Straits Times, 2025, November 22; Bangkok Post, 2025, December 13).

## Rules

The pieces differ from Western chess: the khun (king) moves one square in any direction, the met (“seed”, the queen equivalent) only one square diagonally like the shatranj ferz, the khon (nobleman) one square diagonally or one forward like the shogi silver, the ma (horse) moves as a knight and the ruea (boat) as a rook; the eight bia (cowrie-shell pawns) start on the third and sixth ranks, move one square forward and capture diagonally, with no two-square first move and no en passant (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

There is no castling, but the king may make one knight-like jump per game (sut khun); pawns promote only on the sixth rank and only to the met (bia-ngai), by flipping the piece over; instead of the 50-move rule, makruk uses counting (khaek): with no pawns left the board count must reach 64 (each ruea counts 8, ma 64, etc.), otherwise a piece count decides, and a stalemate is a draw (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-e). Men and women contest separate individual events (standard, rapid, blitz) plus a mixed team event, as at the 2025 SEA Games, and junior age groups exist at national level (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

## History

The game's ancestry runs from chaturanga in 6th-century India through shatranj in Persia to Southeast Asia; Persian and Arab traders are believed to have brought it to Ayutthaya in the 14th century, and it was also transmitted via Buddhist contacts, which is why Cambodia's ouk chatrang shares the same counting rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

Thailand's best-documented icon is Suchart Chaivichit (b. 1956), a five-time national makruk champion (1983, 1987, 1988, 1990, 1991) who also won individual medals for Thailand at the Chess Olympiads of 1982, 1988 and 1996 as a FIDE Master (Wikipedia, n.d.-f).
