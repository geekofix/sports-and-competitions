## Basic Information

Crown green bowls is a precision ball sport played almost exclusively in England — chiefly in the Midlands, Northern England and North Wales — in which two players or teams roll biased wooden bowls across a lawn toward a small white target ball called the jack. What distinguishes crown green bowls from [flat-green lawn bowls](lawn-bowls.html) is the raised "crown" in the middle of the green, around which the biased bowls curve, and the unique rule that the player who sends the jack into play chooses the direction of play in each end. A game is played to 21 points and is governed in England by the British Crown Green Bowls Association (BCGBA), whose Laws of the Game are the official rulebook (British Crown Green Bowls Association [BCGBA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.). At the top of the 2026 season, the ladies' BCGBA Senior Individual Merit was won by Andrea Ainley of Pennfields Bowling Club, while the men's title went to Danny Roberts of the Northern Club in Merseyside, and the great handicap competitions continue at their traditional venues (BCGBA, n.d.-b). The sport is open to women and men, who compete in separate ladies' and men's championships as well as in mixed pairs, alongside age-group events for veterans and under-25 players (BCGBA, n.d.-a) (British Crown Green Bowls Association, n.d.-a) (British Crown Green Bowls Association, n.d.-b). Like [Lawn Bowls](lawn-bowls.html) and [Indoor Bowls](indoor-bowls.html), crown green bowls belongs to the bowls side of the wider boules-and-bowls family that also includes [Pétanque](petanque.html), [Bocce](bocce.html), [Varpa](varpa.html), [Irish Road Bowling](irish-road-bowling.html), [Ice Stock Sport](ice-stock-sport.html), [Shuffleboard](shuffleboard.html) and [Table Shuffleboard](table-shuffleboard.html); all are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Under the BCGBA Laws of the Game, the green is usually a square or rectangle of about 45 × 45 yards (roughly 41 × 41 m) with a raised crown at its center — up to about 30 cm (1 ft) high — and a ditch around the perimeter (BCGBA, n.d.-a). Each player delivers two bowls weighing at least 2 lb (about 0.9 kg); the jack is black or yellow, weighs 666 ± 10 g, is 90.5 mm (3.5 in) wide, carries a two-full bias and is officially re-stamped every seven years; bowls are delivered from a mat about 14 cm (5.5 in) square (BCGBA, n.d.-a). A game runs to 21 points and is divided into ends: the jack sender bowls first in each end and alone decides the direction of play around the crown, which is what makes the crown decisive (BCGBA, n.d.-a). A bowl is delivered with a thumb or finger peg, and after each end the score is counted — one point for every bowl lying closer to the jack than the opponent's best bowl (BCGBA, n.d.-a). Men's and ladies' championships, mixed doubles and veterans'/under-25 categories all use the same laws; there are no equipment differences between the sexes (BCGBA, n.d.-a).

## History

Crown green bowls took shape in Victorian England, when working men in the industrial Midlands and the North began bowling on the crowned village greens of Lancashire and Yorkshire, and the BCGBA has governed the code for well over a century (Wikipedia, n.d.). The two great handicap competitions define the competitive calendar: the Talbot Handicap, staged at Blackpool from 1887 to 1975, and the Waterloo Handicap, first played in 1907 and held at Fleetwood since 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The women's side of the game grew from the early twentieth century, when ladies' championships were added alongside the men's, so that today the ladies' merit and championship events carry the same prestige as the men's (BCGBA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.).
