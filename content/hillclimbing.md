## Basic Information

Hillclimbing (hill climb) is a motorsport discipline in which a single driver races alone against the clock on a closed public mountain road, from a start at the base to a finish near the summit; the premier international series is the FIA European Hill Climb Championship, the oldest FIA championship still running today, organized with the Alliance of Organisers (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile [FIA], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 championship ran ten rounds from April to September 2026 — Rechberg (Austria), Subida al Fito (Spain), Falperra Braga (Portugal), Ecce Homo Šternberk (Czechia), Glasbachrennen Bad Liebenstein (Germany), Coppa Paolino Teodori (Italy), Limanowa (Poland), St Ursanne–Les Rangiers (Switzerland), Ilirska Bistrica (Slovenia) and Buzetski Dani (Croatia) — with the official awards ceremony on 6–8 November 2026 in Arriondas, Spain; the 2026 Category 1 (production cars) overall winner was Matija Jurišić of Croatia (Peugeot 308 TCR), with Reto Meisel of Switzerland winning Group 1 (FIA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women compete in the same categories and classes as men — there is no separate women's trophy at the European Championship. The most famous hillclimb outside Europe is the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb in Colorado, USA, held since 1916 over 19.99 km and 156 turns to a summit of 4,302 m (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

At each event, drivers take one or more timed runs — usually two to three — up the closed course, and the best time counts for the classification; only one car is on the track at a time on narrow courses (pairs on wide ones), and the clock starts at a light gate (FIA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Cars are grouped into Category 1 (production-based touring cars) and Category 2 (single-seaters E2-SS and sports cars E2-SC); since 2020 Category 1 is subdivided by the FIA Performance Factor (Pf) into classes from the single class Pf 15–39 up through classes 2a–5b, with class 5b covering Pf above 199 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Safety equipment follows FIA Appendix J — roll cage, racing seat and harnesses, helmet, HANS device and fire extinguisher are mandatory — and course records are officially recognized per venue, such as Christian Merli's Falperra course record of 1:46.944 set in 2019 in an Osella FA30 (FIA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Competition formats include the FIA European Hill Climb Championship, the FIA Hill Climb Masters for category winners, national championships and the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb; men and women compete in the same classes under identical regulations (FIA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The first hillclimb is generally dated to 1898, when the Côte de la Turbie above Nice, France, hosted the first "course de côte" for automobiles, and the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb has been held since 1916 on the mountain road to the 4,302 m summit in Colorado (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The European Hill Climb Championship has been awarded since 1957 and is run by the FIA with the Alliance of Organisers; the discipline's golden eras included factory efforts by Porsche, Ferrari and Abarth and appearances by Formula One drivers (FIA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the sport's icons are Italy's Christian Merli, record winner of the European title whose Osella prototypes set course records across Europe, Spain's André Vilariño, European champion four times between 1989 and 1992, and Switzerland's Reto Meisel, multiple national champion with his own Mercedes-Benz-based cars (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
