## Basic Information

Harri jasotzaile (stone lifter) is the Basque stone-lifting contest: the harrijasotzaile hoists a heavy, often irregular stone (harria) — from the 100 kg 'harri txikia' up to the 212 kg 'harri handia' and custom quarry stones — from the ground to the shoulder repeatedly within a time limit; the most clean lifts wins. It is the flagship herri kirolak event at festivals across Gipuzkoa, Bizkaia, Nafarroa and Araba. Women's stone lifting (harriti) has grown strongly since the 2010s. Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Aizkolaritza](aizkolaritza.html), [Giza Probak](giza-probak.html), [Ingude Altxatzea](ingude-altxatzea.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

## Rules

A lift counts when the stone rests stably on the shoulder with the arm extended; the lifter may use chalk and the traditional harness (eslinga). Time limits are usually 2–5 minutes; modern championships use weight classes, and women compete in their own categories with lighter standard stones. Dropping the stone on a person or lifting with both hands above the shoulder are fouls (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

## History

Stone lifting derives from quarry and farm work — moving boundary stones and millstones — with contests documented from the 18th century. Generations of great lifters made it a crowd favourite; since the 2000s the Herri Kirolak circuit runs regular championships and the women's harriti teams have made the sport visibly mixed-gender (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
