## Basic Information

Pétanque is a French boules sport in which players throw hollow metal balls (boules) from a stationary circle with both feet on the ground, aiming to place them closer to a small wooden target ball (the jack, or cochonnet) than their opponents. Its official rules are administered internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Pétanque et Jeu Provençal (FIPJP), which counts more than 600,000 licensed players in over 100 federations, making it one of the most widely played boules games in the world.

## Rules

The FIPJP Official Rules govern play. The throwing circle is 50 cm in diameter (some events use larger 1 m or 1.5 m circles); a player throws from inside the circle with both feet on the ground until the boule lands — the rule that gives the game its name, from the Provençal pès tancats, "feet planted". The jack is thrown 6 to 10 meters from the circle. Teams are singles (3 boules each), doubles (3 each) or triples (2 each); the team holding the closest boule after all boules are thrown scores one point for each boulet closer to the jack than the best boule of the opponents, and the game or "end" continues with a new circle drawn where the jack finished. The first team to reach 13 points wins. Measures decide close situations, and rules cover dead jacks, boules knocked out of bounds and replaced circles. World championships are staged in men's, women's, youth and precision-shooting formats.

## History

Pétanque was born in 1907 in La Ciotat near Marseille, when the former jeu provençal champion Jules Lenoir, crippled by rheumatism and no longer able to run, was accommodated with a shortened game in which players threw from a circle without a run-up; Ernest and Joseph Pitiot formalized the new game and organized the first tournament in 1910, and the ground where it began still exists as the Jules Lenoir Boulodrome. The sport spread through Provence and then the whole of France, helped by cheap, industrially cast metal boules — the manufacturer Obut was established at Saint-Bonnet-le-Château in 1955 and its smooth, grooved boules became the global standard. The FIPJP was founded on 8 March 1958 in Marseille by seven national federations, and the first Men's Triplet World Championship followed in 1959 at Spa in Belgium; pétanque is strongly identified with village squares and café terraces across France and its former colonies, and it has been part of the World Games program since 1985. The Confédération Européenne de Pétanque coordinates the European federations, and less formal play worldwide thrives because the game needs only a patch of gravel.
