## Basic Information

Watermelon seed spitting is a novelty competition in which contestants spit watermelon seeds as far as possible, with distances measured from the spit-line to where the seed lands — a humorous folk contest that celebrates summer fruit and has become a fixture of American small-town festivals. It requires little beyond a stock of seeds, a marked lane and a measuring tape, yet competitors develop genuine technique in breath control, seed placement and trajectory.

The sport is best known from Luling, Texas, home of the annual Watermelon Thumping Festival and its seed-spitting championships, and it has produced decades of documented records.

## Rules

Competitors are given a supply of watermelon seeds and take turns spitting from behind a marked line, usually within a set number of attempts (often three). The shot is measured from the line to the final resting point of the seed, and the longest valid spit counts. Typical rules require contestants to keep their feet behind the line, not step across, and forbid foreign substances added to the seed; some contests divide age classes and team events (with distance totals added for a squad). Distances of around 20 to 30 feet are common at championship level.

## History

The oldest and most famous contest grew up in Luling, Texas, whose Watermelon Thumping Festival has featured seed-spitting championships since 1954, alongside crowning a Watermelon Queen. The festival emerged from the town's position as a major watermelon-growing and shipping centre, and the spitting contest became its signature event. The pastime has since spread to watermelon festivals in several states including Indiana, where the annual Pride of the Patch festival runs contests, and the Luling event continues to claim the world championship mantle each June.
