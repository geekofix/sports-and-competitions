## Basic Information

Tricking is the freestyle martial arts-acrobatic sport of tricked kicks: athletes combine martial arts kicks (from taekwondo and other striking arts) with gymnastics tumbling, flips and twists borrowed from breaking and parkour improvisation, creating flowing combos of kicks, flips and transitions. Born in the internet era and organized through communities, gatherings and world championships, it is one of the defining movement arts of modern acrobatic culture.

## Rules

There is no standardized federation rulebook across the global community, but competitions conventionally run as battles or choreographed runs: in battle events, athletes take turns throwing short combo sequences (30 to 60 seconds) judged on difficulty, execution (clean rotation, height, controlled landings), variety and style, with head-to-head brackets deciding winners; in tricking gatherings (sessions) the jam culture rewards creativity over medals. A combo conventionally consists of kicks, flips and transitions linked without stopping, with tricks graded informally by difficulty; landing each trick cleanly and in control is the mark of a good tricker, and the vocabulary names tricks from the tornado kick to the cork and variations on twist-double-backflip families.

## History

Tricking grew from martial arts demonstration culture of the 1990s, when martial artists began blending kicks with gymnastics and breakdance-inspired movements in forms demos; dedicated forums and early videos in the 2000s named the discipline and built the global vocabulary, with the biggest growth through the online communities after YouTube launched. The first large tricking gatherings and world championship events (with the Loopkicks camps in the United States and later European and Asian communities) professionalized the sport's loose structure, with the modern scene featuring major annual gatherings, championship titles, cross-pollination with freerunning, breaking and kung fu cinema, and standout stars whose video parts define the discipline's standards.
