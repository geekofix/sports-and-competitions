## Basic Information

Mounted games are the cavalry-derived equestrian games played at speed on horseback — tent pegging, gymkhana races such as the keyhole, pole bending and barrel race, ball-and-cone relays and mounted polo — governed internationally by the International Mounted Games Association (IMGA) under its Rule Book 2026 (IMGA, n.d.). The IMGA championship calendar includes the World Championship (2025 at the Parc Equestre FFE in Lamotte, France), the European Team Championship 2026 at La Bonde, France, with ten teams including first-time Czechia at U12 level, the World Pairs Championship 2026 at Corné, France, and the World Individual Championship 2026 at Luhmühlen, Germany, on 27 July–2 August 2026 (IMGA, n.d.). Tent pegging, the best-known mounted game, is one of the ten equestrian disciplines recognized by the FEI and is governed by the International Tent Pegging Federation (ITPF), founded on 21 March 2013 in Gurgaon, India, and headquartered in Muscat, Oman, with 45 member countries and rules effective since 1 April 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women and men compete together in teams and individual classes. At the end of the 2026 season the reigning open world pairs champions are Paul Barreau and Anthony Rouquette of France (IMGA, n.d.) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the IMGA Rule Book 2026, a team fields four riders, each riding a set of the games — the IMGA race list includes Ball & Cone, Ball & Flag, Keyhole, Pole Bending, Barrels, Relays, Agility Aces and others — and the team's combined times and faults decide the placings; games are timed races where the fastest clean run wins and faults add penalty seconds (IMGA, n.d.). In tent pegging under the ITPF rules (effective 1 April 2022), the rider gallops down a straight of about 100–120 m, first with a lance and then with a sword, to strike and pick up a small ground peg (6 cm in the first run), scoring points for a clean strike, picking the peg up and the distance it is carried (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Riders wear helmets and protective gear; women and men compete in the same classes without separate categories (IMGA, n.d.).

## History

Mounted games evolved from cavalry training: tent pegging was practiced by mounted riders since at least the 4th century BC and spread with the Eurasian empires; the competitive sport crystallized in the 19th century, when George Gribble organized a military tournament in Dublin in 1868 and Queen Victoria donated a silver cup for the champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Tent pegging became one of the ten disciplines recognized by the FEI, is contested at the Asian Games, and remains a stronghold in India and Pakistan; the ITPF was founded in 2013 and has run a World Cup since 2014 — won by South Africa in Oman in 2014, by Saudi Arabia in George, South Africa in 2023, and by Saudi Arabia again in 2026, with Saudi rider Hassan Asiri the individual world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The IMGA's mounted games championships continue the tradition of the British cavalry games, in which teams of riders compete in a program of races and skill games (IMGA, n.d.).
