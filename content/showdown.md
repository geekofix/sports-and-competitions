## Basic Information

Showdown is a fast-moving table sport for blind and vision-impaired athletes — often described as the blind community's answer to air hockey or table tennis — played by two players at opposite ends of a specially designed table, who bat an audible ball with flat wooden paddles under a centre screen into the opponent's goal pocket; it is governed by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA), whose Showdown Sub-Committee issues the official rules and runs world and regional championships and world rankings (IBSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Sighted players may practise the game but are not allowed in IBSA tournaments; in the United States it is called "power showdown" after Paralympic wrestler Jim Mastro, who promoted it from 2006, and in Oceania the related Australian game "swish" — invented by Ray Hanner in Victoria with a 75 cm wooden board and a 9 cm gap — is played (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Both women and men compete at the highest level: the IBSA world championships crown individual women's and men's champions, and Poland's Elżbieta Mielzcarek was women's world champion at the 2019 Olbia championships alongside men's champion Adrian Sloninka; at the 2025 world championships Poland ranked first team ahead of Finland and Belgium, the 11th IBSA Europe championships in Sofia in August 2024 were also topped by Poland, and the 2026 IBSA Asia championships will be held in Seoul in August 2026 (IBSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The showdown table has an interior of 366 × 122 cm, stands 78 cm high with 14 cm sidewalls, and carries a centre board screen with a 10 cm opening at its base; the goal pockets are 30 cm in diameter in the deck and 30 × 10 cm at the end wall, marked by a tactile 40 cm goal circle (IBSA, n.d.). The ball is 6 cm in diameter, weighs 23–28 g, has a hard smooth surface and contains BBs to make it audible, must be IBSA-approved and is manufactured in Denmark; players wear eyeshades and a glove on the batting hand (IBSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). To serve, a player must bat the ball so it bounces off the side wall exactly once before passing under the centre screen — an incorrect serve costs 1 point to the opponent; each player serves twice in a row, a goal scores 2 points, and one point goes to the opponent when the other player hits the ball into the screen or off the table, touches the ball in the goal area with bat or batting hand, touches it with anything but the bat, traps it for more than two seconds, or touches the eyeshades without permission (Wikipedia, n.d.). A set is won at 11 points with a two-point margin, a match is best of three sets (semifinals and finals best of five), players change sides after each set and in the deciding set after six points, and spectators must stay quiet during play so the players can hear the ball — cheering is allowed only after the referee's whistle (IBSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport is open to all vision-impairment classes, and there are no separate gender rules — women and men play the identical game (IBSA, n.d.).


## History

Showdown was designed in 1977 by Joe Lewis, a totally blind Canadian, so that people with vision impairments could play without sighted assistance, and the totally blind Canadian athlete Patrick York collaborated with Lewis on the refinements and was the major influence on the table design; the first table and game were played in 1980 (Wikipedia, n.d.; IBSA, n.d.). The sport was an international success as a demonstration sport at the 1980 Olympiad for the Physically Disabled in Arnhem, Netherlands, and was later demonstrated at the New York 1984, Seoul 1988, Barcelona 1992 and Atlanta 1996 Paralympic Games (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first official IBSA world championship was held in Stockholm, Sweden, in August 2009 with 16 nations; IBSA Showdown world championships are usually held every four years — the 2019 edition in Olbia, Italy, crowned Adrian Sloninka and Elżbieta Mielzcarek of Poland — and at the 2025 world championships Poland ranked first team, followed by Finland, Belgium, Latvia, Slovakia, Estonia, Slovenia, Germany, South Korea, Bulgaria, Czechia, Denmark, Italy and Switzerland (IBSA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
