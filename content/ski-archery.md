## Basic Information

Ski archery — also called archery biathlon — is a winter sport that combines cross-country skiing with target shooting from a recurve bow, modeled on biathlon but using a bow and arrows instead of a rifle; it is administered within the archery family by World Archery (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Archery, n.d.). Athletes ski a set course — traditionally 12 km for men and 8 km for women — stopping at shooting ranges to loose arrows at 16 cm targets from 18 m, with a 150 m penalty loop skied for every missed target, and the fastest total time wins (Wikipedia, n.d.). The summer equivalent is run archery, admitted as a World Archery discipline in 2003, in which athletes run 400 m or 1,000 m laps and shoot series of arrows at the same 18 m / 16 cm targets, with 60 m or 150 m penalty loops (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate races over different course lengths but with identical shooting rules, distances and target sizes (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Archery, n.d.).

## Rules

World Archery (https://worldarchery.sport/) governs ski archery and run archery alongside the Olympic archery disciplines. In ski archery, the bow — usually a laminated carbon- or glass-fibre recurve — is carried in the hand or on the back while skiing, and the skis may never be removed: in the kneeling position the skis may be unfastened, but the feet must remain in contact with them (Wikipedia, n.d.). Shooting is at folding targets 16 cm in diameter from 18 m, in both standing and kneeling positions; each missed target costs a 150 m penalty loop, and race formats include individual and relay events over courses of 8 km for women and 12 km for men with a shooting bout every 4 km, one of them from the kneeling position (Wikipedia, n.d.). Run archery offers a sprint of three 400 m laps with two shooting bouts — six arrows for four targets, standing then kneeling — and 60 m penalty loops, and a 4 km individual race of four 1,000 m laps with three bouts of four arrows for four targets and 150 m loops (Wikipedia, n.d.). There are no gender-specific equipment or target rules — the same bow rules, 18 m distance and 16 cm target apply to women and men, who race over different course lengths (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Hunting on skis with a bow is ancient — Scandinavian depictions date to around 1000 BC, and the first known Norwegian soldier competition took place in 1767 — but modern competitive ski archery was invented in the 1980s by the Italian Franco Carminati in the Piedmont Alps (Wikipedia, n.d.). It was recognized by the Italian archery federation FITARCO in 1984 and by the international federation FITA (today World Archery) in 1991, and the first World Championship was held in Cogne, Italy, in 1998 (Wikipedia, n.d.). From 2001 to 2005 the discipline was administered by the International Biathlon Union (IBU) under the name archery biathlon, returning to World Archery on 1 April 2005; until 2007 it was practiced in Italy, Slovenia, Austria, Germany, France, Japan and the United States, and today it survives mainly in Russia and Eastern Europe, while run archery has grown in central and western Europe and has had a European Cup since 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
