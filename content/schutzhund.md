## Basic Information

Schutzhund — competed internationally today as IGP (Internationale Prüfungsordnung) and previously known as IPO and Mondioring — is a dog sport developed in Germany in the early 1900s to test the tracking, obedience and protection abilities of working dogs, above all the German Shepherd; it is governed worldwide by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI), which stages the annual IGP World Championship, and by the WUSV (Weltunion der Vereine für Deutsche Schäferhunde), which stages its own world championship, and in 2025 Schutzhund and related working-dog activities were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (Wikipedia, n.d.; WUSV, n.d.). The WUSV World Championship IGP 2025 took place 1–5 October 2025 in Nova Gorica, Slovenia, with 138 handlers from around the world, and the 2026 edition follows 5–11 October 2026 at the same stadium; the 33rd FCI IGP World Championship was held in 2025 (WUSV, n.d.; WDSF, n.d.).

Women compete as handlers on equal terms with men at every level, from the BH companion-dog test to the world championships — Dutch handler Sylvia Ozinga was among the starters at the 2025 world championships — and the leading nations are Germany, the Czech Republic, the Netherlands, Slovakia and Ukraine; among the most successful handlers in the sport's history is Germany's Peter Scherk, FCI world champion in 2009 (Schwanenstadt) and 2010 (Hämeenlinna) with his Belgian Malinois Bendix vom Adlerauge (WDSF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

A Schutzhund/IGP trial consists of three phases, each scored out of 100 points: tracking (Fährte), in which the dog must follow a track laid hours earlier by a track layer — from 600 steps at IGP 1 up to 4,000 steps over three hours at IGP 3 — and indicate the articles the track layer dropped; obedience (Unterordnung), with heelwork on and off lead, position changes on command, recall, retrieves and the send-up (retrieve over a 1 m hurdle); and protection (Schutzdienst), with searches of the field, guarding of a found object, defence against the decoy, the courage test and the release on command (Wikipedia, n.d.; FCI, n.d.). A team must score at least 70 points in each of the three phases to earn the title; the levels run IGP 1, IGP 2 and IGP 3, and the BH/VT companion dog test (obedience plus traffic safety) is the prerequisite for entering IGP 1 (FCI, n.d.). In tracking and protection the dog works off-leash from a harness or flat collar; the decoy is a trained helper (figurant) wearing a padded suit, and modern rules are based on positive, drive-oriented training — the dog bites only the helper's padded sleeve during protection work, never a defenceless person (Wikipedia, n.d.). Any breed may enter, but the intermediate and advanced levels are dominated by German Shepherds and Belgian Malinois, with Dobermanns, Rottweilers, Giant Schnauzers and Bouviers des Flandres also successful; men and women handlers compete under identical rules and in the same classes (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Schutzhund was developed in Germany after Max von Stephanitz (1864–1936) founded the Verein für Deutsche Schäferhunde (SV) in 1899 and sought a working-ability test to preserve the breed's character; the first Schutzhund trials were held in the early 1900s — the first major German Schutzhund competition dates to 1906 — and in Germany only tested dogs may be registered for breeding, which kept the German Shepherd's working traits alive (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport spread internationally as IPO and Mondioring; the WUSV was founded to unite the German Shepherd clubs of the world, and today the FCI stages the annual IGP World Championship (33rd edition in 2025) while the WUSV stages its own world championship in Nova Gorica (Wikipedia, n.d.; WUSV, n.d.). In 2025 the German UNESCO Commission inscribed "Gebrauchshundewesen" — working-dog handling, the human–dog relationship with social responsibility — on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (Wikipedia, n.d.). The founding icon of the sport is Max von Stephanitz (Wikipedia, n.d.).
