## Basic Information

Freestyle kayak (kayak rodeo) is judged acrobatic paddling: the athlete performs tricks — spins, loops, cartwheels, airscrews — on a standing wave or in a hydraulic, scored by a panel for difficulty, variety, execution and linked combinations (ICF, n.d.). Boat classes are kayak (K1), decked canoe (C1), open canoe and squirt boat; the ICF Canoe Freestyle World Championships have been held since 2007, and the American Jackson family — Eric (first K1 world champion 2007), Emily and Dane (four titles) — are its biggest names (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are [Playboating](playboating.html), [Canoe Freestyle](canoe-freestyle.html), [Kayak Cross](kayak-cross.html), [Canoe Slalom](canoe-slalom.html), [Whitewater Rafting Race](whitewater-rafting-race.html), [Hydrospeed](hydrospeed.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Runs last 45–60 seconds (world-championship format: heats, semifinals, final); judges award points for moves (each with a base value) plus bonuses for linked combinations, with penalties for hand touches. Boats are short (about 1.7–2.0 m) with flat hulls and fins for spins; helmets and life jackets are mandatory. Men's and women's K1 and C1 events are held in identical formats; junior and squirt classes exist at national level (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Kayak rodeo grew out of 1970s US river-running culture; the first unofficial world championships were held in the 1990s and the first ICF-sanctioned event in 2007 on the Ottawa River, with a World Cup series from 2008. Eric Jackson won the first official K1 title in 2007, beginning the Jackson family's dynasty (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
