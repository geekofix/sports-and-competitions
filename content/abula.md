## Basic Information

Abula is a traditional Nigerian game similar to volleyball, except that the ball is struck with a wooden bat rather than the hands; it is played at Nigeria's National Sports Festival and in schools (Wikipedia, n.d.). Within the folk and festival games on this site, its closest relatives are [Langa](langa.html), [Ampe](ampe.html), [Asafo Atwele](asafo-atwele.html), [Gidigbo](gidigbo.html), [Kho Kho](kho-kho.html), [Kabaddi](kabaddi.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Two teams hit a ball back and forth over an imaginary or marked net using a wooden bat; a team scores when the ball touches the ground on the opponent's side or the opponents fail to return it. Informal rules vary by region; school versions use a tennis ball and a low rope as net. Played by boys and girls alike in Nigerian schoolyards (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Abula belongs to the family of Nigerian traditional games documented at the National Sports Festival; like langa and ten-ten it reflects pre-colonial play patterns and remains a schoolyard staple (Wikipedia, n.d.).
