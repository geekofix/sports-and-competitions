## Basic Information

Candlepin bowling is a pin-bowling variant played almost exclusively in New England and the Canadian Maritime provinces, governed internationally by the International Candlepin Bowling Association (ICBA), whose bylaws were approved on 9 January 1986 so that bowlers in all member states and provinces play under identical rules (International Candlepin Bowling Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game uses a handheld ball and tall, slender pins that resemble candles — hence the name — on the same 60-foot (18 m) lane as tenpin bowling (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The pin-bowling family also includes [bowling (ten-pin)](bowling.html), [ten-pin bowling](ten-pin-bowling.html), [nine-pin bowling](nine-pin-bowling.html) and [duckpin bowling](duckpin-bowling.html), which differ in pin and ball size and in whether fallen pins are cleared between deliveries.

The defining quirk is the deadwood rule: fallen pins ("wood") that remain entirely behind the deadwood line stay on the lane for the rest of the frame and can be used to carom into standing pins, which makes candlepin a game of angles rather than power and is why the highest sanctioned single game is only 245 pins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; International Candlepin Bowling Association, n.d.-b).

The ICBA crowns separate men's and women's adult champions every fall from state and provincial qualifiers — the 2025 ICBA Championships again featured distinct men's and women's tournaments — and runs a youth championship each November, so women compete at the highest level on equal terms (International Candlepin Bowling Association, n.d.-c). The greatest women's bowler of all time was Stasia Czernicki of Webster, Massachusetts, an eight-time world champion who also held the women's singles, doubles and five-string records; the ICBA's sportsmanship award is named after her (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

A candlepin game is ten frames with three balls per frame, not two as in tenpin. The ball is 4+1/8 to 4+1/2 inches (about 11.4 cm) in diameter, weighs at most 2 lb 7 oz (1.11 kg) — roughly the same as a pin — and has no finger holes; no oil is applied to the lane, so the ball rolls the whole way down (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Candlepins are 15+3/4 to 16 inches (40 cm) tall, only 2+15/16 inches (75 mm) in diameter and about 2 lb 8 oz (1.1 kg) each; because they are vertically symmetrical they may be set on either end. The deadwood line is 24 inches (61 cm) in front of the head-pin spot: wood entirely behind it is live and playable, wood touching or forward of it is dead and removed, and wood in the gutter is dead, so a ball touching gutter wood first scores nothing. The bowler must call "fair" for pins felled to count (International Candlepin Bowling Association, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Scoring follows tenpin conventions, but because strikes are rare a typical league average is around 100 and the sanctioned record game is 245 (Ralph Semb, 1984, matched by Chris Sargent, 2011). Men's and women's tournaments, singles, doubles and team events are all sanctioned separately under ICBA rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; International Candlepin Bowling Association, n.d.-c).

## History

The ICBA dates the first game to Worcester, Massachusetts, in 1880, credited to Justin White, owner of a billiards and bowling hall; a 1987 Sports Illustrated account instead credits billiards player John J. Monsey with inventing and standardizing the game in 1881. In 1906 Monsey created the National Duckpin and Candlepin Congress, which fixed ball size, pin shape and lane characteristics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Originally the pins were thin dowels — one-inch-thick pins resembling actual candles gave the game its name. In 1947 lawyers Howard Dowd and Lionel Barrow invented the first automatic candlepin pinsetter (the "Bowl-Mor", patented 1956). Television made the sport a New England institution: WHDH-TV/Channel 5 and later WCVB-TV broadcast candlepin from 1958 to 1996, the Boston Globe launched its own annual tournament in 1964, and in 1973 WHDH began Candlepins for Cash. The World Candlepin Bowling Council started its Hall of Fame in 1965, and the ICBA was formed in 1986 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
