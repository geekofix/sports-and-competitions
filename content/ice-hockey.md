## Basic Information

Ice hockey is the team sport played on an ice rink in which two teams of six players (five skaters and a goaltender) use sticks to shoot a hard rubber puck into the opponent's goal; it is governed worldwide by the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) (International Ice Hockey Federation [IIHF], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The leading women's national team is the United States, which won Olympic gold at Milano Cortina 2026 (2–1 in overtime against Canada on 19 February 2026) and then its record 12th IIHF Women's World Championship title at the 2026 tournament in Herning and Esbjerg, Denmark (6–16 November 2026), going 7–0–0–0 with 56–4 goals and beating Canada 6–2 in the final — Hilary Knight set the record with her 10th world/Olympic gold medal, and Hayley Scamurra completed a rare treble of Olympic gold, PWHL Walter Cup (Montréal Victoire) and world gold in 2026 (IIHF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2026 Women's World Championship used a new format — ten teams in two balanced groups of five, with the tournament moved to November — and women's hockey has been an Olympic sport since 1998 and an IIHF world championship sport since 1990 (IIHF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the men's side the historic rivalry is between Canada and the USA, with the NHL as the top professional league (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (International Ice Hockey Federation, n.d.-a) (International Ice Hockey Federation, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the IIHF rulebook, the rink is 60 × 30 m with rounded corners, divided by two blue lines and a red centre line; each team fields six players at once — five skaters and one goaltender — from a squad of up to 20 players (18 skaters) (IIHF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match consists of three periods of 20 minutes; if tied, overtime is played 3-on-3 for five minutes in round-robin play (sudden death) and 5-on-5 sudden death in playoffs, followed by a shootout where needed (IIHF, n.d.-a). Fouls are punished with penalties — minor (2 min), double-minor (4 min), major (5 min), misconduct (10 min) — and the fouled team plays a power play while the offender is in the penalty box; icing and offside (the puck must enter the attacking zone ahead of the skater) are the classic stoppages, and play restarts with a face-off (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Body checking is permitted in men's hockey but prohibited in women's hockey, where checking is a penalty — one of the main gender-specific rule differences; both sexes play identical rink, period and offside rules (IIHF, n.d.-a). Two referees and two linesmen officiate (IIHF, n.d.-a).

## History

Modern ice hockey developed in Canada in the mid-19th century: the first recorded organized game was played on 3 March 1875 at the Victoria Skating Rink in Montreal, organized by James Creighton, whose codified rules (six players, a puck, goals) form the basis of today's game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The IIHF was founded on 15 May 1908 in Paris by France, Belgium, Switzerland and Great Britain; the Stanley Cup, first awarded in 1893, became the oldest professional sports trophy in North America, and the National Hockey League was founded in 1917 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Ice hockey has been an Olympic men's sport since the 1920 Antwerp Games and a women's sport since Nagano 1998; the IIHF Women's World Championship has been held since 1990 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons of the men's game include Canada's Wayne Gretzky ("The Great One", holder of the NHL's scoring records), Gordie Howe, Bobby Orr and Maurice Richard; icons of the women's game include Canada's Hayley Wickenheiser, the USA's Angela Ruggiero (four Olympic medals) and Manon Rhéaume, who in 1992 became the first woman to play in an NHL exhibition game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).
