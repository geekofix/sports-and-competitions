## Basic Information

Bike trial is a non-racing cycling discipline in which riders on 20-inch or 26-inch trial bikes navigate obstacle courses — rocks, logs, concrete blocks, stairs — without touching their feet to the ground. Motorcycle trial (moto trial) is the motorized equivalent. Bike trial is governed by the UCI, which runs the UCI Trial World Championships and the UCI Trials World Cup.

## Rules

Under UCI trial rules, riders complete a course of marked "sections" containing obstacles; each time a foot touches the ground ("dab") one penalty point is added, and five dabs in a section means elimination from that section. Riders may choose their line and can stop or move backward without penalty as long as no foot touches. At the World Championships, riders compete in 20-inch and 26-inch categories; the winner is the rider with the fewest penalty points over all sections. In the World Cup, qualification and final rounds are scored the same way.

## History

Motorcycle trials began in the early 20th century as reliability tests for motorcycles over rough terrain without speed. Bicycle trial emerged in the 1970s-80s, especially in Spain and France, as riders adapted trials techniques to pedal bikes.
