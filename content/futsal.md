## Basic Information

Futsal is the FIFA-sanctioned indoor variant of association football: two teams of five players (four outfield players plus a goalkeeper) play on a hard court of 40 × 20 m (internationally 38–42 × 20–25 m) for two halves of 20 minutes of effective time, with unlimited flying substitutions, no offside, and a smaller, low-bounce ball (FIFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by FIFA, which runs the FIFA Futsal World Cup (men's since 1989, women's since 2025) and, since 6 May 2024, the official FIFA Futsal World Ranking — Brazil leads both the men's and the women's ranking (FIFA, n.d.-b). In Europe, UEFA organizes the UEFA Futsal Euro; the 2026 edition (13th) was co-hosted by Latvia, Lithuania and Slovenia from 21 January to 7 February 2026, and Spain won its eighth title by beating defending champions Portugal 5–3 in the final at Arena Stožice in Ljubljana on 7 February 2026 (UEFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The men's FIFA Futsal World Cup 2024 in Uzbekistan was won by Brazil, and the inaugural FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup was held in the Philippines (Philsports Arena, Pasig) from 21 November to 7 December 2025, with Brazil beating Portugal 3–0 in the final, Spain third and Argentina fourth (FIFA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). FIFA opened bidding in September 2026 for the 2028 men's World Cup and the 2029 women's edition (FIFA, n.d.-b). Futsal is the FIFA-codified indoor member of the football family, next to [Indoor Soccer](indoor-soccer.html), [Five-a-side Football](five-a-side-football.html) and [Blind Football](blind-football.html), and within the wider variants family that also includes [Football](football.html), [Beach Soccer](beach-soccer.html), [Seven-a-side Football](seven-a-side-football.html), [Six-man Football](six-man-football.html), [Swamp Soccer](swamp-soccer.html), [Walking Football](walking-football.html), [Fire-ball Soccer](fire-ball-soccer.html), [Amputee Football](amputee-football.html), [Cerebral Palsy Football](cerebral-palsy-football.html), [Powerchair Football](powerchair-football.html), [Royal Shrovetide Football](royal-shrovetide-football.html), [Calcio Fiorentino](calcio-fiorentino.html) and [Cuju](cuju.html).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Brazil's national team, winners of the 2025 inaugural Women's World Cup and number one in the FIFA Futsal World Ranking ahead of Portugal (1,575.32 points), Spain and Argentina (FIFA, n.d.-b). On the men's side, Brazil remain world champions and world number one, with Spain the 2026 European champion and Portugal the 2021 world champion (FIFA, n.d.-b; UEFA, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the FIFA Futsal Laws of the Game 2025/26, a match is played on a rectangular court 38–42 m long and 20–25 m wide (25–42 × 16–25 m for non-international matches), marked with lines, with goals 3 m wide and 2 m high, over two 20-minute halves of effective time (the clock stops for the ball out of play, fouls and free kicks) and a 15-minute half-time (FIFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each team fields five players — four outfield players and a goalkeeper — with unlimited substitutions on the fly during stoppages; the goalkeeper may receive the ball back from a teammate only after it has crossed the halfway line or been touched by an opponent, and may hold the ball for a maximum of four seconds (the "4-second rule," which also applies to kick-ins, corner kicks and free kicks) (FIFA, n.d.-a). There is no offside in futsal (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). From the sixth accumulated foul per half, each further foul is punished with a direct free kick from the 10 m mark (second penalty spot) without a wall; each team may take one timeout per half (FIFA, n.d.-a). In knockout matches, a tie after 20 minutes is followed by two 5-minute halves of extra time and then penalty kicks from the 6 m mark (FIFA, n.d.-a). The ball is size 4 (62–64 cm circumference, 400–440 g, lower bounce) (FIFA, n.d.-a). Men's and women's competitions use identical Laws of the Game with separate tournaments; youth categories U17 and U20 exist, and 14-player squads are registered for FIFA final tournaments (FIFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Futsal traces its organized origins to 1930 in Montevideo, Uruguay, where Juan Carlos Ceriani devised the indoor five-a-side game for the YMCA, taking the court size from basketball and rules from football, handball, basketball and water polo; the first written rules were produced in Brazil in the 1950s, and the Federação Internacional de Futsal (FIFUSA, founded 1971 in São Paulo) organized the first Futsal World Championship in 1982, also in São Paulo (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). When FIFA took over the international game in 1989, it held the first FIFA Futsal World Championship in the Netherlands, won by Brazil, and the sport has since been dominated by Brazil, Spain and Portugal — Brazil won the 2024 World Cup in Uzbekistan, and in 2025 the Philippines hosted the first FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup, won by Brazil over Portugal 3–0 (FIFA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). In Europe, UEFA has run the European Championship since 1996 (six teams), expanded to 16 teams in 2022, and the 2026 edition in the Baltics and Slovenia ended with Spain's 5–3 final win over Portugal in Ljubljana (UEFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Futsal was a demonstration sport at the 2018 Youth Olympics and a medal sport at the Chengdu 2025 World Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
