## Basic Information

Kumis Alu (Kazakh for take the silver) is the traditional equestrian pickup game of Kazakhstan: at full gallop, riders lean from the saddle to snatch silver coins or handkerchiefs from the ground, testing the balance, timing and horsemanship of steppe riders in a display rooted in nomadic feats of skill.

It belongs to the family of Kazakh national games contested at festivals and Naadam-style gatherings, alongside pickup-and-race events, and its close Kyrgyz cousin is the coin-picking Tyin Enmei.

## Rules

Silver coins or handkerchiefs are placed at set intervals along a straight grass track, and riders gallop the course at full speed, leaning down from the saddle to lift each item without slowing or losing the saddle. The winner is the rider who collects the most items, with the fastest time deciding ties; festivals run the event as part of multi-game programmes with youth and open classes.

## History

The ability to snatch objects from the ground at full gallop was a steppe cavalry and hunting skill celebrated across Central Asian nomadic cultures, and games built on the pickup feats became standard festival contests long ago, gathered into the canon of Kazakh national games through the 20th century. Kumis alu is taught in equestrian centres and showcased at national holiday meetings and games tournaments in Kazakhstan, alongside racing and wrestling events, and it appears internationally at nomad culture festivals celebrating steppe heritage.
