## Basic Information

The Kirkwall Ba Game is the mass football of Orkney's capital: on Christmas Day and New Year's Day, hundreds of men of the town divide into Uppies and Doonies and fight to carry a leather ba (ball) to goals at opposite ends of the town — the Uppies touching it to a wall in the south, the Doonies getting it into the basin of the sea in the north. The game can surge through streets, closes and gardens for hours, and the young men who score carry the ba home as a trophy.

The Ba is one of the living survivors of medieval street football in Scotland, alongside festival cousins such as the Royal Shrovetide Football in England.

## Rules

The ba is thrown up at the Mercat Cross at 1 p.m. to begin the men's game, and the scrummaging pack wrestles it — with rules basically reduced to no vehicles and the town itself as the field — with the Uppies trying to touch the ba against a wall in the south of the town and the Doonies trying to get it into the water at the north. Boys' and girls' games are held earlier the same days, and once a side wins, the ba is presented to a chosen player. Membership of Uppie or Doonie follows family tradition, with each player's side usually inherited through the family.

## History

Street football between parish halves was common in Scottish burghs from at least the 1600s, and records of the Kirkwall Ba go back to a first recorded mention in 1650. The game survived Victorian prohibitions that killed many cousin games in other towns and continued through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, helped by Orkney island pride; it remains a twice-yearly ritual and identity marker for Orkney families.
