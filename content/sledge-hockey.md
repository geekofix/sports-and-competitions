## Basic Information

Sledge hockey — officially renamed Para ice hockey by the International Paralympic Committee on 30 November 2016 — is ice hockey played from a sled, governed worldwide by World Para Ice Hockey under the IPC (IPC, n.d.-b). The sport was invented in the early 1960s at a rehabilitation centre in Stockholm, Sweden, where two Swedish players attached two skate blades under a metal sled and used shortened hockey sticks with pick ends to propel themselves (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It became a Paralympic medal sport at Lillehammer 1994 — Sweden won the first gold — and has been mixed-gender since Vancouver 2010, with women eligible to play on the men's teams (IPC, n.d.-b). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Paralympic Games, held 7–15 March 2026 at the PalaItalia in Milan with eight teams, the United States beat Canada 6–2 in the final on 15 March 2026, their fifth consecutive Paralympic gold (2010–2026) — making the USA the first country to win men's and women's ice hockey and sledge hockey golds at the same Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Japan's Akari Fukunishi became the fourth woman ever to play Paralympic sledge hockey at Milano Cortina 2026, and the United States also won the 2025 World Para Ice Hockey Championship, where the 17-year-old Liam Cunningham was the youngest member of the squad (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the current leading women in the sport is Akari Fukunishi of Japan, one of the pioneers of women's participation at the Paralympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Para ice hockey follows the IIHF rulebook with modifications defined by World Para Ice Hockey: teams field six players including a goaltender, on standard 60 × 30 m ice rinks, playing three 15-minute periods (IPC, n.d.-b). Players sit on sleds roughly 80 cm high with two skate blades underneath and use two sticks — one with a pick end for propulsion on the ice and one with a blade end for shooting and passing (IPC, n.d.-b). Body checking is forbidden; players propel with their picks and control the sled with the blade ends. Since Vancouver 2010 the teams are mixed-gender: women may be rostered on the men's national teams, and a separate women's tournament has grown alongside, while junior and development programmes exist below the senior Paralympic level (IPC, n.d.-a). Classification is based on lower-limb function, and the Paralympic tournament brings together eight qualified nations; the World Championship is held annually outside Paralympic years (IPC, n.d.-a).

## History

Sledge hockey was invented in the early 1960s at a rehabilitation centre in Stockholm, Sweden, when two Swedish players fitted skate blades under a sled and used sticks with pick ends, and a five-team championship was held in Stockholm in 1969 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). International competition began in 1971 with five European teams, and the sport spread to Great Britain in 1981, Canada in 1982, the United States in 1990, and Estonia and Japan in 1993 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It appeared as a demonstration sport at the Örnsköldsvik 1976 Winter Paralympics and became a full medal sport at Lillehammer 1994, where Sweden won gold; it has been mixed-gender since Vancouver 2010, and the IPC renamed it Para ice hockey on 30 November 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the sport's icons is the American Rico Roman, a three-time Paralympic gold medallist who also served in the US Army, alongside fellow American Josh Sweeney, both retired from the international game (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Declan Farmer, with five Paralympic golds between 2014 and 2026 for the United States, is another defining figure of the modern era (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
