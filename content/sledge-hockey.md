## Basic Information

Sledge hockey — known since 2016 as Para ice hockey — is the ice hockey adaptation for athletes with physical impairments in the lower body, played on a standard ice hockey rink by players seated on sledges (sleds), propelling themselves with two-headed sticks whose blade ends in picks. It has been a Winter Paralympic sport since Lillehammer 1994 and is today one of the most popular events of the Winter Paralympic programme, governed internationally by World Para Ice Hockey, a division of the International Paralympic Committee.

## Rules

Players sit on aluminium or steel sledges mounted on two skate blades, strapped in at the waist and legs, and move and handle the puck with sticks that have a blade at one end and metal picks at the other end of the shaft: the picks dig into the ice to propel, turn and stop, and the blade plays the puck, so sledge hockey is genuinely full contact, with checking and the pace of standing ice hockey. Games follow IIHF-style rules — three periods, six players per side (including a goaltender), icing and offside, penalties in the box — with rule adaptations for the sledge (e.g. Teeing: charging an opponent with the sledge front is penalised). Team rosters are mixed-gender (women have played at the Paralympics since 2010), and eligibility requires a permanent lower-body impairment of a degree that makes ordinary skating impossible — amputation, spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, leg shortening and similar — with classification based on impairment impact on skating.

## History

The sport was invented in the early 1960s at a rehabilitation centre in Stockholm by two Swedish men with disabilities who wanted to keep playing hockey and designed a sledge with two skate blades on a metal frame that let the puck pass underneath, using sticks fitted with round poles and bike handles. Cross-national competition began in 1971 among five European teams; Great Britain founded a team in 1981, Canada in 1982, the United States in 1990, and Estonia and Japan in 1993 — a spread that made sledge hockey a natural flagship for the Winter Paralympics, into which it entered at Lillehammer 1994 with Sweden winning the first gold. Paralympic history since has been dominated by the United States and Canada — whose rivalry delivered the golden-goal 2010 final — and more recently by the USA's multiple titles and by a 2020s circuit including strong programmes in the Czech Republic, Italy, South Korea, and China; in November 2016 the IPC renamed the sport "Para ice hockey" (and its sanctioning body World Para Ice Hockey) for linguistic reasons, but the sledge-hockey name remains in everyday use across the game's heartlands.
