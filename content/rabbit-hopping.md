## Basic Information

Rabbit hopping (Swedish: *kaninhoppning*, also called rabbit agility) is an animal sport in which domestic rabbits, wearing a harness and led on a leash by their handlers, hop over a course of small hurdles modeled on horse show jumping; women and men both compete as handlers, and male and female rabbits (bucks and does) are entered alike (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport began in Sweden in the late 1970s and is now governed by national federations such as Sveriges Kaninhoppares Riksförbund (SKHRF) in Sweden, the Rabbit Hopping Organization of America (RHOA, founded 2001) and the American Hopping Association for Rabbits and Cavies (AHARC, chartered with the American Rabbit Breeders Association in 2013) in the United States, with the competitive disciplines detailed on [rabbit show jumping](rabbit-show-jumping.html) (SKHRF, n.d.; Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia, n.d.). Under this site's ethics policy it is treated as a recognized, regulated sport with trained companion animals: federation rules require harness-and-leash handling and forbid forcing an unwilling rabbit, putting animal welfare at the center of every competition (Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia, n.d.). The 2026 European Rabbit Jumping Championships were held 9–11 October 2026 in Lysekil, Sweden (AFP, n.d.).

## Rules

Under SKHRF rules, a handler leads the rabbit through a course of obstacles with drop rails, earning faults for each knocked rail or refusal, much like equestrian show jumping; a set course clock (usually 60 minutes) applies, and running out the clock means disqualification, with time used only to break ties (SKHRF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Courses come in straight, crooked and agility-style variants plus high jump and long jump, and ladder classes govern hurdle heights and lengths: Mini up to 26 cm high and 30 cm long (6–8 obstacles), Easy 30/45 cm (8), Medium 38/65 cm (10), Advanced 45/75 cm (10) and Elite 50/80 cm (12), with a minimum 2.50 m between obstacles (3 m in Elite) (Topend Sports, n.d.). Rabbits earn promotion pins to advance a class — roughly the leading 20 percent, and a rabbit with no more than two faults per round, with zero faults earning a pin automatically; in high and long jump three attempts are allowed and clearing 60 cm or 160 cm earns a pin, with Elite entry at 50/120 cm and certificates from 70/180 cm (SKHRF, n.d.). Welfare provisions are explicit: lifting the rabbit over an obstacle is a fault per obstacle, returning it for a fresh approach is a correction, and three corrections count as one fault (not judged in mini/easy classes), and no rabbit may be forced to jump (SKHRF, n.d.). All breeds may compete, though dwarf breeds under 2 kg and giants over 5 kg find the jumps and distances hard; the ideal jumper has long legs and a medium-long back (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Rabbit hopping started properly in the late 1970s, when the Varalöv Kaninklubb rabbit club in southern Sweden began arranging jumping competitions with rules borrowed from horse show jumping and later reformed for rabbits; the first Swedish championship was held in Helsingborg in 1986 with about 30 rabbits, and the first straight-course championship in Stockholm in 1987 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Sveriges Kaninhoppares Riksförbund was founded on 3 September 1994, Norway joined in the early 1990s, Denmark started Kanin Hop clubs in 1993, and a second Swedish body, Sveriges Kaninhoppare, was founded in March 2024 (SKHRF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport reached the United States in the 1980s: the Rabbit Hopping Organization of America was founded in 2001 with help from the Danish judge Aase Bjerner, the first official US national competition ran at the 2011 ARBA convention in Indianapolis, and the AHARC was chartered with the ARBA in 2013, with rabbit hopping also shown at the 2017 North Carolina State Fair (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia was founded in 2013, again with the Bjerner family, and Freya Pocock Johansson founded Rabbit Hopping New Zealand in 2015 (Rabbit Hopping Society of Australia, n.d.). The standing records are the highest jump of 107 cm, set in March 2023 by Holloway GtCh Tennessine of the Czech Republic, and the longest jump of 301 cm, set in August 2017 by Miss Pinky's Grand Champion Harajuku "Dobby" of Sweden (Wikipedia, n.d.).
