## Basic Information

Chess960 (Fischer random chess) is standard chess with one of 960 possible starting positions of the back-rank pieces (bishops on opposite colors, king between rooks), chosen at random per game; castling rules are adjusted accordingly. It was invented by Bobby Fischer in 1996 to eliminate memorized opening theory; FIDE held its first world championship in 2019 (Wesley So won the 2019 event; Magnus Carlsen won the 2022 title). Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Crazyhouse](crazyhouse.html), [Bughouse](bughouse.html), [Capablanca Chess](capablanca-chess.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (FIDE, n.d.).

## Rules

All standard chess rules apply; only the initial array is randomized from the 960 legal positions (SP000–SP518 numbering by Reinhard Scharnagl). Castling is permitted if the king and rook have not moved and the path is clear and not attacked. Games are rated separately from classical chess on FIDE's list (FIDE, n.d.).

## History

Fischer proposed the variant in 1996 in Buenos Aires; it is also called Fischerandom. FIDE recognized it and ran the first official world championship in Mainz 2008 (Levon Aronian) and a rapid/blitz world title from 2019. The 2019 FIDE World FR Championship in Oslo featured Carlsen, So, Caruana and Nepomniachtchi (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
