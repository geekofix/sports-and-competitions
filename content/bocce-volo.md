## Basic Information

Bocce volo — known internationally as lyonnaise — is the throwing discipline of the bocce family in which teams of two to four players throw heavy metal or resin balls over a central string obstacle, aiming to land as close as possible to the pallino; the related boule lyonnaise is played outdoors on a long alley. The World Petanque and Bowls Federation (World Petanque and Bowls Federation [WPBF], n.d.-a) groups volo with pétanque and raffa, while the Fédération Internationale de Boules covers boule lyonnaise and volo. At the XX Mediterranean Games in Taranto 2026 (August 22–25), the volo events were held at Montemesola Sport Hall alongside pétanque and raffa (WPBF, n.d.-a) (World Petanque and Bowls Federation, n.d.-a) (World Petanque and Bowls Federation, n.d.-b).

Women's volo/lyonnaise is well established: women's teams compete at the WPBF world championships and at continental level, playing the same alleys, balls and scoring as the men (WPBF, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Volo is played on an indoor court about 18–19 m long (lyonnaise alleys outdoors run up to 27.5 m) with a central string stretched across the middle at about 2–2.5 m height that the ball must clear on every throw (WPBF, n.d.-a). Teams of two, three or four players each have a set number of balls (typically four per player), thrown one-handed underarm from the end of the court; the ball may not bounce before the string. Scoring follows bocce convention — the team with the closest ball to the pallino scores one point per ball nearer than the opponent's best — and matches are played to 15 points or within a time limit. Equipment: resin or steel balls of about 106–110 mm and 900–1,100 g for men; women and juniors use slightly lighter balls, and youth categories shorten the court (WPBF, n.d.-b). Fouls include crossing the throwing line, failing to clear the string and touching the pallino; men's and women's events share identical rules apart from ball weight classes.

## History

Boule lyonnaise and volo developed in Lyon and northern Italy in the 19th century; lyonnaise became the dominant boules game in the Lyon region, with dedicated "boulodromes" and clubs, and volo is its indoor court version. The WPBF (founded in Monaco in 1985 as CMSB) has organized joint world championships of pétanque, raffa and volo since the 1980s, with the 2022 edition in Mersin gathering 52 teams from 37 countries; the 2026 Mediterranean Games in Taranto again staged all three disciplines together (WPBF, n.d.-a). Women's volo has a long club tradition in France and Italy, with women's national championships and a growing women's world championship presence (WPBF, n.d.-b).
