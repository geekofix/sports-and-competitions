## Basic Information

Tamburello (called tambass in Piedmont) is a court game invented in the northern provinces of Italy in the 16th century — a modification of the ancient pallone col bracciale, bearing the same relation to it as squash does to racquets — named after the tambourine-shaped racket used to strike the ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is played by men and women in many nations, above all in Italy and France, and was part of the Mediterranean Games programme; in Italy the national federation is the FIPT (Federazione Italiana Palla Tamburello, CONI-recognized), in France the FFJBT, and internationally the Fédération Internationale de Balle au Tambourin (FIBaT) coordinates world and continental championships (Fédération Internationale de Balle au Tambourin, n.d.; Federazione Italiana Palla Tamburello, n.d.). The sport has several disciplines — Open (outdoor, in the sferisterio), Indoor (tamburello a 3), Tambeach, Tambutennis and Tambourelli — each with men's and women's categories, and world championships have been held since 2012, won in 2012 by France (men and women), in 2016 by Italy and in 2022 by France (men) and Italy (women) (Fédération Internationale de Balle au Tambourin, n.d.). European club cups have run since 1996 (men), 2001 (women) and 1993 (indoor) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In the Open game, a full-sized court (sferisterio) is 82–91 m (90–100 yards) long and about half as wide, divided laterally by the cordino into the battuta and the rimessa; five players form a side, each carrying the round plastic-frame tamburello — 28 cm in diameter for adults, 26 cm for children, weighing roughly 430–520 g — while the service is struck with the oval tamburina bat (maximum 36 cm) from the trampolino square at one corner of the battuta (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The served ball must cross the middle line; teams then hit it back and forth, the side that fails to return it or sends it out of court losing the point, with games scored as in lawn tennis; in the sferisterio variant the lateral wall may be used for rebounds (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Indoor discipline (tamburello a 3) is played on a 34 × 16 m court with eight-player squads of which three are on the field, a 6.7 cm, 40 g rubber ball, and a 5 cm bar across the center line for line faults; the first team to 13 games wins, each game requiring four winning plays (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Tambeach is played on a 24 × 12 m sand court divided by a 2.15 m net, one or two players per side, the ball struck in flight before it touches the ground (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate categories in every discipline, alongside youth categories (Federazione Italiana Palla Tamburello, n.d.).

## History

The tamburello's ancestor was already played by the ancient Romans; in 1555 Antonio Scaino da Salò described the game in his Trattato del Giuoco della Palla, and the first Italian championship was held in 1896, with definitive rules fixed in 1920 and professional 5- and 3-a-side play after 1930 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The FIPT was founded on 14 November 1926 in Florence with Pietro Pucci as first president (Federazione Italiana Palla Tamburello, n.d.). In France the first tambourins were made in 1861 in Mèze by a cooper, a French federation emerged in the 1920s and was re-founded by Max Rouquette in 1939, and in 1955 the French adopted the Italian "open game" rules, enabling annual France–Italy matches from that year (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The international federation FIBaT was founded on 25 October 1987 in Sanremo by the FIPT and the FFJBT, with headquarters in Rome (Fédération Internationale de Balle au Tambourin, n.d.). Through Italian and French emigrants the game spread worldwide — as tamboreu in Brazil, tambourelli in Scotland and tamburello clubs in many other countries (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
