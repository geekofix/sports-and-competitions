## Basic Information

The decathlon is the ten-event combined athletics competition for men (the women's equivalent is the heptathlon): over two days athletes contest the 100 m, long jump, shot put, high jump and 400 m on day one, and the 110 m hurdles, discus throw, pole vault, javelin throw and 1500 m on day two. Each event is scored with the World Athletics combined-events scoring tables, and the athlete with the highest total points wins. It is governed by World Athletics and is part of the Olympic Games and the World Championships.

## Rules

Under the World Athletics competition rules, decathletes compete in one group (or seeded groups) with a set timetable; each discipline has its own rules (e.g., three attempts in field events, false starts disqualify in sprints). Points are awarded per performance using the WA scoring tables (for example, faster times and longer distances earn more points). The competition must be completed in two consecutive days with at least 30 minutes between events. The Olympic decathlon has been part of the modern Games since Stockholm 1912.

## History

The decathlon was invented for the 1912 Stockholm Olympics as a test of the all-around athlete, won by Jim Thorpe of the USA (later stripped of his medals over amateurism rules, restored in 1982). World Championships decathlon titles began in 1983. The world record is held by Kevin Mayer of France with 9,126 points, set at the 2018 Décastar meeting in Talence. Other legends include Daley Thompson (Great Britain), two-time Olympic champion (1980, 1984), and Roman Šebrle (Czech Republic), who broke 9,000 points first in 2001.
