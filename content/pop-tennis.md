## Basic Information

POP Tennis — until 2015 usually called paddle tennis — is a racquet sport adapted from tennis, played on a court smaller than a tennis court with a lower net, solid perforated paddles and a depressurized tennis ball; since 2015–16 the American governing body has branded the game POP Tennis to distinguish it from platform tennis and padel (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International POP Tennis Association (IPTA) governs the sport in the United States, and the same 50-by-20-foot court is used for singles and doubles, with doubles the dominant form (International POP Tennis Association, n.d.). The game is open to women and men, who compete in the same draws — women's singles, men's singles, women's doubles, men's doubles and mixed doubles are all played at every IPTA national championship (International POP Tennis Association, n.d.).

## Rules

The court measures 50 feet baseline to baseline and 20 feet across, with the service line 3 feet in from the baseline, creating service boxes of 10 by 22 feet, and the net stands 31 inches high at the center (Wikipedia, n.d.). Players use a solid, stringless paddle that may be perforated and a reduced-pressure tennis ball. Serves are underhand with only one serve allowed per point, struck from behind the baseline and alternating between the deuce and ad courts, and must land in the diagonally opposite service box. Scoring mirrors tennis — 15, 30, 40, advantage, with tiebreak sets — and the small court strongly favors net play, producing a fast, reaction-based doubles game (International POP Tennis Association, n.d.). Match formats are best-of-three sets at national championships, with age divisions from junior to senior and open.

## History

POP Tennis was invented in 1898 by Frank Peer Beal, an Episcopal minister in Albion, Michigan, as a recreation for children and a stepping stone to tennis, on a court of 18 by 39 feet — exactly half a tennis court; in 1915 he persuaded New York City's parks department to lay out courts in Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village, the first tournament followed in 1922 and the United States Paddle Tennis Association was formed in 1923 (Wikipedia, n.d.). By 1941 the game was played in almost 500 American cities; Murray Geller's post-war rules modernization enlarged the court and instituted the underhand serve to make the game more attractive to adults, and Scott Freedman won 19 world singles titles in the late 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.). In 2015–16 the USPTA rebranded the sport as POP Tennis and renamed itself the International POP Tennis Association; today the game is played on public courts across the United States, most famously at the historic courts built in 1962 at Venice Beach, California (International POP Tennis Association, n.d.).
