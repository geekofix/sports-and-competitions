## Basic Information

Kyudo ("the way of the bow") is the modern martial art of Japanese archery, practiced in a traditional dojo (kyudojo) with the long asymmetric Japanese bow (yumi, about 2.21 m) and bamboo arrows. The archer shoots standing at a target (mato) 28 m away (kinteki), and the art emphasizes etiquette, posture and the eight-stage shooting form (shaho hassetsu) over mere accuracy — the goal is shin-zen-bi, the unity of truth, goodness and beauty. In Japan the All Nippon Kyudo Federation (ANKF), founded in 1949, governs the art; internationally, the International Kyudo Federation (IKYF), formed in 2006, organizes gradings and World Taikai competitions.

## Rules

In competition (taikai), archers shoot in groups of five on the shajo, each shooting four arrows at a 36 cm target 28 m away; hits are scored by concentric rings, and team matches add the scores. All movements — from the bow (rei) to the eight stages of the shot, including the full draw (kai) and the release (hanare) — are judged for correct form. Practitioners wear the traditional kyudogi (white jacket), hakama (wide trousers) and white tabi socks, and advance through dan ranks (from 1 kyu up to 8 dan) and the titles renshi, kyoshi and hanshi in examinations (shinsa).

## History

Japanese archery (kyujutsu) dates back to the Yayoi period and was formalized into schools such as the Heki-ryu, Ogasawara-ryu and Honda-ryu during the samurai era. After World War II, the All Nippon Kyudo Federation was founded in 1949 and published the Kyudo Kyohon manual in 1953, standardizing one modern shooting form (shaho hassetsu) that most practitioners follow today while the old schools survive alongside. The IKYF was established in 2006 with 28-32 member nations; the first international competition was held in 2007 and the World Taikai in Tokyo 2010, Paris 2014, Tokyo 2018 and 2022. Kyudo is not an Olympic sport — Olympic archery follows World Archery rules — but it is practiced in more than 30 countries.
