## Basic Information

Bull riding is rodeo's most dangerous and famous event: a rider wraps one hand in a braided bull rope around a 2,000-pound bucking bull and must stay on for 8 seconds with the other hand in the air. Rides are scored out of 100 (up to 50 for the bull's bucking and 50 for the rider). The Professional Bull Riders (PBR) is the premier tour; the PRCA sanctions it at its rodeos including the National Finals Rodeo.

## Rules

Under PBR and PRCA rules, the 8-second clock starts when the bull's shoulders pass the 8-foot mark out of the chute; the rider must have one hand in the rope (the "bull rope" with a flat braided tail wrapped around the hand) and the free hand in the air — touching the bull, themselves or their equipment with the free hand means a disqualified ride (0 score). Spurs are worn. Judges score the bull's performance (bucking, spin, drops) and the rider's control out of 50 points each. At PBR events, the top scores from qualifying rounds advance to the final; the highest combined total wins the event.

## History

Bull riding grew out of ranch work and informal contests in Texas and the American West in the late 19th century; the first organized rodeo bull riding contests date to the 1920s-30s. The Professional Bull Riders was founded in 1992 as a spin-off of the PRCA and grew into a global arena sport with events in the US, Canada, Brazil and Australia. Lane Frost, the 1987 world champion who died from injuries in 1989, remains a legend — the PBR's protective vest rule was introduced in his honor. Stars today include José Titot Vilela (BRA) and American champions.
