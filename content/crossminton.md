## Basic Information

Crossminton (formerly speedminton) is a racket sport invented in 2001 in Berlin by Bill Brandes, played without a net: two players stand in opposite squares and hit a heavy shuttlecock called a speeder back and forth. The speeder travels far faster than a badminton shuttle, making crossminton one of the fastest racket sports. It is governed by the International Crossminton Organisation (ICO), founded as the ISBO on 25 August 2011 in Berlin and renamed in 2016, which publishes the ICO Rules of Crossminton and runs World Championships.

## Rules

Under the ICO Rules of Crossminton, the court consists of two squares of 5.5 m side set 12.8 m apart, with no net between them. The server stands behind the baseline of their square and serves diagonally into the opponent's square; play continues as a rally until the speeder lands or goes out. A point is scored when the opponent faults (hits out, into the ground, or fails to return). Games are played to 16 points, win by two, and the match is best of three games. Players switch sides after each game. Rackets are lighter and shorter than badminton rackets, and eye protection is recommended.

## History

Bill Brandes developed speedminton in Berlin in 2001 as a netless, wind-resistant racket game. The International Speedminton Organisation (ISBO) was founded on 25 August 2011 in Berlin, the sport was renamed crossminton in 2016 under the ICO, and World Championships have been held since 2011, biennially. The sport is played recreationally and competitively across Europe and Asia, with the speeder reaching several hundred kilometres per hour in elite smashes.
