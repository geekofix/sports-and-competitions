## Basic Information

Crossminton — formerly known as speed badminton — is a racket sport played without a net: two players (or doubles pairs) stand in opposite 5.5 × 5.5 m squares 13 m apart and rally a heavy shuttlecock, called a spear, over an imaginary center line until it lands in the opponent's square or goes out (International Crossminton Organisation [ICO], n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport was invented in 2001 in Berlin by Bernd-Volker Brahms, who wanted a faster, harder-hitting version of badminton that could be played anywhere without a net, and it was renamed crossminton in 2016 when the International Crossminton Organisation (ICO) was founded to govern it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The spear weighs 17 g and is hit at speeds among the highest in any racket sport — well over 400 km/h in elite play — which is why crossminton is played outdoors or in large halls with eye protection recommended (ICO, n.d.). The pinnacle events are the ICO World Championships and the ICO World Tour; the 2026 ICO World Championships were held in Brandenburg, Germany, from 13 to 16 August 2026, and the 2026 World Tour ran across Europe and Asia (ICO, n.d.). Crossminton was demonstrated as an exhibition sport in Tokyo in 2026, raising hopes of future Olympic inclusion (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Crossminton is played in men's and women's singles, men's, women's and mixed doubles under identical rules — the court, spear and scoring do not differ by gender — with junior and senior age categories; at the end of 2026 the leading current players are the 2026 world champions in each category and the leaders of the ICO World Tour rankings (ICO, n.d.). ## Rules

Under the ICO Laws of Crossminton, the court consists of two squares of 5.5 × 5.5 m whose inner edges are 13 m apart, marked on any surface — grass, sand, gym floor; there is no net, and play happens over an imaginary line at the center of the court between the two squares (ICO, n.d.). The spear (17 g, plastic with a cork or rubber tip) is served with an underhand hit from the ground or out of the hand into the diagonally opposite square; after the serve, players rally, hitting the spear so that it flies over the center line into the opponent's square (ICO, n.d.). Scoring is rally point: every rally awards a point to the winner — a point is scored when the spear lands in the opponent's square, when the opponent hits the spear out of bounds or into the imaginary line/obstacle, or when the opponent fails to return it; the spear may be hit once per side and may not be caught or held (ICO, n.d.). A game is played to 16 points, win by 2, and a match is the best of three games; sides are changed after the first game and at the halfway point of the deciding game (ICO, n.d.). Formats: singles and doubles for men, women and mixed, in open, junior (U18) and senior age-group categories at the World Championships and World Tour (ICO, n.d.). Safety rules: play is prohibited near streets and glass, and eye protection is recommended indoors (ICO, n.d.). The laws are identical for all genders and age groups — only the competitive categories differ (ICO, n.d.).

## History

Bernd-Volker Brahms, a German badminton player and coach, developed the game in 2001 in Berlin, originally calling it speed badminton; the first tournaments were held in Germany in the early 2000s and the sport spread to Norway, Poland, Japan and beyond (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In 2016 the International Crossminton Organisation was founded and the name was changed to crossminton to avoid confusion with badminton's own "speed badminton" training drills; the ICO now publishes the official laws and sanctions the World Championships and the World Tour (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2010s saw spear speeds climb past 400 km/h and the first world championships attract players from more than 20 countries. The 2026 season featured the World Championships in Brandenburg (13–16 August 2026), the World Tour across Europe and Asia, and an exhibition appearance in Tokyo in 2026 that raised hopes of future Olympic inclusion (ICO, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons of the young sport include the multiple world champions from Germany, Norway and Poland — among them the German pioneer players around Brahms himself — and the fastest recorded spear strikes belong to the elite men's singles players of the 2020s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
