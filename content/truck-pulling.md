## Basic Information

Truck and tractor pulling is a motorsport in which modified farm tractors, semi trucks or gas/diesel pickups drag a weighted sled along a measured track; the sled’s weight slides forward during the pull, increasing resistance until the vehicle can go no further — farthest distance wins (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the motorsport family with a strong agricultural root.

*Figure: Acroterion. (2022-06-11). John Deere GP tractor pulling the weighted sled [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/John_Deere_GP_tractor_pulling_MD1.jpg*

The premier US body is the National Tractor Pullers Association (NTPA), founded in 1969, which runs the Super Stock, Pro Stock and Modified classes with a standardized sled and scales (Wikipedia, n.d.). Internationally the European Tractor Pulling Championship (ETPC, Euro Cup) is governed under the ETPC rules with classes from 2.5 t Stock up to 10 t Super Stock (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

A pull is measured in "full pull" (100 m/330 ft) or the measured distance in feet/inches; classes limit engine size, fuel type (diesel, methanol, gasoline, turbine in some classes), weight and tyre size (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety: roll cages, kill switches, scatter shields on flywheels, fire suits, and driver licensing by the sanctioning body (NTPA, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same classes — women have won national titles.

## History

Tractor pulling began as informal contests at 19th-century county fairs; the NTPA standardized the sport in 1969 in Louisville, Kentucky, with the first National Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.). The European championship started in the 1970s and grew into the Euro Cup with hundreds of teams (Wikipedia, n.d.).
