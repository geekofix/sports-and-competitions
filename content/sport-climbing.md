## Basic Information

Sport climbing is the competitive form of rock climbing, contested on artificial walls with roped protection and standardized routes. Its international governing body, the International Federation of Sport Climbing (IFSC), runs World Cup circuits in three disciplines "+E+" lead, boulder and speed "+E+" which combined made their Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games and have brought climbing to a global audience of millions.

## Rules

In lead climbing, athletes climb a long, difficult route roped from below within a set time, scoring by the highest hold reached without falling; a top on the first attempt wins, with count-backs used to resolve ties. In bouldering, climbers tackle a series of short problems without ropes above thick crash pads, scored by the number of tops and zones reached within a limited number of attempts and minutes per problem. In speed climbing, two climbers race up an identical 15-metre wall on auto-belays over a standardized route in seconds, the faster of the two advancing; elite men and women top out in well under seven seconds. Olympic and championship formats combine these disciplines into boulder-and-lead and separate speed medal events, with route setters designing fresh climbs whose difficulty, style and movement remain unknown to the athletes until competition week.

## History

Sport climbing grew out of the free-climbing revolution of the 1980s, when climbers in France, Italy and the United States began drilling permanent bolts into rock faces to push athletic difficulty without the risks of traditional gear. The first indoor climbing walls followed within a decade and quickly became the training and competition venue of the sport; difficulty contests of the 1980s evolved into a formal World Cup circuit in the 1990s, and the International Federation of Sport Climbing was founded in 2007 to unify lead, bouldering and speed under one umbrella. Competition climbing boomed worldwide, was voted into the Olympic programme, appeared first at Tokyo 2020 with one combined title per gender and again at Paris 2024 with separate boulder-and-lead and speed events, while the IFSC World Cup season and biennial World Championships anchor the professional calendar.
