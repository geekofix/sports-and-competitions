## Basic Information

Hand games are fast Dene dexterity and guessing games played at the Arctic Winter Games: one player hides a small object (traditionally a marked bone or pebble) in one hand while the opponent guesses, with rapid hand movements and singing; the 'magician' scores by fooling the guesser (AWG, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Individual and team hand games are contested, with men's, women's and youth divisions. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The object is hidden in one of two fists; after a rapid shuffle the guesser points to the hand. Points are scored for correct guesses and for successful deception; matches are best-of series. Variants include 'hand games with a song' where chanting accompanies the shuffle (AWG, n.d.).

## History

Hand games are among the oldest Dene games, played around campfires for entertainment and gambling, and became a formal Dene Games discipline at the Arctic Winter Games in 1990 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
