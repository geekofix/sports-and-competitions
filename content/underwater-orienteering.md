## Basic Information

Underwater orienteering is CMAS's precision diving discipline: competitors in scuba gear navigate a mapped course at the bottom of a lake or calm sea using a compass, depth gauge and distance meter, finding and rounding marker controls without surfacing, against the clock (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport was created by Soviet divers in the late 1950s; the first competitions were held at Angera on Lake Maggiore, Italy, in 1961 and at the Wörthersee in Austria in 1962, the first international competition took place in the Crimea in 1965 with Austria, Bulgaria, Hungary, Italy and the USSR, and the first European Championship followed in 1967 (Wikipedia, n.d.). CMAS — the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques, founded in 1959 with Jacques Cousteau as inaugural president — established its Orienteering Commission in January 1969, with Valentin Stachevski of the USSR as first president, and staged the first CMAS World Championship in 1973 at Lokve, Yugoslavia, with biennial editions in odd years ever since (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate categories as well as mixed team events, across adult (over 21) and youth (15–21) age groups (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Events are staged on a marked underwater course in a natural water body, typically 2–15 m deep: each competitor dives with a compass, depth gauge and distance meter and follows a course defined by compass bearings and distances disclosed on the course map before the start, rounding control markers such as buoys (Wikipedia, n.d.; CMAS, n.d.). The classic events are the M-course (an individual course of about 590 m with three rounding buoys and a time limit), the 5-point course (introduced in 1968, with marked points at 80, 100, 120, 150 and 200 m), the star course (from 1987), the Monk course (from the 1970s), a parallel course and the straight 100 m compass swim; team events add a relay-style 1150 m course (Wikipedia, n.d.). Scoring is by time plus accuracy — missed or wrongly rounded controls add penalties — and surfacing during a scored course is a disqualification; each diver tows a surface buoy so officials can track them (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's and women's competitions use identical courses and rules in separate categories, with mixed team events alongside, in adult (over 21) and youth (15–21) classes; the world federation is CMAS, which runs the biennial world championships (CMAS, n.d.).

## History

Underwater orienteering was created by divers in the Soviet Union in the late 1950s, who formalized compass-and-depth navigation as a competitive sport; the first competitions were held at Angera on Lake Maggiore, Italy, in 1961 and at the Wörthersee in Austria in 1962 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first international competition took place in the Crimea in 1965 with teams from Austria, Bulgaria, Hungary, Italy and the USSR, and the first European Championship in 1967 introduced the 40 m and 1000 m races, the M-course and a 1150 m team event (Wikipedia, n.d.). CMAS, founded in 1959 in Monaco with Jacques Cousteau as its inaugural president, established its Orienteering Commission in January 1969 — its first president was Valentin Stachevski of the USSR — and held the first CMAS World Championship in 1973 at Lokve, Yugoslavia; world championships have since run biennially in odd years, including Neuglobsow (GDR) 1985, Leningrad 1987, Győr area (Hungary) 2009, Störitztal near Berlin 2011 and Kazan 2013 (CMAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The 5-point course was introduced in 1968 and the star course in 1987, and the sport's heartlands remain Central and Eastern Europe, where the training method still serves as the core open-water navigation skill for divers worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.).
