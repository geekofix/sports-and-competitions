## Basic Information

Heliskiing (and its snowboard sibling heliboarding) is freeride skiing or snowboarding in remote, ungroomed terrain reached by helicopter; competitive formats include the Freeride World Tour (FWT) qualifier series and events such as the Swatch Nines, where riders are judged on line choice, fluidity, control, jumps and style on a big-mountain face (FWT, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Unlike resort skiing there are no lifts or groomed runs — each run starts with a helicopter drop onto a selected face, and safety requires avalanche transceivers, probes and shovels plus guide supervision.

## Rules

Competitive freeride rules (FWT): each rider gets one run on the chosen face (two in finals), judged by five judges on a 100-point scale across the criteria above; the field is cut from qualifiers to the final; separate men's and women's categories, plus junior categories; helmet mandatory, avalanche airbag recommended; runs are filmed and judged live. Heliskiing as a commercial activity is regulated by national aviation and mountain-guide rules (FWT, n.d.).

## History

Heliskiing began commercially in 1965 in the Bugaboos, British Columbia (Canada Mountain Holidays, CMH), and in the Alps from the 1970s; the first freeride competitions date to the late 1990s and evolved into today's Freeride World Tour, founded in 2008 as the Swatch Freeride World Tour and held across venues in North America, Europe, Japan and New Zealand, with separate men's and women's tours (Wikipedia, n.d.).
