## Basic Information

Rabbit show jumping (Swedish: kaninhoppning) is the competitive form of [rabbit hopping](rabbit-hopping.html), in which trained rabbits are led through standardized jumping courses — straight, crooked, high jump and long jump — in pursuit of fault-free rounds and titles. The sport is administered by national federations such as the Swedish Sveriges Kaninhoppares Riksförbund and the American Hopping Association for Rabbits and Cavies, and it is most strongly established in Scandinavia, though championships now draw entries from across Europe and beyond.

## Rules

Official competitions consist of a straight course, a crooked course, high jump and long jump. Straight and crooked courses are divided into levels with set obstacle counts and dimensions: Mini courses run to a maximum height of 26 cm and length 30 cm, Easy 30/45 cm, Medium 38/65 cm, Advanced 45/75 cm and Elite 50/80 cm, with eight to twelve obstacles depending on the class. Rabbits earn promotion points to move up a level and are placed according to faults — knocked rails and refusals — with the 60-minute course clock and time used only to break ties. In the high jump and long jump, the bar rises or the jump lengthens round by round until only one rabbit clears, like the puissance competition in equestrian show jumping. Any breed may compete, with long legs, a medium-long back and a calm temperament considered the ideal conformation for a jumping rabbit.

## History

Rabbit show jumping began in Sweden in the 1970s — the first national championship, on a straight easy course, was staged in Stockholm in 1987 — and grew into a federation-governed sport with the founding of the Swedish national federation in 1994. Norway joined the sport in the early 1990s and Denmark in 1993, and international rule harmonization among the Nordic countries produced a common framework at the end of the 1990s. The United States followed with the Rabbit Hopping Organization of America in 2001, whose rules were established with help from the Danish judges Aase and Rasmus Bjerner, and with the American Hopping Association for Rabbits and Cavies, chartered with the American Rabbit Breeders Association in 2013. The record books are led by European jumpers: the world record for the highest jump stands at 107 cm, set by Holloway GtCh Tennessine, owned by Marie Kozubková of the Czech Republic in March 2023, and the longest jump record of 301 cm was set by Miss Pinky's Grand Champion Harajuku "Dobby", owned by Julia Samson of Sweden, in August 2017.
