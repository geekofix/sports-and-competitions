## Basic Information

Jukskei (Afrikaans for "yoke game") is a traditional South African throwing game played by two teams of four on a rectangular court marked with two parallel lines. Each team places a V-shaped rubber block called a skei on its line, and the opposing throwers hurl their skei from behind their own line, trying to land it across the opponents' line or to knock their skei over. The game is governed in South Africa by the official jukskei association and is played at club, school and championship level, especially in the Western Cape and Gauteng.

## Rules

The court has two parallel lines a set distance apart (typically 13 to 16 m in competition). At the start of each end, each team places its skei upright on its own line; teams alternate throwing from behind their line, and a throw is valid when the skei comes to rest fully across the opponents' line or knocks the opponents' skei over. Points are awarded for crosses and knockdowns, and an end is won by the team with the most points, with matches played to a set score or over a set number of ends. Each team of four consists of two throwers and two skeiers who place and retrieve the skei; players rotate roles. Throws must be made underhand from behind the line.

## History

Jukskei is named after the wooden yoke (juk) used to harness oxen and is believed to have been played for generations by Voortrekker and farming communities in the South African interior, who used a piece of V-shaped iron or later rubber as the skei. The game was codified into modern rules in the 20th century, and the South African Jukskei Association now organizes national championships with men's, women's and youth divisions. The game spread with Afrikaans-speaking communities and is today also played at schools and in the South African diaspora, prized as a game of accuracy, power and nerve.
