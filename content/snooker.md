## Basic Information

Snooker is a cue sport played on a baize-covered table of 12 by 6 feet with 22 balls — 15 reds worth one point each and six colours worth two (yellow) to seven (black) — in which players score by potting balls in order and the highest break possible is 147; it is governed professionally by the World Professional Billiards and Snooker Association (WPBSA), whose World Snooker Tour carries the official world ranking (WPBSA, n.d.-a). The 2026 World Snooker Championship at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield, 18 April–4 May 2026, marked the 50th consecutive year of the championship at the Crucible: Wu Yize of China beat Shaun Murphy 18–17 in the final to win his first world title and become the second-youngest champion in history, with a record 11 Chinese players in the last 32 and Mark Allen's 145 the highest break; the Crucible contract runs to 2045 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). As of 19 November 2026 there have been 248 official maximum 147 breaks in professional play. Zhao Xintong won the 2026 Tour Championship final against Judd Trump (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the current leading women, Mink Nutcharut of Thailand has been the world number one in women's snooker since June 2025, won the 2022 World Women's Championship 6–5 against Wendy Jans, and is the only woman to have made a 147 break (in practice, March 2019); the 2024 World Mixed Doubles title went to the Belgian-Chinese pair Brecel and Evans (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

The WPBSA rules of snooker are played on a table with a playing area of 11 ft 8½ in by 5 ft 10 in and cushions, using 22 balls of 52.5 mm diameter: 15 reds (1 point) and the colours yellow (2), green (3), brown (4), blue (5), pink (6) and black (7) (WPBSA, n.d.-a). After the break-off, reds must be potted alternately with a colour, which is then respotted; once the reds are gone, the colours are potted in ascending order and the black finishes the frame. The maximum break is 147 (15 reds with the black each time, then the colours). A foul — missing every ball, potting the cue ball, or playing out of turn — gives the opponent a minimum of 4 points and up to 7 depending on the ball involved, and after a foul the opponent may take a "free ball" or require the offender to replay from the original position; a tied frame is decided by the black being respotted on its spot and the players drawing lots for the break (WPBSA, n.d.-a). Matches are played best-of-frames, with ranking events and the Triple Crown — UK Championship, Masters and World Championship — forming the season on the World Snooker Tour, which also runs women's, mixed-doubles, senior (over-40) and junior (under-21) events; men and women compete in separate professional circuits, with women's snooker organised by World Women's Snooker under the WPBSA umbrella (WPBSA, n.d.-b).

## History

Snooker was invented in 1875 in Jabalpur, India, by British Army officers stationed there, and the name is said to come from a slang term for inexperienced soldiers; Sir Neville Chamberlain, later British prime minister, formalised the rules in 1882 while stationed in India (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first World Championship was held in 1927 at Camkin's Hall in Birmingham, won by Joe Davis, who went on to win 15 world titles between 1927 and 1946 and was never beaten in a world final (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). His younger brother Fred Davis won eight world titles in the late 1940s and 1950s, John Pulman won the 1957 and 1964 championships, and Welshman Ray Reardon dominated the 1970s with six titles from 1970 to 1978 — all now retired or deceased icons of the Crucible era, which the championship joined in 1977 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Steve Davis of England, who made the first televised 147 in 1982, won six world titles in the 1980s and pioneered the modern athletic game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
