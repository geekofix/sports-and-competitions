## Basic Information

Turkey bowling is the American holiday novelty game that turns a frozen turkey into a bowling ball: a participant slides a frozen bird down a supermarket aisle, ice rink or party-room lane at a set of ten liquid-filled plastic bottles arranged as pins, and scores as in ten-pin bowling (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game was invented in 1988 by Derrick Johnson, a grocery clerk at Lucky's supermarket in Newport Beach, California, after he watched his manager slide a frozen turkey down an aisle and knock over a two-liter soda bottle; Johnson then appointed himself president of the self-styled "Poultry Bowlers Association" and coined the game's slang — the fowl line (foul line), gobbler (three strikes), Butterball (a gutter ball) and wishbone (the 7–10 split) (Wikipedia, n.d.). The best-known staged version is WJW Fox 8's annual "Turkey Bowl" in Cleveland, Ohio, held since 1999 as a canned-goods charity event with a celebrity invitational — quarterback Bernie Kosar won the 2023 edition and the 25th anniversary was celebrated in 2024 — and hockey arenas run intermission turkey bowls on the ice (Fox 8 Cleveland, n.d.). Women and men bowl in these events under identical house rules. The bird is a dead, frozen turkey — animal-rights protests have shadowed some events, leading a 2003 event in Manchester, England, to use plastic turkeys and prompting the rescue of a live turkey at a 2007 New York event (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The setup is deliberately improvised but constant in its elements: ten bottles — commonly one- or two-liter soda bottles partly filled with liquid for stability — are racked in the familiar ten-pin triangle at the end of a "lane," which may be a supermarket aisle, a gym floor, a soap-covered plastic sheet or a hockey rink's ice, and the bowler slides a frozen turkey of roughly 12 lb (about 5.5 kg), still wrapped, from the fowl line without lifting it into the air — the bird must slide, not be thrown (Wikipedia, n.d.). Scoring follows ten-pin bowling: the number of bottles knocked down is counted per delivery, with adapted frames (house rules commonly allow two or three deliveries per turn), strikes and spares recognized by the traditional scoring tables, and the highest total after the agreed number of frames wins (Wikipedia, n.d.). House rules also govern lane boundaries, resetting fallen bottles between turns, whether the turkey may be re-frozen, and team formats, and events are staged at supermarket holiday promotions, ice-rink intermissions and office or charity parties rather than under a single federation's rulebook — men's and women's participants play by the same rules (Fox 8 Cleveland, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Turkey bowling was invented in 1988 by Derrick Johnson at Lucky's supermarket in Newport Beach, California, when a manager's sliding frozen turkey knocked over a two-liter soda bottle — Johnson saw the bowling potential, founded the tongue-in-cheek Poultry Bowlers Association and gave the game its vocabulary of fowl line, gobbler, Butterball and wishbone (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game spread through Thanksgiving-season local-news segments, supermarket promotions, office parties and charity events in the late 1980s and 1990s, and hockey intermission shows on ice kept it visible (Wikipedia, n.d.). WJW Fox 8 in Cleveland formalized the charity version as the annual Fox 8 Turkey Bowl in 1999, switching to canned goods and adding a celebrity invitational that Bernie Kosar won in 2023; the event marked its 25th anniversary in 2024 (Fox 8 Cleveland, n.d.). Animal-rights protests accompanied several high-profile events — a 2003 Manchester, England, event used plastic turkeys, and a live turkey at a 2007 New York event was rescued — and the game has never grown beyond its seasonal novelty status, living on in holiday promotions and town tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.).
