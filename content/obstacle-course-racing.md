## Basic Information

Obstacle course racing (OCR) is a mass-participation racing sport in which athletes run, climb, crawl, jump and carry their way over a course of man-made obstacles, ranging from short 100 m sprint formats to multi-kilometer endurance events with walls, cargo nets, monkey bars, mud pits and water obstacles (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.). Commercial race series such as Spartan Race, Tough Mudder and the Warrior Dash made the sport globally popular from the late 2000s onward, and women and men compete in separate elite heats at every major event (Spartan Race, n.d.). Since 2026 the discipline has been integrating into the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM), which runs the Obstacle World Championships, and obstacle racing will become the fifth discipline of the [modern pentathlon](modern-pentathlon.html) at the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics; the short, technical ninja-style format is covered under [ninja sport](ninja-sport.html) (World Obstacle, n.d.).

## Rules

OCR events are timed races against the clock, usually with waves of starters released at intervals (Spartan Race, n.d.). Distances range from a 100 m sprint to 3 km, 5 km, 10 km and ultra formats of 20 km and more, with 20 to 40 or more obstacles per course (World Obstacle, n.d.). Mandatory obstacles must be completed, and failure to complete an obstacle results in a penalty — typically burpees — or disqualification in elite racing; some formats include a mandatory carry such as a bucket, sandbag or tire (Spartan Race, n.d.). Elites and age-group racers compete in separate heats, with men's and women's elite categories at every championship, and electronic timing decides the results (World Obstacle, n.d.). At championship level the UIPM runs events over 100 m, 400 m and 3 km plus a relay, with the same obstacle specifications as the obstacle discipline of the modern pentathlon (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.).

## History

Modern obstacle course racing grew out of military endurance training and events such as the British Tough Guy race (1987); the commercial boom began around 2010 with Spartan Race (founded 2010 by Joe De Sena), Tough Mudder (2010) and the Warrior Dash (2011), which turned OCR into a global mass-participation phenomenon with millions of finishers per year (Wikipedia, n.d.). In parallel, competitive obstacle racing was organized internationally by World Obstacle (FISO), founded in Lausanne in 2018 and granted observer status by GAISF in 2021, which ran the OCR World Championships and the ninja-style events (World Obstacle, n.d.). In 2026 World Obstacle's structure dissolved into the UIPM, which had already added obstacle racing as the fifth discipline of the modern pentathlon for Los Angeles 2028, unifying elite obstacle competition under one federation (Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
