## Basic Information

Ringette is a winter team ice sport invented in Canada in 1963 in which players use a straight stick to pass and shoot a blue rubber ring, aiming to score in the opponent's net. It was created for girls by Sam Jacks of North Bay, Ontario, with the first official rules drafted in 1964–1965 by Mirl Arthur "Red" McCarthy in Espanola, Ontario. Ringette is played internationally by tens of thousands of athletes and is governed nationally by Ringette Canada and internationally by the International Ringette Federation, with Canada and Finland the sport's traditional powerhouses.

## Rules

Ringette is played six-a-side (five skaters plus a goaltender) on an ice hockey rink, with no body checking allowed and no offside. The game's signature constraint is the blue line rule: the ring, which has an official diameter of 16.5 cm, may not be carried over either blue line but must be passed across it, forcing constant distribution play. Play restarts with a free pass from one of five free pass circles, where only the player taking the free pass may enter the circle and must release the ring within five seconds of the whistle. A 30-second shot clock, introduced in Canada in 2002, is used at competitive levels and above and gives possession to the goaltender when it expires. Sticks are straight with tapered, grooved drag-tips and a maximum length from the ice to the player's underarm, and full protective equipment including a helmet with ringette facemask and neck guard is mandatory. At elite level (National Ringette League and World Ringette Championships) games are played in four 13-minute quarters, while recreational games are typically two periods of 18 to 24 minutes.

## History

Ringette was invented in 1963 by Sam Jacks, a recreation director in North Bay, Ontario, and the first team in history was a group of girls from Espanola, Ontario, who tested the rules Mirl "Red" McCarthy drafted between 1964 and 1965. The Ontario Ringette Association formed in 1969, Ringette Canada followed in 1974, and the sport spread to Finland, whose SM Ringette league has run since 1987–88, and to Sweden and other nations. Canada's national team first toured Finland in 1979, ringette entered the Canada Winter Games in 1991, and the first World Ringette Championships were held in Gloucester, Ontario, in 1990, with Canada and Finland dominating the event ever since. The National Ringette League, North America's elite showcase for players aged 18 and up, was founded in 2002 and awards the Jeanne Sauvé Memorial Cup to its playoff champion; the sport today counts well over 30,000 registered players in Canada and is one of only four major ice-skating team sports, alongside ice hockey, bandy and rinkball.
