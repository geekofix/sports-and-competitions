## Basic Information

Snow snake is a Dene game played at the Arctic Winter Games: a greased wooden stick (the 'snake', traditionally made of hardwood, 1–3 m) is thrown with an underhand sliding motion so it travels as far as possible across smooth snow or ice; the longest throw wins (AWG, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Long and short snake divisions exist; men and women compete separately at the Games. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The snake is thrown from a marked lane; it must slide (not tumble) and the throw is measured from the starting line to where the head of the snake stops. Grease (traditionally seal fat) reduces friction. Short snakes (~1 m) and long snakes (up to 3 m+) are separate events (AWG, n.d.).

## History

Snow snake comes from Dene hunting and travel culture — the game mimics sliding a spear or a sled runner over snow. It has been a Dene Games event at the Arctic Winter Games since 1990 and appears at WEIO (Wikipedia, n.d.).
