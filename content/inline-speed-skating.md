## Basic Information

Inline speed skating — also called roller speed skating — is the sport of racing on inline skates, governed worldwide by World Skate, which has run the World Championships officially since 1966 (unofficially since 1937) and publishes the 2026 Speed Rulebook (World Skate, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport has two disciplines: track racing on 200 m banked or flat ovals (300 m time trial, 500 m, 1,000 m, points race, elimination race, points-elimination and 3,000 m relay) and road racing over distances from sprints to the 42.195 km marathon (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment is regulated: a maximum of five wheels per skate, wheel diameter up to 110 mm in general and 125 mm in the marathon (World Skate, n.d.-a). Colombia has been the dominant nation for decades with 553 medals in all championships combined, and Belgium's Bart Swings set the men's marathon world benchmark of 56:45 in Berlin in 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the top of the women's sport in 2026 are the leading road and track specialists from Colombia, Italy, Spain and South Korea, who headline the World Skate Games 2026 program just as the men's stars do (World Skate, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the World Skate Speed Rulebook, track events are raced on a 200 m oval (minimum 125 m) with banked or flat turns, either counterclockwise; skaters may draft behind each other, and lapping is allowed — a lapped skater may continue unless the referee waves them off in mass-start events (World Skate, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 300 m event is a timed individual time trial; in the 500 m and 1,000 m the field is reduced by heats; the points race awards points at intermediate sprints (10/5/3/2/1 and 1 for the lap leader) and the final sprint is doubled; the elimination race removes the last skater at each bell lap; the points-elimination combines both; the 3,000 m relay is skated by teams of three (World Skate, n.d.-a). Road races are held on open or closed road circuits over 200 m to the full marathon; mass starts, drafting and team tactics are allowed, and a skater who falls may rejoin (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Equipment rules limit wheels to five per skate and 110 mm diameter (125 mm in the marathon), require a hard helmet, and frames must not project beyond the wheels (World Skate, n.d.-a). Fouls include cutting across, obstruction, holding and dangerous skating, punished by warnings, relegation or disqualification (World Skate, n.d.-a). Men and women race the same standard distances — including the full 42.195 km marathon for women — in separate categories, and junior and masters classes use shorter distances and smaller wheels (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Organized inline speed skating began in the 1930s, with unofficial world championships from 1937 and the first official World Championships in 1966 under the international roller-skating federation that became part of World Skate in 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Inline skates themselves date to John Joseph Merlin's demonstration in London in 1760, but racing on inlines became popular in the 1980s and 1990s, with events such as the Berlin Marathon — where Bart Swings set the 56:45 course record in 2022 — and the 24 Heures du Mans rollers drawing huge fields (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport was demonstrated at the Buenos Aires 2018 Summer Youth Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Colombia emerged as the powerhouse of the 2010s, winning 553 medals at world championships overall, and the World Skate Games, first held in 2017 in Nanjing and returning in 2026, gather all roller speed disciplines (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the icons are Bart Swings of Belgium, marathon and track star, and the Colombian sprint dynasty around the world titles of the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
