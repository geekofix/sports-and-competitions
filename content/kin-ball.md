## Basic Information

Kin-Ball is a team sport invented in 1986 in Quebec, Canada, by physical education teacher Mario Demers, and governed internationally by the International Kin-Ball Sport Federation (FIKB). Its two distinctive features are the giant ball — 1.22 m in diameter and only 1 kg in weight — and the fact that three teams of four players compete at the same time on a 20 m x 20 m court, instead of the usual one-versus-one format. The sport is played by several million people in more than 30 countries, above all in Canada, the US, Japan, Belgium and France.

## Rules

The three teams wear the official colors black, grey and blue (pink is sometimes used instead of blue). The team holding the ball is the attacking team and must first designate one of the other two teams as the defender by calling "Omnikin!" followed by that team's color. All four players of the attacking team must be in contact with the ball when it is hit, with a body part above the hips, and the ball must travel at least 1.8 m. The designated defending team tries to control the ball; if it succeeds, it becomes the attacking team. A team commits a foul, for example, if the ball touches the ground, is hit out of bounds or downward, if not all players touch it, or if the color call is wrong; each foul gives one point to each of the other two teams. A game is played in periods of about 10 minutes: when one team reaches 9 points in a period, the team with the fewest points leaves the court, and the remaining two play until one reaches 11 points. The winner is the first team to win three periods.

## History

Mario Demers created Kin-Ball in 1986 in Trois-Rivières, Quebec, with the goal of a sport that emphasizes teamwork, participation and respect and lets players of different abilities play together. The Fédération Québécoise de sport KIN-BALL was founded soon after, followed by national federations in Japan (Masanobu Yoshida), Belgium and many other countries, and the International Kin-Ball Sport Federation (FIKB) now counts around 3.8 million players in over 30 countries. World Cups have been held since 1997, and the sport is taught in schools across Canada, Europe and Asia. The UK federation, Kin-Ball UK, was formed in 2018.
