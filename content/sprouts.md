## Basic Information

Sprouts is the paper-and-pencil game invented in 1967 by John Horton Conway and Michael S. Paterson at Cambridge: start with n spots; each move adds a curve connecting two spots (or a spot to itself) and adds a new spot; no more than three lines may meet at a spot; the last player able to move wins. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Dots and Boxes](dots-and-boxes.html), [Chomp](chomp.html), [Nim](nim.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Conway, J. H. & Paterson, M. S, 1967).

## Rules

Start with n spots; a move: draw a curve from one spot to another (or a loop) not crossing existing lines, and add a new spot on the curve; each spot max degree 3; the player unable to move loses; Conway's prize (£10— ) for the 3-spot game analysis (Conway, J. H. & Paterson, M. S, 1967).

## History

Invented by Conway and Paterson in 1967 at Princeton/Cambridge (published in Martin Gardner's Scientific American column); the max-moves diagrams (AndrewKepert, 2013, CC0) show the math; played in math olympiad circles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
