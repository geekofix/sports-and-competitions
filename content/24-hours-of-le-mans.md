## Basic Information

The 24 Hours of Le Mans is the world's oldest active endurance race, held annually since 1923 on the Circuit de la Sarthe near Le Mans, France. It is organized by the Automobile Club de l'Ouest (ACO), forms the centerpiece rounds of the FIA World Endurance Championship — see also [World Endurance Championship](world-endurance-championship.html) on this site — and is one leg of motorsport's Triple Crown alongside the Monaco Grand Prix and the Indianapolis 500 (FIA World Endurance Championship, n.d.-a) (FIA World Endurance Championship, n.d.-b).

The most recent edition, held on 13–14 June 2026, was won by the No. 7 Toyota GR010 Hybrid of Mike Conway, Kamui Kobayashi and Nyck de Vries, ending Ferrari's three-year winning streak of 2023–2025 (FIA World Endurance Championship [FIA WEC], n.d.-b). Among the current top teams is the all-female Iron Dames squad, which races a Lamborghini in the LMGT3 class.

## Rules

The race runs for exactly 24 hours from a Saturday afternoon start; the winner is the car that covers the greatest distance, and every class has its own winner as well (Automobile Club de l'Ouest [ACO], 2025). Entries compete in three classes: the top Hypercar class for Le Mans Hypercar (LMH) and Le Mans Daytona h (LMDh) prototypes, the pro-am LMP2 prototype class, and the GT-based LMGT3 class, which replaced the former LMGTE category in 2024 (ACO, 2025; FIA WEC, n.d.-a).

Each car is crewed by a team of drivers (three in the top classes) who share the driving in stints. The regulations deliberately cap driving loads to keep fatigue and risk low: a single stint is limited to a maximum of 240 minutes at the wheel, no driver may drive more than 14 hours in total across the race, and in LMP2 and LMGT3 the amateur Bronze and Silver-rated drivers must each drive at least six hours, while any driver must complete at least 60 minutes to score championship points (Porsche, n.d.; DailySportcar, 2025). There are no gender-specific rules: classifications are skill-based, with the FIA grading every driver from Bronze through Silver, Gold and Platinum, and female crews such as the Iron Dames squad have competed in the GT class.

Refueling, tire changes and repairs are only allowed in the pits, and work on the car during refueling is restricted by procedure rules. Grid positions are set in qualifying sessions followed by the timed Hyperpole shootouts for the fastest cars in each class (DailySportcar, 2025). To be classified, a car must cross the finish line under its own power after the 24 hours, and only cars completing a set percentage of the winner's distance are classified in the results (ACO, 2025).

Variants: sprint races in the Le Mans Cup series and historic eras such as Group C ran under different technical regulations, and the 24 Hours of Le Mans motorcycle race, the 24 Heures Moto, follows its own rulebook on the same circuit.

## History

The race was created in 1923 by the Automobile Club de l'Ouest, driven by ACO secretary Georges Durand and journalist Charles Faroux, with the deliberate idea of testing production cars' durability over a full day; the first edition ran on 23–24 June 1923 and was won by André Lagache and René Léonard in a Chenard-Walcker (ACO, n.d.). The 1955 catastrophe, in which a crash killed more than 80 spectators, became a turning point for motorsport safety worldwide and led to fundamental circuit and organizational reforms.

The manufacturer eras came in waves: Bentley and Alfa Romeo dominated the interwar years, Ferrari's prototypes defined the early 1960s, and Ford's 1-2-3 finish in 1966 with Bruce McLaren, Chris Amon and Ken Miles remains legendary. The Group C era of the 1980s produced record speeds; Mazda became the first Japanese manufacturer to win in 1991 with the rotary-engined 787B; and Tom Kristensen holds the all-time record with nine victories, ahead of Jacky Ickx with six and Derek Bell with five (ACO, n.d.; Encyclopedia Britannica, n.d.). Since 2012 the race has counted as a round of the FIA World Endurance Championship with double championship points, and the Hypercar top class returned works manufacturers — Ferrari's 499P won overall in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (a hat-trick), before Toyota's No. 7 GR010 Hybrid took the 2026 edition (FIA WEC, n.d.-a; FIA WEC, n.d.-b).
