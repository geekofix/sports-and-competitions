## Basic Information

Six-man football is a variant of American gridiron football played with six players per side instead of eleven, designed for small rural high schools that cannot field a full team. It is played chiefly in rural areas of Texas, Nebraska and other Great Plains states and in rural Canada, and it is known for high-scoring, wide-open games: with the whole field spread between six players, every player is an eligible receiver and the scores are enormous.

## Rules

Six-man is played on an 80-yard by 40-yard field (instead of the 100 by 53\u2153-yard eleven-man field), with the 40-yard line at the centre. All six players are eligible receivers, and the quarterback must be an interior lineman at the snap or the offence is penalised; the offense must gain 15 yards, not 10, for a first down. Scoring differs from the 11-man game: a kicked extra point is worth 2 points (a run or pass conversion only 1 point), and a field goal is worth 4 points — encouraging deep kicking. Matches consist of four quarters (in Texas of 10 minutes), and games are famous for their shootouts: first team to 45 points by half-time or later can win by mercy rule in some states (the UIL uses a 45-point differential clock rule). Texas high-school six-man is governed by University Interscholastic League rules with NCAA rules as base plus the six-man exceptions; adult six-man followed the same basic format.

## History

The game was invented in 1934 by Stephen Epler, a high-school coach in Chester, Nebraska, who adapted the rules of eleven-man football for schools too small to fill a full team; it spread so fast across the sparsely populated Plains that within five years Texas and other states had adopted it, and at their 1930s\u20131960s peak, hundreds of rural schools played the game, with more than 160 six-man teams in Texas alone in the 1960s. In rural communities six-man football became a Friday-night institution, and the Friday-night culture of small Texas and Plains towns helped it survive even as school consolidation shrank the number of six-man schools; adult amateur six-man leagues folded one by one, with the last high-profile adult circuit, the San Antonio-based Texas Sixman Football League, converting to eight-man after 2012. Today the game thrives at the high-school level in Texas, where the state championship is a headline event, and in Nebraska, New Mexico, Colorado, Kansas, Montana and parts of Canada, with private-school and home-school leagues broadening its base; an annual Six-Man All-Star Game and a network of websites and coaching clinics keep the shrinking-field game flourishing.
