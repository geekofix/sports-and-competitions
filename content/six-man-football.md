## Basic Information

Six-man football is a variant of American football played with six players per side instead of eleven, on a shortened field; it is overwhelmingly a high-school phenomenon in the United States — Texas above all, where more than 2,000 schools field teams — and it is governed at the high-school level by state associations under the NFHS (NFHS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The game keeps the core of American football — four downs, forward passing, end zones — but with six players the field is typically 80 × 40 yards (some states use the full 120 × 40), games are played in four 15-minute quarters, and scoring includes the usual touchdowns and field goals plus the drop-kicked extra point (Wikipedia, n.d.). Six-man football also has a small professional echo, with occasional all-star and charity games, but its heart is the Texas high-school scene, where the first official state championship game was played in 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Six-man football is played by both women and men — girls' and mixed six-man leagues exist alongside the boys' high-school competition, and women's flag and tackle variants share the same shortened-field format; the rules are identical for all genders, with no separate women's field dimensions at high-school level (NFHS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Each team fields six players — typically a quarterback, a running back or wing, receivers, and a line that plays both ways — on a field of 80 × 40 yards in Texas (full-length 120 × 40 in some other states) with goal lines and end zones as in eleven-man football (Wikipedia, n.d.). The offensive system is the four-down system: a team has four attempts to advance ten yards, may punt or attempt a field goal on fourth down, and scores six points for a touchdown, with the try after touchdown worth one point by kick or two by run/pass, and three for a field goal (NFHS, n.d.). Quarters last 15 minutes, and the passing game is more prominent than in the eleven-man game because the field is narrower; special teams cover kicks and punts with the same six players (Wikipedia, n.d.). There are no gender-specific rule differences — girls' leagues use the same field and scoring (NFHS, n.d.).

## History

Six-man football grew out of small Texas towns in the 1930s, when schools with too few students to field eleven players improvised a six-a-side version; the format was codified over the following decades and Texas high schools today play it under the University Interscholastic League, with more than 2,000 schools participating (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first official six-man state championship game was held in 2019, won by Jonesboro-Hodgekins, and the sport has since produced its own celebrities — the Milford Bulldogs of Milford, Texas, photographed taking the field against the Avalon Eagles in 2007, are among the best-known small-school programs (Wikipedia, n.d.). Outside Texas, six-man or six-a-side football survives in a few other states, in youth leagues and in occasional charity all-star games (NFHS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
