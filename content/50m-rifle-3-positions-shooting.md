## Basic Information

50m Rifle Three Positions is the classic ISSF rifle event: shots are fired from the prone, standing and kneeling positions at a target 50 meters away. It has been on the Olympic program since Rome 1960, was removed for Tokyo 2020, and was reinstated for Paris 2024; the women's event has been Olympic since Sydney 2000. The discipline anchors the ISSF World Championships and World Cups (International Shooting Sport Federation [ISSF], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). See also [Shooting](shooting.html) and [10m Air Rifle Shooting](10m-air-rifle-shooting.html) on this site (International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.-a) (International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.-b) (International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.-c).

Among the athletes shaping the event today are Switzerland's Chiara Leone, who won Olympic gold in the women's 50m rifle three positions at Paris 2024 with an Olympic record, and China's Liu Yukun, who won the men's Olympic title at Paris 2024 with 463.6 points, ahead of Ukraine's Serhiy Kulish (461.3) and India's Swapnil Kusale, who took bronze (Olympics.com, n.d.; ISSF, n.d.-b). In the women's qualification at Paris 2024, Sagen Maddalena of the United States set the pace with 593 points (Olympics.com, n.d.). At the 2026 ISSF World Championship in Doha (November 1–14, 2026) — the 55th edition and the first competition counting as a qualifier for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics — India's Niraj Kumar won bronze in the men's three positions event (ISSF, n.d.-c).

## Rules

Under the ISSF rules, shooters use a .22 Long Rifle (5.6 mm) rimfire rifle at 50 m. The qualification consists of 40 shots in each position — prone, standing and kneeling — 120 shots in total, with decimal scoring (maximum 10.9 per shot) (ISSF, n.d.-b). The top eight advance to the final, where shooters fire further strings in each position and the lowest-scoring athlete is eliminated after set stages until one remains. Equipment rules differ by position: a sling may be used for prone and kneeling, but in standing the rifle must be held unsupported. Men and women shoot the identical event and distances; the only differences are the separate men's and women's competitions and rankings (ISSF, n.d.-b; ISSF, n.d.-c). A related variant is 300m Rifle Three Positions at the ISSF World Championships — the same three positions at 300 m with centerfire rifles — so the discipline exists in both 50 m and 300 m versions (ISSF, n.d.-a).

## History

The event has been an Olympic staple since Rome 1960. Wu Xiaoxuan of China won the first Olympic gold medal ever for her country in this event at Los Angeles 1984 — one of the most celebrated achievements in Chinese sport. Other Olympic champions include Matt Emmons of the USA (Athens 2004), Sergei Martynov of Belarus (London 2012) and Niccolò Campriani of Italy (Rio 2016), while the women's event, added for Sydney 2000, has been won by athletes such as Du Li of China and Barbara Lechner of Germany (Olympics.com, n.d.). The ISSF World Championships, first held in 1897, have included rifle three positions for over a century, and the 2026 edition in Doha, Qatar, is the 55th (ISSF, n.d.-c).
