## Basic Information

Headis (header table tennis) is a sport invented in 2006 by German sports student René Wegner in Kaiserslautern, played on a table tennis table with a reinforced net and a special soft rubber ball that may be touched only with the head. Two players stand on opposite sides of the table and rally by heading the ball, combining table tennis footwork with football-style headers; an annual Headis World Championship is held, and the brand is run by Headis GmbH.

## Rules

Players may only touch the ball with the head; hands and arms are not allowed. The ball must cross the net and bounce on the opponent's half before being headed back — volleys and table contact are permitted, allowing diving headers. A set is won at 11 points with a two-point margin, and matches are best of three or five sets (the official format is two winning sets). To prevent players from standing on the table, one part of the body must touch the floor after each ball contact. Serves alternate every two points (or every five in some variants); let serves are replayed.

## History

René Wegner developed Headis in 2006 at the "Wesch" pool hall in Kaiserslautern, Germany, and trademarked the brand; the first Headis World Championship followed, and by 2017 an estimated 80,000 people worldwide played, with events in Las Vegas, Shanghai and Sydney. The sport gained international media coverage through MLS Soccer, Fox Sports and Vice, and Headis GmbH licenses franchise partners worldwide. It remains a niche but well-documented novelty sport with an official ball and net sold commercially.
