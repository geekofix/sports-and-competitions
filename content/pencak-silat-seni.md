## Basic Information

Pencak silat seni is the artistic competition discipline of [pencak silat](pencak-silat.html), the traditional martial art of Indonesia and the Malay world. Seni consists of choreographed empty-hand and weapon forms — solo (tunggal), doubles (ganda) and team (regu) — performed before a jury and judged on technique, precision, flow and fidelity to the traditional movement vocabulary (jurus) (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Seni is one of the standard categories at the Southeast Asian Games and at the world championships of the International Pencak Silat Federation (PERSILAT), alongside the sparring discipline [tanding](pencak-silat-tanding.html). Seni is contested by men and women in separate classes — a women's seni performance in the GASMI style is pictured below — and UNESCO's 2019 inscription of pencak silat traditions highlighted the artistic aspect as a core element of the heritage (UNESCO, 2019).

## Rules

Seni competition is governed by the International Pencak Silat Competition Rules and Regulations issued by PERSILAT (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Competitors salute (salam persilat) before entering the arena and greet the jury and competition chair before their performance, which begins at the sound of a gong. Each federation may enter one athlete per seni event, with men's and women's classes. Solo performances last a set time and combine compulsory jurus with the athlete's own movement sequences and, in the weapons class, traditional weapons such as the toya (stick); doubles and team categories are judged on coordinated combat choreography. The jury scores precision, power, stability and the harmony of the performance with its accompanying music, and deductions are applied for dropped weapons, broken sequences or leaving the arena (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Men's and women's seni events use identical scoring criteria and time limits.

## History

Artistic forms have been part of pencak silat since the art's village origins, where jurus were taught as flowing sequences and performed at ceremonies with gamelan-style accompaniment (UNESCO, 2019). When PERSILAT was founded in Jakarta on 11 March 1980, seni was established as a formal competition category beside tanding, and the international rulebook was developed jointly by the four founding national federations of Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Brunei (International Pencak Silat Federation, n.d.). Seni entered the Southeast Asian Games with the wider pencak silat program at the 1987 SEA Games in Jakarta and remains a medal sport, with Indonesia traditionally strongest but Vietnam, Thailand, Singapore and Malaysia all winning medals (Wikipedia, n.d.). The recognition of the traditions of pencak silat by UNESCO in December 2019 highlighted the artistic aspect of the discipline as a core element of the heritage (UNESCO, 2019).
