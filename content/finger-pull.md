## Basic Information

Finger pull is an Arctic strength grip event: two competitors sit facing each other, hook the little finger (or finger) of one hand through a small wooden toggle, and try to pull the opponent's hand over a centre line — a test of grip mimicking holding a fish by the tail (AWG, n.d.; WEIO, n.d.). It is contested at the Arctic Winter Games and WEIO in men's, women's and youth categories. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Competitors sit on the floor facing each other, feet braced; the pulling finger is hooked through the toggle at arm's length. The bout ends when one competitor's hand crosses the centre board; best of three pulls decides. Left and right hand pulls are separate events (WEIO, n.d.).

## History

Finger pull derives from Inuit and Dene subsistence skills — gripping fish, tools and sled ropes — and has been a WEIO event since the 1970s and an AWG Arctic sport since 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
