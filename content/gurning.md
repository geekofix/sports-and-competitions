## Basic Information

Gurning is the art of pulling the ugliest possible face — competitive grimacing — and its home is the World Gurning Championship, held every year in mid-September as the climax of the Egremont Crab Fair in Egremont, Cumbria, England, a fair chartered by Henry III in 1267 (Egremont Crab Fair, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Contestants grimace while wearing a horse collar — "gurning through a braffin" — and the most distorted face wins; the championship is contested in three classes, men's, women's and juniors, and Guinness World Records recognizes it as the longest-running gurning world championships (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the 759th fair on 19 September 2026, more than 76 competitors — 31 of them juniors — took part: in the women's event Claire Lister (née Spedding) reclaimed her title ahead of Lynn Kelly, the men's title went to local man Ged Eccles (a first win after decades of trying, with actor Will Mellor second and Daniel Shutt third), and Bobby Donald won the junior championship (BBC News, 2026; Cumbria Crack, 2026).

## Rules

In the World Gurning Championship at Egremont, each contestant puts their head through a horse collar (the "braffin"), which is placed around the neck, and then pulls the ugliest, most expressive face possible — judges score the distortion of the eyes, mouth, teeth and overall expression while the collar is worn (Egremont Crab Fair, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The competition runs in heats with the strongest gurners advancing to a final, and it is divided into three classes: men's, women's and juniors, each with its own champion; women and men compete under identical rules in separate classes, and the women's title is a fully established part of the programme (BBC News, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). There is no physical contact between competitors, and the event is a light-hearted tradition rather than a combat sport — the only record of endurance is the longest sustained gurn, held by Jovante Carter at 1 minute 2 seconds according to Guinness World Records (Guinness World Records, n.d.). Local variants exist at other fairs and fêtes, where the same "through the horse collar" format is used (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Egremont Crab Fair was chartered by Henry III in 1267 and is one of England's oldest fairs; the gurning contest was described as an "ancient tradition" in the Cumberland Paquet of 1852 and has been a fixture of the fair since at least the 1500s, while the first official World Gurning Championships took place in the 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; BBC News, 2026). The origin story says the contest began as mockery of a village idiot, to whom a horse collar was thrown over the head as a joke — and who was then rewarded with pints of ale for his ugly face, inspiring the competition (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the icons of the championship are Tommy Mattinson, who won the men's title a record 19 times (most recently in 2024), Anne Woods, who won the women's title a record 28 times before her death in 2015 (her record ratified by Guinness in 2010), and Peter Jackman, a four-time champion from 1998 whose "Bela Lugosi" gurn became famous after he had his teeth removed in 2000 to improve it (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
