## Basic Information

Pac-Man is Namco's 1980 maze-chase arcade game (eat all dots, avoid the four ghosts, power pellets turn the tables); beyond high-score play it has a small but real esport scene — the Pac-Man World Championship run by Bandai Namco from 2007 to 2016 — and high-score competitions on original arcade hardware with officially tracked records (Wikipedia, n.d.) (Bandai Namco, n.d.). Tournaments play the original 1980 ROM with locked dipswitch settings; open to all genders.

## Rules

High-score competition rules: original arcade ROM, standard dipswitch settings (three lives), no pause; a game ends when all lives are lost; the highest score wins; verification by video recording and referees (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Created by Toru Iwatani at Namco and released in Japan in May 1980, Pac-Man became a global phenomenon — the highest-grossing arcade game of its era, a Billboard No. 1 single and an animated TV series; Billy Mitchell's famous perfect score of 3,333,360 became one of the best-documented records in arcade history (Wikipedia, n.d.).
