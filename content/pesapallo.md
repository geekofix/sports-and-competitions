## Basic Information

Pesäpallo (Finnish for "nest ball", also known as Finnish baseball or pesis) is a bat-and-ball sport that is the national game of Finland, invented in the 1920s by the athlete and physical educator Lauri "Tahko" Pihkala. Two teams take turns batting and fielding, superficially resembling [baseball](baseball.html) but with a different field, a vertical pitch and radically different tactics. The top league, Superpesis, runs men's and women's championships, and the Pesäpallo World Cup is played internationally about every three years.

## Rules

The batting team scores runs (juoksut) by hitting the ball and advancing along the base path back to home base, while the fielding team tries to put the batter and the runners out. The most striking differences from baseball concern the field and the pitch. The three bases are not arranged in a diamond but lead away from home base in a spread pattern, and the pitcher (lukkari) stands directly opposite the batter at the home base, throwing the ball vertically so that it rises at least one meter and drops onto a circular pitching plate of 60 cm diameter placed in front of the batter, who strikes the ball as it drops. A hit that would be a home run in baseball counts as a foul in pesäpallo instead, so all runs are effectively of the inside-the-park variety and the game rewards placement and quick thinking over raw power. A men's field measures about 93.5 by 62 meters (women's about 82 by 56 meters), far larger than a baseball infield, and fielders may put runners out by catching the ball and touching the base ahead of the runner, as in baseball. The ball is yellow, resembles a tennis ball but is heavier, with a circumference of about 21.6–22.2 cm; its weight varies by series. Games are decided over a set structure of innings, with the team scoring more runs winning the match, and a tied match goes to extra innings until a winner is determined.

## History

Pihkala developed the game from Finnish kuningaspallo ("king ball") and North American baseball, and a first game under essentially modern rules was played at Kaisaniemi Park in Helsinki on 14 November 1920. The rules were refined over the following years and the first Finnish championship was decided in 1922. Pesäpallo was a demonstration sport at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics, where Pihkala threw a ceremonial first pitch before a crowd of over 19,000. The championship series were reorganized under the Superpesis name in 1990, and the professionalism and media visibility of the league grew steadily. The sport is also played in Sweden, Estonia, Germany, Switzerland, Australia and Japan, and a Pesäpallo World Cup has been staged since 1992—Finland has never been seriously challenged in it.
