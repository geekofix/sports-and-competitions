## Basic Information

Mounted archery (horseback archery) is the sport of shooting arrows with a bow while riding a horse at speed, usually at a gallop along a straight track. It was historically a defining skill of the Eurasian steppe peoples — Scythians, Huns, Hungarians, Mongols, Turks and others — and survives today in traditional forms such as Korean gungsul, Hungarian and Turkish acabaq styles and Japanese yabusame. Modern competition is organized by the World Federation of Equestrian Archery (WFEA) and the International Horseback Archery Alliance (IHAA), which holds biennial world championships. Related disciplines include plain [archery](archery.html) and ski archery.

## Rules

In WFEA competition, the archer rides a 90 m (or shorter) straight track and must shoot within a set run time (20 seconds for the main runs); points are scored for hits on targets placed along the track, and a run is invalid if the archer steps onto the course without permission, exceeds the run time or fails to score. Competitions are run in the Korean, Hungarian and acabaq styles, each with its own target and scoring system, plus a mogu (Mongolian) style. The IHAA grades horseback archers through student and horse-archer levels at walk, trot and canter, and its biennial World Championships contest individual and team titles in eventing disciplines. Riders wear safety helmets and use horse bows appropriate to the style.

## History

Horse archery was used for hunting, herding protection and war across the Eurasian steppes and by the Hungarians, Mongols, Chinese and Turkic peoples in the Middle Ages; in East Asia it was honored in the Japanese samurai tradition of yabusame, which still exists in ceremonial form. In the Americas, mounted archery developed among peoples such as the Comanches on the North American prairies. The modern sport was revived in the 20th century in Hungary, Korea, Mongolia and Japan, and international organizations were founded to standardize styles and run world championships: the WFEA publishes the international rules, and the IHAA, based in the United States, runs its World Championships since the 2010s with events such as the 2025 edition in Tennessee.
