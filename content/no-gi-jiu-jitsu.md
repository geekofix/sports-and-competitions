## Basic Information

No-gi jiu-jitsu (no-gi grappling, submission wrestling) is the form of [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](brazilian-jiu-jitsu.html) practiced without the traditional gi (kimono): athletes wear a rash guard and grappling shorts or spats, so grips are taken on the body and clothing grips are banned (IBJJF, n.d.-a). It is the competition format used in MMA and by the premier submission grappling events — the ADCC Submission Wrestling World Championship (biennial, founded 1998) and the IBJJF No-Gi World Championship (ADCC, n.d.; IBJJF, n.d.-b). Because no fabric can be grabbed, the technical game emphasizes wrestling-style takedowns, leg locks and body locks. Women compete in their own weight divisions at both ADCC and the IBJJF No-Gi Worlds, with champions such as Elisabeth Clay and Ffion Davies among the most decorated no-gi grapplers (IBJJF, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under IBJJF no-gi rules, athletes score points for takedowns, guard passes, mounts, back control and sweeps (for example 2 points for a takedown, 4 for mount), and the match is won by submission (tap, verbal or referee stoppage) or by points after the time limit; heel hooks are allowed only at brown and black belt in the adult divisions (IBJJF, n.d.-a). ADCC rules allow all leg locks at every level; matches last 10 minutes in weight classes plus an open Absolute division, with negative points for pulling guard to discourage stalling, overtime and a Superfight format at the world finals (ADCC, n.d.). Competitors must wear an approved rash guard and shorts; no gi, shoes or jewelry are permitted (IBJJF, n.d.-a). Women's and men's divisions use identical scoring and time limits, differing only in the weight classes; youth divisions restrict heel hooks and slam techniques for both genders (ADCC, n.d.; IBJJF, n.d.-a).

## History

Brazilian jiu-jitsu was originally a gi art, but vale tudo and early MMA contests in Brazil (from the 1920s onward) forced fighters to grapple without clothing grips, developing the no-gi game (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 10th Planet Jiu-Jitsu school, founded by Eddie Bravo in 2003, popularized no-gi training in the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Abu Dhabi Combat Club (ADCC), founded in 1998 by Sheikh Tahnoon Bin Zayed Al Nahyan together with his instructor Nelson Monteiro, created the biennial Submission Wrestling World Championship in Abu Dhabi (first edition 1998), which became the most prestigious no-gi event in the world (ADCC, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The IBJJF added a No-Gi World Championship to its calendar alongside the gi Worlds, and women's divisions were added to both as the sport grew (IBJJF, n.d.-b). Champions such as Royler Gracie, who characterized ADCC as the beginning of "the modern era of submission grappling", and later Andre Galvao defined the early era of the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.).
