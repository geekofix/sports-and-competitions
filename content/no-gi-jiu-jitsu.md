## Basic Information

No-gi jiu-jitsu (no-gi grappling, submission wrestling) is the form of [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](brazilian-jiu-jitsu.html) practiced without the traditional gi (kimono): athletes wear a rash guard and grappling shorts or spats, so grips are taken on the body and clothing grips are banned. It is the competition format used in MMA and by the premier submission grappling events: the ADCC Submission Wrestling World Championship (biennial, founded 1998) and the IBJJF No-Gi World Championship. Because no fabric can be grabbed, the technical game emphasizes wrestling-style takedowns, leg locks and body locks.

## Rules

Under IBJJF no-gi rules, athletes score points for takedowns, guard passes, mounts, back control and sweeps (for example 2 points for a takedown, 4 for mount), and the match is won by submission (tap, verbal or referee stoppage) or by points after the time limit; heel hooks are allowed only at brown and black belt in the adult divisions, while ADCC allows all leg locks at every level. ADCC matches last 10 minutes (plus overtime and a Superfight format at the world finals) in weight classes plus an open Absolute division, with negative points for pulling guard to discourage stalling. Competitors must wear an approved rash guard and shorts; no gi, shoes or jewelry are permitted.

## History

Brazilian jiu-jitsu was originally a gi art, but vale tudo and early MMA contests in Brazil (from the 1920s-1990s) forced fighters to grapple without grips on clothing, developing the no-gi game. The 10th Planet Jiu-Jitsu school, founded by Eddie Bravo in 2003, popularized no-gi training in the United States. The Abu Dhabi Combat Club (ADCC), founded in 1998 by Sheikh Tahnoon Bin Zayed Al Nahyan, created the biennial Submission Wrestling World Championship in Abu Dhabi (first edition 1998, held every two years since 2001), which became the most prestigious no-gi event in the world. The IBJJF added a No-Gi World Championship to its calendar alongside the gi Worlds. Champions such as Gordon Ryan, Andre Galvao and the Ruotolo brothers have defined the modern no-gi era.
