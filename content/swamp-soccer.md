## Basic Information

Swamp soccer (Finnish *Suopotkupallo*) is association football played in a drained peat bog: two teams of six battle in knee-deep mud on a 60 × 35 m pitch, with unlimited substitutions, no offside and restarts taken by dropping the ball onto a foot. The game was invented in 1998 in Hyrynsalmi, Kainuu, Finland — the idea came from local entrepreneur Jyrki Väänänen, the "Swamp Baron", after top Finnish cross-country skiers had long trained in the bogs — and the first Finnish championship was held the same year with 13 teams (Swamp Soccer World Championships, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The European Championship followed in 1999 and the World Championship has been held annually since 2000 at the Vuorisuo bog by Ukkohalla, where the 2025 edition (18–20 July) drew about 200 teams and more than 2000 players; a UK World Cup ran in Dunoon from 2004 to 2015 with more than 100 teams from 25 countries, and Istanbul hosted in 2016 (Swamp Soccer World Championships, n.d.). Matches are played in men's, women's and mixed divisions — in mixed series at least two female players must be on the field at all times (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

A swamp soccer match runs two 10-minute halves with six players per side (five outfield plus a goalkeeper), unlimited squad size and substitutions, and no offside rule on the 60 × 35 m pitch with a 5 m penalty area (Swamp Soccer World Championships, n.d.). The goalkeeper may hold the ball only within 3 m of his own goal, and all restarts — throw-ins, corners and penalties — are taken by dropping the ball onto a foot rather than throwing or kicking it in, because of the mud (Wikipedia, n.d.). A team must keep at least four players on the field to continue, a match is forfeited below that, and in the mixed series at least two female players must be on the pitch at all times; players wear studded boots or bare feet and the bog itself is the hazard (Swamp Soccer World Championships, n.d.). Teams swear a fair-play oath to respect the Vuorisuo bog at the World Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's, women's and mixed divisions are scored identically under the same three points for a win (Swamp Soccer World Championships, n.d.).

## History

The idea was born in 1998 in Hyrynsalmi when Jyrki Väänänen proposed playing football in the local bog, inspired by cross-country skiers' bog training; the first Finnish championship the same year attracted 13 teams, the European Championship began in 1999 and the World Championship in 2000 (Swamp Soccer World Championships, n.d.). The game spread to the UK, Turkey, Iceland, Sweden, Russia and Brazil — by 2020 roughly 260 teams worldwide played organized swamp soccer, with the Dunoon, Scotland, World Cup (2004–2015) the biggest international event outside Finland (Wikipedia, n.d.).
