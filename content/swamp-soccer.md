## Basic Information

Swamp soccer (Finnish suopotkupallo) is the Finnish-born variant of football played in knee-deep bog: two six-a-side teams slog through a swamp pitch chasing a ball, with the terrain "+E+" not tactics "+E+" the real opponent. Since 1998 it has staged official championships, and since 2000 the Swamp Soccer World Championships at Hyrynsalmi in the Kainuu region have made it one of Finland's best-known novelty world titles.

## Rules

Rules adapt football to the bog: matches are two short halves (typically 10 to 13 minutes), teams field six players including the goalkeeper on a pitch roughly 60 by 35 metres of muck, squads are unlimited with free substitutions, and there is no offside. Restarts are minimalist "+E+" corners, penalties and kick-ins are taken with a simple drop of the ball onto a foot "+E+" and a strict safety and fair-play oath governs conduct; mixed divisions require women on the field at all times, and teams that fall below the minimum number of players forfeit. Given the terrain, games are contests of lung-busting strength as much as skill, and the championship rules require contestants to respect the Vuorisuo bog itself.

## History

Swamp soccer grew from Finnish summer training: cross-country skiers, athletes and soldiers used the soft bog terrain as conditioning, and in 1998 the first Finnish championship was organized at Hyrynsalmi in Kainuu (with thirteen teams), followed by European championship status in 1999 and World Championship status from 2000, typically contested by well over a hundred teams each July. The sport spread internationally, with a high-profile Swamp Soccer World Cup staged at Dunoon in Scotland from 2004 to 2015, drawing over a hundred teams from dozens of countries at its peak. The Finnish championship remains the sport's flagship, with divisions for men, women, mixed, veterans and youth, and an estimated several hundred active teams worldwide keep the mud flying.
