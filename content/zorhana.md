## Basic Information

Zorhana (zorkhana, “house of strength”) is the traditional Azerbaijani and Iranian system of strength games and folk wrestling performed in a special arena; it was shown as a demonstration sport at the first World Nomad Games in 2014 (described there as “a form of wrestling from Azerbaijan and Iran”) and at the 2016 Games. UNESCO inscribed “Pahlevani and zoorkhaneh rituals” (2010) and “Pehlevanliq culture: traditional zorkhana games, sports and wrestling” (Azerbaijan, 2022) on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Women's zorkhana clubs have also emerged in Iran in recent decades.

## Rules

A zorkhana is a sunken, roughly octagonal arena (the kud or sufra) about 8–10 m across with a raised dais around it; sessions open with ritual exercises to music (drum and zurna), including swinging heavy wooden clubs (mil) and stone or metal weights (sang/bargir, up to 200 kg), millstone lifts and push-up rhythms (shena), and close with koshti — folk wrestling bouts. In Azerbaijani pehlevanliq, games and sports with weapon-like heavy apparatus (swords, shields, bows) are demonstrated, and bouts are held in rounds; victory goes to the wrestler who puts both of the opponent's shoulders on the mat. Champions (pehlevans) are celebrated with songs and titles.

## History

The zorkhana tradition goes back to pre-Islamic Persia and is documented in Parthian times; it trained warriors and became a beloved folk institution in Iran, Azerbaijan, Afghanistan and Iraq. In Azerbaijan, zorkhanas were centers of pahlavan competitions — notable Baku pehlevans included Muhammad Huseyn and Ali Hummat — and the culture was inscribed by UNESCO in 2022; the Iranian varzesh-e pahlavani was inscribed in 2010. Shah Ismail Khatai was himself a famed wrestler who defeated an Ottoman champion at Chaldiran in 1514. The tradition was demonstrated at the World Nomad Games in Cholpon-Ata in 2014 and 2016, and zorkhana performances are part of Azerbaijan's Victory Sports Festival in Baku.
