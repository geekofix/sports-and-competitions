## Basic Information

Kunan chabysh (“young horse race”) is the Kyrgyz long-distance race for young horses — two- to three-year-olds — ridden by child jockeys, contested at the World Nomad Games as part of the equestrian program alongside flat racing (at chabysh), zhorgo salysh, byshty zhorgo and alaman chabysh. At the 2026 Games in Cholpon-Ata the kunan chabysh was run over 12,000 m and won by the Kazakh horse Nurshashu, with Paris (Kyrgyzstan) second and Bestau (Kazakhstan) third. Child jockeys are typically 7–14 years old and weigh very little, which is why the young horses can carry them at speed; the races are a highlight of the hippodrome program at Cholpon-Ata on Lake Issyk-Kul. Within the Central Asian nomad games on this site, its closest relatives are [Zhorgo Salysh](zhorgo-salysh.html), [Mongolian Horse Racing](mongolian-horse-racing.html), [Baiga](baiga.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The kunan chabysh is a mass-start cross-country race over a set distance fixed by the organizers for each edition (12 km at the 2026 Games; other classes at the same Games ran 6 km for byshty zhorgo, 11 km for zhorgo salysh, 25 km for alaman chabysh and a long-distance marathon). Only young horses of the kunan age class may run, each with a child jockey; horses are identified and checked by officials, prizes are awarded to the leading horses and jockeys, and the race is timed at the hippodrome finish. Fouls such as dangerous riding are penalized.

## History

Horse racing of young horses with child jockeys is a core tradition of the Kyrgyz summer festivals; the name chabysh (“race at full gallop”) also gives the flat-racing discipline at chabysh. Kunan chabysh has been a competitive sport at every World Nomad Games since the inaugural 2014 edition in Cholpon-Ata, where the equestrian program comprised kok-boru, er enish, kunan chabysh, zhorgo salysh and alaman baige. The cognate Kazakh baiga festival includes the kunan distance class (1.2–2 km for the youngest horses), and Mongolian Naadam races similarly use child jockeys on cross-country courses.
