## Basic Information

Acrobatic gymnastics is a FIG discipline in which pairs and groups perform choreographed routines combining balance, dynamic (tempo) and combined elements — lifts, throws, catches and pyramids — to music on a 12 × 12 m floor. It has been governed by the Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique (FIG) since 1999, after the International Federation of Sports Acrobatics (IFSA) had organized the sport since 1973 (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique [FIG], n.d.-a). The five competition categories are women's pair, men's pair, mixed pair, women's group of three and men's group of four (FIG, n.d.-a). See also [Gymnastics](gymnastics.html) and [Aerobic Gymnastics](aerobic-gymnastics.html) on this site (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-b).

Among the athletes shaping the sport today are the Israeli women's pair, who won gold in balance (29.010) and combined (28.910) at the 30th Acrobatic Gymnastics World Championships in Pesaro, Italy (September 24–27, 2026) — an event that drew 249 senior athletes from 30 countries and gave Israel its first-ever team title with 88 points, ahead of Russia and the United States. The USA men's pair, the Ossolodkov brothers, completed a golden sweep of the men's pair events (FIG, n.d.-b; USA Gymnastics, 2026).

## Rules

Routines are performed on a 12 × 12 m sprung floor and last no longer than 2 minutes 30 seconds. Each routine must contain a required number of elements from three groups — balance, dynamic and combined — plus a difficulty score, an execution score and an artistry score, judged by a panel of judges under the FIG Code of Points (FIG, n.d.-a). The five categories differ in composition: women's pair (two women), men's pair (two men), mixed pair (one man, one woman), women's group (three women) and men's group (four men); the base typically lifts, throws or holds the top, who performs acrobatic elements. Age groups are junior and senior, and athletes progress from qualification to final. Men and women compete in separate categories but under the same Code of Points (FIG, n.d.-a).

## History

Acrobatic gymnastics grew out of circus acrobatics and partner acrobatics in the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe in the mid-20th century, where pair and group pyramids were already performed at festivals and in circus. The IFSA was founded in 1973 and organized the first World Championships in 1975; the FIG took over the discipline in 1999 and has run the World Championships annually since (FIG, n.d.-a). Nations such as Ukraine, Russia, Great Britain, the United States and China have dominated the medal tables, and the Ukrainian women's pair — pictured above at the 2014 Worlds in Levallois-Perret, France — are among the most decorated pairs in the sport's history, while on the men's side Bulgarian and Russian pairs set the standard for decades (FIG, n.d.-b).
