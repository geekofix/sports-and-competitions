## Basic Information

Futnet (also called foot tennis or "futevôlei de rede" in some regions) is a net sport played with a football-sized ball over a net (about 1.1 m high), in which players may use any body part except hands and arms, with a maximum of three touches per side and a limited number of ball bounces per team in official UNIF competitions. It is governed by the Union Internationale de Futnet (UNIF), founded in Humenné, Slovakia, which runs the Futnet World Cup and European Championships.

## Rules

Under the UNIF Laws of Futnet, teams of one (singles), two (doubles) or three (triples) play on a rectangular court roughly 9 m x 18 m for doubles, with a net around 1.1 m high. Each team may touch the ball up to three times, and under UNIF rules a maximum of three ball bounces (drops) is allowed per team in competition. A point is scored when the ball lands in the opponent's court, the opponents exceed the touch/drop limits, or a fault is committed. Matches are best of three sets to 11 or 15 points. Serves are underhand from behind the end line. National associations may adjust touch and drop limits per category.

## History

Futnet emerged in Czechoslovakia in the 1970s-80s as "nohejbal" (foot volleyball) and spread through Central and Eastern Europe, South America (especially Brazil and Argentina) and Asia. The Union Internationale de Futnet (UNIF) was founded in Humenné, Slovakia, in 2010 (statutes registered in the Czech Republic) and has organized the Futnet World Cup and European Championships since the 2010s. The strongest nations are the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Brazil, Argentina, Hungary and Russia. The sport is closely related to footvolley (beach, 2v2, higher net) and sepak takraw (kick-only).
