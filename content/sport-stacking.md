## Basic Information

Sport stacking (formerly cup stacking) is the timed sport of stacking and unstacking a set of specially designed plastic cups in predetermined sequences, racing against the clock or head-to-head. A gym-class phenomenon turned global competition, it is governed by the World Sport Stacking Association (WSSA), which sanctions tournaments worldwide and ratifies world records in a range of individual, relay and age-group events.

## Rules

Stackers compete on cups in three core sequences: the 3-3-3 (three stacks of three), the 3-6-3 (a three-stack, a six-stack, another three-stack) and the cycle (a longer pattern that takes the stacker from a 3-6-3 through stacked and unstacked sequences back to a 3-6-3 finish). Each sequence must begin from a starting position with hands flat, every up-stack and down-stack must be completed in order, and a fumbled cup "+E+" fallen, improperly nested or touched out of turn "+E+" forces correction before the sequence may continue while the clock keeps running. Events are decided by electronically timed runs, with the fastest time winning; relay events add exchange rules between stackers, and age divisions keep competition fair from elementary school through masters.

## History

Cup stacking began as a recreational novelty in southern California in the early 1980s, appearing on television before making its way into physical education programs as a fun way to build hand-eye coordination and ambidexterity. A decisive step came in the late 1990s, when teacher and coach Bob Fox brought the activity to a national school audience and founded Speed Stacks; in 2001 he created the World Cup Stacking Association, which was renamed the World Sport Stacking Association in 2005 as the sport took on its modern name. Under the WSSA, stacking grew into a worldwide competitive scene with sanctioned tournaments in dozens of countries, televised record attempts and world records in the 3-3-3, 3-6-3 and cycle events that continue to fall as new generations of stackers push the sport faster.
