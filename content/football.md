## Basic Information

Football (association football, soccer) is the world's most popular sport: two teams of eleven players try to score by getting a ball into the opponent's goal, using any part of the body except hands and arms (goalkeepers excepted within their penalty area), on a pitch of 105 m × 68 m (100–110 m × 64–75 m allowed) for 90 minutes (IFAB, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game is governed by the Laws of the Game, maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB) — FIFA plus the four British associations — and organized at world level by FIFA, founded in 1904 in Paris with 211 member associations today (FIFA, n.d.-a; IFAB, n.d.-a). The FIFA World Cup, held every four years since 1930, is the biggest sporting event on earth; the 2026 FIFA World Cup was the 23rd edition, staged June 11 – July 19, 2026 in the United States, Canada and Mexico at 16 venues, and the first with 48 teams and 104 matches — twelve groups of four, with the top two and the eight best third-placed teams advancing to a new round of 32 — drawing a record attendance of about 7 million; the draw took place December 5, 2025, at the Kennedy Center in Washington, D.C., and the opening match was Mexico versus South Africa at Estadio Azteca in Mexico City (FIFA, n.d.-b). The final on July 19, 2026, at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey ended Spain 1–0 Argentina after extra time, Ferran Torres scoring in the 106th minute — Spain's second world title after 2010, coming on top of UEFA Euro 2024 (FIFA, n.d.-b) (FIFA, n.d.-a).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Spain's national team, number one in the FIFA Women's World Ranking ahead of the USA, England — European champions of 2025 after beating Spain on penalties — Japan and Brazil (FIFA, n.d.-c). Spain's Aitana Bonmatí, winner of the Ballon d'Or Féminin in 2023, 2024 and 2025, is the outstanding player of the moment, and the next FIFA Women's World Cup will be the 2027 edition in Brazil, opening June 28, 2027, with the final at the Maracanã (FIFA, n.d.-c; FIFA, n.d.-d). On the men's side, the 2026 World Cup awards went to Golden Ball Rodri (Spain), Golden Boot Kylian Mbappé (10 goals — an all-time World Cup record of 22 across two tournaments and his second consecutive Golden Boot), Golden Glove Unai Simón (Spain, a record seven clean sheets), Best Young Player Pau Cubarsí, the Fair Play Trophy to the Netherlands and the Goal of the Tournament to Sidny Lopes Cabral of Cape Verde (FIFA, n.d.-b).

## Rules

The IFAB Laws of the Game (17 laws) are the single worldwide rulebook (IFAB, n.d.-a). Law 1 defines the field of play (105 m × 68 m standard, goals 7.32 m wide and 2.44 m high); Law 3 fixes the number of players at eleven including one goalkeeper, with a minimum of seven, and substitutions — five permitted in FIFA competitions, plus one additional in extra time (IFAB, n.d.-a). Law 5 gives the referee full authority, assisted by two assistant referees and a video assistant referee (VAR, in use at the World Cup since 2018, with semi-automated offside technology since 2022) (IFAB, n.d.-a). The match lasts two halves of 45 minutes (Law 7), with added time; knockout matches unresolved after 90 minutes go to two 15-minute halves of extra time and then to penalty kicks (Law 7/10 procedure). Law 11 (offside) penalizes a player in an offside position who becomes involved in active play; Law 12 distinguishes direct and indirect free kicks and the penalty kick (awarded from the 11 m mark for a direct-free-kick offence in the penalty area), and defines cautions (yellow card) and sending-offs (red card) (IFAB, n.d.-a). Restart rules (throw-in, goal kick, corner kick, Law 15–17) complete the basic code; recent amendments include the eight-second limit for a goalkeeper holding the ball (2025) (IFAB, n.d.-a). Formats: domestic leagues and cups, the UEFA Champions League (league phase since 2024/25), continental championships, and the World Cup format introduced in 2026 (group stage plus a round of 32, round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, third-place match and final) (FIFA, n.d.-b). Men and women play under identical Laws; FIFA runs separate Women's World Cup (since 1991; 2027 in Brazil), Olympic tournaments (men since 1900, women since 1996) and youth World Cups (U-20 and U-17, men's and women's), while five-a-side and seven-a-side football are separate Paralympic disciplines (FIFA, n.d.-a; FIFA, n.d.-d).

## History

Modern football was codified in England: the Sheffield Rules (1858) predate the Football Association, founded in London in 1863 under Ebenezer Cobb Morley, whose 1863 Laws of the Game banned carrying the ball in the hands (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). FIFA was founded in Paris on May 21, 1904, by seven nations, and the first FIFA World Cup was played in Uruguay in 1930, won by the hosts; the trophy was named after FIFA president Jules Rimet (1921–1954) (FIFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The world championship has produced the sport's defining icons: Pelé of Brazil, the only player with three World Cup titles (1958, 1962, 1970); Diego Maradona of Argentina, scorer of both the "Hand of God" and the "Goal of the Century" in the 1986 quarter-final; Franz Beckenbauer, who won the 1974 World Cup as captain and the 1990 as coach; Johan Cruyff, the Dutch pioneer of "total football"; and in the modern era Zinedine Zidane, Ronaldo Nazário and Lionel Messi, who lifted the trophy in 2022 after eight Ballon d'Or awards and reached the 2026 final (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The women's game was formalized with the first FIFA Women's World Championship in China in 1991, and its icons include the USA's Mia Hamm and Abby Wambach and Brazil's Marta, record six-time FIFA World Player of the Year (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
