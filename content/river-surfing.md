## Basic Information

River surfing is the sport of riding waves in rivers — stationary standing waves, tidal bores or upstream waves — instead of ocean swells; it has been documented since 1972 on the Eisbach, a 2-km side arm of the Isar in Munich's Englischer Garten near the Haus der Kunst, where a standing wave about 1 m high, with a flow of roughly 5 m/s at 20 m³/s, attracts up to 100 surfers a day and a community of around 1,000 active surfers (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Surfing on the Eisbach wave was officially permitted from 2010 and an annual contest is held there; after dredging works temporarily destroyed the wave in November 2025, activists prevented further work in December 2025 and the wave was back by 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Other famous spots include the Mur wave in Graz, Austria (built 2001, rebuilt 2004 by the KanuClub Graz), the Almwelle in Salzburg, Montreal's Habitat 67 wave on the Lachine Rapids, and tidal bores such as the Petitcodiac River in Canada, which holds the North American record ride of 24 July 2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Both women and men surf rivers, and competitive surfing in general falls under the International Surfing Association (ISA), which stages the World Surfing Games (ISA, n.d.).

## Rules

River surfing has no single worldwide rulebook; it is organized locally around individual waves, while competitive surfing is governed by the ISA, whose judging scale (0–10 per wave, best waves count) and contest formats — man-on-man heats, maneuver criteria, time limits — are also used at river wave contests (ISA, n.d.). Safety rules at the classic river spots are strict: a leash is mandatory on the Eisbach — a 33-year-old surfer drowned there in April 2025 after getting tangled — a helmet is recommended, and the local etiquette gives the right of way to the surfer closest to the wave's peak, while dropping in on another rider is an interference (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the Eisbach only one surfer rides the main wave at a time and others queue on the riverbank; the smaller E2 wave downstream, the Floßlände and the Isar at Wittelsbacherbrücke during floods serve as alternative lines (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A related river discipline is boatercross, where paddlers race head-to-head down a rapid course, which is run under canoeing federation rules rather than surfing rules (ISA, n.d.). Both women and men compete in river surfing contests, with mixed and women's categories at larger events (ISA, n.d.).

## History

Claims of surfing a tidal bore date back to 1955 on the Severn in England, but documented river surfing begins in 1972 on the Eisbach in Munich, where pioneers such as Dieter Deventer have ridden the wave since 1973 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The wave became an urban surfing landmark, was officially permitted for surfing in 2010, and has hosted an annual contest ever since; its temporary disappearance after dredging in November 2025 sparked protests and it was restored by 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Other milestones are the artificial Mur wave in Graz built in 2001 and rebuilt in 2004, the Almwelle in Salzburg, Montreal's Habitat 67 wave, and bore surfing on the Petitcodiac, whose 24 July 2013 ride set the North American record (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
