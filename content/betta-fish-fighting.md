## Basic Information

Betta fish fighting is a traditional Thai contest in which two male Siamese fighting fish (Betta splendens, "pla kat") are placed in a small tank and spar until one retreats; in modern competitions, winners are decided by which fish shows more aggression or by displays of fins and color. The fish has been bred for fighting in Thailand for centuries and was declared Thailand's national aquatic animal in 2019.

## Rules

In traditional matches, two male bettas are put into a darkened tank divided by a glass pane; when the pane is lifted they flare their gills and bite at each other, and the first to back off loses — historically with gambling on the outcome. In modern show competitions such as the International Plakad Competition in Bangkok, fish are judged on color, finnage and fighting spirit rather than fought to the death. Only males are used, since they are highly territorial; females are never pitted against each other in fights.

## History

Siamese fighting fish have been bred and fought in Thailand since at least the Thonburi era (18th century); King Rama V regulated and taxed the fights in the mid-19th century. The fish was given its scientific name Betta splendens in 1910 by the British ichthyologist Charles Tate Regan, and it became a popular aquarium fish worldwide. In 2019 the Thai government officially declared it the national aquatic animal, and competitions now emphasize breeding and beauty alongside traditional sparring.
