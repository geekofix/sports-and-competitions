## Basic Information

Freediving is competitive breath-hold diving in which athletes descend on a single breath, with or without fins, and are judged on depth (constant weight), distance (dynamic) or time (static apnea). The two main governing bodies are AIDA International (AIDA), which runs the AIDA Depth World Championships, and CMAS (World Underwater Federation), which runs the CMAS World Championships with pool and depth disciplines.

## Rules

At AIDA competitions, disciplines include Constant Weight (CWT, depth with fins), Constant Weight No Fins (CNF), Free Immersion (FIM, pulling on the rope), Dynamic With Fins (DYN, horizontal distance), Dynamic No Fins (DNF) and Static Apnea (STA, breath-hold time). Athletes declare a target depth/distance/time; exceeding it scores, failing it or surfacing early with signs of impairment scores less or none. Safety divers and medical protocols are mandatory, and blackouts lead to disqualification. At CMAS championships, pool events (static apnea, dynamic with/without fins, surface, obstacle) and depth events (constant weight with/without fins, free immersion, no limits) are contested with electronic timing and judges. Anti-doping follows the WADA Code for both bodies.

## History

Breath-hold diving has ancient roots in pearl and sponge diving (Greek sponge divers, Japanese ama, Korean haenyeo), but competitive freediving emerged in the 1950s-60s when Italian Enzo Maiorca and later Jacques Mayol broke depth records, popularized by Luc Besson's 1988 film The Big Blue. AIDA was founded in 1992 in Nice by Mayol, Maiorca and others after disagreements with CMAS over no-limits depth records; CMAS, founded 1959, had organized underwater sports since the 1960s. Today world records exceed 130 m in constant weight (Alexey Molchanov, 131+ m) and 11+ minutes in static apnea (Stéphane Mifsud, 11:35 in 2009; Budimir Šobat's 24:11 is the Guinness record for pure oxygen-assisted breath-holding, not a competition record). Freediving is a regular discipline at the World Games.
