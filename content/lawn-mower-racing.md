## Basic Information

Lawn mower racing is an informal motorsport in which competitors race modified ride-on lawn mowers — with the cutting blades removed for safety — on small oval or figure-of-eight grass tracks. It is governed in the UK by the British Lawn Mower Racing Association (BLMRA), founded in 1973, and in the US by the U.S. Lawnmower Racing Association (USLMRA), founded in 1992; races include sprint championships and the BLMRA's flagship 12-hour endurance race.

## Rules

Racing mowers must be commercially produced domestic lawn mowers, modified only within the association's regulations; cutting blades must be removed or fully covered, and drivers wear full-face helmets, gloves, boots and a neck restraint. Engines may be tuned within class limits — the USLMRA's stock classes cap engines at 24 hp, while modified classes are open — and mowers must have working brakes, a kill switch and leg protectors. Races are run in groups (BLMRA Groups 1-4 from basic cylinder mowers to modified lawn tractors) as sprints of 15-30 minutes or laps, plus endurance events; points are awarded per finishing position, from 25 for the win down to 1 for tenth and the championship is decided over the season. The BLMRA's rules explicitly ban sponsorship, cash prizes and engine modification in its main classes to keep it the "cheapest form of motorsport".

## History

Lawn mower racing began in Britain in 1973, when rally co-driver Jim Gavin and friends in Wisborough Green, West Sussex, frustrated by the cost of motorsport, watched the groundsman mowing the cricket pitch and decided to race mowers — 80 mowers attended the first meeting, and the BLMRA was formed the same year. The first 12-hour endurance race was held in 1978 and has been won by motor racing legends Sir Stirling Moss and Derek Bell; actor Oliver Reed also competed. In the US, Bruce Kaufman organized the first race in 1992 as an April Fools' promotion for STA-BIL fuel stabilizer, and the USLMRA was founded the same year. Today races are held across the UK, US, Europe and Australia, attracting everyone from youth classes to former professional drivers.
