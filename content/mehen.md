## Basic Information

Mehen ('snake') is the ancient Egyptian coil game of the Old Kingdom (c. 2750–2250 BC): played on a coiled-serpent board with lion-headed pieces; players move pieces around the coils according to throws, aiming to reach the snake's head. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Senet](senet.html), [Latrunculi](latrunculi.html), [Alquerque](alquerque.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Oriental Institute Museum, n.d.).

## Rules

Coiled serpent board with a spiral of squares; lion pieces move by throw; the goal is to reach/land on the head square; rules are reconstructed from museum boards (Oriental Institute, British Museum, Berlin, Cairo) (Oriental Institute Museum, n.d.).

## History

Mehen boards and pieces survive from the Old Kingdom (Dynasties 3–6, c. 2750–2250 BC) — the Oriental Institute alabaster set (Daderot photo) and the Cairo/Berlin/Petrie boards; it predates senet in popularity and faded by the New Kingdom (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
