## Basic Information

Basque pelota (pelota vasca) is a family of ball games played against a wall (fronton) or in a court, using the hand, a wooden paddle (paleta), a leather glove or a large curved basket (chistera). The international governing body is the Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque (FIPV), an IOC-recognized federation founded in 1929, which organizes the Basque Pelota World Championships every four years.

## Rules

Under FIPV rules, the internationally recognized modalities are played on four standardized venues (trinquet and 30 m, 36 m and 54 m frontons). Hand pelota is played to 22 or 40 points depending on the venue; a point is scored when the ball is not returned, lands out of bounds or fails to reach the required distance. The serve must land in the service zone after bouncing beyond the service line. Different modalities use different equipment — bare hand, paleta (wooden paddle), pala corta (short bat), cesta punta (chistera) and frontenis — each with its own scoring and court dimensions.

## History

Pelota is one of the oldest ball games in Europe, with roots in the jeu de paume of medieval France; Basque villages have played against their church walls for centuries. The FIPV was founded on 19 May 1929 in Buenos Aires by the French, Spanish and Argentine federations. The first Basque Pelota World Championships were held in 1952, and the sport spread through the Americas with Basque emigration. Pelota was contested at the 1900 Paris Olympics and was a demonstration sport at the 1968 and 1992 Games.
