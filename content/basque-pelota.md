## Basic Information

Basque pelota (pelota vasca) is a family of ball games in which players hit a ball against a wall called a fronton, either with the bare hand (mano), a wooden paddle (paleta), a leather paddle (pala), or a curved basket (cesta punta, as in jai alai); the international governing body is the Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque (Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque [FIPV], n.d.-a), founded in Buenos Aires in 1929. The pinnacle event is the World Championships (Mundial), and the 20th edition (XX Mundial de Pelota Vasca) was held October 15–24, 2026 in Buenos Aires, Argentina, and Colonia del Sacramento, Uruguay, with 480 pelotaris from 15 delegations competing in nine modalities for men and women; Spain won the team classification with seven gold medals, Argentina took the men's paleta cuero final 2–0, and Euskadi (the Basque Country) competed for the first time as an independent country, winning the women's debut event 2–1 against Spain; the next Mundial is scheduled for Bilbao in 2030, and FIPV president Xabier Cazaubon was re-elected for 2026–2030 (FIPV, n.d.-b). Women's pelota has grown steadily: beyond the 2026 Mundial's women's modalities, Spanish players such as those photographed in Zarauz represent the strong women's club scene, and women now compete internationally in mano, paleta goma, frontenis and cesta punta at senior level (FIPV, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de Pelote Basque, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Pelota is played on a fronton consisting of a flat playing wall (frontis), a left side wall and a floor; the standard international fronton for cesta punta and pala is about 36 m long and 10–11 m wide, the 30 m fronton is used for paleta cuero and pala corta, and the enclosed trinquete is 28.5 m long and 9 m wide (FIPV, n.d.-a). Each modality has its own ball and implement: mano uses a small leather ball (pelota de mano), paleta goma a rubber ball struck with a wooden paddle, paleta cuero and pala corta a heavier leather ball, frontenis a pressurized ball with strung rackets, cesta punta a hard ball thrown with a long curved basket (cesta) at speeds beyond 300 km/h, xare a small wicker basket, and frontball (played in American frontons) a rubber ball. Matches are played to a set number of points — commonly 22, 30 or 40 depending on the modality — with a serve alternating between teams; a point is won when the ball bounces twice, hits a second wall illegally or goes out of bounds, and in mano the ball must be struck on the first bounce. Men's and women's competitions use the same frontons and rules, with lighter balls and shorter distances in youth categories; the women's programme has been part of the World Championships since the early editions and was expanded again at the 2026 Mundial (FIPV, n.d.-b).

## History

Pelota descends from the medieval jeu de paume and was played in the Basque Country from at least the Middle Ages, with the first codified rules appearing in the 19th century; the game travelled with Basque emigrants to Argentina, Uruguay, Mexico, Chile and the United States, where the fronton became a social institution. The FIPV was founded in Buenos Aires in 1929, the first World Championships were held in San Sebastián in 1952, and pelota appeared at the Paris 1900 Olympics as a demonstration sport and again as a demonstration sport at the Mexico City 1968 and Barcelona 1992 Games (FIPV, n.d.-b). Frontenis, the racket variant, was codified in Mexico in the early 20th century. On the women's side, female pelotaris have competed internationally since the late 20th century, and the 2026 Mundial in Buenos Aires and Colonia del Sacramento marked a milestone with women's events in several modalities and the debut of a Basque women's national team (FIPV, n.d.-a).
