## Basic Information

Flag football is the non-contact variant of [American Football](american-football.html): two teams of five players advance the ball down the field, but instead of tackling, defenders stop the ball carrier by pulling one of the two flags attached to the carrier's belt (IFAF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed worldwide by the International Federation of American Football (IFAF), which runs the biennial IFAF Flag Football World Championships — the 2026 edition was held in Düsseldorf, Germany, from 13 to 16 August 2026 — and flag football will make its Olympic debut at Los Angeles 2028, where six men's and six women's teams will play 5 v 5 at BMO Stadium from 15 to 22 July 2028 (IFAF, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.) (International Federation of American Football, n.d.-a) (International Federation of American Football, n.d.-b). Flag football is the non-contact member of the football-codes family — [American Football](american-football.html), [Arena Football](arena-football.html), [Sprint Football](sprint-football.html), [Canadian Football](canadian-football.html), [Australian Rules Football](australian-rules-football.html), [Gaelic Football](gaelic-football.html) and the rest listed under Important Links.

At the 2026 World Championships in Düsseldorf, the United States men won their record seventh world title, defeating Italy in the final, with Canada taking bronze; Italy and Canada thereby qualified for the men's tournament at LA 2028, while on the women's side Canada and Mexico qualified for the Olympic tournament through their performances in Düsseldorf (IFAF, n.d.-a). Women's and men's teams play under identical rules; mixed-gender flag football is also widely played at club and school level (IFAF, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Flag football is played 5 against 5 on a field about 70 yards (64 m) long and 25 yards (23 m) wide, with 10-yard (9 m) end zones; matches consist of two 20-minute halves with a running clock except in the final two minutes (IFAF, n.d.-b). The offense gets four downs to cross midfield and four more to score a touchdown (6 points); after a touchdown the conversion is worth 1 point from the 5-yard line or 2 points from the 12-yard line, and a safety is worth 2 points (IFAF, n.d.-b). The quarterback may not run with the ball past the line of scrimmage, and there are no kickoffs — each series starts with the ball placed on the offense's own 5-yard line (IFAF, n.d.-b). All defensive players must be behind the line of scrimmage at the snap, and a defender may not cross it before the snap (IFAF, n.d.-b). A tackle is made by pulling one flag from the ball carrier's belt; the ball is spotted where the flag was pulled; if a flag comes off in play, the ball is dead at that spot (IFAF, n.d.-b). Pass interference, holding and illegal contact are fouls; the field is marked in 5-yard intervals, and each team has two timeouts per half (IFAF, n.d.-b). Men and women play identical rules in separate or mixed tournaments; youth and wheelchair flag football variants exist under national associations (IFAF, n.d.-b).

## History

Flag football grew out of touch and flag variants of American football played in the United States from the 1940s onward — on military bases, at colleges and in parks — as a safer way to play the game without tackling (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Federation of American Football (IFAF), which governs the sport internationally and organizes the World Championships of tackle and flag football, organizes the IFAF Flag Football World Championships every two years since the 2010s. The IOC approved flag football for the LA 2028 Olympic program at its session in Mumbai in October 2023, in the 5 v 5 format (Olympics.com, n.d.). The United States has dominated the men's world championships, winning seven titles through 2026, while the women's game has grown rapidly, with the USA, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Panama among the leading nations (IFAF, n.d.-a).
