## Basic Information

Wing foiling (wingfoiling) is a water sport in which a rider stands on a hydrofoil board and holds a handheld inflatable wing that catches the wind — with no mast, boom or lines connecting wing to board — so the rider powers the board, rises on the hydrofoil and glides above the water's surface. The freestanding wing gives a blend of windsurfing, kitesurfing and foiling, and the sport can be raced around courses or surfed on swell, making it popular in light winds where other sail sports struggle.

Since the late 2010s wing foiling has become one of the fastest-growing water sports worldwide, with an international race tour and inclusion in sailing's youth pathway events.

## Rules

Course racing formats use fleet races around buoy tracks, with rules borrowed from sailing's right-of-way framework (port gives way to starboard, windward keeps clear of leeward) plus equipment class rules covering board and foil dimensions. Freeride and wave winging contests are judged subjectively on jumps, rotations and wave rides like tow-surfing and windsurfing freestyle formats. The hydrofoil lifts the board at speed, so riders must manage flight altitude with mast foot pressure, and competition rules include safety requirements (leash, buoyancy aid) and format rules for starts, penalties and rescues in federated events under World Sailing's wing racing framework introduced over the 2020s.

## History

The wing concept is old — handheld sail experiments date to the 1980s with Tom Magruder's Wind Weapon and inflatables by others — but the sport ignited when hydrofoil boards matured through kitesurfing and SUP foiling in the late 2010s, with the influx of purpose-built inflatable wings taking off between 2018 and 2020. Brands and riders from windsurfing and kitesurfing communities drove explosive growth, national sailing federations added wing racing programmes, and World Sailing-sanctioned class racing and youth circuit events emerged. Elite crossover athletes from other sail sports have won international titles, and the sport continues to expand with race series, freestyle contests and foil-assist wave events worldwide.
