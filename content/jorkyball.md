## Basic Information

Jorkyball is a fast indoor football variant played 2 vs 2 in a cage of about 9.80 × 4.80 × 2.70 m, where the walls and the ceiling are in play and rebounds are part of the game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was invented in 1987 by Gilles Paniez in the garage of his home in Lyon, France, where he first played 3-on-2 before settling on 2 vs 2, and it was shown as an exhibition at the 1990 FIFA World Cup in Italy (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The goals are small — 110 × 10 cm — and the ball weighs 200 g with a 14 cm diameter (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Jorkyball International Federation (JIF) was founded in 2014 with its headquarters in Rome and Gilles Paniez as president, and by March 2025 thirteen nations were affiliated; the JIF World Cup was held in Canada in 2018, Japan in 2021 and Poland in 2023, the first JIF World Female Cup took place in 2015, and the European Championship 2021 was held in France (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.). Special world rules apply 7–11 July 2026 at worldjorkyball.com (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 2026 the leading women are the players of the French and Hungarian women's national teams — last European champions — while the men's world title holders from the 2023 World Cup in Poland lead the men's game (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.).

## Rules

A jorkyball pitch is a cage roughly 9.80 × 4.80 m with a height of 2.70 m; the walls and ceiling are in play, so the ball may be played off them like in squash, and only the goals — 110 × 10 cm openings at each end — are out of play (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.). Teams are 2 vs 2, and each player has a fixed role per set: a striker and a defender; the roles swap every set (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The striker may not enter the kickoff areas (the zones in front of each goal) and takes the kickoffs; the defender may not cross into the opponent's half (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A match is best of three sets, each set to 7 goals; if the score is tied, a golden goal decides (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ball (200 g, 14 cm) is restarted by kickoff from the kickoff area after a goal, and by throw-in from the wall when it leaves play (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The goalkeeper role does not exist as a specialist position — both players defend their goal — and contact is limited: tackling is forbidden, but shoulder-to-shoulder challenges are allowed (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.). Men's and women's competitions use the identical pitch, ball and rules; women's World Cup and European Championship have been held since 2015 and 2021 respectively (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.).

## History

Jorkyball was invented in 1987 by Gilles Paniez in the garage of his home in Lyon, where he and friends played a 3-on-2 wall game that he refined into 2 vs 2; it was presented as an exhibition sport at the 1990 FIFA World Cup in Italy, which gave it international exposure (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Clubs multiplied in France, Hungary, Poland and Switzerland through the 1990s and 2000s, and the Jorkyball International Federation was founded in 2014 with its seat in Rome, Gilles Paniez as president (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.). The JIF World Cup has been held in Canada (2018), Japan (2021) and Poland (2023), the first World Female Cup in 2015, and the European Championship in France in 2021 (Jorkyball International Federation, n.d.). Among the icons is the inventor Gilles Paniez himself, still the federation president in 2026, and the French international Sébastien Picard, a leading player of the 2020s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
