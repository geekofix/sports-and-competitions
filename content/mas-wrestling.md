## Basic Information

Mas-wrestling (Yakut: mas tard'yhyy) is the traditional stick-pulling sport of the Sakha (Yakut) people of the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) in Russia, often called a national sport of Yakutia. Two athletes sit facing each other on the floor with their feet braced against a wooden board, each gripping a wooden stick called the mas, and try to pull the stick out of the opponent's hands or lift the opponent off the board. It is governed internationally by the International Mas-Wrestling Federation (IMWF).

## Rules

A bout takes place on a flat board with a foot support; both competitors sit facing each other, hold the mas with both hands and try to seize it from the opponent's grip or to lift the opponent's seat off the ground. The athlete who loses the grip or is lifted loses the bout. Competitions are held for men and women in several weight categories and in an absolute (open weight) category, with athletes required to be at least 18 years old and to pass a medical examination. The official competition rules of the International Mas-Wrestling Federation apply at World and European Championships, which are staged in Yakutsk and other venues.

## History

Mas-wrestling has been practiced in Yakutia for centuries as part of the traditional Ysyakh summer festival and the national games of the peoples of the North, where it developed alongside other strength contests. It spread to neighboring regions of Siberia and to other countries, and the International Mas-Wrestling Federation now organizes World Championships that have been held regularly in Yakutsk since the early 2000s, with later editions also staged in Bishkek (Kyrgyzstan) and Ulaanbaatar (Mongolia). The sport is also featured at the International Sports Games of the Peoples of the North and has gained a following in Europe, North America and South America through national federations.
