## Basic Information

Ulama is the modern survival of the Aztec ballgame: a hip-ball contest played in Mexico (above all in the state of Sinaloa) in which teams strike a heavy solid rubber ball with the hip, keeping it in play on a long narrow court. Regarded as one of the oldest continuously played ball sports in the world and the oldest known game with a rubber ball, it descends directly from the Mesoamerican ballgame played for well over three thousand years.

## Rules

The game is played on a long narrow court (a tastei, tens of metres long) divided by a centre line: in the most common form, ulama de cadera (hip ulama), teams of five or more strike a roughly 3-to-4-kilogram rubber ball using only the hip or upper thigh, scoring points (rayas) when opponents fail to return the ball, let it stop before crossing the centre line, touch it with a wrong body part or commit errors such as touching a teammate; the first team to a set total wins, and scores can reset under traditional scoring quirks, making games long. Two other forms share the family: ulama de antebrazo (forearm ulama, smaller ball and courts, often played by women) and ulama de mazo (stick or paddle variant), while the modern organized U.S. and Mexican associations play standardized hip-ulama formats with two timed halves, seven players a side and hip-only contact.

## History

The Mesoamerican ballgame was played from at least the second millennium BC by Olmec, Maya and Aztec civilizations (nearly 1,500 ancient ball courts survive archaeologically), tied to ritual and sometimes sacrifice; the Spanish suppressed the game after the conquest, but it survived in Sinaloa's rural communities, where it has been passed down for centuries. Modern revival began with cultural presentation in the twentieth century (including exhibition at the Mexico City 1968 Olympic Games), documentation projects and association-building in the 2000s, and today ulama is played both in its Sinaloan heartland and by revived clubs in Mexico City, the United States (with its own Mesoamerican Ballgame Association and national team), Belize and Central America, including international tournament series of the ancestral pok-ta-pok form held since 2015.
