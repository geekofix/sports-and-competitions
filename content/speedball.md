## Basic Information

Speedball is the competitive tournament format of paintball: teams of five (classic 5-man; seven- and ten-man variants also exist) play on a symmetrical field of inflatable bunkers, scoring points by eliminating opponents and by pressing a buzzer at the opponent's start base; the premier professional circuit is the National Xball League (NXL), which since 2015 runs Xball events across the United States and Europe, with a women's professional division (WNXL) since 2021 (NXL, n.d.-a). The annual NXL World Cup in Kissimmee, Florida, draws thousands of players and hundreds of teams, and the 2026 season opens with the Tampa Bay Open on 19 March 2026 at Raymond James Stadium (NXL, n.d.-b). Women's and men's teams compete in parallel divisions under the same rules.

## Rules

In Xball speedball, two teams of five race a game clock of 12–15 minutes depending on the division, playing point after point: each point begins with a two-minute staging, and a team scores by pressing its buzzer at the opponent's start base, with elimination of all five opposing players also ending the point; the team with the most points when the clock expires wins, subject to a mercy rule in lopsided games (NXL, n.d.-a). The field is a mirrored layout of inflatable bunkers, 150 × 120 ft at NXL events, with a 2 × 1.2 m start base centred on each back boundary (NXL, n.d.-a). Markers are capped at 10.2 balls per second; from 2026 each player may carry a maximum of five pods plus one electronic loader per point, and new tiebreaker metrics decide drawn games (NXL, n.d.-b). Fouls — overshooting, hits on bunkers, playing on after being hit, illegal equipment — bring penalties up to the loss of points or players, enforced by referees who check paint (NXL, n.d.-b). Women's and men's divisions use identical fields, markers and rules; amateur events also run 10-man and classic 5-man divisions (NXL, n.d.-b).

## History

Paintball was invented in 1981 by Charles Gaines, Bob Guice and Bill Cross, who worked for the Nelson Paint Company; the first game, called "Survival", was played on 27 June 1981 on Long Island, New York (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The speedball format emerged in the late 1980s at the SC Village field in Southern California, designed to be spectator-friendly with a central open lagoon; in 1996 Brass Eagle introduced the first fully inflatable bunker field, launching the modern tournament era (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Professional leagues followed — the PSP series ran from the early 2000s to 2014, and the NXL was revived in 2015 by Tom Cole, adding the annual World Cup in Kissimmee and NXL Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
