## Basic Information

Glinski's hexagonal chess is played on a board of 97 hexagonal cells (three hexagon orientations) invented by Władysław Gliński in 1936; pieces move along the six cell directions, giving bishops six diagonals and queens twelve. Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Capablanca Chess](capablanca-chess.html), [Chess](chess.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Chessvariants.com, n.d.).

## Rules

Hexagonal board with 97 cells; pawns move forward and capture diagonally-forward; rooks along straight cell lines; bishops along the six diagonal directions; queen = rook+bishop; knights jump in the L-shape adapted to hex cells; king as in chess. Castling on the back rank (Chessvariants.com, n.d.).

## History

Władysław Gliński, a Polish draughts champion, published the variant in 1936; modern enthusiasts (e.g. the Hexagonal Chess community and 'Glinski's hexagonal chess' on chessvariants) play it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
