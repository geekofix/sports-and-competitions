## Basic Information

Enduro mountain biking is a gravity-race discipline in which riders are timed downhill through a series of linked special stages on rugged, natural terrain, with untimed liaison sections — uphill transfers or liaison trails — linking them (UCI, n.d.-b). It is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), which runs the UCI Enduro World Cup and the UCI Mountain Bike Enduro World Championships; the 2026 UCI Enduro World Cup overall titles went to Great Britain's Ella Conolly in the women's elite class — her second consecutive overall — while the 2026 UCI Enduro World Championships were held in Finale Ligure, Italy, where Australia's Ryan Gilchrist won the elite men's rainbow jersey and Germany's Raphaela Richter the elite women's title (UCI, n.d.-a; n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current riders are — in the women's elite field — 2026 world champion Raphaela Richter of Germany and two-time World Cup overall champion Ella Conolly of Great Britain, and — in the men's elite field — 2026 world champion Ryan Gilchrist of Australia and 2026 E-Enduro world champion Adrien Dailly of France (UCI, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Enduro races are run under the UCI Mountain Bike Regulations: a competition consists of a number of special stages — typically four to six for the World Cup, more for the World Championships — which are timed downhill, separated by liaison sections that are untimed but must be completed within a generous time limit, with transfers often on foot or by shuttle (UCI, n.d.-b). The rider's result is the sum of the special-stage times plus any penalties for missing a stage or exceeding the liaison limit; the fastest total time wins (UCI, n.d.-b). Riders must wear a full-face helmet, and long-sleeve jerseys, shorts and full-finger gloves are mandatory at World Cup and World Championship level; knee pads and back protectors are required, and bikes must pass a technical check (UCI, n.d.-b). Classes include elite men and women, junior men and women (under-19), and E-Enduro with power-assisted bikes limited to 25 km/h assistance (UCI, n.d.-b). Women's and men's elite fields race the same stages with identical rules, and the rainbow jersey goes to the world champion for the following season (UCI, n.d.-b).

## History

Enduro mountain biking was born in the late 2000s at Finale Ligure on the Italian Riviera, which had hosted downhill racing every year since 2008 and became the birthplace of the enduro format as it is known today (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Enduro World Series (EWS), launched in 2013, made the format a global championship, and the UCI adopted enduro in 2023, folding the series into the UCI Enduro World Cup and creating the first UCI Enduro World Championships (UCI, n.d.-b). Icons of the format include the French enduro pioneer Nicolas Vouilloz, a ten-time world champion across downhill and enduro, and on the women's side the British rider Tracy Moseley, a six-time downhill world champion who also won enduro titles; Finale Ligure remains the spiritual home of the discipline and hosted the first UCI Enduro World Championships in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
