## Basic Information

Enduro mountain biking is a timed gravity discipline in which riders race down a series of predominantly downhill "stages" linked by untimed transfer stages; only the timed stages count toward the result. It is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) as part of the UCI Mountain Bike World Series, with the Enduro World Championship as its premier event.

## Rules

At the UCI Enduro World Championship and Enduro World Cup events, the course consists of 4-6 timed stages (typically 1-3 minutes each) connected by liaison/transfer sections that are ridden at a set pace within a time allowance. The total of all stage times determines the ranking; penalties are added for missing time windows, leaving the course or missing a stage. Riders must wear a full-face helmet, and at elite level also knee pads, elbow pads and a back protector; a chest protector is required at some events. Bikes must be mountain bikes (no motor), and riders may use one bike per event. Categories include elite men and women, under-23, juniors and age-groups, with seeded start orders based on qualifying or results. Punctures or mechanicals in stages count against the clock — there is no neutral service mid-stage at World Championships.

## History

Enduro MTB emerged in the early 2000s in France and the UK (the "Megavalanche" in France, 1995, is an early mass-start enduro-style race) as a format between downhill racing and cross-country, emphasizing all-mountain riding. The Enduro World Series (EWS) launched in 2013 and popularized the timed-stage format globally, with Greg Minnaar, Richie Rude and Cecile Ravanel among its champions. In 2021, the UCI integrated enduro into the UCI Mountain Bike World Series with an official Enduro World Cup and World Championship (first held in Val di Sole, Italy, 2021). The sport appeals to enduro motorcycle and gravity-bike fans, with events at famous bike parks such as Finale Ligure, Whistler and Crans-Montana.
