## Basic Information

Alquerque (also qirkat) is the 5x5 ancestor of draughts from Moorish Spain (11th century): pieces move one step diagonally forward or sideways; one step in any of the— diagonal directions, and capture by jumping over an enemy piece to an empty square; multiple jumps allowed. It is depicted playing in Alfonso X's 1283 Libro de los juegos. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Draughts](draughts.html), [Konane](konane.html), [Latrunculi](latrunculi.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Alfonso X of Castile, 1283).

## Rules

5x5 board with 12 pieces per side in the starting array; moves one cell along the grid lines/diagonals as drawn; captures by custodial jump (over adjacent enemy into empty). Modern 'alquerque' rules (diagonal moves on the points) are standardized by the Alquerque group (e.g. Merkel's notation board) (Alfonso X of Castile, 1283).

## History

Alquerque came to Spain with the Moors (11th c.) and is shown in the 1283 Libro de los juegos (Alfonso X); it is the direct ancestor of modern draughts/checkers (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
