## Basic Information

Footbag net is a court game played one-on-one or two-on-two over a 5-foot net: players use basic kicks, serves, spikes and digs to keep a footbag aloft and score points while serving (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). The court is 20 feet wide and 44 feet long, divided in half by the net into four equal serving quadrants, the same dimensions for singles and doubles (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). In singles play, two kicks are allowed per side (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). The International Footbag Players’ Association (IFPA), a U.S. nonprofit, publishes the Official Rules of Footbag Sports and runs the world championships (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

Net footbag is closely related to sepak takraw — in Poland, for example, national sepak takraw and footbag-net athletes such as Michał Klimczak compete in both — and it is played worldwide wherever footbag culture is strong, from the United States to Southeast Asia (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men’s, women’s and mixed singles and doubles events are offered at the Footbag World Championships (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the IFPA Official Rules, the net footbag court is 20 ft wide by 44 ft long with a 5 ft net and four equal serving quadrants, for both singles and doubles (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). Each player or team attempts to score while serving; in singles, two kicks are allowed per side (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). The tournament director selects one of three official scoring systems for each event — rally scoring, side-out scoring, or game-set-match (games to four points, six games to a set, best of three sets, like tennis) — and different events may use different systems (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). Faults such as touching the net or exceeding the kick limit lose the point (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.). Open to all genders; mixed doubles are standard at many events (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.).

## History

Footbag itself grew out of the 1970s hacky-sack fad in the United States and was organized by the International Footbag Players’ Association, which codified freestyle, net and games disciplines and has published official rules since the 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Net footbag emerged as the competitive court version of the game, with world championships bringing together players from the footbag and sepak takraw communities in the U.S., Europe and Asia (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Today the Footbag World Championships include net singles and doubles alongside freestyle events (International Footbag Players’ Association, n.d.).
