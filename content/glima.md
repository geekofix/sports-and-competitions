## Basic Information

Glima is Iceland's national wrestling sport, a standing upright grappling style in which two wrestlers grip each other above the waist and try to throw or force the opponent to the ground using strength, balance and technique — without going to the ground themselves. It is governed in Iceland by the Icelandic Glima Association (Íslenska glímufélagið) at glima.is, which organizes the annual Icelandic championships and international glima events.

## Rules

In competition glima, wrestlers start standing, gripping each other's belt or upper body above the waist; the aim is to throw the opponent so that any body part above the legs touches the ground first, or to force a fall. Holds below the belt, leg grabs and going to the ground oneself are not permitted in the classic rules; the match is decided by the first fall, with rounds and best-of formats at championships. Three main styles are recognized: "hose-glima" (belt grip), "sleeve-glima" and "back-glima." Matches are short, explosive bouts, and the referee awards the fall. The Icelandic championship (Íslandsmót í glímu) is held annually, and glima is also demonstrated at Nordic and Viking festivals.

## History

Glima is one of the oldest documented sports in Iceland, described in the medieval sagas (e.g., Grettis saga, where Grettir Ásmundarson wrestles) and practiced by Vikings; the word appears in Old Norse literature from the 12th century. It was the sport of the Icelandic Commonwealth (930-1262) and remained central to Icelandic culture, with competitions at the Alþingi. After declining in the 19th century, glima was revived in the early 20th century with the founding of the modern glima association. Today it is Iceland's national sport (þjóðaríþrótt), practiced in schools and clubs, with the annual national championship and growing international interest, including the World Glíma Championship organized since the 2010s.
