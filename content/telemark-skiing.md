## Basic Information

Telemark skiing is downhill skiing on free-heel equipment — the heel is not fixed to the ski — and is defined by the telemark turn, a lunging turn in which one knee drops while the skis stay parallel; it is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), which runs the FIS Telemark World Cup (since the 1995 season) and biennial World Championships (FIS, n.d.-b, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport has never been part of the Olympic programme.

The three individual disciplines are the classic (a long course combining a giant-slalom section, a skating section, jumps and telemark turns), the sprint (a shorter course with one jump and one telemark section) and the parallel sprint (head-to-head knockout racing), plus a team parallel sprint; at the 2025 FIS Telemark World Championships in Les Contamines, France (19–23 March 2025), Norway's Trym Nygaard Løken swept all three men's events and Germany's Johanna Holzmann all three women's events, with Norway winning the team parallel sprint (FIS, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the 2025–26 World Cup leaders, the women lead: Norway's Gøril Strøm Eriksen heads the women's overall, sprint and parallel sprint standings, with Switzerland's Nicolas Michel leading the men's (FIS, n.d.-d).

## Rules

Under FIS telemark regulations, the classic is raced on a course with a giant-slalom section, a cross-country (skating) section, jumps and a telemark section, and the result combines the racing time with style points awarded by judges for the telemark turns, jumps and skating section; the sprint uses a shorter course with a single jump and a single telemark section; the parallel sprint is a head-to-head dual on two identical courses decided by time (FIS, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Equipment is free-heel: telemark bindings (75 mm or NTN) allow the heel to lift, with flexible boots; skis follow FIS length rules and a helmet is mandatory; men and women compete in separate events, with senior and junior categories at the World Championships and World Cup (FIS, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The telemark turn is named after the Telemark region of Norway, where Sondre Norheim (1825–1897) of Morgedal pioneered the technique in the 1860s and won the first known competitions there in the late 1860s; Norwegian immigrants carried the style to North America in the 19th century, and the first modern telemark races were held in Norway in the early 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The statue of Sondre Norheim in Morgedal, pictured, commemorates the father of modern telemark skiing (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Telemark appeared as a demonstration discipline at the Winter Olympics before the FIS organized the World Cup from 1995 and biennial World Championships; Swiss and Norwegian riders dominated the early decades — Amélie Wenger-Reymond and Bastien Dayer swept the 2021 world titles in Melchsee-Frutt (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
