## Basic Information

Palín is the traditional stick and ball game of the Mapuche people of Chile, a cousin of hurling and shinty: two teams on a long, narrow field (the paliwe) strike a small leather ball with curved wooden sticks, aiming to drive it over the opponent's goal line. It is one of the oldest documented team games of the Americas and is recognized in Chile as living traditional sport heritage.

The game is played by community clubs across south-central Chile, with regional championships and men's and women's teams keeping the tradition alive as living Mapuche culture.

## Rules

Matches are played on a long, narrow field, shortened for modern organized play, between two teams, commonly six a side in today's club form. Players strike the small ball with curved wooden sticks, advancing it toward the opposite goal line; physical challenges are regulated, matches run in two halves to a set score of goals or a time limit, and referees enforce community league rules in a spirit of sportsmanship that the Mapuche tradition treats as part of the game itself.

## History

Palín was played by the Mapuche long before the colonial era, and Spanish chroniclers described the spectacle of long matches played between communities. The game survived prohibitions during the colonial and republican periods because communities kept playing it quietly, and the twentieth century brought formal organization into clubs and federations with regional championships. Chile recognizes palín as part of its traditional sport heritage, and the game is taught in communities of La Araucanía and beyond as living Mapuche culture.
