## Basic Information

Speed flying is paragliding with a small, fast wing flown close to the terrain: the pilot launches on foot with skis or on foot down a slope and swoops just above the ground at speeds that can exceed 100 km/h, combining paragliding and skiing (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is an FAI air sport under CIVL (hang gliding & paragliding) and belongs to the air-sports family.

*Figure: Tsungam. (2021-02-14 14:33:06). Speed flyer swooping above a snowy slope [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Detmold_-_2021-02-14_-_Linnenkamp_%28DSC03594%29.jpg*

Competitions are judged on speed runs down a marked course and on precision/slalom tasks; the FAI ratifies speed-flying records and organizes world championships in the discipline (FAI, n.d.). Wings are typically 9–14 m², much smaller than a paraglider, with high trim speed (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Safety rules require a paragliding licence, helmet, reserve parachute and training on the small wing before flying low; speed flying is considered an advanced, high-risk discipline and is forbidden in some areas (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same events, with separate record categories.

## History

Speed flying emerged in the early 2000s in the French Alps when paragliders began downsizing wings and flying close to slopes; the term and the discipline were formalized under the FAI CIVL in the 2010s, with the first world championships (speed flying) held alongside paragliding events (Wikipedia, n.d.). Pioneers include French pilots who flew the steep couloirs of the Haute-Savoie; today the discipline is strongest in the Alps, the Pyrenees and the US West.
