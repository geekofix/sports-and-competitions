## Basic Information

Demolition derby is a full-contact motorsport in which five or more drivers deliberately ram their cars into each other on a dirt or paved arena; the last driver whose vehicle is still operational wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Events are staged mainly at county and state fairs in the United States and at dedicated banger-racing tracks in the UK and Europe, and derbies originated in the United States in the 1940s–1950s, with Australia's first event in January 1963 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

A typical fair programme runs in stages: cars are split into heats of eight to twelve, heat winners advance, early knockouts get a consolation round, and the survivors meet in the feature event for the top payout (CountyFairsUSA, n.d.). Alongside the main derby many shows add rollover competitions, where drivers take runs at a ramp to roll their car as many times as possible (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Women compete on equal footing — many fairs run classes open to all drivers aged 16 and over, with a notarized parental permit for 16- and 17-year-olds, and women's-only or mixed feature events are standard at larger shows (Shaner Promotions, n.d.). The headline US show is the Demolition Derby at the Illinois State Fair in Springfield, whose 2026 edition ran on 22 August 2026 in the Multi-Purpose Arena (Illinois State Fair, n.d.).

## Rules

Rules vary by promoter, but the core format is uniform: every car is stripped of glass and loose interior parts, the driver's door is painted white with black numbers, blaze orange, or another high-contrast scheme so officials can identify it, and deliberately hitting the driver's-side door is forbidden (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Drivers sign liability waivers, wear helmets and (often) neck braces, and cars are tech-inspected before the event.

A car is out when it cannot move under its own power — typically after a time limit set by the promoter (often 2–3 minutes without a hit). Scoring in points-based formats awards points for hits, with the winner also taking the last-car-running prize. In the UK "banger" variant, the Unlimited National Banger World Final at Foxhall Stadium, Ipswich, crowns the world champion of oval dirt-track demolition racing (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Safety rules have tightened over the decades: mandatory roll cages in most classes, fuel-cell or relocated battery rules, and immediate red-flag stoppage when a driver is hurt. Rollover competitions use compact hatchbacks, because their low weight rolls most easily (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Demolition derbies grew out of the 1940s US stock-car scene and became grandstand staples at American county fairs through the 1960s; Australia's first derby was held in January 1963, and in the UK derbies (often called "destruction derbies") traditionally close a day of banger racing (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The sport also became a video-game genre, from the 1984 Bally/Midway arcade game Demolition Derby through FlatOut, Wreckfest, BeamNG.drive and the vehicular-combat games Carmageddon and Twisted Metal (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The UK banger-racing world final is a British institution: the Unlimited National Banger World Final at Foxhall Stadium near Ipswich draws the country's top drivers each autumn, and the 2019 edition is a fixture of the national banger calendar (Mattbull1, 2019; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
