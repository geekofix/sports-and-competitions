## Basic Information

Ice stock sport (Eisstockschießen, often nicknamed "Bavarian curling") is a precision team sport played on a long lane of ice. Teams of four players slide heavy ice stocks — short sticks with a handle — down the lane toward a small rubber target disc called the Daube at the far end, trying to leave their stocks as close to it as possible or to knock the opponents' stocks away. The sport is governed internationally by the International Federation of Ice Stock Sport (IFI), founded in 1950, and is played above all in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and South Tyrol in Italy, with a growing international following.

## Rules

The game is played on an ice lane of about 30 m. Each team fields four players, and in each end every player slides two stocks (eight per team), alternating with the opposing team. A stock may be aimed directly at the Daube or used to hit opposing stocks out of the scoring area. After all stocks of an end are played, the team whose stock lies closest to the Daube scores one point for every stock nearer to the Daube than the opponent's best stock, up to a maximum of eight points per end. The leading team places the Daube at the start of the next end. Besides the team competition, there are two further disciplines: target shooting (Zielwettbewerb), in which stocks must be stopped in concentric rings worth 2, 4, 6, 8 or 10 points, and distance shooting (Weitschießen), in which the longest valid shot wins.

## History

Ice stock sport has been played for centuries in the Alpine regions of Bavaria, Austria, South Tyrol and Switzerland, originally on frozen ponds, streets and village greens, where farmers and craftsmen competed in winter. The rules were codified in the early 20th century, and the International Federation of Ice Stock Sport was founded in 1950 to organize international competition, including biennial World Championships. The sport was a demonstration event at the Winter Olympic Games in Garmisch-Partenkirchen 1936 and Innsbruck 1964. Today it is played competitively on prepared ice rinks as well as at traditional village tournaments.
