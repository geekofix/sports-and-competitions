## Basic Information

Foot orienteering is the classic running discipline of orienteering, in which competitors navigate with map and compass through terrain (forest, park, urban) to visit a series of control points in the fastest time. It is governed by the International Orienteering Federation (IOF), which runs the World Orienteering Championships (WOC) with sprint, middle, long and relay distances, and the World Cup.

## Rules

At IOF World Championship events, competitors receive a map only at the start (except in relay, where the next runner gets the map at the exchange). Controls are marked with orange-and-white flags and an electronic punch (SI card); the competitor must visit all controls in the correct order and then cross the finish line. The fastest total time wins; ties are not broken (shared medals possible). Distances: sprint about 3 km with 15-20 controls (run in parks/urban, ~15 min), middle about 5-6 km (~40 min), long about 12-15 km (~90 min), and relays (3-4 legs). Mass start is used for sprint and knockout sprint finals; individual start with intervals for middle/long. Mis-punching or skipping a control means disqualification. Map reading, route choice and running speed all count.

## History

Orienteering was developed in Norway in the late 19th century, with the first recorded competition in 1897 near Oslo, and spread to Sweden, where national organization began in the early 1900s. The IOF was founded in 1961 in Copenhagen by ten nations. The World Orienteering Championships have been held annually since 1966, with the first edition in Fiskars, Finland. Sweden, Norway, Finland and Switzerland dominated for decades, and orienteering is a school sport across Scandinavia. The sprint discipline was added to the WOC program in 2001 in Lausanne. Among the sport's legends are France's Thierry Gueorgiou (eight world titles) and Sweden's Tove Alexandersson.
