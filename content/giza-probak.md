## Basic Information

Giza probak (human test) is the Basque human stone-dragging contest: a team of pullers harnessed by ropes drags a heavy stone sledge (up to about 750 kg in classic town-square events, more in modern championships) along a measured course against the clock. It is the human-powered counterpart of idi probak and zaldi probak. Within the Basque rural sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Idi Probak](idi-probak.html), [Zaldi Probak](zaldi-probak.html), [Harri Jasotzaile](harri-jasotzaile.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

## Rules

The stone sledge (narria) is weighed in advance; pullers wear a harness across chest and shoulders and may grip a rope. The team must move the stone the full marked distance within the time limit; the fastest team wins. Modern rules add medical checks and safety barriers; women's and mixed teams compete in their own categories at many festivals (Euskal Herri Kirolak Elkartea, n.d.).

## History

Human stone dragging is documented at Basque fêtes from the 19th century — the 1990 photograph of a stone drag contest at Machete Square in Vitoria-Gasteiz shows the urban tradition. The 'dema harria' (challenge stone) of Santiagomendi is a famous example. The sport is governed within the Herri Kirolak circuit (Wikipedia, n.d.).
