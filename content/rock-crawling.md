## Basic Information

Rock crawling is the off-road discipline of driving purpose-built 4x4s over extreme rock terrain — boulders, ledges and slickrock — at walking pace, judged on clean lines and technical skill (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the off-road family with Ultra4 and mud bogging.

![4x4 climbing at Moon Rocks OHV Area](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Moon_Rocks_OHV_Area_%2854457022863%29.jpg?width=640)

*Figure: BLM Nevada. (Taken on 1 November 2023, 17:10:42). 4x4 climbing at Moon Rocks OHV Area [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Moon_Rocks_OHV_Area_%2854457022863%29.jpg*

Competitions (e.g. the King of the Hammers rock-crawling classes, USRCC events, European RFC-style trials) run on courses with gates; a vehicle is penalized for touching a gate, using a winch in some classes, or failing to complete within the time limit (Wikipedia, n.d.). Vehicles are stripped, tube-framed crawlers with portal axles, big tyres (40+ inches), locking differentials and winches (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Safety rules require roll cages, five-point harnesses, helmets, fire extinguishers and a drivable-out requirement; spotters guide the driver over obstacles (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same classes — women have won national crawling titles.

## History

Rock crawling grew out of 1940s–50s California "Jeep trials" and the Sierra/Tierra del Sol desert club scene; the Rubicon Trail and Moab Easter Jeep Safari made it famous in the 1980s–90s, and competitive crawling with gates and classes formalized in the 2000s with events like the King of the Hammers (Wikipedia, n.d.).
