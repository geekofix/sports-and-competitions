## Basic Information

Bocce raffa is the fast, attacking specialty of Italian bocce: after a run-up of two to four steps, the player hurls the ball so it rolls at high speed and knocks away the opponent's ball near the pallino, or moves the pallino itself. It is governed by the Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale (CBI) and is the dominant discipline at the World Bocce Championships and Italian championships.

## Rules

Under CBI regulations, a raffa throw must be delivered from behind the foul line after a run of at most four steps; the ball may be thrown to hit the opponent's ball, the pallino, or another ball, and play continues with the positions as they lie after the throw (measured balls may be replaced only per the rules). In international competition, each match is played over a set number of ends (usually to 12-15 points), with one point per ball closer to the pallino than the opponent's best. World championships are contested in singles, doubles, triples and team events for men and women.

## History

Raffa developed in 19th-century Italy as a more aggressive alternative to punto, and became the premier competitive specialty of Italian bocce. The CBI, founded in 1983 in Chiasso, organized the first unified world championships of raffa and volo in 2009 in Bevagna, Italy. International raffa has spread through the World Pétanque & Bowls Federation to over 100 countries, with strong scenes in South America, North Africa and Asia.
