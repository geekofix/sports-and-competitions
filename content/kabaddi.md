## Basic Information

Kabaddi is a contact team sport from South Asia played between two teams of seven on a rectangular court divided by a midline. A "raider" crosses into the opponents' half chanting "kabaddi, kabaddi" on a single breath, tries to tag as many defenders as possible and return to his own half; the defenders try to tackle him before he gets back. It is governed internationally by the International Kabaddi Federation, in India by the Amateur Kabaddi Federation of India (AKFI), and its professional showcase is the Pro Kabaddi League, watched by hundreds of millions.

## Rules

The court measures 13 m x 10 m for men (smaller for women and juniors), with a midline, a baulk line 3.75 m from the midline and a bonus line 1 m in front of the baulk line, plus lobbies. Each team fields seven players, with up to five substitutes; a match consists of two 20-minute halves. Teams alternate raids: the raider must cross the baulk line, touch defenders and return to his own half while continuing the "kabaddi" chant without inhaling — if he breathes in or is tackled in the opponents' half, he is out and the defense scores a point. Each successful tag scores a point, crossing the bonus line after a touch earns an extra bonus point, and tagging all seven defenders in one raid (an "all out") earns bonus points and revives the raiding team's players. The team with the most points after 40 minutes wins.

## History

Kabaddi is ancient: the Mahabharata describes Abhimanyu trapped in the chakravyuha formation, an early echo of the raid-and-defend principle, and the game was played across South Asia for millennia. Modern standardized rules were compiled by the All India Kabaddi Federation, and the Amateur Kabaddi Federation of India was founded in 1973. Kabaddi was a demonstration sport at the first Asian Games in 1951 and again in 1982, and has been a medal event since the 1990 Beijing Asian Games; India won every Asian Games kabaddi gold from 1990 to 2014 until South Korea's upset in 2018. The Pro Kabaddi League, launched in 2014, professionalized the sport and made stars of raiders such as Anup Kumar, the "Bonus ka Badshah". The Kabaddi World Cup has been held since 1990.
