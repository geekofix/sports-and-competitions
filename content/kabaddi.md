## Basic Information

Kabaddi is a contact team sport from the Indian subcontinent in which a raider enters the opposing half, tags as many defenders as possible and returns to his own half while chanting "kabaddi" on a single breath (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Seven players per side take the court — from a registered squad of twelve with up to five substitutes — on a 13 × 10 m court for men and an 11 × 8 m court for women and juniors, divided by a mid-line with a baulk line 3.75 m from the mid-line, a bonus line 1 m in front of the baulk line and 1 m lobbies along the sides (Asian Kabaddi Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match lasts two 20-minute halves with a 5-minute break, a raid is limited to 30 seconds, and the raider must keep chanting "kabaddi" on one breath (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each defender tagged scores 1 point; crossing the bonus line with at least six defenders on the court scores 1 bonus point; a "super tackle" when three or fewer defenders remain scores 2 points; an all-out (lona) scores 2 bonus points and revives all tagged defenders; after two consecutive empty raids the third is a do-or-die "golden raid" worth 1 point (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Kabaddi Federation (IKF) was founded in 2004 and held the first Kabaddi World Cup in Mumbai in 2004 (also 2007 and 2016); kabaddi has been an Asian Games medal sport since 1990 for both men and women, and the Asian Games 2026 in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, ran 21–26 September 2026 (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the top of the women's game, India won the 2026 Asian Games gold medal over Iran and remain the defending champions in both the men's and women's events, with the Pro Kabaddi League's lobby rule — harmonized with IKF/AKF rules from Season 9 in 2022 — governing the elite professional game in India under the Amateur Kabaddi Federation of India (AKFI) (Asian Kabaddi Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the IKF standard-style rules, the court is 13 × 10 m for men and 11 × 8 m for women and juniors, with a mid-line, a baulk line 3.75 m from the mid-line on each side, a bonus line 1 m in front of each baulk line and 1 m lobbies on both sides (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Seven players per side start the match; substitutes (up to five, from a 12-player roster) enter during the match and tagged defenders leave the court until revived (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The raider crosses the mid-line into the opponents' half, tags defenders with hand or foot and must return across the mid-line before his breath gives out — the continuous "kabaddi" chant is monitored by officials; failure to chant, crossing back without tagging, or a 30-second raid limit results in the raider being put out (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.). Each tagged defender scores 1 point for the raiding team; the bonus line adds 1 point if the raider crosses it while at least six defenders are still on the court; if three or fewer defenders remain, the tackle is a super tackle worth 2 points; tagging all seven defenders (all-out) scores 2 bonus points and revives the whole defending team (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Two consecutive empty raids make the third a do-or-die raid: failure scores a point for the defense (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.). Knockout-stage ties are decided by sudden-death raids with five players per side (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men's and women's kabaddi use the same rules and scoring; only the court dimensions differ (11 × 8 m for women), and the Pro Kabaddi League in India applies the harmonized lobby rule since Season 9 (2022) (Asian Kabaddi Federation, n.d.). Variants include circle-style kabaddi (played in a circle, popular in Punjab), beach kabaddi and indoor kabaddi (Asian Kabaddi Federation, n.d.).

## History

Kabaddi's roots reach back to ancient India — the Mahabharata describes the warrior Abhimanyu trapped in the chakravyuha formation, a situation likened to the modern raid — and the game was codified in the early 20th century in the western Indian state of Maharashtra, spreading to Punjab, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It was demonstrated at the 1936 Berlin Olympics and has been on the Asian Games program since 1990, with women's kabaddi medalled at the Games since the 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Kabaddi Federation was founded in 2004 and staged the first Kabaddi World Cup in Mumbai that year, with further editions in 2007 and 2016, and a World Kabaddi event was held in the West Midlands, England, in March 2025 (International Kabaddi Federation, n.d.). The Pro Kabaddi League, launched in India in 2014, professionalized the sport and its lobby rule was harmonized with IKF/AKF rules for the Asian Games 2026 (Asian Kabaddi Federation, n.d.). India has been the dominant nation — defending Asian Games champion in both the men's and women's events, with the women's team winning gold over Iran at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya — and among the icons is Pawan Sehrawat, the "Hi-Flyer," one of the greatest raiders of the Pro Kabaddi League era (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
