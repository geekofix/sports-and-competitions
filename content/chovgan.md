## Basic Information

Chovgan (also spelled *chovqan*, *chovken*, *chovgān* — Persian for "polo mallet game") is the ancient Persian ball game played on horseback with a long-handled mallet, widely regarded as the direct predecessor of modern polo (Wikipedia, n.d.). Two teams ride out on a rectangular field and strike a wooden ball through the opponent's goal; in the modern Azerbaijani game each side fields six riders — four attackers and two defenders — split into two halves of 15 minutes each, called *azertag*, while the traditional UNESCO-recognized form uses five riders, two fullbacks and three forwards (International Chovgan Federation [ICFED], n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The game is played on the Karabakh horse, Azerbaijan's national horse breed, and in 2013 UNESCO inscribed chovgan on the List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding for Azerbaijan (UNESCO, n.d.). The international governing body, the International Chovgan Federation (ICFED), was established on 2 February 2024 and organized the first IGF World Chovgan Championships in November 2023 in Azerbaijan — won by the hosts over Morocco — the first IGF European Championship from 3 to 7 June 2025, and the II World Chovgan Championship from 7 to 13 June 2026 at the Bina Equestrian Center in Baku, where Azerbaijan again crowned itself world champion, ten teams from five continents taking part and the hosts defeating Uzbekistan 4–2 in the final, with Kuwait as special invitee (ICFED, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). Chovgan is open to women as well — women's teams play in Azerbaijan's domestic scene — and a Nowruz celebration featuring chovgan in Isfahan is pictured below.

## Rules

Under the ICFED rules, chovgan is played on a grass field about 100 m long with goals at each end, by two teams of six riders (four attackers, two defenders) in the modern game; each match consists of two 15-minute halves (*azertag*) with a short break, and the team scoring more goals wins (ICFED, n.d.). Players strike a hard wooden ball — about 6–8 cm in diameter — with a long mallet (the *chovgan*) and may use the horse's body to shield it; dangerous play such as swinging the mallet at a rider or horse is a foul punishable by free hits, and matches are refereed on horseback by a chief referee and assistants (ICFED, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The traditional Azerbaijani form recognized by UNESCO uses five riders per side — two fullbacks and three forwards — and retains the older scoring and substitution customs (UNESCO, n.d.). There are no separate women's rules: men and women compete under identical rules, with women's teams in Azerbaijan's league and growing women's participation internationally; age categories follow the junior/senior split common in equestrian sport (ICFED, 2026). The official rule source is the ICFED, the world governing body founded 2 February 2024 (icfed.org) (ICFED, n.d.).

## History

Chovgan is one of the oldest known ball games, depicted on Persian miniatures and reliefs from at least the 6th century AD and described by poets such as Nizami and Ferdowsi; it was the favorite sport of the Persian nobility and of Shah Ismail I of the Safavid dynasty in the 16th century (Wikipedia, n.d.). A 15th-century Persian miniature from the *Divan of Hafez* (1470) showing a game of chovgan is pictured below. Through Persian and Central Asian influence the game spread westward and became the direct ancestor of modern polo, which the British codified in India in the 19th century (Wikipedia, n.d.). In Azerbaijan the game survived as a village tradition, was revived in the 20th century, and was inscribed by UNESCO in 2013 on the List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding — largely because the number of master players and Karabakh horses had dwindled (UNESCO, n.d.). That inscription triggered a revival: Azerbaijan hosted the first international tournaments, and the International Chovgan Federation was founded on 2 February 2024 to govern the sport globally, holding its first world championship in Baku in November 2023 and the second in June 2026 (ICFED, 2026).
