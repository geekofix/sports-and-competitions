## Basic Information

3-gun — more generally multigun — is a practical shooting discipline in which each stage requires the competitor to engage targets with a combination of handgun, rifle and shotgun, moving through the course as fast as accuracy allows (Wikipedia, n.d.). It sits in the action-shooting family together with IPSC/USPSA practical shooting, Steel Challenge and cowboy action shooting, and is sanctioned in the United States mainly by USPSA (the US region of the International Practical Shooting Confederation, IPSC) and by 3-Gun Nation (3GN) (Wikipedia, n.d.).

The USPSA Multigun Championship — yearly since 1990, first held at PASA in Barry, Illinois — is the premier national event, and 3-Gun Nation runs a professional points series culminating in a year-end shoot-off (Wikipedia, n.d.). Divisions reflect equipment levels: Open/Unlimited (optics, compensators, bipods), Tactical/Practical (one rifle optic, iron sights on pistol and shotgun), Limited/Factory (one non-magnifying optic), Heavy (major power-factor calibres such as .45 ACP and .308 Win), plus PCC and rimfire classes (Wikipedia, n.d.). Scoring is hit-factor — points divided by time — with penalties for misses, and matches are freestyle: the competitor solves each stage as they see fit (USPSA, n.d.).

## Rules

Each match consists of several stages with cardboard IPSC/USPSA targets, steel plates and shotgun clay birds; rifles and shotguns use minimum power factors, and the heavy division requires major calibres (Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches are run under the USPSA Rifle, Shotgun and Multigun Rules (current edition 2026) or the IPSC rulebooks internationally; 2-gun and 4-gun variants exist (USPSA, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same divisions, with separate Lady categories in some rulesets; age categories include juniors (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The first 3-gun matches were the Soldier of Fortune games in Missouri starting in 1979, unsanctioned but hugely influential (Wikipedia, n.d.). IPSC — founded in 1976 in Columbia, Missouri — later formalized multigun as separate rifle, shotgun and pistol component matches, while USPSA (incorporated 1984) created its own transition-based multigun (Wikipedia, n.d.). Daniel Horner, a 10-time USPSA Multigun champion in the Tactical division (2007–2018), and Tim Yackley, the youngest NRA World Shooting Champion (2018), are among the best-known multigun shooters (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2018 USPSA Multigun Championship in Boulder City, Nevada, remains the reference event in the sport’s photography.
