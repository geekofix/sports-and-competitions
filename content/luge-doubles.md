## Basic Information

Luge doubles is the two-athlete luge event: two riders share one longer, heavier sled down the ice track — the front athlete steers with the feet and calves, the rear with the shoulders — reaching speeds above 140 km/h; it is governed by the International Luge Federation (FIL), founded in 1957, and the men's doubles has been an Olympic event since Innsbruck 1964 (International Luge Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). From Milano Cortina 2026 the doubles is also a women's event — the first women's doubles Olympic champions were crowned at the Cortina Sliding Centre on 11 February 2026, 62 years after the men's event debuted, replacing the former mixed “open” doubles (International Luge Federation, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the 2026 Olympic champions, the women lead: Italy's Andrea Voetter and Marion Oberhofer won the first women's doubles gold on the home track, beating Germany's Dajana Eitberger and Magdalena Matschina by just 0.022 seconds, with Austria's Selina Egle and Lara Kipp third; in the men's event Italy's Emanuel Rieder and Simon Kainzwaldner took gold after a superb second run — Italy's first luge Olympic gold since Torino 2006 (International Luge Federation, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under FIL rules the Olympic doubles contest is decided by a single run, timed electronically from the release of the start handles to the finish light to the thousandth of a second; athletes start seated, rock on two handles to build momentum and then propel with spiked gloves on the ice (International Luge Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Doubles contests are single runs at all major championships, while singles races use two heats at the World Cup and four at the Olympics (International Luge Federation, n.d.-a).

The doubles sled is longer and heavier than the singles sled and there are combined weight limits for sled plus athletes; false starts, weight violations and equipment irregularities bring penalties or disqualification, and the track must be FIL-homologated (1,000–1,400 m of refrigerated ice with banked curves) (International Luge Federation, n.d.-a). Men's and women's doubles use identical sleds, tracks and rules since the women's event was added for 2026 (International Luge Federation, n.d.-b).

## History

Competitive luge originated in Swiss health resorts in the 19th century and the FIL was founded in 1957; the doubles has been on the Olympic programme since Innsbruck 1964, and the team relay — one women's singles, one men's singles and one doubles run per nation — since Sochi 2014 (International Luge Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Germany's Tobias Wendl and Tobias Arlt, pictured with their trophies at the 2026 Altenberg World Cup, became the most decorated Olympic lugers of all time with seven gold and one bronze medal across five Games — their fourth doubles gold coming at Milano Cortina 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Italy's doubles tradition has long been among the strongest in the sport, culminating in the double gold at the 2026 home Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
