## Basic Information

Kancha (also goti, gauchali in Nepal) is the marble game of the Indian subcontinent: players flick glass marbles (kanche) with the thumb to hit and capture opponents' marbles from a marked circle or pit. It is played by children — boys and girls alike — across India, Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh, and in diaspora communities; it belongs to the worldwide family of marble games (marbles) with cognates such as bujal bazi in Afghanistan and the Korean five-field game. Marble-shooting contests are held at school and club level in South Asia. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [Gilli Danda](gilli-danda.html), [Gutte](gutte.html), [Pithoo](pithoo.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

A circle is drawn in the ground and each player puts one marble (kancha) in the centre; players take turns flicking their own marble from the rim of the circle with the thumb, aiming to knock other marbles out of the circle — each marble knocked out is captured — while keeping their own marble inside. If the shooter's marble leaves the circle, the turn ends. Variants include aiming at a single target marble and team play; the player with the most marbles at the end wins. Marbles are traditionally small glass spheres, but clay, stone or steel balls are also used.

## History

Marble games are ancient in South Asia — bujal bazi with sheep knucklebones is documented in Afghanistan, and kancha/goti play is described across the subcontinent for centuries. The game travelled with migrants and traders: the same flicking game is known as guchu in Korea, rela or pitjnam elsewhere in Asia, and marble games are played worldwide. In Nepal the cognate gauchali is a standard children's game, and sculptural reliefs in Punjab show village children playing marbles.
