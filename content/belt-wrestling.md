## Basic Information

Belt wrestling is one of the oldest recorded sports: two belted contestants grapple by holding each other's belt and try to throw each other to the ground, and hundreds of national styles exist worldwide, of which Alysh and Kurash of Central Asia are the most widely competed internationally (United World Wrestling [UWW], n.d.-a). Alysh is recognised by UWW (since 2008, under its predecessor FILA) as the primary international belt wrestling style and is regulated globally by the International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh (IFWA), while the International Belt Wrestling Association (IBWA) has staged world championships since 2002 — in the openweight class, with weight classes added in 2003 and freestyle (belts) and classic style (kushaks) from 2005 (International Belt Wrestling Association, n.d.). Belt wrestling is on the programme of the 2013 Summer Universiade, the Asian Games and the Asian Indoor Games, and the VI World Nomad Games (August 31–September 6, 2026, Kyrgyzstan — over 100 countries, about 3,000 athletes and 43 sports, with the kok-boru final won by Kyrgyzstan 12–0 over Kazakhstan) featured alysh, kurash and Qazaq Kuresi; the Belt Wrestling Asian Championships 2026 were held in Bishkek in March 2026, and the UWW World Belt Wrestling Championships 2026 are scheduled for December 16, 2026 in Goa, India (UWW, n.d.-b). On the women's side, athletes compete in their own weight classes — Turkmenistan's Zuhra Madraimova and Nasiba Surkiewa won world gold in 2017 — and Israel's Gregory Rudelson is among the best-known male belt wrestlers (International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh, n.d.) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-a) (United World Wrestling, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In the international styles, contestants wear a jacket, trousers and a belt (kushak) and must hold the opponent's belt or jacket at all times; the aim is to throw the opponent onto the mat, and throws are scored according to the part of the body that lands — in alysh, 1, 2 or 6 points by body part, with the first wrestler to reach 6 points winning the bout (UWW, n.d.-a). Bouts last about five minutes, with shorter times for children, women and elders, and are contested in weight classes in both classic style (holding the kushak) and freestyle (holding the belt); a fall or a throw that lands the opponent on the back can end the bout immediately. Men's and women's competitions are run separately in parallel weight classes, and youth categories use shorter bouts and adapted grips (International Belt Wrestling Association, n.d.). Kurash, the Uzbek sister style, uses a similar upright format with its own scoring, which is why the two styles split after 2005.

## History

Belt wrestling appears in the Epic of Gilgamesh (circa 2000 BC), where Gilgamesh and Enkidu grapple by the belt, and one of the oldest illustrations of wrestling is a bronze statuette from Khafaji near Baghdad, dated to about 2600 BC and now in the National Museum of Iraq; Chinese chronicles of the 11th century and the scholar Avicenna also describe belt wrestling (UWW, n.d.-a). The modern international version was started in 2001 by Bayaman Erkinbayev in Kyrgyzstan — Erkinbayev, later a member of parliament, was assassinated in 2005 — and was developed with Rif Gaynanov until 2005, when the ways separated into the styles now called Alysh and Kurash; UWW (FILA) recognised alysh in 2008, and the IBWA world championships have run since 2002 (International Belt Wrestling Association, n.d.). Women's belt wrestling has been part of the international programme since the early IBWA editions, with Turkmenistan's Zuhra Madraimova and Nasiba Surkiewa among the 2017 world champions, and the sport is a permanent fixture of the World Nomad Games in Kyrgyzstan (International Federation of Wrestling on Belts Alysh, n.d.).
