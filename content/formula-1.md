## Basic Information

Formula One (F1) is the highest class of single-seater auto racing, governed by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) and commercially run by the Formula One Group. Cars race on purpose-built and street circuits worldwide in the FIA Formula One World Championship, with the Drivers' and Constructors' World Championships decided over a season of Grands Prix (24 races in 2025).

## Rules

Under the FIA F1 Sporting and Technical Regulations, each team fields two cars with hybrid power units (1.6 L V6 turbo + ERS, capped fuel flow and energy deployment). Race weekends include practice, qualifying (three-stage knockout Q1/Q2/Q3, with sprint qualifying weekends added since 2021) and the race of about 305 km (190 miles, Monaco 260 km) or a maximum of 2 hours, with a minimum of 57 laps at Monaco. Points are awarded to the top 10 (25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-3-1, plus 1 for fastest lap if in top 10). Tires are supplied by a single manufacturer (Pirelli since 2011) with dry/intermediate/wet compounds; each driver must use at least two different dry compounds in a dry race. DRS (drag reduction system) is allowed in designated zones when within 1 second of the car ahead. Penalties include drive-through, time penalties and grid drops for infringements.

## History

The FIA, founded in 1904 in Paris, established the World Championship for Drivers in 1950 (first race at Silverstone, won by Giuseppe "Nino" Farina in an Alfa Romeo). The Constructors' Championship followed in 1958. Legends include Juan Manuel Fangio (5 titles), Jim Clark, Jackie Stewart, Niki Lauda, Ayrton Senna, Michael Schumacher (7 titles, 1994-95, 2000-04), Lewis Hamilton (7 titles, record 103 wins) and Max Verstappen (4 consecutive titles 2021-2024). Iconic circuits include Monaco, Monza, Silverstone and Spa. The 2026 regulations introduce fully sustainable fuel, more electric power (50/50 split) and active aerodynamics.
