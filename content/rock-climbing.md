## Basic Information

Rock climbing is the sport of ascending rock faces, using the body's strength and balance, with the climber protected by rope and anchors belayed by a partner, or climbing short bouldering problems close to the ground protected only by crash pads. As an umbrella discipline it encompasses trad climbing, sport climbing, bouldering, aid climbing and free soloing, and its competitive forms are governed worldwide by the International Federation of Sport Climbing (IFSC), which has held world championships since 1991 and made its Olympic debut at Tokyo 2020 (held in 2021). Modern competitive climbing is described in detail on the [Sport Climbing](sport-climbing.html) and [Lead Climbing](lead-climbing.html) pages of this encyclopaedia; this page covers the outdoor umbrella discipline.

## Rules

Outdoor rock climbing has no match-style rule book; its governing conventions are safety systems and grading. In roped climbing, the leader climbs from the ground up clipping the rope into protection — pre-placed bolts in sport climbing, or cams and nuts the leader places in trad climbing — while a belayer takes in slack; a fall is part of the game, and climbs are graded by difficulty on regionally differing scales, most commonly the Yosemite Decimal System in North America and the French numerical grades elsewhere. Bouldering compresses the same difficulty into a few hard moves close to the ground, protected only by crash pads rather than a rope. A displayed ascent is valid when the climber starts and finishes on agreed holds and the first free ascent of a route establishes its grade, with disputes about style — free versus aid, redpoint versus on-sight — resolved by a long-established ethics tradition rather than officials. Ethics also protect the rock itself: in many areas, such as the Elbe Sandstone of Saxony, placing metal protection is banned and climbers tie knots in slings instead, while elsewhere bolting is regulated by local climbing associations.

## History

Recreational rock climbing separated from mountaineering in the late 19th century, when Walter Parry Haskett Smith's 1886 ascent of the Napes Needle in the English Lake District is often cited as the start of climbing for its own sake in Britain, while continental climbers developed graded free climbing in the Alps and the Elbe Sandstone of Saxony. The 20th century brought bold steps: in the United States, climbers in Yosemite, most famously Royal Robbins and Warren Harding's rival 1957–1958 and later ascents of El Capitan's face, established big-wall standards and the Valley's climbing counterculture; in 1993 Lynn Hill made the first free ascent of the Nose route on El Capitan, a landmark once declared unclimbable. In Europe, Wolfgang Güllich's 1991 first ascent of Action Directe, the world's first 9a, and the rise of the redpoint style redefined the difficulty ceiling, which has since advanced to the 9c grade. Sport climbing's competitive history began with events such as Sportroccia, first held at Bardonecchia in 1985, leading to the creation of the International Climbing Competitions Committee in 1997 and the founding of the International Federation of Sport Climbing in 2007, world championships from 1991 and Olympic inclusion at Tokyo 2020, at which Janja Garnbret and Alberto Ginés López won the combined titles.
