## Basic Information

Soft tennis is a racket sport played mainly in East and South Asia, similar to tennis but with a lighter, softer rubber ball and lighter rackets, governed worldwide by the International Soft Tennis Federation (ISTF), founded in Tokyo in 1973 (ISTF, n.d.-a). The World Soft Tennis Championships have been held since 1975 — the 16th edition took place in Taizhou, China, in 2019 and the 17th in Anseong, South Korea, 1–11 September 2024 — and the sport has been on the Asian Games programme since Hiroshima 1994 (ISTF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Rena Temma of Japan — born 2007 — won the women's singles gold, beating Ri Jin-mi 4–3 in the final, and also took team gold and mixed doubles silver; she had already won the 2025 Asian singles title and became the youngest All Japan singles champion in 2024 at age 16 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Soft tennis follows tennis rules with adaptations for the softer ball: singles and doubles are played on a court with the same dimensions as a tennis court, over a net slightly higher than in tennis, with matches usually played best of three or five sets under tennis-like scoring — four-point games with deuce and set tie-breaks — the softer ball producing longer, higher-bouncing rallies (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ball is a hollow rubber ball about 66 mm in diameter that deforms on impact, and rackets are lighter with smaller heads than tennis rackets (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A serve must land in the diagonally opposite service box, and play follows tennis rules for lets, faults, volleys and scoring. Events at world and continental level comprise men's and women's singles, men's and women's doubles, mixed doubles and team competitions, plus junior categories (ISTF, n.d.-b). Women and men play under identical rules on identical courts; mixed doubles is a standard event (ISTF, n.d.-b).

## History

Soft tennis originated in Japan at the end of the nineteenth century, when tennis — introduced to Japan around 1878 — was adapted with a soft rubber ball and lighter rackets because hard tennis balls were expensive to import (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Asian Soft Tennis Federation was founded in 1955 by Japan, Korea and Chinese Taipei, ran Asian championships from 1956, was dissolved in 1974 and re-established in 1988; the International Soft Tennis Federation was founded in Tokyo in 1973, and the first World Soft Tennis Championships were held in Hawaii in 1975, then biennially until 1987 and every four years since (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport joined the Asian Games programme at Hiroshima 1994 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
