## Basic Information

Lapta (лапта) is a Russian folk bat-and-ball game, documented since the 14th century, that resembles baseball and cricket and is often described as a precursor of baseball — bats and balls from the 1300s were found in excavations at Novgorod. Two teams play on an outdoor field of about 42 m x 53 m: one team bats while the other serves and fields. Since 1990, annual Russian championships have been held and the sport has experienced a revival.

## Rules

The game is played on a field about half the size of a football pitch, marked with parallel sidelines called salo. The batting team fields six players; the server stands next to the batter (unlike baseball's pitcher) and hits/tosses the ball toward him. Each batter gets two chances to hit the ball over a 10 m line; a successful hit lets runners sprint to the far kon line and, if possible, back to the gorod (home) line. Runners score points for completed runs (traditionally 2 points) while the fielding team tries to hit runners with thrown balls; a runner hit by the ball is out. A game lasts about an hour in two halves, and the team with the most points — or the first to have all its players complete runs — wins. A traditional lapta bat is flat and spade-shaped.

## History

Lapta is documented in medieval Russian manuscripts from the 14th century, and 14th-century balls and bats were excavated in Novgorod, making it at least as old as cricket and much older than baseball. Peter the Great encouraged his elite guard to play it for fitness, and the writer Aleksandr Kuprin praised its virtues of loyalty, accuracy, speed and resourcefulness. During the Soviet period lapta was promoted as a national sport: Russian championships began in 1958 and it was included in the Spartakiads from 1959, with thousands of teams in the 1950s. After a decline, a government decree in the late 1980s supported its development alongside baseball and softball, and since 1990 annual championships have revived the game across Russia.
