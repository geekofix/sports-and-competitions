## Basic Information

Dambe is the traditional boxing of the Hausa people of northern Nigeria, southern Niger and southwestern Chad: one fist — the "spear" — is wrapped in cloth and tightly knotted cord, the other hand is the open "shield", and a round ends when a fighter's hand, knee or body touches the ground ("killed"), when there is no activity, or when a halt is called (Wikipedia, n.d.; BBC, 2020). Matches are three rounds with no time limit, accompanied by drumming and ceremony, traditionally performed by travelling butcher and fisherman guilds at harvest festivals; Nigeria's Sports Minister Sunday Dare announced a national league in 2019, and the African Warriors Fighting Championship has modernised the sport (BBC, 2020). Within the combat sports family on this site, its closest relatives are [Kokawa](kokawa.html), [Laamb](laamb.html), [Boxing](boxing.html), [Bare-knuckle Boxing](bare-knuckle-boxing.html), [Lethwei](lethwei.html), [Muay Thai](muay-thai.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The spear fist is wrapped in a strip of cloth covered by knotted cord; dipping the wrap in resin with broken glass is now illegal. The lead leg may be wrapped in chain for blocking; kicks with the unwrapped leg are allowed. No formal weight classes — bouts are between fairly matched fighters. Men compete; women's dambe is rare. Traveling companies (Yan Dambe) stage outdoor shows with music and ritual (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Dambe emerged among Hausa butcher (maharba) and fisherman guilds travelling trade caravans between Kano, Katsina, Sokoto and Zaria during the trans-Saharan trade era; it served as war preparation and a rite of passage. 'Shago' (Umaru Adamu) is regarded as the most famous dambe fighter of all time (Wikipedia, n.d.; Green, 2005, via JALT).
