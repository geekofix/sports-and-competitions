## Basic Information

Ice cross downhill is the winter racing sport in which up to four athletes on ice skates race simultaneously down a steep, walled ice track with banked turns, rollers, jumps and drops, the top two of each heat advancing (All Terrain Skate Cross Federation [ATSX], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The discipline was popularized worldwide as Red Bull Crashed Ice from 2001 to 2019 and has been sanctioned since 2019 by the All Terrain Skate Cross Federation (ATSX), which runs a world ranking with competition levels ATSX 1000, 500, 250 and 100 awarding points toward the world championship; the 2025/26 season was the debut of the ATSX Ice Cross World Cup with events in Austria and Canada (finals at Winterleiten), following the 2024/25 finale (ATSX, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among today's leading athletes are Canada's Cameron Naasz, the 2019 world champion, the 2022/23 world champions Mirko Lahti (men) and Veronika Windisch (women), and on the women's side Jacqueline Legere and Amanda Trunzo of North America and Finland's Salla Kyhälä, a former ringette player (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Races are run in heats of four athletes; the first two across the finish line advance to the next round, and the winners reach the final (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The track is a prepared ice course enclosed by side walls (boards) and may include banked turns (berms), rollers, table-top jumps and drops; athletes wear ice hockey-style skates with sharpened blades, a full-face helmet and a back protector, and start from a starting gate on a signal — false starts are penalized (ATSX, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). ATSX-sanctioned events are graded in levels ATSX 1000 (top international), 500, 250 and 100; points from these events feed the ATSX world ranking and determine qualification for the world championship (ATSX, n.d.-a). Men and women compete under identical rules and on identical courses in separate categories (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The first Crashed Ice event was held in 2001 in Quebec City, Canada, and Red Bull turned it into a world series that ran until 2019 with famous stops in Quebec City, Edmonton, Belfast and other cities; the ATSX was founded to sanction the sport independently from 2019 and gradually took over the discipline, organizing its own world championship and, from the 2025/26 season, the ATSX Ice Cross World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). Early icons include Canada's Cameron Naasz, 2019 world champion, and Kyle Croxall, while Germany's Jasper Felder — a former bandy player — won a record seven Crashed Ice events (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
