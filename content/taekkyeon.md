## Basic Information

Taekkyeon is the traditional Korean martial art of fluid, dance-like movements: rhythmic triangular footwork, sweeping kicks and leg-trips, and kicks and hand techniques designed to unbalance or throw an opponent. Distinct from taekwondo (a common misconception), it is a UNESCO-recognized heritage practice and a combat sport contested at Korean national festivals and international events, the first martial art inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity list.

## Rules

The sport's signature is pumbalbgi, the stepping-on-triangles footwork: competitors move in a continuous rolling rhythm, and matches are decided by legal attacks "+E+" kicks to the body or head, sweeps, inner and outer leg trips, and throws applied to an opponent's leg "+E+" that knock the opponent down or out of the ring. Judged bouts award points for clean takedowns and strikes, penalize dangerous or excessive techniques, and ban brutal moves like grabbing and joint attacks, in keeping with the art's principle of dominance without unnecessary damage. Competition formats range from festival duels to full tournament brackets at the national festival, with weight classes and standardized rules produced by the Korean Taekkyeon Association, and practice has preserved the folk-game character: dance-like sequences, group training and community celebration remain central alongside the sporting side.

## History

Taekkyeon descends through Korean folk games documented from the Joseon dynasty and earlier, when kings reportedly practiced it and one famously banned public matches over the gambling that surrounded them. It survived the twentieth century through a handful of masters, above all Song Deok-gi, who preserved the art and transmitted it after the Korean War. In 1983 South Korea designated taekkyeon Important Intangible Cultural Property No. 76, the first martial art so honored, and in 2011 UNESCO inscribed it on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, again a first for any martial art. The Korea Taekkyeon Association joined the national sports council structure and debuted the art as an official event at the National Sports Festival in 2022, while a sister World Taekkyeon organization spreads the art internationally through schools and events.
