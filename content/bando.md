## Basic Information

Bando is a defensive unarmed martial art of Myanmar, one discipline within the wider Burmese fighting complex called thaing (lethwei is its regulated boxing, banshay its weapons practice, naban its wrestling). Training proceeds in stages — stances and footwork, then blocking and parrying, and only finally offense — mostly through solo and partnered forms (aka), ending in free sparring. The American Bando Association teaches nine animal styles (bull, boar, cobra, viper, python, panther, tiger, scorpion, eagle) within a 9×9 matrix of techniques and principles. Men and women train together; world bando championships are held. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Lethwei](lethwei.html), [Chin Lone](chin-lone.html), [Muay Thai](muay-thai.html), [Kickboxing](kickboxing.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).

## Rules

Bando has no single sport rule set — it is a martial art rather than a ring sport — but competitive bando follows point sparring and form (aka) competition rules of the national and international federations: graded ranks (coloured belts, black belt only after at least five years), black-belt tests covering empty-hand forms, stick and bladed weapons, sparring and a fitness test. In the US system the curriculum is organized as a 9×9 matrix so that principles transfer between techniques. Safety rules govern weapon and sparring practice (TraditionalSports.org, n.d.).

## History

Burmese martial arts, traced by tradition to at least 1000 AD through Indian and Chinese influence, were banned publicly under British rule (1885–1948). In 1933 Gurkha officers founded the Military Athletic Club at Maymyo; after WWII U Ba Than Gyi, director of Burma's national physical education program, united the regional styles into the modern Hanthawaddy system (1948 National Bando Association, Mandalay). His son Maung Gyi began teaching in Washington, D.C. in 1960 and founded the American Bando Association in 1967; the European Bando Thaing Federation (member of the International Thaing Bando Association) was structured from the 1960s–80s (United World Muay Thai Association, n.d.).
