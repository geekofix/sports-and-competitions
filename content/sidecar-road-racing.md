## Basic Information

Sidecar road racing is motorcycle road racing with a passenger-carrying sidecar ("chair") rigidly attached to a solo motorcycle, contested on closed public-road circuits such as the Isle of Man TT Mountain Course of 37.73 miles (60.73 km); the FIM Sidecar World Championship has run since 1949 — the only surviving original class of the inaugural 1949 FIM road-racing championship — and was known as "Superside" until 2017, when maximum engine capacity was cut from 1000 cc to 600 cc (FIM, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the Isle of Man TT, Ryan and Callum Crowe won four consecutive Sidecar TT races through 2025, Ben and Tom Birchall set a lap record of 120.645 mph in 2023 (Race 2), and the Crowe brothers set the outright lap record of 121.021 mph in 2025; the 2026 TT Sidecar class was suspended on 28 May 2026 after crashes in qualifying on safety grounds (Isle of Man TT Races, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Women compete on equal terms as both drivers and passengers: the FIM notes a female sidecar driver as the first woman to win a world championship in a non-segregated FIM discipline, and at TT level crews must have raced together at least three times on the same outfit during the season, with a passenger licence required; from 2027 a new production-derived engine framework applies (FIM, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

 Within the motorsport family on this site, its closest relatives are [Sidecarcross](sidecarcross.html), [Motocross](motocross.html), [Motorcycle Racing](motorcycle-racing.html), [Enduro](enduro.html), [Formula One](formula-one.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The FIM Sidecar World Championship runs two classes: Superside (up to 600 cc since 2017, previously 1000 cc prototypes) and F2 (600 cc four-stroke production-based, or 350 cc two-stroke, since 1990); in 1980 the FIM banned pure prototype chassis, and today's outfits use frames from makers such as Windle, LCR and ART with engines up to 600 cc (FIM, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Each machine carries a driver and a passenger, both licensed; the passenger sits in the sidecar and leans out in corners because a sidecar combination does not lean like a solo (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). TT Sidecar races are run over several laps of the 37.73-mile Mountain Course with championship points for the leading finishers, and there is no gender split — women and men race in the same classes (FIM, n.d.; Isle of Man TT Races, n.d.).

## History

The first sidecar race is credited to 1923, when Freddie Dixon with passenger Walter Denny lapped a Douglas banking sidecar at over 53 mph; sidecar racing at Monza is documented in the 29 April 1923 Critérium des side-cars photographed by Agence Rol, and the TT first ran a sidecar class in the 1920s with championship status from 1954 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIM included sidecars in its inaugural 1949 world championship, and they remained a championship class continuously — the TT class ran 1954–1976 before the world title moved fully to short circuits; the F2 production class was added in 1990 and prototypes were banned in 1980 (FIM, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). England's Tim Reeves won the sidecar world championship eight times, the Ryan and Callum Crowe brothers won four straight Sidecar TTs through 2025, and the Birchall brothers hold the 2023 lap record of 120.645 mph with the Crowes' 121.021 mph outright record in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Isle of Man TT Races, n.d.).
