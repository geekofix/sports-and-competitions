## Basic Information

Elephant polo is a variant of polo played on elephants, best known through the King's Cup Elephant Polo Tournament in Hua Hin, Thailand (first held in 2001 by the Anantara hotel group) and historically governed by the Thai Elephant Polo Association (TEPA), which maintained official regulations from 1982. Matches are played with a mahout (elephant handler) steering and a player hitting a standard polo ball with a long-handled mallet.

## Rules

Under the TEPA regulations, a team has three players on elephants, and each match consists of two chukkas (periods) of 7 minutes each; elephants are changed at halftime so no animal plays both chukkas. The player uses a mallet about 2-2.1 m (7 ft) long to hit a standard polo ball between the opponent's goal posts. The mahout directs the elephant, and the player may only strike the ball when it is within reach; fouls follow polo rules adapted for elephants. Safety rules cover the welfare of the elephants, including restrictions on the use of the ankus (bullhook) and mandatory veterinary checks. Matches are played on a reduced-size field compared to horse polo.

## History

Polo-like games on elephants have historical roots in India, Thailand and Nepal, where elephants were used in royal processions and warfare games. Modern competitive elephant polo was revived in Nepal in the early 20th century and formalized in Thailand with the first King's Cup Elephant Polo Tournament in 2001 at Anantara Hua Hin, organized by hotelier William Heinecke with teams sponsored by international brands. The TEPA codified the rules and ran tournaments also in Nepal (King's Cup in Meghauli). In October 2018, TEPA announced the end of elephant polo matches in Thailand following allegations by animal-welfare group PETA about the treatment of elephants; the sport continues in Nepal in adapted, welfare-focused formats. The King's Cup became a major charity fundraiser for elephant conservation.
