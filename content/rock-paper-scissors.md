## Basic Information

Rock Paper Scissors is a two-player hand game in which both players simultaneously reveal one of three shapes — a fist (rock), a flat hand (paper) or V-shaped fingers (scissors) — and rock beats scissors, scissors beat paper, paper beats rock, while identical throws tie (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Played worldwide as a quick decision-making game, it is also organized as a competitive mind sport: the World Rock Paper Scissors Association (WRPSA) has run it as a sport since 2015 with a numbered official rulebook (ruleset v1.0, 2026 edition), Elo-style world rankings built from recorded results and free weekly online Opens (WRPSA, n.d.). Earlier, the Toronto-based World RPS Society, founded by the brothers Douglas and Graham Walker, staged world championships from 2002 to 2009 — the 2003 Toronto event drew 320 competitors for a C$5,000 prize — and the USARPS League held Las Vegas events from 2006 to 2008 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women and men compete on equal terms in open brackets (WRPSA, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the WRPSA ruleset v1.0, the referee — or both players together — calls the four-beat count "Rock, Paper, Scissors, Shoot!" while both players keep a closed fist, and on "Shoot" both reveal one of the three legal shapes at the same time; rock beats scissors, scissors beat paper, paper beats rock, and matching throws tie and are replayed (WRPSA, n.d.). Reveal timing is regulated: priming (moving early) or a late reveal is a foul, changing the shape after the reveal forfeits the round, and the referee's decision on disputes is final (WRPSA, n.d.). Matches are best-of-three — the first player to win two throws takes the match — with ties re-thrown, and the rulebook also covers event procedure and conduct (WRPSA, n.d.). The game is open to women and men in the same open categories, with world rankings published from recorded tournament results (WRPSA, n.d.). Beyond sport, RPS has settled real disputes: in 2005 Christie's and Sotheby's used it to decide a consignment worth more than US$20 million (scissors beat paper), in 2006 a US federal judge ordered it for a deposition dispute, and in 2018 an English football referee was suspended for three weeks after using it to pick a coin toss (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A 2014 study by Zhejiang University with 360 students found predictable patterns: winners tend to repeat their winning throw, while losers shift to the throw that would have beaten their opponent's last move (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Hand games of this type have roots in Han-dynasty China, and the Japanese variant jan-ken spread through Asia before reaching Europe in the 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitive play began with the World RPS Society in Toronto, where Douglas and Graham Walker organized world championships from 2002 to 2009, including the 320-competitor 2003 event with a C$5,000 prize, a 2004 broadcast on Fox Sports Net and their Official RPS Strategy Guide published by Simon & Schuster in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The USARPS League then ran its own Las Vegas events at Mandalay Bay from 2006 to 2008 with a $50,000 prize won by Matti Leshem (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Since 2015 the WRPSA has professionalized the game with its rulebook, rankings and online Opens, and its best-known champion is Ken "Whitey" Watson, who defeated Keisuke Honda in a Pepsi-sponsored Japan event broadcast on ESPN8 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
