## Basic Information

Counter-Strike is the world's premier competitive first-person shooter: two teams of five — counter-terrorists and terrorists — play round-based matches on defusal and hostage maps, with Valve's Counter-Strike 2 (CS2), released in September 2023, as the current competitive version (Valve, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The pinnacle of the calendar is the Major Championships, sponsored by Valve with 32 qualified teams, a $1.25 million prize pool and best-of-five grand finals; the IEM Cologne Major 2026 was won by Team Falcons 3–0 over FURIA on 21 June 2026 — the first Major title for m0NESY, karrigan and NiKo, with TeSeS and kyousuke completing the roster — making Falcons the current Major champions, with the next Major, PGL Singapore, scheduled for 25 November–13 December 2026 and 2027 Majors announced for Buenos Aires and Shanghai (HLTV, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Team Vitality won the Paris Major 2023 and then back-to-back Majors at Austin 2025 (2–1 over The MongolZ) and Budapest 2025 (3–1 over FaZe), with Vitality's star rifler ZywOo collecting three Major MVP awards; The MongolZ became the first Asian team to reach HLTV world No. 1 in September 2025 after winning the 2025 Esports World Cup, and Brazil's FalleN retired in 2026 at IEM Rio (HLTV, n.d.). The official world team ranking is published by HLTV (HLTV, n.d.). Counter-Strike is an open, mixed-gender esport — there are no gender restrictions in the base game or the Major circuit — and a dedicated women's professional scene (for example the ESL Impact League) runs alongside the open events (Valve, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are the Falcons roster — Ilya "m0NESY" Osipov, pictured below, with captain karrigan and NiKo — plus stars such as donk, who set the HLTV rating record of 1.45 at the Shanghai 2024 Major and was named 2024 player of the year, and ZywOo of Vitality (HLTV, n.d.). ## Rules

Competitive Counter-Strike is played under Valve's official ruleset: matches are best-of-one, best-of-three or best-of-five maps in a veto process; a map is won by the first team to 13 rounds (win by 2, capped at 24 rounds, after which overtime blocks of three rounds are played, win by 2, with $10,000 starting money) (Valve, n.d.). Each round lasts up to 1 minute 55 seconds; in defusal maps the terrorists win by detonating the bomb (40-second fuse, 10 seconds to defuse, 5 with a defuse kit) or by eliminating the counter-terrorists, while the counter-terrorists win by defusing, eliminating the terrorists or letting the clock run out (Valve, n.d.). Teams start with $800 and a maximum of $16,000; weapons, armor, grenades and a defuse kit are bought in the buy phase at the start of each round, and a loss-bonus ladder rewards teams on losing streaks up to $3,400 (Valve, n.d.). Kill rewards depend on the weapon (for example $300 for most rifles, $100 for the AWP); after 12 rounds teams switch sides, and half-time lasts a few minutes (Valve, n.d.). The active-duty defusal map pool in the premier circuit comprises seven maps — Anubis, Ancient, Inferno, Mirage, Nuke, Overpass and Train/Vertigo depending on the season — plus hostage maps in some formats (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Match administration uses Valve's anti-cheat (VAC) and, at Majors, additional anti-cheat and coaching restrictions; team rankings are published continuously by HLTV, and the premier circuit (Majors plus partner events such as the IEM circuit and the Blast circuit) determines the 32 Major invites via the Valve regional ranking (HLTV, n.d.). There are no gender-, age- or classification-based rule differences in the open game; women's and junior circuits apply the identical rules in their own tournaments (Valve, n.d.).

## History

Counter-Strike began in 1999 as a Half-Life mod created by Minh "Gooseman" Le and Jess "Cliffe" Cliffe, who wanted a realistic tactical shooter; Valve acquired the rights and released Counter-Strike as a standalone game in 2000, with Counter-Strike 1.6 (2003) becoming the long-lasting competitive standard and Counter-Strike: Source (2004) and Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (2012, developed with Hidden Path Entertainment) following before CS2 replaced Global Offensive in September 2023 with the subtick system (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first CS:GO Major, DreamHack Winter 2013, was won by Fnatic; the most successful team in Major history is Astralis of Denmark with four Major titles, and its AWPer Nicolai "device" Reedtz (b. 1995) and Emil "Magisk" Reif (b. 1998) are among the defining players of the era, with teammate Peter "dupreeh" Rasmussen holding the record of five Major titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Other icons include Sweden's Christopher "GeT_RiGhT" Alesund (b. 1990) and Gabriel "FalleN" Toledo (b. 1991), whose career ended at IEM Rio 2026, and Oleksandr "s1mple" Kostyliev (b. 1997) of Natus Vincere, widely regarded as the greatest rifler of all time (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The modern French era was dominated by ZywOo and Vitality from 2023, culminating in the back-to-back Major wins of 2025, before Team Falcons — built around the teenage Russian AWPer m0NESY — lifted the Cologne Major in June 2026 (HLTV, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
