## Basic Information

Whist is the classic four-player partnership trick-taking game with a full 52-card deck and a trump suit; it is the direct ancestor of bridge. Played since the 18th century in England; the 1874 caricature 'Le whist' (Bertall) shows its social heyday. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Euchre](euchre.html), [Contract Bridge](contract-bridge.html), [Pinochle](pinochle.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Bertall, 1874).

## Rules

52-card deck, 13 each; no bidding in plain whist — the last card turned is trump; follow suit compulsory; highest trump or highest of led suit wins the trick; high cards (HCP) count for honors. Variants: bid whist, etc (Bertall, 1874).

## History

Whist evolved from 'triumph' in the 17th century and dominated English card rooms in the 18th–19th centuries (the leather whist marker by Charles Goodall, 1860, is preserved); it directly begat bridge (1890s) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
