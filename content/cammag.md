## Basic Information

Cammag (also written camman) is a traditional stick-and-ball game of the Isle of Man, closely related to Scottish shinty and Irish hurling. Players use a wooden stick called a camman to hit a small leather ball, the crick, and try to drive it towards the opposing goal. There is no national governing body; the game survives as a local custom with informal, parish-specific rules, most famously in the winter matches played around St Stephen's Day (26 December).

## Rules

Cammag is played between two teams on a road, field or beach, with a goal at each end marked by trees, posts or a gap. Teams hit the crick with the camman in any direction, and the side that drives the ball through the opponents' goal scores. Because there is no standardized rulebook, details such as team size, the number of goals needed to win and the allowed strokes vary from parish to parish and from match to match; local custom and the agreement of the players govern the game. Matches are traditionally played in winter, from St Stephen's Day towards Easter, and can involve large numbers of players.

## History

Cammag is the Manx descendant of the Gaelic stick-and-ball games; Irish hurling and Scottish shinty share the same roots, and cammag appears in records of the Isle of Man from 1760 onwards. At its height in the 18th and 19th centuries it was played across the island at fairs and festivals, but it declined sharply in the 20th century. Today organized matches survive in a handful of parishes, where revival efforts keep the old Manx name and customs alive.
