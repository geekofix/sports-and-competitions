## Basic Information

Slalom (SL) is the most technical alpine skiing discipline: the shortest course with the tightest, quickest turns around a series of gates set in pairs, raced twice with the times combined. It is governed by the FIS and has been an Olympic event since Garmisch-Partenkirchen 1936 (International Ski and Snowboard Federation [FIS], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-c).

Among the athletes who shape the discipline today, the leading women come first: the United States' Mikaela Shiffrin — with more than 100 World Cup victories the most successful alpine skier of all time — won Olympic slalom gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 in 1:39.10. Among the men, Switzerland's Loïc Meillard took Olympic gold in 1:53.61, ahead of Austria's Fabio Gstrein and Norway's Henrik Kristoffersen (FIS, n.d.-a; FIS, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under FIS rules, each athlete makes two runs; in the second run the top 30 starters begin in reverse order of their first-run results, and the two times are added — the lowest combined time wins. Gates are set in quick combinations — hairpins and flushes — and skiers may use their poles to block gates; both ski tips must cross each gate line, and missing a gate means disqualification. The course must meet fixed vertical-drop ranges per category, and equipment follows FIS specifications, including the mandatory helmet (FIS, n.d.-c). A parallel slalom variant — two skiers racing head-to-head on identical courses — is also contested at World Cup level. Men and women race under the identical rule set.

## History

Slalom has been on the Olympic program since 1936 and the FIS World Ski Championships since 1931. Its quick-turn character produced icons such as Sweden's Ingemar Stenmark, with 86 World Cup victories, Austria's Marcel Hirscher, who won eight consecutive overall World Cup titles from 2012 to 2019, and Italy's Alberto Tomba. On the women's side, the USA's Mikaela Shiffrin and Slovakia's Petra Vlhová have defined the modern era, with Shiffrin adding Olympic slalom gold at Milano-Cortina 2026 (FIS, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
