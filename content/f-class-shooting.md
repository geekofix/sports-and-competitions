## Basic Information

F-Class is a long-range rifle discipline shot prone with a bipod or front rest at distances from 300 to 1,000 yards (about 300–900 m), using a telescopic sight; the name comes from the target’s extra "F" ring (half the diameter of the TR target’s smallest ring) (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the fullbore long-range family together with Palma/Target Rifle, and is governed by the International Confederation of Fullbore Rifle Associations (ICFRA), formally founded in July 2003 (ICFRA, n.d.).

ICFRA manages the F-Class World Championships and standardizes the rules worldwide; the discipline splits into F-Open (any calibre up to 8 mm, any bullet weight) and F-TR (Target Rifle) in which the rifle is limited to .308 Winchester or .223 Remington with iron sights and a 155-grain or 90-grain match bullet (Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches are fired in strings of 15–20 shots per target at 600, 800/900 and 1,000 yards, with the centre count counting (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Shooters fire prone from a bipod (F-Open) or sling with rear bag (F-TR); wind flags and mirage reading are as important as the rifle, because a 1,000-yard shot drifts several minutes of angle in a light breeze (ICFRA, n.d.). Scoring uses the fullbore target rings, with the X-ring (centre) worth the most and used for tie-breaks (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same matches; junior and Palma (team) categories exist at major championships (ICFRA, n.d.).

## History

The Palma match — a team event at 800/900/1,000 yards named after the Palma Trophy first shot in 1876 at Creedmoor, Long Island — is the ancestor of modern fullbore competition; the Palma Council governed it until the ICFRA took over world-championship management in 2003 (Wikipedia, n.d.). F-Class itself grew out of Palma shooting in the 1980s–90s when shooters added scopes and bipods, and the first dedicated F-Class world championships were held in the 2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today F-Class world championships alternate with the Palma match and draw entries from more than 20 countries.
