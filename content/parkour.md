## Basic Information

Parkour (from the French parcours du combattant, "obstacle course") is a movement discipline in which practitioners, called traceurs, overcome obstacles in their environment using only their bodies, moving as quickly and efficiently as possible from point A to point B through running, jumping, vaulting, climbing and rolling. It was developed in the early 1990s in Lisses, a suburb of Paris, by David Belle and the group Yamakasi, inspired by the military training method of Georges Hébert that Belle's father Raymond, a Paris firefighter, taught him. In February 2017 the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) recognized parkour as a competitive discipline with two formats, [parkour speed](parkour-speed.html) and [parkour freestyle](parkour-freestyle.html); the FIG runs the Parkour World Cup (since 2018) and World Championships, and parkour debuted at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham. It is related to [gymnastics](gymnastics.html), [obstacle course racing](obstacle-course-racing.html) and [ninja sport](ninja-sport.html).

## Rules

As a practice, parkour has no points and no competition: the goal is efficient, controlled movement, and traceurs choose their own lines through the urban or natural environment. In competitive parkour under FIG rules, the two disciplines are speedrun and freestyle. In speedrun, athletes race against the clock over a standardized obstacle course, and the fastest time wins. In freestyle, athletes perform an acrobatic routine on the course, judged under a code of points for difficulty, execution, variety and flow. Safety rules govern the construction of competition courses, and athletes compete in men's and women's categories.

## History

Raymond Belle, born in French Indochina in 1939, became a legendary Paris firefighter and passed on Georges Hébert's Méthode naturelle, including the parcours du combattant, to his son David. In the late 1980s and early 1990s, David Belle and his friends in Lisses and Évry, including Sébastien Foucan, Chau Belle Dinh, Yann Hnautra and Malik Diouf, formed the group Yamakasi and developed parkour, renaming "parcours" with a "k" to distinguish it. The 2001 film Yamakasi, produced by Luc Besson, and the 2003 BBC documentary Jump London brought the discipline to world attention, and from 2004 videos spread virally on the internet. The FIG announced its integration of parkour on 24 February 2017, which led parts of the community to found Parkour Earth on 4 July 2017; the FIG Parkour World Cup began in 2018 within the FISE festival, and parkour was contested for the first time at a major multi-sport event at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham.
