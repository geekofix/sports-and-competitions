## Basic Information

Parkour (from the French parcours du combattant, "obstacle course") is a movement discipline in which practitioners, called traceurs, overcome obstacles in their environment using only their bodies, moving as quickly and efficiently as possible from point A to point B through running, jumping, vaulting, climbing and rolling (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was developed in the early 1990s in Lisses, a suburb of Paris, by David Belle and the group Yamakasi, inspired by the military training method of Georges Hébert that Belle's father Raymond, a Paris firefighter, taught him (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In February 2017 the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) recognized parkour as a competitive discipline with two formats, [parkour speed](parkour-speed.html) and [parkour freestyle](parkour-freestyle.html); women and men compete in separate categories at the FIG Parkour World Cup and World Championships (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). The FIG runs the Parkour World Cup (since 2018) and World Championships, and parkour debuted at the World Games 2022 in Birmingham (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). It is related to [gymnastics](gymnastics.html), [obstacle course racing](obstacle-course-racing.html) and [ninja sport](ninja-sport.html).

## Rules

As a practice, parkour has no points and no competition: the goal is efficient, controlled movement, and traceurs choose their own lines (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In competitive parkour under the FIG, speedrun athletes race a standardized obstacle course against the clock — fastest time wins, with falls and missed obstacles costing time penalties — while freestyle athletes perform a 60-90 second routine on a course, judged on difficulty, execution, flow and creativity (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Courses are built from standardized modules — walls, rails, boxes — and must be safety-inspected; athletes compete in men's and women's categories, plus junior and senior age groups, under identical course rules (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Safety rules require helmets in some junior events, medical cover at all FIG events, and prohibit dangerous stunts in freestyle (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.).

## History

Parkour was developed in the early 1990s in Lisses, near Paris, by David Belle and his friends of the group Yamakasi — Sébastien Foucan, David Malgorné and others — who trained on the playgrounds and buildings of the banlieue, adapting Georges Hébert's "méthode naturelle" taught by Raymond Belle (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The term "parkour" was coined by Hubert Koundé, who changed Hébert's "parcours du combattant" (the 'd' was dropped to make it sound more dynamic) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The discipline spread worldwide through films such as Yamakasi (2001) and the video game Mirror's Edge (2008), and the FIG recognized parkour in 2017, launching the Parkour World Cup in 2018 and the first World Championships in 2018 in Tokyo (International Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Founder David Belle, pictured below, later became an international ambassador for the discipline (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
