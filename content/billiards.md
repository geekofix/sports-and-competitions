## Basic Information

Billiards is the umbrella term for cue sports played on a cloth-covered table: carom billiards (no pockets, 3-cushion), snooker and English billiards (pocket tables with points for cannons and pot), and the pool family. The World Confederation of Billiards Sports (WCBS) governs carom, snooker and English billiards internationally; the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA) governs pool disciplines.

## Rules

In three-cushion carom (the premier discipline at the World Championships), two players share a pocketless table with three balls (white, yellow, red); a point is scored when the cue ball hits both object balls, with at least one cushion contacted before the second object ball (or after, depending on the rule set). Games at the World Championships are played to 40 points. In snooker, players pot red balls (1 point) and colors (2-7 points) alternately on a 12 x 6 ft table, with the highest break and frame scores deciding matches — best of 35 frames at the World Championship final. Pool games (8-ball, 9-ball) are played under WPA rules.

## History

Cue sports descend from outdoor ground billiards played in Europe since the 15th century; the first indoor tables appeared in the 1400s-1500s. Carom billiards became a precision art in 19th-century France, snooker was invented by British officers in India in 1875, and pool emerged in the United States in the late 1800s. The WCBS was founded in 1923 (as UIFAB) and the WPA in 1965; the World Three-Cushion Championship has been held since 1926.
