## Basic Information

Beach sprint rowing (coastal rowing's sprint format) is a race in single sculls over 500 m to 1 km on flat water, starting and finishing on the beach: the rower runs into the water, rows out around a buoy, and sprints back to the sand. It is governed by World Rowing, which organizes the World Rowing Beach Sprint Championships, and was a Youth Olympic sport at Buenos Aires 2018.

## Rules

Under World Rowing beach sprint rules, the course is about 500 m out to a turning buoy and 500 m back (shorter for juniors), timed from the beach start to the beach finish. The athlete runs from the start line, launches the boat, rows around the buoy, and runs the boat onto the beach at the finish. Competitions use heats, repechages and finals; boats are provided by the organizer and are the same for all competitors in a category. At the World Championships, men's and women's events are held in senior and U23 categories.

## History

Beach sprint rowing evolved from traditional surf-boat racing and coastal rowing in Australia and Europe. World Rowing (FISA) codified the beach sprint format in the 2010s and held the first World Rowing Beach Sprint Championships in 2015 in Italy. The discipline debuted at the Youth Olympic Games in Buenos Aires 2018 and is a regular part of the World Rowing calendar alongside the flat-water world championships.
