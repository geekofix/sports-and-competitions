## Basic Information

Touchtennis is a modified version of tennis played on a compact court with a white cut-foam ball and shorter 21-inch (53 cm) rackets; it is played in pubs, parks and indoor venues and is regularly featured on Sky Sports (Wikipedia, n.d.). It was created in 2002 by the English businessman Rashid Ahmad to entertain his young daughter in the back garden, and he built it into a professional tour with official world rankings and four Grand Slam events plus Masters tournaments (Touchtennis, n.d.). The tour runs separate women's and men's singles and doubles rankings with their own Grand Slam titles, and women and men compete under identical rules in singles, doubles and mixed events; the LTA in the United Kingdom and the RFET in Spain use touchtennis as their official mini-tennis format to bring new players into the game (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The official touchtennis court is 12 m × 5 m for singles and 12 m × 6 m for doubles, with a tolerance of up to 25 cm on all lines, and can be taped onto almost any flat surface — a badminton court works with a lowered net and a re-taped service line (Wikipedia, n.d.; Touchtennis, n.d.). The official ball is 8 cm (3.1 in) in diameter and made of white cut foam — about 75% slower than a tennis ball — and official rackets are 20.9–21.1 inches (53.1–53.6 cm) long with a head of at most 107 square inches (690 cm²), at least 12 cross and 14 main strings and no textured or hexagonal strings (Touchtennis, n.d.). Only one serve is permitted — no second serves and no lets: a serve that clips the net and lands in is "justice" and stays in play — and a serve may not be volleyed; scoring is 15-30-40 like tennis but with no advantage, so at deuce the receiver chooses the side and the next point decides the game (Touchtennis, n.d.). Sets are played to 4 games and must be won by two clear games, with a tiebreak to 5 points (sudden death at 4–4) at 4–4; matches are best of three sets, or best of five in the four Slams, with singles tiebreaks first to 5 points and a deciding point at 4–4 (Touchtennis, n.d.). Players change ends after every odd game as in tennis, tournament entrants must be 16 or older, one audible obscenity is an immediate point penalty and three mean disqualification, and smashing a racket means disqualification plus a £20 fine; a thrown racket still counts as a shot if it does not cross to the opponent's side (Touchtennis, n.d.). Women and men play the same rules in their own and in mixed doubles, where one player per team must stay inside the service area until the serve has bounced (Touchtennis, n.d.).

## History

Touchtennis was created in 2002 by Rashid Ahmad, a London-based businessman, as a way of entertaining his young daughter in the back garden; he then formalized the game with official court dimensions, equipment and rules and built a tour complete with rankings, four Grand Slams, Masters events and smaller tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport gained mainstream exposure through regular Sky Sports coverage, and the Lawn Tennis Association (LTA) in Britain and the RFET in Spain adopted it as their entry-level mini-tennis format (Touchtennis, n.d.). Notable players include former and current ATP professionals Fernando González (Chile, Olympic silver medalist 2008, retired 2012), Marcus Willis, Jeff Tarango and Chris Eaton, while TV personalities Bear Grylls and Miranda Hart are well-known supporters and tournament players; the founder himself collected 42 singles titles (20 Slams and 22 Masters) on his own tour (Wikipedia, n.d.).
