## Basic Information

Blanket toss (nalukataq) is the signature Inuit event: a competitor stands in the middle of a walrus-hide or bearded-seal-skin blanket held by 40–50 pullers and is tossed as high as possible, judged on height, balance and style in the air (WEIO, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is a medal event at the Arctic Winter Games and the World Eskimo Indian Olympics; at the 2026 WEIO in Fairbanks, Eden Hopson (Utqiaġvik) set a new women's record and Bernard Clark (Wasilla) won the men's (ICT News, 2026). Women and men compete in separate categories. Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The blanket is made of walrus hide or bearded seal skin with rope loops for handles; 40–50 pullers toss in unison on a count. Judges score balance, height and form; the athlete must land on their feet to keep the score. One-foot/two-foot takeoff variants exist at some games (WEIO, n.d.).

## History

Nalukataq originated in the whaling celebration after a successful hunt — the crew was tossed to spot whales and to celebrate, throwing candy to the crowd. It became a formal competition at the first Arctic Winter Games medal programme in 1974 and at WEIO from 1961 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
