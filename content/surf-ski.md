## Basic Information

Surf ski is ocean racing on a narrow, sit-on-top kayak (surfski) built for open-water downwind paddling: athletes paddle with a double-bladed paddle and ride waves ("runs") to gain speed, with races from 10 km sprints to 50 km+ marathons (International Canoe Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The World Surfski Championships are sanctioned under the ICF ocean-racing umbrella, and elite athletes such as South Africans Dawid Mocke (multiple world champion) and women's stars such as Alison Zerbst have made it a global professional sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Within the board and tow water sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Surfing](surfing.html), [Kitesurfing](kitesurfing.html), [Stand Up Paddleboarding](stand-up-paddleboarding.html), [Canoe Ocean Racing](canoe-ocean-racing.html), [Va'a](va-a.html), [Outrigger Canoeing](outrigger-canoeing.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Races are mass-start or start-wave events on open-ocean courses, usually downwind; ski dimensions are classed (elite, junior, masters hulls), paddles are wing or blade style, and leash and flotation rules apply for safety. Distances range from about 10 km to over 50 km; the ICF Canoe Ocean Racing World Championships include surfski events. Men's and women's fields race the same courses, with separate classifications (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The modern surfski evolved in South Africa and Australia in the 1950s–60s from lifeguard rescue craft, with the first dedicated racing skis built in Cape Town; the sport exploded globally in the 2000s with the Molokai–Oahu race and the ICF ocean-racing programme. Dawid Mocke has won a record number of world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
