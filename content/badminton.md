## Basic Information

Badminton is a racket sport played over a net with a lightweight shuttlecock, in singles or doubles; two or four players hit the shuttlecock back and forth, scoring when it lands in the opponent's court. It is governed by the Badminton World Federation (BWF), founded in 1934, and has been an Olympic medal sport since Barcelona 1992. It is one of the fastest racket sports: smashes regularly exceed 400 km/h (250 mph), and the current recorded fastest smash is over 490 km/h (Badminton World Federation [BWF], n.d.-a) (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-a) (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-b) (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-c). Badminton is the shuttlecock member of the racket family — [Tennis](tennis.html), [Table Tennis](table-tennis.html), [Crossminton](crossminton.html) (its netless cousin), [Pickleball](pickleball.html), [Para Badminton](para-badminton.html) — with the full list under Important Links.

The 2026 season was headlined by the BWF World Championships in New Delhi, India (Indira Gandhi Arena, August 17–23, 2026), the 30th edition: South Korea's An Se-young defended her women's singles title by beating Japan's Akane Yamaguchi 21–17, 21–14, France's Alex Lanier became the first Frenchman to win the men's singles world title (beating Japan's Kodai Naraoka 21–11, 21–11), and France's Thom Gicquel and Delphine Delrue took the first French mixed doubles world title (BWF, n.d.-b). The 2026 Malaysia Open went to Kunlavut Vitidsarn (men's singles) and An Se-young (women's singles) (BWF, n.d.-c).

Among the players who shape the game today, the women come first: South Korea's An Se-young, the 2024 Olympic and two-time (2023, 2026) world champion in women's singles, and Japan's Akane Yamaguchi, the 2026 world finalist; on the men's side, Denmark's Viktor Axelsen, the 2020 Olympic and 2022 world champion, and France's Alex Lanier, the 2026 world champion, lead the way (BWF, n.d.-b; BWF, n.d.-c).

## Rules

Under the BWF Laws of Badminton, the court is 13.4 m long and 6.1 m wide for doubles (5.18 m wide for singles), with the net 1.55 m high at the posts (1.524 m at the center). Matches at the World Championships, World Tour and Olympics are best of three games to 21 points, won by two clear points (capped at 30); the server scores on their own serve only, and at 20-all the side that gains a two-point lead wins the game (BWF, n.d.-a).

The serve must be hit with the shuttle below waist level (the base of the shuttle below 1.15 m for adults under the 2024 height rule), into the diagonally opposite service court; in singles the service court is long and narrow, in doubles short and wide. During the rally, a player may not touch the net, may not hit the shuttle twice, and a shuttle landing on a line is "in." A fault gives the point to the opponent. In doubles, the serving side alternates service courts after each point won, and the receiving side rotates its two players after each serve (BWF, n.d.-a).

Equipment: rackets must weigh 78–94 g unstrung, and the feathered shuttle (16 feathers) or nylon shuttle is used; at the World Championships and Olympics only feathered shuttles are used. Umpires control the match, call faults and lets, and the tournament referee can overrule. There are no gender-specific rule differences — men and women play the identical laws on identical courts; the only differences are competitive divisions. Age classes: BWF runs Junior (U19) and senior categories, and the Olympics feature elite only. Para-badminton, governed by BWF as well, uses six sport classes (WH1/WH2 wheelchair, SL3/SL4 standing lower limb, SU5 upper limb, SS6 short stature) with modified court sizes and net heights for some classes (BWF, n.d.-b).

## History

Badminton descends from battledore-and-shuttlecock, played for centuries in China, Greece and India, and from the game of "poona," played by British officers in India in the 1860s. The modern game takes its name from Badminton House in Gloucestershire, England, where the Duke of Beaufort entertained guests with the game in the 1870s (BWF, n.d.-a). The first All England Open was held in 1899 — the world's oldest badminton tournament, still run today under the name All England Open Badminton Championships — and the International Badminton Federation (now BWF) was founded in 1934 in London with nine member nations (All England Open Badminton Championships, n.d.).

Badminton became a full Olympic medal sport at Barcelona 1992; the early Olympic eras were dominated by Indonesia and China in doubles and by Denmark's players in singles. Landmark champions include Indonesia's Alan Budikusuma and Hendra Setiawan (men's doubles gold 1992), China's Lin Dan ("Super Dan," Olympic gold 2008 and 2012), and on the women's side Indonesia's Susi Susanti (1992 gold), China's Zhang Ning (2004, 2008) and Spain's Carolina Marín (2016 gold, the only non-Asian women's singles Olympic champion). Denmark's Viktor Axelsen won the men's singles gold at Tokyo 2020, and South Korea's An Se-young won the women's singles gold at Paris 2024 at age 22 (Olympics.com, n.d.).
