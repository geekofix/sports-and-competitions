## Basic Information

The rustic race (corrida rústica) is the 100-metre sprint of the World Indigenous Games, run barefoot or in simple footwear on a dirt track — a nod to running as a universal indigenous skill. It was a competitive event at the 2015 World Indigenous Games in Palmas, Brazil (with athletics, archery, canoeing, football, tug of war and the indigenous disciplines), and again at the 2017 Maskwacis Games. Men's and women's heats, semifinals and finals are held. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [World Indigenous Games](world-indigenous-games.html), [Xikunahati](xikunahati.html), [Corrida de Tora](corrida-de-tora.html), [100m Sprint](100m-sprint.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Cultural Survival, n.d.).

## Rules

Standard sprint rules adapted: 100 m on a dirt/clay track, staggered or straight lanes, false-start disqualification, electronic or hand timing; heats, semifinals, final. Footwear is free (traditionally none); the event is open to indigenous athletes of the participating nations (Cultural Survival, n.d.).

## History

The 100 m rustic race was among the competitive events of the first World Indigenous Games (Palmas, Tocantins, 23 October–1 November 2015; ~2,000 athletes from 23 countries and ~50 ethnic groups, opened by President Dilma Rousseff), alongside canoeing, archery and the spear toss; it continued at the 2017 Games in Maskwacis, Alberta (Canada) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
