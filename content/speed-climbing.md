## Basic Information

Speed climbing is the timed racing discipline of climbing: two competitors climb identical, standardised 15-metre routes on an artificial wall simultaneously, the faster to the top buzzer winning. Governed by the International Federation of Sport Climbing (IFSC), it has been an IFSC World Cup and world championship discipline since the founding era of competition climbing and an Olympic event \u2014 first inside the combined format of Tokyo 2020, then as a standalone medal event from Paris 2024 \u2014 where the world's best now race up in under five seconds.

## Rules

Competition speed climbing takes place on an artificial 15-metre wall with a 5-degree overhang, IFSC-certified and identical every time and everywhere \u2014 the same twenty hand-holds in the same order, at a difficulty of around 6b (roughly YDS 5.10c) that most recreational climbers could complete, but with the intent of pure speed; competitors are secured by an auto-belay system from the top of the wall. Races are one-on-one knockouts: two wall lanes, two athletes, a start beep, and the first to hit the touchpad at the summit wins (with false starts and pad-triggered time rankings deciding placements); the women's world record now dips under six seconds and the men's under five \u2014 Sam Watson of the United States recorded 4.64 seconds at the 2025 IFSC World Cup in Bali, and Aleksandra Miros\u0142aw of Poland, the dominant women's racer, has repeatedly reset her own mark (2025 world title included). Outside competition, speed climbing also exists as a discipline on cliffs (recording speed records up classic routes) and on special poles and trees in the tradition of the sport, but the timed wall format is its organised core.

## History

Speed climbing is the oldest form of competition climbing — speed contests on cliffs and artificial structures were staged in the former Soviet Union and Eastern Bloc decades before lead or bouldering competition existed internationally, producing the first world-class wall racers. The modern standardised 15-metre format \u2014 identical, homologated routes that made records comparable \u2014 was developed in the 2000s and adopted by the IFSC as the rapid-fire complement to its lead and bouldering disciplines; world records on the standard wall began falling rapidly thereafter (5.60s-era times falling first to Evgeny Vaytsekhovsky-era records and then through Aleksandra Miros\u0142aw and Iuliia Kaplina-era marks, to the current sub-5-second men's and sub-6-second women's bests). Speed entered the Olympics inside the Tokyo 2020 sport-climbing combined event, and its breakout moment came at Paris 2024, where it became a standalone Olympic event \u2014 Veddriq Leonardo of Indonesia winning the men's title, Miros\u0142aw the women's \u2014 confirming speed climbing as one of the Games' most explosive new disciplines.
