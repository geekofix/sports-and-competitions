## Basic Information

Varzesh-e Bastani (varzesh-e pahlavani, "ancient sport") is the traditional athletics of the Iranian zurkhaneh, the "house of strength": in a domed hall with a sunken octagonal pit, athletes swing heavy wooden clubs (meels), spin the bow-shaped iron kabadeh, lift the stone sang plates like shields and wrestle koshti pahlavani bouts, all to the drum beats and epic recitation of a morshed (master) (UNESCO, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The pahlevani and zurkhaneh rituals were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity on November 16, 2010, and the sport is governed internationally by the International Zurkhaneh Sports and Koshti Pahlavani Federation (IZSF), founded on October 10, 2004, which organizes world championships (International Zurkhaneh Sports Federation, n.d.; UNESCO, n.d.). Long a men's house, the zurkhaneh today also has women's sections in Iran, where female athletes train the same exercises and compete in national festivals (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

A session in the zurkhaneh is led by the morshed, who beats the zarb (clay goblet drum) and recites from the Shahnameh, setting the rhythm for the athletes in the pit; the traditional sequence runs through meel-bazi (club swinging), kabadeh spinning — the most demanding exercise — sang (stone plate) lifting and rotating, shena (zurkhaneh push-ups), charkh (spinning) and finally paired koshti pahlavani wrestling bouts as the climax (UNESCO, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Etiquette and humility rank as highly as strength: athletes greet the morshed and elders, and rank progression through the pahlevan titles is governed by traditional protocol under national federation safeguarding (Wikipedia, n.d.). In competition, athletes are judged on the execution, rhythm and completeness of the exercises and the wrestling bouts under IZSF rules, in men's and — in Iran's modern women's sections — women's categories (International Zurkhaneh Sports Federation, n.d.).

## History

The zurkhaneh tradition fuses pre-Islamic Persian warrior training with the pahlavani code of chivalry and later Sufi spirituality; the 13th-century pahlevan and poet Pouria-ye Vali is regarded as the founder who structured the modern zurkhaneh, and the gyms flourished in their present form during the Safavid and Qajar eras (Wikipedia, n.d.). UNESCO inscribed the pahlevani and zurkhaneh rituals on the intangible heritage list on November 16, 2010, and the International Zurkhaneh Sports and Koshti Pahlavani Federation — established on October 10, 2004 and today counting 72 member countries — runs the world zurkhaneh championships and works to standardize koshti pahlavani rules internationally (International Zurkhaneh Sports Federation, n.d.; UNESCO, n.d.).
