## Basic Information

Varzesh-e Bastani (ancient sport), also known as Varzesh-e Pahlavani, is the traditional athletics of the Iranian zurkhaneh (house of strength): an octagonal pit gymnasium in which athletes swing heavy meels (Persian wooden clubs), spin the bow-shaped kabadeh, lift the sang shield-plates and perform callisthenic holds, all to the beat of a drum and the declamation of Persian epic poetry.

Rooted in pre-Islamic Persian tradition fused with Sufi ethics, the pahlevani rituals of the zurkhaneh are inscribed by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage of humanity, and athletes progress through the pahlevan ranks under national federation safeguarding.

## Rules

A session, led by a morshed (master drummer and reciter) who sets rhythm and chants from the Shahnameh, moves through the traditional gymnastics sequence: the meel juggling with the heavy wooden clubs, spinning of the kabadeh, a heavy iron-core bow, the most demanding exercise, lifting and rotating of the sang stone plates like shields, push-up and whirling callisthenic elements, and paired wrestling bouts (koshti pahlavani) as the summit of the practice. Rank progression rewards years of discipline with pahlevan titles bestowed under traditional protocols: etiquette, humility and respect for elders rank as highly as strength, and federation rules preserve the standard rituals of the octagonal pit arena.

## History

Varzesh-e bastani developed in ancient Persia as a training of warriors and Pahlavani code of chivalry, preserved via the zurkhaneh institutes through the centuries, with its modern form shaped in Safavid and Qajar era gyms and a folk revival in the 20th century tied to Iranian national identity. UNESCO inscribed the pahlevani and zurkhaneh rituals on its intangible heritage list in 2010, and Iran's zurkhaneh federation and the International Zurkhaneh Sports Organization administer the sport's world zurkhaneh championships today.
