## Basic Information

Karting is motor racing in small, open-wheeled, single-seat vehicles called go-karts; the first go-kart was built in 1956 in Los Angeles by Art Ingels, with Lou Borelli, and 2026 marks the sport's 70th anniversary (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). International kart racing is governed by the FIA through its Commission Internationale de Karting (CIK-FIA), founded in Geneva in 1962, which sanctions the FIA Karting World Championship — first held in 1964 and the second-oldest FIA world title — the European Championship and the International Karting Ranking (IKR), established in 2023 (FIA Karting, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The main categories are the direct-drive classes OK (the primary class, 125 cc two-stroke, from age 14), OK-Junior, OK-N and Mini, the gearbox classes KZ and KZ2, and Superkart with 250 cc twin-cylinder engines of about 100 hp (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Drivers need an international licence — Grade E (15+), F (14–15) or G (12–14) as of 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the top of the women's side, Polish driver Klara Kowalczyk competes in the F1 Academy DyD karting programme, the leading female pathway into single-seater racing; at the 2026 FIA Karting Worlds, Dean Hoogendoorn of the Netherlands (Parolin) won the OK title at La Conca, Viktor Gustafsson of Sweden (CRG) the KZ title at Le Mans on 20 September 2026, and Joe Venant the OK-J title (FIA Karting, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Karting is regulated by the FIA International Karting Commission (CIK-FIA) through the FIA International Sporting Code and the specific karting regulations (FIA Karting, n.d.-a). A kart consists of a tubular chassis, a single-cylinder engine and four wheels; the premier direct-drive class OK uses a 125 cc two-stroke engine without gearbox, driven from age 14 (OK-J from 12), while KZ and KZ2 use 125 cc shifter engines with a six-speed gearbox, and Superkart runs 250 cc twin-cylinder engines producing about 100 hp (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Races take place on FIA-homologated circuits (typically 1,000–1,500 m long) in a format of free practice, timed qualifying, qualifying heats, a pre-final and a final, with points awarded for the final classification (FIA Karting, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). To compete internationally, drivers must hold a CIK-FIA international licence — Grade E (from 15), F (14–15) or G (12–14) as of 2026 — issued through their national sporting authority (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Karting Ranking (IKR), operated by the CIK-FIA since 2023, awards points for the eight best results of each registered driver, weighted by class, age group and level of competition, and names an IKR Driver of the Year at the FIA Prize Giving (FIA Karting, n.d.-b). Men and women race in the same classes under identical regulations; women's participation is specifically promoted through programmes such as the FIA Girls on Track and the F1 Academy (FIA Karting, n.d.-a). Rule source: FIA / CIK-FIA, https://www.fiakarting.com/ (FIA Karting, n.d.-a).

## History

The first go-kart was built in 1956 by Art Ingels, a former pilot and hot-rod builder, in a Los Angeles parking lot; with Lou Borelli he founded Go-Kart Manufacturing Co. and the new sport spread quickly across the United States, with the first race held in 1957 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Karting reached Europe in 1958–1960, and in 1962 the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) created the Commission Internationale de Karting (CIK) in Geneva as its karting commission (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first CIK World Championship was held in 1964, making it the second-oldest FIA world championship; in 1968 the CIK launched the first World Cup for juniors, and in 1981 Alain Prost — winner of the 1973 junior direct-drive world cup — became the first CIK champion to win the Formula One World Drivers' Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The homologation of chassis and engines in 1981 ushered in the "Golden Era" (1981–2006); later eras moved from 100 cc direct-drive through KF to the current OK/KZ categories, and Superkart emerged as the fastest kart class (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the icons are Susanna Raganelli of Italy, the only female FIA karting world champion (1966), and the graduates to Formula One Max Verstappen (KZ world champion 2013) and Lando Norris (KF world champion 2014) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
