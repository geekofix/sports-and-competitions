## Basic Information

Canarian wrestling (lucha canaria) is the traditional wrestling style of the Canary Islands, Spain. Two wrestlers grapple in a circle of sand called a terrero; the first whose body touches the sand with anything other than the feet loses the fall. Grips, holds and deflections are called mañas. The sport is governed by the Federación de Lucha Canaria and, at national level, integrated into the Federación Española de Lucha (FEL). Major tournaments are held in Tenerife and Gran Canaria.

## Rules

Under the official rules published by the Federación de Lucha Canaria, the bout starts standing with both wrestlers holding a maña. The objective is to throw or bring the opponent down so that any part of the body except the feet touches the sand; the wrestler who achieves this wins the fall, and the match is decided by two falls. Traditionally there was no time limit; modern regulated bouts are time-limited and supervised by referees. Dangerous holds and unsportsmanlike conduct are penalized. The rules were codified in 1872, unified in a first technical regulation in 1960, and the style was integrated into the Spanish federation structure from 1984.

## History

Lucha canaria has pre-Hispanic roots among the Guanches, the indigenous people of the Canary Islands, and developed into a regulated sport in the 19th century. Wrestling tournaments (luchas) became central to local festivities, especially the Virgen de la Candelaria celebrations in Tenerife, and famous luchadores are celebrated as local heroes. Today the sport is practiced in terros across the islands, with growing youth and women's categories, while remaining a core part of Canarian cultural identity.
