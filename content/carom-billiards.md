## Basic Information

Carom billiards is a cue sport played on a pocketless table: the cue ball must strike both object balls, and in the premier discipline — three-cushion — it must also contact at least three cushions before the second object ball (UMB, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). The family of disciplines includes three-cushion, one-cushion (cadre), straight rail (balkline) and artistic billiards, governed worldwide by the Union Mondiale de Billard (UMB), founded in Madrid on 1 June 1959 (its amateur predecessor UIFAB dated to 1923), together with the World Carom Billiards Sports (WCBS) (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 UMB World Championship in three-cushion was held in Blois, France, from 23 to 27 September 2026, with 48 players in 16 groups of three — the top two of each group advancing to the knockout phase played to 50 points — under new world-championship rules applicable from 1 January 2026; the title holder was Frédéric Caudron of Belgium, who won the 2025 edition in Antwerp in an all-Belgian final against Eddy Merckx (UMB, 2026). Recent world champions include Myung Woo Cho (2024), Phuong Vinh Bao (2023), Tayfun Taşdemir (2022) and Dick Jaspers (2021) (UMB, 2026). Women compete in their own world championship — the UMB World Championship Ladies 3-Cushion, held in Gradignan, France, from 15 to 17 September 2026, the ladies' event having run since 1999 — and the current world number one in the UMB ladies' ranking (edition 05/2026) is the Dutch player Therese Klompenhouwer, ahead of Kim Ha Eun of South Korea (UMB, 2026). On the men's side, the Dutchman Dick Jaspers (born 23 July 1965), a five-time world champion who set the record of 40 points in just four innings in 2017, remains one of the leading players; he is pictured below (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In three-cushion billiards, played on a 10-foot match table (2.84 × 1.42 m) with 61.5 mm balls of about 210 g on a heated cloth, the cue ball must hit one object ball and then at least three cushions before hitting the second object ball; each legal carom scores one point, and a foul — failing to make a legal carom — simply ends the turn without points (UMB, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). Matches at world championships have been single frames to 40 points since 2012 (from 1994 to 2011 a best-of-five set system to 45 points was used); at the 2026 championship the group stage is played to 40 and the knockout rounds to 50, with a 30-second shot clock per shot under the UMB rules effective from 1 January 2026 (UMB, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). In straight rail (balkline), the table is divided by lines and the cue ball must contact at least one cushion before the second object ball within the regulated zones; in one-cushion (cadre), the table is divided into small squares and the carom must be made with cushion contact; artistic billiards is judged on the execution of pre-set figures (UMB, n.d.). Men's and women's events follow the same rules and table specifications; women play their own UMB and CEB (Confédération Européenne de Billard) championships, and junior and senior categories are separated by age (UMB, 2026; CEB, 2026).

## History

Carom games descend from the 15th-century French outdoor game of jeu de mail and spread to indoor pocketless tables in the 18th and 19th centuries; straight rail (balkline) was the dominant form in the 1800s, and three-cushion was codified in the late 19th century (Wikipedia, n.d.). The amateur world body UIFAB was founded in 1923, and the professional UMB was founded on 1 June 1959 in Madrid; the UMB World Three-cushion Championship has been held since 1928 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Three-Cushion World Cup circuit was founded in 1985 by the German patron Werner Bayer and has run since 1986 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the icons of the sport is the American Willie Hoppe (1887–1959), the dominant straight-rail champion of the 1920s–1940s, pictured below with Kinrey Matsuyama in 1927; other legends include the Belgian Raymond Ceulemans, record holder with 21 world titles, the Swede Torbjörn Blomdahl with six, and Dick Jaspers with five; the most successful female player of all time is the Dutchwoman Therese Klompenhouwer, a multiple ladies' world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.).
