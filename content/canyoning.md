## Basic Information

Canyoning — called *canyoneering* in the United States and *kloofing* in South Africa — is the sport of traveling downstream through a mountain gorge on foot and in the water, combining hiking, swimming, climbing, rappelling (abseiling), jumping into plunge pools and sliding down natural waterslides (Wikipedia, n.d.). Its heartland is France — especially the Verdon Gorge — together with Spain's Sierra de Guara and the Alpine countries, and it is now practiced on every continent, from Madeira's levadas to the canyons of New Zealand (FFME, n.d.). In France the sport is organized by the FFME (Fédération Française de la Montagne et de l'Escalade) through its canyon school, alongside the canyon commission of the French caving federation (FFS) (FFME, n.d.). Internationally, the Fédération Internationale de Canyonisme (FIC) coordinates the sport and runs the RIC (Rassemblement International de Canyoning) meeting series: the VALLIS/RIC 2026, the largest world canyoning gathering of 2026, brought about 400 participants from 25–26 nations to Arouca and the Serra da Freita in Portugal from 17 to 21 June 2026, organized by ECDC Portugal, the Municipality of Arouca, the Portuguese camping and mountaineering federation and the FIC; the 2027 RIC follows in Elbasan, Albania (FIC, n.d.; O Jogo, 2026). Professional guides are certified worldwide through ICOpro, the global benchmark for canyoning certification (ICOpro, n.d.). Two rappels are pictured below.

Women and men canyon in the same groups, and women compete at the highest level of the sport.

## Rules

There is no single world rulebook; in France the FFME publishes federal rules, safety guidance and equipment standards, and routes are graded on the French scale — a "v" grade for vertical difficulty (rappels), an "a" grade for aquatic difficulty and an "e" grade for engagement/commitment, for example v3 a3 II for a moderate, mostly dry canyon (FFME, n.d.). Standard equipment is a full wetsuit, a climbing helmet (EN 12492), a harness, canyoning shoes with grippy soles and semi-static ropes of 8.5–9.5 mm, plus a throw bag for rescue; groups descend together under a qualified leader, use releasable rappel systems and never rappel into water that has not been checked (FFME, n.d.). Jumps are allowed only into deep pools verified by the group, and environmental rules — staying on marked trails, leaving no trace and respecting other parties — are part of the federal regulations (FFME, n.d.). In competitive canyon racing (descente chronométrée), teams are ranked by total time on a marked course. The sport is practiced by women and men at every level, and ICOpro certification (Canyoneer levels 1–3 and instructor grades, e.g., autonomous on v3 a3 II canyons at Canyoneer 3) is open to all genders and sets the international safety standard for guiding (ICOpro, n.d.).

## History

Canyoning grew out of caving and mountaineering in mid-20th-century France, where the big Alpine and Pyrenean gorges were first descended by mixed caving–climbing teams; from the 1980s the sport spread rapidly through the Sierra de Guara in Spain, the Verdon in France and the canyons of Madeira, becoming a mass outdoor activity (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FFME's canyon school formalized guide training and the route-grading scale that is still used across Europe (FFME, n.d.). The FIC's history begins with the first international canyon festival, named RIC (Rassemblement International de Canyon); the RIC series has since rotated through Switzerland, Italy, Réunion and Portugal, with the 2026 edition in Arouca drawing around 400 participants from 25 nationalities and the 2027 edition planned for Elbasan, Albania (FIC, n.d.; O Jogo, 2026). Women now work as certified ICOpro guides, route setters and competitors at every level, trained and assessed through the same international pathway as men (ICOpro, n.d.).
