## Basic Information

Mahjong is a tile-based game for four players, originating in 19th-century China, in which players draw and discard tiles to complete a hand of four sets (chi, pon, kan) plus a pair; a complete hand wins the round and scores points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The most widely played competitive variant is riichi mahjong (Japanese mahjong), which adds a declared riichi (one tile away from winning) and dora bonus tiles (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). International competition is organized under several rule sets: the Mahjong Competition Rules (MCR) used at the World Mahjong Championship (the 8th edition is scheduled for 18–22 October 2026 in Kunming, China), the European Mahjong Association (EMA, founded in June 2005 in Nijmegen by Martin Rep), and the World Riichi Championship (WRC, founded in 2014 by the brothers Quentin and Valérian Thomas of Tri Nitro Tiles; the 4th edition was held in Tokyo in July 2025, with the next scheduled for 2028 in New York) (European Mahjong Association, n.d.; World Riichi Championship, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women compete at the highest level in every rule set — Ildikó Hargitai of Hungary became the first female European champion at the 2011 OEMC in Venice, and European riichi champions include Maude Grimberg of France, Désirée Heemskerk of the Netherlands and Anastasiia Veremenko of Ukraine (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

## Rules

A standard set has 136–144 tiles: three suits of 1–9 (characters, bamboo, dots), four winds and three dragons, plus optional flowers and seasons (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Players sit on four sides of a square table; 13 tiles are dealt to each player (14 to the dealer), and play proceeds clockwise with each player drawing one tile from the wall and discarding one, declaring chi (sequence), pon (triplet) or kan (quad) from discards when applicable (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A hand wins when it contains four sets plus one pair; in riichi mahjong the player must also have declared riichi or be tenpai (one tile away) to win on a discard, and must have at least one yaku (scoring pattern) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Scoring in MCR is based on points and limit hands (up to 8 points minimum to win a hand), while riichi uses mangan and han/fu scoring with dora adding value (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At tournaments, players rotate seats each round and standings are decided by session points; team events add the four players' scores (European Mahjong Association, n.d.). There are no separate men's and women's rules — all championships are open to all genders, and women have won the open European championship outright (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

## History

Mahjong developed in China in the mid-to-late 19th century from earlier card games such as madiao, and it spread worldwide in the early 20th century, with regional variants emerging in Japan (riichi), America (American mahjong with jokers) and Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first World Mahjong Championship under MCR rules was held in Tokyo in 2002, won by Japan's Masato Chiba, and the EMA was established at the first Open European Mahjong Championship in Nijmegen in June 2005, promoted by Dutch player Martin Rep (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The World Riichi Championship began in 2014 in Puteaux, near Paris, founded by Quentin and Valérian Thomas (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the women's side, Ildikó Hargitai's 2011 OEMC victory in Venice made her the first female European champion, and the European riichi titles have since been won by several women, including Maude Grimberg, Désirée Heemskerk and Anastasiia Veremenko (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
