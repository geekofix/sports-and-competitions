## Basic Information

Trampoline gymnastics is the FIG-governed Olympic discipline performed on the competitive trampoline: athletes bounce to heights of up to roughly 10 metres, executing routines of 10 continuous skills (somersaults and twists in prescribed shapes) between upright takeoff and landing. Contested at the Olympic Games since Sydney 2000 in individual competition, and with synchronized trampoline and the companion disciplines of tumbling and double mini trampoline also part of the FIG world championship programme, it is gymnastics' airborne branch.

## Rules

Individual routines are set routines (a compulsory element list in qualification) and voluntary routines: each routine consists of 10 consecutive elements without pause, each skill's difficulty (number of somersaults and twists) entering the difficulty score, while execution judges deduct for faults such as leg separation, bent knees, poor shape or travel toward the frame. Time of flight is measured electronically in the Olympic format (longer flight pushing scores upward all else equal), routines must finish on the feet, and interrupting a routine by touching the frame, the safety platform or spotting ends the skill value. Judging panels run per FIG's trampoline code of points, and world rankings feed quota places to the Olympic Games.

## History

The trampoline apparatus was invented in the 1930s by George Nissen and Larry Griswold, and competitive trampolining organized internationally in the postwar decades: the International Trampoline Federation staged the first world championships in 1964, the sport's technical sophistication grew through the era of acrobatic pioneers, and the FIG absorbed the discipline in 1999 (bringing tumbling and double mini trampoline with it). Olympic status arrived at Sydney 2000 with individual titles for men and women, and the discipline remains at gymnastics' elite edge, with strong programs in the traditional strongholds (China, Russia's successor states, Japan and Canada among them) and world championships held annually alongside its sister disciplines.
