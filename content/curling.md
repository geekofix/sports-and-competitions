## Basic Information

Curling is a precision team sport played on ice in which two teams of four slide granite stones toward a circular target (the house); teammates sweep the ice in front of the moving stone to control its speed and curl. It is governed by the World Curling Federation (WCF), which runs the World Men's, Women's and Mixed championships, the Olympic tournament and the World Cup circuit. Curling has been an Olympic sport since Nagano 1998 (demonstration in 1992).

## Rules

Under the World Curling rules, each team delivers eight stones per end (a round), alternating shots, and only stones resting in the house count: after each end, the team whose stone is closest to the button (center) scores one point per stone closer than the opponent's best. Stones must cross the hog line to be in play and must stop before the back line; the skip directs play and stands at the far end while the vice sweeps. Sweeping lowers friction and can make the stone travel farther and curl less. If the score is tied after the regular ends (ten at championships), an extra end is played; the team with the hammer (last stone) has the advantage.

## History

Curling originated in 16th-century Scotland, where games were played on frozen lochs with stones from riverbeds; the first written rules date to 1838 in Paisley, Scotland. The Royal Caledonian Curling Club was founded in 1838. The sport spread to Canada and the US with Scottish emigrants, and the first Olympic demonstration was in 1924 and 1988/1992 before full medal status in Nagano 1998. Today the WCF has over 70 member associations, and stars include Canada's Kevin Martin and Sweden's Niklas Edin.
