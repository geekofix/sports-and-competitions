## Basic Information

Curling is a precision team sport played on ice in which two teams of four slide granite stones toward a circular target, the house, while teammates sweep the ice in front of a moving stone to control its speed and curl (World Curling Federation [WCF], n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the World Curling Federation (WCF), founded in 1966, which runs the World Men's, Women's, Mixed Doubles and Wheelchair championships, the World Cup circuit and the Olympic tournament (WCF, n.d.-b). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, held at the Cortina Olympic Stadium from 4 to 22 February 2026, Sweden's Anna Hasselborg skipped the women's team to gold, Great Britain's Bruce Mouat took the men's gold, and the Swedish siblings Jenny and Rasmus Wranå won mixed doubles gold, beating the United States in the final — the first Olympic mixed-doubles medal ever for the USA — while Italy's Stefania Constantini and Amos Mosaner took bronze (WCF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the 2026 World Championships, Switzerland's Xenia Schwaller won the 47th women's title at the BKT Canadian Tire World Women's Curling Championship in Calgary, 14–22 March 2026, and the 67th men's edition ran at the LGT Arena in Ogden, Utah, 27 March to 5 April 2026 (WCF, n.d.-b). Curling has been a full Olympic medal sport since Nagano 1998 (Olympics.com, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in women's curling — Olympic champion Anna Hasselborg (Sweden) and Olympic mixed-doubles bronze medalist Stefania Constantini (Italy), pictured below, and — in men's curling — Olympic champion Bruce Mouat (Great Britain) and Olympic mixed-doubles champion Rasmus Wranå (Sweden) (WCF, n.d.-b) (World Curling Federation, n.d.-a) (World Curling Federation, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Curling is played under the WCF Rules of Curling and Rules of Curling Championships, the official rulebook (WCF, n.d.-a). The sheet is 45.72 m long and 4.42–5.00 m wide, marked with the house (12 ft / 3.66 m diameter rings with the button at the center), the tee line, the hog line, the back line and the hack; each team delivers eight stones per end, alternating shots, and only stones resting in the house count — after each end the team whose stone lies closest to the button scores one point for each of its stones closer than the opponent's best (WCF, n.d.-a). A stone must cross the hog line to remain in play and must stop in front of the back line; the team with the last stone of the end holds the hammer, an advantage offset by the alternating delivery order (WCF, n.d.-a). Stones are made of granite from Ailsa Craig, Scotland, weighing 17.24–19.96 kg (38–44 lb) with a handle; sweeping with brooms lowers friction and makes the stone travel farther and curl less (WCF, n.d.-a). Championship games consist of ten ends with 38 minutes of thinking time per team plus one 60-second timeout; a tied game goes to an extra end (WCF, n.d.-a). In mixed doubles there are six stones per team, eight ends and 22 minutes of thinking time (WCF, n.d.-a). Violations — a hog-line touch, a burned stone (touched between hog line and tee line), or an out-of-turn play — result in the offending stone being removed or the end being adjusted, and the five-rock free-guard-zone rule protects placed stones in championship play (WCF, n.d.-a). Men's and women's competitions use identical rules and equipment; wheelchair curling is a mixed-team discipline played with six stones per side and no sweeping (WCF, n.d.-a).

## History

Curling originated in sixteenth-century Scotland, where games were played on frozen lochs with riverbed stones, and the first written rules were drawn up in Paisley in 1838; the Royal Caledonian Curling Club, founded the same year in Perth, is regarded as the grandfather of the sport and codified the early rules (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scottish emigrants carried the game to Canada and the United States, and it was demonstrated at the Olympics in 1924 and again in 1988 and 1992 before gaining full medal status at Nagano 1998 (Olympics.com, n.d.). The International Curling Federation was founded in 1966 in Perth and became the World Curling Federation in 1991 (WCF, n.d.-b). Icons of the game include Canada's Kevin Martin, Olympic gold medallist in 2010, Sweden's Niklas Edin, Olympic champion in 2022 and multiple world champion, and on the women's side Sweden's Anette Norberg, who won Olympic gold in 2006 and 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
