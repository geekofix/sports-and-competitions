## Basic Information

Tractor pulling (power pulling) is motorsport's heaviest drag race: a machine (a tractor or purpose-built pulling vehicle) drags a weight-transfer sled down a dirt track, with the sled's weight box moving forward to make the pull progressively harder, and the winner is the machine that pulls the sled farthest. From American farm-state fairs to European arenas, it crowns the world's most powerful tractors, trucks and even aircraft engines on wheeled frames.

## Rules

The pulling sled is the sport's defining apparatus: as the pull begins, a weight box rides forward on the sled's rails, multiplying the load on the pulling machine, and the course measures 100 metres (330 feet) with the distance pulled deciding placing among all entrants who do not reach the full pull (a full pull triggers a pull-off between the finalists). Classes divide the sport by machine type and power source (farm tractors, modifieds with multiple engines, super-stock diesels, two-wheel and four-wheel-drive trucks, and minis with motorcycle or car engines), each with strict rules on weight limits, engine capacity and fuels, plus safety requirements (kill switches, hitch heights and containment) and officials flagging each run.

## History

The sport grew from early twentieth-century American farm rivalry when horses and tractors competed at county fairs, formalized in the late 1960s with the founding of the National Tractor Pullers Association and the standard weight-transfer sled. Europe built its own strongholds (the Netherlands and Scandinavia above all) and developed the sport indoors with arena-scale machinery, and pulling is now organized worldwide with world and European championships in multiple classes. The top modified tractors run multiple thundering engines and thousands of horsepower, and each summer's pulling season draws tens of thousands of spectators at fairs across America and Europe.
