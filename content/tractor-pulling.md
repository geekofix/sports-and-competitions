## Basic Information

Tractor pulling (power pulling) is a motorsport in which antique, stock or heavily modified tractors — and in the top classes multi-engine machines with up to around 14,000 hp — pull a weighted sled along a 100 m (330 ft), 11 m-wide dirt track, the winner being the vehicle that pulls it farthest (Wikipedia, n.d.). In the United States the sport is sanctioned by the National Tractor Pullers Association (NTPA, founded 1969), whose flagship is the Grand National Championships at Bowling Green, Ohio; in Europe the European Tractor Pulling Committee (ETPC) runs the Eurocup and European Championship, and the Lucas Oil Pro Pulling League operates as a professional North American circuit (National Tractor Pullers Association, n.d.; European Tractor Pulling Committee, n.d.). Women compete in the same classes alongside men, and the current NTPA and ETPC champions head their respective divisions (National Tractor Pullers Association, n.d.).

## Rules

The track is 100 m long and 11 m wide; the sled starts with its weight box over the rear axle line and, as the tractor moves forward, a weight is winched forward, progressively loading the sled until the tractor can go no further — the pull is measured to the nearest inch and a "full pull" is any pull past the 100 m mark (Wikipedia, n.d.). If two or more tractors complete a full pull, they return for a pull-off, again over the full distance with the weight box starting farther forward (Wikipedia, n.d.). Classes are defined by weight and engine rules: farm-stock tractors near production specification, Super Stock and Pro Stock (up to 11,100 cm³ with unrestricted turbochargers), Limited Pro Stock (10,500 cm³ / 640 cu in, 100 mm turbo limit, 4,200–4,300 kg), Mini Modified (9,420 cm³ with a 14-71 supercharger), Heavy Modified multi-engine machines, and Super Semi truck classes; the ETPC runs Light Modified, Heavy Modified, Mini Modified, Super Stock, Pro Stock and 3.6 t / 4.5 t Super Sport classes (Wikipedia, n.d.; European Tractor Pulling Committee, n.d.). Safety rules require roll cages, kill switches, fire extinguishers and driver restraints (National Tractor Pullers Association, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same classes under identical rules (National Tractor Pullers Association, n.d.).

## History

The sport began with stone boats: the first contests in the late 1920s — commonly dated to 1929 — saw horses and tractors dragging weighted sleds at county fairs (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first national-level championships were organized in 1967 by the Northwestern Ohio Tractor Pullers Association, and in 1969 representatives of eight states — Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Ohio and Pennsylvania — founded the NTPA in Columbus, Ohio to unify rules and classes, with the early motto "Pull on Sunday, plow on Monday" (Wikipedia, n.d.; National Tractor Pullers Association, n.d.). Since 1987 the NTPA has been managed by World Pulling International (WPI), which also handles its marketing; the NTPA's Grand National Championships at the Wood County Fairgrounds in Bowling Green, Ohio, became the unofficial world championship of the sport (National Tractor Pullers Association, n.d.). Engine displacement grew from 7,400 cm³ to over 11,000 cm³ until the NTPA capped it at 10,700 cm³ with natural aspiration in 1989, before superchargers and turbochargers were reintroduced and multi-engine machines reached roughly 14,000 hp (Wikipedia, n.d.). The ETPC brought the sport to a European championship level, with the 2025 Eurocup classes from Light Modified to 4.5 t Super Sport (European Tractor Pulling Committee, n.d.). Iconic machines include the "Buckeye Bulldog" Super Semi of J.R. Collins (d. 2022), a five-time NTPA Super Semi champion (Wikipedia, n.d.).
