## Basic Information

AirBadminton is the outdoor variant of badminton launched by the Badminton World Federation (BWF) together with HSBC on May 13, 2019, when BWF president Poul-Erik Høyer Larsen and Chinese star Dong Jiong unveiled the new AirShuttle shuttlecock outside the Tianhe Gymnasium in Guangzhou, China (Badminton World Federation [BWF], n.d.-a). The AirShuttle is designed to resist wind — rallies continue in winds up to 12 km/h — and can be played with normal badminton rackets on grass, sand, hard courts, beaches or streets, which makes the game playable almost anywhere (BWF, n.d.-b). Since 2025 the BWF has staged an inaugural AirBadminton World Cup, continental championships followed in 2026 — Africa in Entebbe, Uganda (September 16–19, 14 countries, more than 120 athletes) and Oceania in Brisbane (August 28–30) — and the sport will make its debut at the World Games 2029 in Karlsruhe, Germany (BWF, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in the same singles, doubles and mixed events (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-a) (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-b) (Badminton World Federation, n.d.-c).

## Rules

AirBadminton follows the Laws of Badminton — rally-point scoring to 21, win by two, capped at 30, best of three games — with outdoor adaptations. The court can be set up on any flat surface and its dimensions adjusted to the available space, with a portable net; the BWF provides free court-marking templates and a free scoring app. One distinctive outdoor rule is the dead zone: a player may not step into the opponent's dead zone to play the shuttle, but may enter it after playing the shot. Singles and doubles are both played, and the AirShuttle — with its nylon skirt — flies stably in light wind where a feather shuttle would not (BWF, n.d.-b).


## History

The BWF developed AirBadminton between 2018 and 2019 as part of its strategy to grow participation beyond traditional badminton halls, and launched it worldwide in Guangzhou in May 2019. The inaugural BWF AirBadminton World Cup was held in 2025, the sport was included in the ANOC World Beach Games program, and in September 2026 its debut at the World Games 2029 in Karlsruhe was announced (BWF, n.d.-a).
