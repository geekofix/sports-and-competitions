## Basic Information

Valencian pilota (pilota valenciana) is the traditional handball of the Valencian Community in Spain, in which players called pilotaris strike a small leather ball — vaqueta, badana or tec — against a wall or over a net, either bare-handed or with a glove (Wikipedia, n.d.-e). The sport is governed regionally by the Federació de Pilota Valenciana (FPV), created in 1984, which regulates the modalities and organises the competitions — its 2026 calendar includes the JECV titles in escala i corda, raspall, galotxa, frontó, frare, one wall and llargues, plus the Copa Raspall; internationally the FPV has been a member of the International Ball Game Confederation (CIJP) since 1992, which runs the annual International Championships (Federació de Pilota Valenciana, n.d.-a; Federació de Pilota Valenciana, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-f). Valencian pilota is the Valencian handball of the pelota/wall family — [Basque Pelota](basque-pelota.html), [Balle Pelote](balle-pelote.html), [Frontenis](frontenis.html), [Jai Alai](jai-alai.html), [Jeu Provençal](jeu-provencal.html) and the other games listed under Important Links.

Only two modalities are played professionally — escala i corda and raspall, with 52 professional pilotaris across 17 trinquets — and the women's game has grown strongly: the 2026 calendar includes dedicated women's raspall and escala i corda competitions (Copa Caixa Popular Raspall Femení, JECV femení), with leading pilotaris such as Ana, Natalia, Irene, Marina and Mar Alfonso contesting the raspall femení circuit, while on the men's side stars such as Álvaro, Sarasol II and Waldo headline the escala i corda leagues (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Federació de Pilota Valenciana, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-f).

## Rules

The direct games are played face-to-face: in escala i corda — the most prestigious variant — two teams play in a trinquet divided by a 1.8 m net (the corda) and a partida is won at 60 points (tants), with points also scored by landing the ball in special marked areas such as the galeries; raspall is played in a trinquet without a net, the ball may bounce as often as needed and games are played to 40 points, and because players must constantly bend low it is considered the hardest variant (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Llargues, said to be the oldest form, is played only in regular streets with no net: each point is won twice, the first time the ball is stopped a ratlla (line) is marked and the second time it is thrown over that line the sender scores; it is played to 50 points or 10 games (jocs) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Other variants include galotxa (two nets, played in streets using their irregularities, typically 14 jocs), the small galotxetes of the Vinalopó, and the indirect wall games frontó and frare derived from Basque pelota; balls are served alternately and in escala i corda and raspall must be struck before the second bounce (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Federació de Pilota Valenciana, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in separate categories in all professional modalities, and an adapted raspall is played from wheelchairs (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

## History

Pilota has been played in the streets of València for centuries — so much so that on 14 June 1391 the city council banned it inside the city walls, a date now commemorated annually as Pilota Day (Dia de la Pilota Valenciana) on 14 June (Wikipedia, n.d.-f; Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

After the Confederación Española de Pelota Vasca was created in 1925 and the Valencian federation was folded into the Spanish body — whose Madrid-appointed presidents knew little of the hand-played game — a protest by former professional player Enrique Menéndez led to the independent Federació de Pilota Valenciana in 1984; among the most celebrated retired pilotaris are Genovés I, Juliet d'Alginet, Nel de Murla and Sarasol I (Wikipedia, n.d.-f; Wikipedia, n.d.-e).
