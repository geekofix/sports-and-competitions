## Basic Information

Coffin wars is a novelty combat sport in which two fighters, placed inside a coffin in a grappling position, try to escape it — the first to get out wins the round; it is staged as refereed, medically supervised entertainment, above all at Professional League Network (PLN) events in the United States in the 2020s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A bout consists of two rounds; if the rounds split one apiece, a third round is fought with the fighters locked on their knees, and the referee and medical staff are present throughout (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The name also refers to the related folk spectacle of coffin racing: the Emma Crawford Memorial Coffin Races in Manitou Springs, Colorado, held annually since 1994, in which teams of five — a "corpse" named Emma plus four costumed mourners — race 195-yard heats downhill, judged on fastest time and best costumes, with the Coffin Cup contested against Nederland, Colorado's Frozen Dead Guy Days, since 2002 (Coffin Race, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Coffin wars and coffin racing are two faces of the same American coffin-themed entertainment tradition; both are open to men and women — women's bouts and mixed crews compete — and are organized by event promoters rather than a sport federation (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Emma Crawford's headstone in Crystal Valley Cemetery, Manitou Springs — pictured in the History section — is the origin of the race tradition.

 Within the folk and festival games family on this site, its closest relatives are [Cheese Rolling](cheese-rolling.html), [Egg Tapping](egg-tapping.html), [Shovel Racing](shovel-racing.html), [Lawn Mower Racing](lawn-mower-racing.html), [Bed Racing](bed-racing.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

In a coffin-wars bout, two competitors are placed inside a coffin-sized box in a grappling hold and released on the referee's signal; the first fighter to free himself or herself and get out of the coffin wins the round, and a round ends in a draw only if neither escapes within the time limit (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Matches are best-of-two rounds, with a decisive third round fought from a knees-locked position if the first two rounds split; the referee can stop the bout at any time and medical staff must be ringside — the format is explicitly staged as safe, refereed entertainment, not a no-rules fight (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the coffin-racing variant, teams of five carry or push their "Emma" in a coffin over a 195-yard course; heats are timed, and the overall winner is decided by fastest times plus costume and showmanship points, with men's, women's and mixed team categories at the major events (Coffin Race, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There is no world federation and no unified rulebook: coffin wars follows the rules of the promoting event (PLN shows in the 2020s), and coffin racing follows the rules of each race organizer, coordinated through coffinrace.com (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Gender and age differences are organizational: women's and men's bouts/teams are classified separately at PLN events and at the Emma Crawford races, and youth races exist at some festivals (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The racing tradition comes from Emma Crawford, a Manitou Springs, Colorado, resident who died in 1894 and was buried in the Crystal Valley Cemetery in a coffin on the steep hillside above the town; legend says friends and family had to carry her coffin back up the hill several times a year as it slid down in the thawing snow, and in 1994 the town founded the Emma Crawford Memorial Coffin Races in her honor — her headstone is pictured below (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The races grew into a major autumn festival, and since 2002 Manitou Springs has contested the Coffin Cup against Nederland, Colorado, home of Frozen Dead Guy Days (Coffin Race, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The combat variant — coffin wars — emerged in the 2020s as a novelty bout format at PLN (Professional League Network) events in the United States, where fighters escape from coffins in staged, refereed rounds; it was covered by outlets such as MiddleEasy in 2024 as part of the growing "slap fighting"-style entertainment scene (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women participate in both forms: female mourners' crews race at Manitou Springs, and women's coffin-wars bouts have been staged at PLN shows (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). No single founder exists for the combat variant; the race tradition's figure is Emma Crawford herself (1850s–1894) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
