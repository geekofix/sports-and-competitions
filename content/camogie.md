## Basic Information

Camogie is the women's variant of the Irish stick-and-ball field game *hurling*, played by around 100,000 women in Ireland and worldwide, largely among Irish communities, and governed by the Camogie Association (An Cumann Camógaíochta) within the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) (The Camogie Association, n.d.). The 2026 Glen Dimplex All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship — the premier inter-county competition, known for sponsorship reasons as the Glen Dimplex Senior All-Ireland Championship — was won by Cork, who defeated Galway 2-14 to 0-19 in the final at Croke Park in August 2026 before a record final crowd of 33,419; second-half goals from Saoirse McCarthy and Orlaith Cahalane sealed Cork's record 31st title, their first since 2022 (GAA, 2026; RTÉ Sport, 2026). Ten county teams contested the 2026 senior championship: Clare, Cork, Dublin, Galway, Kilkenny, Limerick, Offaly, Tipperary, Waterford and Wexford. Camogie is recognized by UNESCO as an element of Ireland's intangible cultural heritage (The Camogie Association, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the official rules of play of the Camogie Association, a senior inter-county match is played by 15 players a side on a GAA field of 130–145 m (142–159 yd) in length and 80–90 m (87–98 yd) in width, for 60 minutes divided into two halves of 30 minutes — shorter than the 70 minutes of senior hurling (The Camogie Association, n.d.). Teams score by hitting the sliotar (ball) between the posts: a goal is worth 3 points and a point over the crossbar 1 point. Camogie uses a smaller size-4 sliotar than hurling's size-5, and the metal band on the camogie stick (hurley) must be covered with tape. Distinctive rule differences from hurling include a 45 m free puck for the defending team when the ball is hit wide (65 m in hurling) and the goalkeeper pucking out from the 13 m line after conceding a goal (in hurling, from the end line). Ties in championship matches are resolved by multiple 2×10-minute sudden-death periods of extra time, the first team to score winning; goal-line decisions are made by the referee and umpires. Fouls are penalized with frees, yellow and red cards under the GAA disciplinary code, and striking, catching the ball by hand (other than the goalkeeper within her own area) and dangerous play are prohibited (The Camogie Association, n.d.; GAA, n.d.). Matches range from senior inter-county and club championships down to under-14, under-16, minor and junior grades, and there are separate competitions for third-level (Ashbourne Cup) and club teams (All-Ireland Senior Club Camogie Championship) (The Camogie Association, n.d.).

## History

Camogie shares its roots with hurling, one of the world's oldest field games, and organized women's play developed from the late 19th century; the Camogie Association was founded in 1904 (some sources give 1905) and the first All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship was played in 1932, won by Dublin, who dominated the early decades with 26 titles in all (The Camogie Association, n.d.; Moran, 2011). The legendary Wexford dual player Lil Kirby — pictured with her camogie stick in 1934 — was one of the greatest exponents of both codes in the 1930s and 1940s. In 1999 camogie moved to the full GAA field size and 15-a-side, adopting the standard butterfly formation used in hurling. Cork have been the dominant force of the 21st century, winning their 31st title in 2026 after earlier victories including 2016, 2020 and 2022, with Galway their chief modern rivals (three consecutive finals between them from 2024 to 2026) (GAA, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.).
