## Basic Information

Cheerleading is the team sport of stunts, pyramids, tumbling, jumps and dance performed on a sprung mat, originally developed to lead crowds at American football games and now run as a judged sport with routines of about 2 minutes 30 seconds scored on a 100-point system (International Cheer Union, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Cheer Union, founded in 2004, which received full IOC recognition in 2021 and counts 119 member national federations (2023); cheerleading is not yet on the Olympic programme (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; International Cheer Union, n.d.-e). The 2026 ICU Junior World & World Cheerleading Championships were held in Orlando, USA, on 26–27 April 2026, and the 2026 ICU Cheerleading World Cup followed in Athens, Greece, on 20–22 November 2026 (International Cheer Union, n.d.-b; International Cheer Union, n.d.-a).

Among the teams defining the 2026 season, Finland's cheerleading world champion Tissari da Costa Cristian (pictured at the 2025 ICU Worlds) represents the Nordic elite alongside the perennial powerhouses of the USA and the Philippines, whose national team Cheer Pilipinas has medalled at the ICU Worlds since 2014; the 2019 ICU World Cheerleading Worlds gold medalists show the depth of the sport (International Cheer Union, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). Divisions range from school and club teams to all-star elite squads, with all-girl and co-ed categories — the women of the sport are its biggest stars at every level.

## Rules

At ICU events teams perform on a 9 × 9 m foam cheerleading mat; the routine is at most 2:30 and combines a cheer (worth 10 of the 100 points at the World Cup), stunts, pyramids, tumbling, jumps and dance, judged by a panel on a 100-point scoresheet (International Cheer Union, n.d.-a; International Cheer Union, n.d.-d). Divisions include Team Cheer from Beginner (L0) to Premier (L6) levels, Partner Stunt, Group Stunt, Game Day, and Performance Cheer styles — pom, hip hop, jazz and high kick — plus doubles, with age groups from youth (12–14) through junior and senior to masters, and adaptive abilities divisions (International Cheer Union, n.d.-b; International Cheer Union, n.d.-e).

Safety rules are strict: no tumbling over, under or through another stunt, pyramid or prop; bases may not support a top person while inverted or in a backbend; pyramid transitions require controlled dismounts; spotters are required for extended stunts, and athletes must meet the age criteria of their division (International Cheer Union, n.d.-d). Men's and women's teams compete in separate all-girl/all-boy and co-ed categories under identical rules and scoring (International Cheer Union, n.d.-e).

## History

Organized cheerleading began in the United States in the 1880s–1890s: in 1884 Princeton students led yells, and in November 1898 Johnny Campbell of the University of Minnesota led the crowd in the first organized “Rah! Rah! Rah!” — the birth of cheerleading (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women joined in the 1920s, and Lawrence “Herkie” Herkimer of Minnesota invented the herkie jump in the 1930s–40s and founded the Universal Cheerleaders Association (UCA) in 1948; competitive cheerleading with score sheets emerged in the 1980s, and the International Cheer Union was founded in 2004 and recognized by the IOC in 2021 (International Cheer Union, n.d.-e; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). The Miami-Wilmington football game cheerleader of 1913, pictured, shows the early sideline tradition (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
