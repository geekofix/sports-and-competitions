## Basic Information

Bartitsu is the eclectic self-defence method developed in England 1898–1902 by engineer Edward William Barton-Wright (1860–1951), combining Shinden Fudo Ryu jujutsu and Kodokan judo with British boxing (Queensberry rules), French savate, Swiss schwingen and Pierre Vigny's cane (la canne) fighting — the first Western system to deliberately mix Asian and European arts for civilian street defence. The name is a portmanteau of "Barton" and "jujitsu", defined by its creator as "self defence in all its forms". It went dormant after c. 1905 and has been revived since 2002 (Bartitsu Society). Men and women trained from the outset — the club offered women's self-defence classes. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Judo](judo.html), [Boxing](boxing.html), [Fencing](fencing.html), [Ninjutsu](ninjutsu.html), [Aikido](aikido.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Bartitsu Society, n.d.).

## Rules

Bartitsu had no sport rule set — it was a self-defence curriculum. Barton-Wright's 1902 "Great Anglo-Japanese Tournament" challenge matches pitted his students (Yukio Tani, Sadakazu Uyenishi, Armand Cherpillod) against experts of other styles under common rules — anticipating MMA by a century. Modern revival (Bartitsu Society, canonical 1899–1902 syllabus vs. neo-Bartitsu) trains kata, sparring and stick fighting; graded ranks follow the koryu/dan tradition (Bartitsu Society, n.d.).

## History

Barton-Wright, born in India, studied jujutsu and judo in Japan in the 1890s (among the first Europeans to do so), returned to London in 1898 and publicized his "New Art of Self Defence" in Pearson's Magazine (1899–1901). The Bartitsu Academy of Arms and Physical Culture ("Bartitsu Club") opened at 67b Shaftesbury Avenue, Soho, in 1899, with William Grenfell (Baron Desborough) and Sir Cosmo Duff Gordon among early patrons; it closed around 1905. Arthur Conan Doyle immortalized a fictionalized version as "baritsu" in the 1903 Sherlock Holmes story The Adventure of the Empty House. Women's self-defence pioneer Edith Garrud (a suffragette bodyguard trainer) learned jujitsu from Bartitsu Club instructors (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
