## Basic Information

The Olympic Esports Games are a planned international multi-esport event run by the International Olympic Committee (IOC), in which multiple virtual sports and video games come together at a single venue in the style of a multi-sport Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The titles on the programme are selected in connection with the international federations of traditional Olympic sports, so the competition mixes sport-simulation games such as cycling, sailing and shooting with virtual-world versions of real sports (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). The 142nd IOC Session approved the creation of the Games by a unanimous vote on 23 July 2024, and on 11 February 2025 the IOC announced that the inaugural edition would be held in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, in 2027, with the Road to the Games qualification pathway already starting in 2025 (International Olympic Committee, 2025) (Forbes, 2025). The Games are intended to be staged every two years between the Summer and Winter Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On 30 October 2025 the IOC announced that it would organize the Games on its own after the partnership with the Saudi Arabian Olympic Committee and the Esports World Cup Foundation broke down; as of May 2026 the IOC Esports Commission had been disbanded and planning for the inaugural Games was paused, leaving the 2027 Riyadh edition’s future uncertain (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Like all IOC competitions, the Games are open to all genders, with men and women competing together in the same events (International Olympic Committee, n.d.).

The Olympic Esports Games are the culmination of a long rapprochement between the Olympic Movement and competitive gaming: esports were first included at the 2007 Asian Indoor Games, became a medal event at the Asian Games from 2022 and at the Southeast Asian Games from 2019, and the 2017 Olympic Summit in Lausanne agreed that competitive gaming could be considered a sporting activity provided it does not infringe Olympic values (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Because traditional esport staples such as first-person shooters were considered incompatible with those values, the IOC’s own events deliberately feature sport-linked titles instead; exhibition events at the 2023 Singapore edition included Rocket League, the Street Fighter series and NBA 2K (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The immediate predecessor of the Games was the Olympic Esports Series: after an online-only Olympic Virtual Series in 2021, the inaugural Olympic Esports Week was held in Singapore in June 2023, where the top three in each event received gold-, silver- and bronze-coloured trophies instead of medals; no edition followed in 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A separate Saudi-led national-team event, the Esports Nations Cup, was announced in 2025 for 16 titles with finals first planned for November 2026 and later postponed to November 2027 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

As an IOC-sanctioned event, the Olympic Esports Games operate under the Olympic Charter together with the rulebooks of the selected game publishers and the relevant international federations, with standardized hardware, anti-cheat software and referee oversight at the venue (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). The programme is deliberately restricted to titles with a connection to traditional Olympic sports chosen by their international federations; games whose default format involves shooting other characters — including most first-person shooters and battle-royale combat modes — are excluded on Olympic-values grounds (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Athletes qualify through the Road to the Games, a series of IOC-sanctioned competitions run ahead of each edition, and compete in a multi-event format at a single host city, with the Games scheduled biennially in the gap years between the Summer and Winter Olympics (International Olympic Committee, 2025). In the predecessor Esports Series no medals were awarded — the top three received trophies — and eligibility, age limits and roster rules were set per event by the IOC and publishers; men and women competed in the same open events, with no separate gender categories (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The IOC first debated esports at the 2017 Olympic Summit in Lausanne, where leaders agreed that competitive “eSports” could be regarded as a sporting activity, and an IOC Esports Forum followed in 2018; the absence of a single governing federation and concerns over violent content kept esports out of the Olympic programme itself (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Under IOC President Thomas Bach (IOC President from 2013), the movement moved to its own events: the online Olympic Virtual Series of 2021 featured five esports across ten events, the Olympic Esports Series was announced in November 2022, and the inaugural Olympic Esports Week took place in Singapore in June 2023 under the leadership of Vincent Pereira, the IOC’s head of virtual sports and esports (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In June 2024 the IOC Executive Board decided to create a dedicated Olympic Esports Games, the 142nd IOC Session in Paris approved the project unanimously on 23 July 2024, and the first edition was confirmed for Riyadh 2027 on 11 February 2025 with the Esports World Cup Foundation as founding partner (International Olympic Committee, 2025). That partnership ended on 30 October 2025, when the IOC said it would pursue a different partnership model, and by May 2026 planning had been paused (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
