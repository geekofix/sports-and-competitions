## Basic Information

Ninjutsu is the art of the shinobi — covert agents of feudal Japan (espionage, infiltration, sabotage, assassination and close combat) — now taught as a martial art by modern schools. The largest organizations are the Bujinkan (founded by Masaaki Hatsumi, 34th soke of Togakure-ryu, who inherited nine ryuha from Toshitsugu Takamatsu in 1972) and the Genbukan (Shoto Tanemura, 1984, 36 divisions, the "ninja sanjurokkei"), plus To-Shin Do, AKBAN and others. Training covers taijutsu (unarmed), weapons (sword, staff, shuriken, kusarigama), stealth and survival; ranks run kyu–dan. Men and women train; international taikai are held yearly. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Aikido](aikido.html), [Judo](judo.html), [Bartitsu](bartitsu.html), [Karate](karate.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Ninjutsu is a martial art, not a ring sport: schools use kyu/dan grading, kata and randori-style sparring under each organization's rules; weapons practice uses blunted or training weapons with full protective gear in Bujinkan/Genbukan. There is no single competition rule set — some schools hold no-sport policies. The 18 ninjutsu skills (taijutsu, kenjutsu, sojutsu, naginatajutsu, kyujutsu, shurikenjutsu, etc.) form the traditional curriculum; historians note that the romanticized lineage claims of some modern schools are disputed (Bugei Ryuha Daijiten) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Ninjutsu traditions (Iga-ryu, Koka-ryu, Togakure-ryu) crystallized in the Iga and Koka provinces in the 14th–16th centuries; after the Meiji Restoration (1868) the arts went underground. Toshitsugu Takamatsu (1889–1972, "the last shinobi") passed nine schools to Masaaki Hatsumi (b. 1931), who founded the Bujinkan in the 1970s and took ninjutsu public worldwide from 1982; Shoto Tanemura split to found the Genbukan in 1984. Donn Draeger called Fujita Seiko "the last of the living ninja"; the Iga Ninja Museum names Jinichi Kawakami as inheritor (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
