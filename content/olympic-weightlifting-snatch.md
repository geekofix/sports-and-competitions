## Basic Information

The snatch is one of the two lifts contested in Olympic [weightlifting](weightlifting.html), alongside the [clean and jerk](olympic-weightlifting-clean-and-jerk.html): the barbell is lifted from the ground to full arm extension overhead in one continuous movement, with a wide grip and a deep squat under the bar (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-a). Because it is the faster, more explosive of the two lifts and is performed first in competition, the snatch often sets the tone for the contest. Weightlifting has been an Olympic sport for men since Athens 1896 and for women since Sydney 2000, with identical lifts and rules for both genders; among today's leading snatchers are Ukraine's Anastasiia Hotfrid, pictured below — Olympic champion Tokyo 2020 in the women's +75/+87 kg class and multiple world champion — and China's Li Wenwen on the women's side and Georgia's Lasha Talakhadze on the men's side (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In the snatch the lifter grips the barbell wide, pulls it from the platform in one continuous motion and catches it overhead in a deep squat with arms fully locked, then stands up with the bar held motionless until the referees' down signal; any pause during the pull, any contact of the bar with the head, or a downward movement of the bar during the catch invalidates the attempt (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-a). Each lifter receives three attempts at the snatch before the clean and jerk, and the heaviest successful snatch counts toward the two-lift total; the athlete with the highest combined total wins, with ties broken by the lower bodyweight (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-b). At the World Championships and Olympic Games the competition is divided into bodyweight categories — ten per gender from 2025, twelve per gender at Los Angeles 2028 — and the best lifter across all categories is additionally decided with the Sinclair coefficient (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Men and women lift under identical technical rules, on the same 20 kg bar for women's and men's senior events respectively adjusted per category, judged by the same three-referee system (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-b).

## History

The snatch has been contested since the first modern Olympic Games in Athens 1896, when the one-hand and two-hand snatch events were held, and it became a permanent part of the Olympic program from 1920 onward (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). In the early decades lifters such as the American Charles Vinci — Olympic champion 1948 and 1952 — and later the Bulgarian Naim Süleymanov, the "Pocket Hercules", set the standard; Süleymanov lifted three times his bodyweight in the snatch at the 1988 Seoul Olympics (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women first contested the snatch at the Sydney 2000 Olympics (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). The lift remains the opening event of every major weightlifting competition, and at Los Angeles 2028 the Olympic program will use twelve new bodyweight categories with equal numbers for men and women (International Weightlifting Federation, n.d.-b).
