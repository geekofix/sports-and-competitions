## Basic Information

Gymnastics is the umbrella sport of acrobatic, strength and flexibility skills, organized internationally by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG, founded 1881). At the Olympics and FIG World Championships, the disciplines are artistic gymnastics (men's and women's apparatus), rhythmic gymnastics, trampoline and aerobic gymnastics; FIG also governs parkour, acrobatic gymnastics and wheel gymnastics.

## Rules

In artistic gymnastics, men compete on six apparatus (floor, pommel horse, rings, vault, parallel bars, high bar) and women on four (vault, uneven bars, balance beam, floor), each routine judged on difficulty (D-score) and execution (E-score, starting from 10.0) by panels. At the Olympics, teams of five gymnasts compete, with four scores counting per apparatus; all-around and apparatus finals follow qualifying. Rhythmic gymnastics uses the apparatus (ribbon, hoop, ball, clubs, rope) in individual and group routines. Trampoline is judged as in the trampoline entry on this site. FIG scoring codes are updated every Olympic cycle.

## History

Modern gymnastics was systematized in the 19th century by Friedrich Ludwig Jahn ("Turnvater Jahn") in Germany and Per Henrik Ling's Swedish gymnastics; the FIG was founded in 1881 in Liège, and gymnastics has been at every modern Olympics since Athens 1896 (men only; women since Amsterdam 1928). The Soviet Union dominated from the 1950s with stars such as Larisa Latynina (9 Olympic golds, record until 2012), Nikolai Andrianov and Nadia Comăneci, who scored the first perfect 10 at Montreal 1976. Simone Biles (USA) is the most decorated gymnast of the modern era (multiple Olympic and world titles, skills named after her). Romania, China, Japan, the US and Russia are the leading nations today.
