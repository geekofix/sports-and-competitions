## Basic Information

Superbike racing is production-based motorcycle road racing: heavily modified but recognizably road-derived superbike motorcycles (1,000cc-class machines from manufacturers such as Ducati, Kawasaki, Yamaha, BMW and Honda) race on closed circuits. Governed by the FIM, the Superbike World Championship has since 1988 been the premier production-racing series, running twin races per weekend alongside national superbike championships worldwide.

## Rules

Superbikes are modified versions of road-going 1,000cc machines: the same engine architecture and frame lineage as the showroom bike, but with race-tuned engines, full race suspension and brakes, and strict technical regulations (limited engine tinkering, controlled electronics and mandated safety equipment) designed to keep costs lower than the all-prototype MotoGP class. Race weekends feature free practice, Superpole qualifying and two full-distance races (traditionally with a third sprint race in modern weekends); riders score championship points per race with the season's cumulative total deciding the title, and the performance-differentiating tools are setup, tires and rider skill rather than prototype budgets.

## History

The Superbike World Championship was founded in 1988 to give production-based machines a world championship of their own, and its early years belonged to American champions Fred Merkel and then Doug Polen's Ducati era, before a golden run of Britons (Carl Fogarty's four titles) and Australians made the series a genuine global headline act alongside Grand Prix racing. The formula proved commercially vital: manufacturers raced on Sunday what they sold on Monday, and national superbike series (the British Superbike Championship, MotoAmerica and others) fed riders to the world stage. The 2010s and 2020s have seen the championship remain the top production class, with Jonathan Rea's unmatched six consecutive world titles between 2015 and 2020, and a modern era in which Ducati, Kawasaki and Yamaha continue to contest a title that remains among motorsport's most closely fought.
