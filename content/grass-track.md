## Basic Information

Grass track racing is motorcycle speedway on a grass (or sand/clay) oval instead of the classic shale 400 m track — the European short-circuit scene centered on Germany, Poland, the Czech Republic and Britain (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the speedway family of motorsport.

![Grasbahnrennen (grass track race) at Bielefeld](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Grasbahnrennen_-_Bielefeld.jpg?width=480)

*Figure: Michael Kipshagen alias MacYukon. (3 October 2007 (original upload date) (Original text: 3. Juni 2007)). Grasbahnrennen (grass track race) at Bielefeld [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Grasbahnrennen_-_Bielefeld.jpg*

Grass-track ovals are typically 400–800 m; bikes are 500 cc single-cylinder speedway machines with no brakes, run on methanol (Wikipedia, n.d.). Races are heats plus a final over 4–6 laps with a tape or electronic start (Wikipedia, n.d.). The German Grasbahnrennen (photo: Bielefeld, Klein-Krotzenburg) and the British Masters Grass Track Championship at tracks like Skegness are the classic events (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Safety: leather suits, helmets, chest/back protectors and a kill switch are mandatory; medical cover is required at events (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — women’s grass track championships exist in Germany and Britain.

## History

Grass track speedway began in Britain in the 1920s when riders raced on farm fields and beaches before shale tracks became standard in the 1930s; the grass format survived on the continent, especially in Germany and Eastern Europe, where Grasbahnrennen remain hugely popular with sidecar classes too (Wikipedia, n.d.).
