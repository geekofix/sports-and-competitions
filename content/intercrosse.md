## Basic Information

Intercrosse — also known as softcrosse, modcrosse or pop lacrosse — is a non-contact team sport played with an all-plastic stick and a soft hollow ball, codified in Quebec, Canada, in the 1980s and governed by the Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse (FIIC), founded in Paris in 1985 (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two teams of five players — four field players plus a goalkeeper — face each other on a 40 × 20 m court (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Because body contact is forbidden and no protective equipment is required beyond optional gloves and a cup, intercrosse is genuinely mixed-gender: at the annual World Games since 1987, teams are randomly mixed men and women, and the World Championship, held since 1999 and biennially since 2006, plus the European Cup since 2010, welcome men's, women's and mixed categories (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). More than 20 nations play the sport, with strongholds in the Czech Republic and Quebec (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.). At the top level in 2026, the Czech Republic, Canada, Switzerland and France lead both the men's and women's world rankings — the women's world champions are fielded on equal terms with the men's sides (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under FIIC rules, intercrosse is played on a 40 × 20 m court with goals of 1.2 × 1.2 m at each end (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each team has five players, one of them the goalkeeper; the stick is made entirely of plastic and the ball is a soft hollow ball about the size of a tennis ball, so no helmet, pads or other protective gear are needed — only a protective cup and optional gloves are recommended (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A match consists of two halves; the ball carrier may hold the ball for a maximum of five seconds and must pass or shoot, and the attacking team must attempt a shot within 30 seconds of gaining possession (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.). There is no body contact of any kind: defenders may only intercept or block the ball with their stick, and any contact is a foul punished by a free pass (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Restarts are taken from the sideline or behind the goal; a goal counts one point, and the team with the most goals after regulation time wins, with overtime in knockout matches (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.). Men's and women's teams play under identical rules; at the World Games the teams are drawn as mixed squads at random, which makes intercrosse one of the few truly co-ed team sports at world level (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Intercrosse was invented and codified in the early 1980s in Quebec, Canada, where players adapted lacrosse into a safer, non-contact game that could be played in gymnasiums without protective gear, and the Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse was founded in Paris in 1985 to govern it internationally (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport spread quickly in the Czech Republic, where it became a school and club staple, and in Canada, Switzerland and France (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Intercrosse has been on the program of the World Games — the multi-sport event for non-Olympic sports — every edition since 1987, where randomly mixed men's and women's teams compete, and the first Intercrosse World Championship was held in 1999, becoming biennial in 2006; the European Cup followed in 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2026 World Championship continues the biennial tradition with mixed, men's and women's divisions (Fédération Internationale d'Intercrosse, n.d.). Because the early development was led by physical-education teachers in Quebec and Prague rather than by famous individuals, the sport's icons are its national team captains — the Czech and Canadian players who have dominated the world titles since the 2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
