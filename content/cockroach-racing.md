## Basic Information

Cockroach racing is an unusual novelty sport in which large cockroaches race around a circular track; the first insect to reach the edge wins. The best-known event is the World Championship Cockroach Races at the Story Bridge Hotel in Brisbane, Australia, held every Australia Day (26 January) since 1982, with proceeds going to charity. Similar races are run at US colleges, for example Loyola University Maryland's "Madagascar Madness".

## Rules

At the Brisbane races, the cockroaches are placed in bottles in the center of a ring about 6 m across; when the bottles are lifted, the first roach to reach the edge of the ring wins the heat, and winners advance through rounds to the final. There are separate heats for different species and sexes, and betting among spectators is traditional. The first recorded winner in 1982 was a cockroach nicknamed "Soft Cocky". Rules are informal and set by the organizers; animal welfare and hygiene rules apply at the venue.

## History

The Story Bridge Hotel in Brisbane began the races in 1982 as a quirky Australia Day attraction, and they became an annual tradition drawing crowds and media coverage. Cockroach racing also spread to US universities, where events such as Loyola Maryland's Madagascar Madness (racing Madagascar hissing cockroaches, including presidential-prediction races) became campus traditions. The sport remains a light-hearted novelty event rather than an organized sport with a governing body.
