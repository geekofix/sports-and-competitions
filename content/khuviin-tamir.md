## Basic Information

Khuviin tamir — with tamir, “sport” or “athlete,” the Mongolian word for sport — refers to the traditional mounted sports of the Mongolian steppe that are contested at Naadam and at the World Nomad Games: above all mounted archery (bait kharvaa, shooting at a target at full gallop), horseback feats and the mounted games of the festivals. At the 2026 World Nomad Games the archery program included Hungarian-style horseback archery with 118 athletes, alongside Kyrgyz-style and Turkish-style archery. Mongolian horsemanship is legendary: cavalrymen could slide down the side of a galloping horse to shield themselves from arrows while returning fire with the bow held under the horse's chin. Women compete in archery and racing at Naadam. Within the Central Asian nomad games on this site, its closest relatives are [Mongolian Archery](mongolian-archery.html), [Cirit](cirit.html), [Naadam](naadam.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

In horseback archery competitions the rider gallops along a course — at the World Nomad Games the Hungarian-style course — and shoots a set number of arrows at targets (traditionally leather cylinders or rings) placed along the track; hits score points, with bonuses for targets knocked down at full gallop, and the fastest clean round wins. Riders use the traditional composite bow and a thumb draw with a leather thumb ring, and horses are specially trained to run steady lines past the targets. Safety rules cover the course, the target zones and veterinary checks of the horses; men's and women's divisions are contested.

## History

Mounted archery was the decisive weapon system of the Mongol armies of Chinggis Khan in the 13th century; contemporary accounts describe Mongol cavalry shooting with deadly accuracy at full gallop. The tradition survives in the mounted archery contests of Naadam and the World Nomad Games (horseback archery appeared as “Traditional Horseback Archery (Mounted Archery)” at the 2018 Games, contested by Turkish and Kyrgyz teams, and with 118 athletes in the Hungarian style in 2026) and in festivals such as Mongolia's Golden Eagle Festival. Illustrations from the Ilkhanid period show Mongol horse archers in action.
