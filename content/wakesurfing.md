## Basic Information

Wakesurfing is the towed water sport closest to its surfing roots: riders trail a wake-surf-specific boat (loaded to throw a big, clean wake), pull up on a short rope, then drop the rope and surf the boat's endless wave, carving, snapping and airing without any tow after the start. The fastest-growing of the towed board sports since the 2010s, it has professional tours, a devoted boat industry and world championship circuits.

## Rules

Boats are purpose-configured for the sport with ballast, wake-shaping devices and surf-specific speed systems, and riders use wakesurf boards (from high-volume skim-style boards to surf-style shapes) with fins and no bindings: starting with a short tow rope, the rider pumps onto the wake's face, throws the rope into the boat and surfs the pocket. Competition formats include surf-style divisions (judged on rail-to-rail turns, snaps, 360s and style with barefoot-like variety, scored like a surf heat) and skim-style divisions (judged on skate-inspired tricks such as shuvits, spins and airs, with riders mixing styles on their own boards); rules govern boat speed and configuration, the surf side (left or right wake), run length and scoring under the event circuits' published standards.

## History

Wakesurfing began as a backyard practice of the earliest towed-water-sports decades (riding the wake sans rope, as documented from the 1950s and popularized anecdotally through the surf-behind-boat culture), taking modern shape in the 1990s and 2000s as boat builders engineered wake-shaping hulls and riders developed purpose-made boards; the sport boomed in the 2010s as wakesurf-specific boats, ballast systems and contest circuits (such as the world championship series and pro wake events) consolidated its professional structure. Today wakesurfing drives a major industry segment, opened the towed sports to a wider age range (its low-speed, low-impact wave suits all riders) and contests world-class events that reward style above all, and it carries the sensation of ocean surfing to lakes and rivers worldwide.
