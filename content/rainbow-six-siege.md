## Basic Information

Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six Siege is a tactical five-on-five shooter developed by Ubisoft Montreal, in which teams of attacking and defending operators battle over objectives in destructible indoor environments, and one of the world's established esports titles. Its competitive calendar is built around two international event tiers: the mid-season Six Majors in changing host cities and the season-closing world championship, the Six Invitational, which fields 20 teams and a three-million-dollar prize pool, the largest in the game's circuit, with the world champion taking home one million dollars.

## Rules

Competitive Siege is played as a best-of series of maps, and each map is contested in rounds to 7 wins with overtime rules if the score reaches 6-6, teams swapping between attack and defense halfway through. Attackers choose from breach points on a building, carrying drones to scout, while defenders reinforce walls and set traps to protect the bomb sites or hostage; unique operator gadgets and destructible walls, floors and barricades create layered tactical options — wall-bangs, vertical play and intel duels — rather than raw aim alone. At the Six Invitational, the 20 teams are divided into four groups of five, with the top four of each group advancing to a double-elimination playoff and the grand final played best-of-five. Official international play is governed by Ubisoft's global standings points system, which teams earn through regional leagues (North America, Europe MENA, South America, Asia Pacific) and the Six Majors to qualify.

## History

Rainbow Six Siege launched in December 2015 on PC and consoles, and its first world championship — the inaugural Six Invitational, named for the game's release window — was held in Montreal in February 2017, with separate PC and Xbox events won by Continuum and Elevate. Growth was rapid: the 2018 Invitational moved to the Ubisoft Montreal studio with a $500,000 prize pool, 2019 reached $2 million, and since 2020 the prize pool has been $3 million, the game's status as a top-tier esports title secure. The circuit globalized: the Invitational was staged in Paris (2021), Stockholm (2022), São Paulo (2024), Boston (2025) and Paris again in 2026, when FaZe Clan became the first back-to-back world champion since the event began, having beaten Team Secret in the final; G2 Esports (2019, 2023) and FaZe (2025, 2026) hold two titles apiece and Brazil's w7m esports won in 2024. The 2025 rebrand of the game to Siege X, regional league restructuring and third-party event organizers such as BLAST have kept the professional scene in continuing evolution.
