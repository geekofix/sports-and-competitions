## Basic Information

Folkrace is a Swedish grassroots rallycross class run on gravel ovals with near-standard production cars — historically Volvo 240/740/940 and similar — making it one of the cheapest forms of circuit racing (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the rallycross/stock-car family of motorsport.

![Folkrace cars at the Caravanhallen Cup](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Folkrace_caravanhallen_cup_%286109773142%29.jpg?width=640)

*Figure: bengt-re from Sweden. (2011-09-03 13:49). Folkrace cars at the Caravanhallen Cup [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 2.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Folkrace_caravanhallen_cup_%286109773142%29.jpg*

Folkrace ovals are typically 600–1,000 m of mixed gravel and asphalt; races run heats and finals over a set number of laps, with contact allowed within reason (Wikipedia, n.d.). Cars are production-based with mandatory safety equipment — roll cage, racing seat, harness, helmet — and engine modifications limited to keep costs down (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Classes include Folkrace standard (near-stock), plus modified categories; the Swedish championship (Svenska Folkracecupen / Caravanhallen Cup as in the photo) runs at venues across Sweden (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same classes, with separate ladies’ cups at some events.

## History

Folkrace began in Sweden in the 1950s–60s as farmers and mechanics raced old Volvos on farm tracks; the class became so popular that dedicated ovals were built, and Swedish folkrace spread to Norway, Finland and the Baltics (Wikipedia, n.d.). It remains the entry point for many Swedish racing drivers, including future touring-car and rallycross stars.
