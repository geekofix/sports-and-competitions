## Basic Information

Coastal rowing is open-water rowing along sea coasts and on lakes in boats built for waves, governed by World Rowing. The endurance format is raced over 4 to 6 km around turning points in solos, double sculls and coxed quadruple sculls at the annual World Rowing Coastal Championships (the 2026 edition is in Qingdao, China). Its sprint sibling, beach sprint rowing, is a head-to-head knockout race that starts with a run on the beach and will make its Olympic debut at Los Angeles 2028.

## Rules

In the coastal endurance race, crews navigate a marked course of 4-6 km around buoys on open water; the first crew to cross the finish line wins, and races are run in heats with semifinals and finals at championships. In beach sprint, athletes start behind their boats on the sand (Le Mans start), sprint about 50 m to the boat, row 250 m out to a turning buoy, row back, then sprint to the finish; the fastest athletes race several times in a knockout bracket. Boats are shorter and wider than flat-water shells with self-bailing cockpits; life jackets are mandatory.

## History

Coastal rowing grew out of open-water rowing traditions in coastal communities, especially in France, Italy, Spain and the UK, and World Rowing (FISA) formalized the discipline with annual world championships since 2008. Beach sprint was created as a spectator-friendly format and held its first World Rowing Beach Sprint Finals in Shenzhen, China, in 2019; the 2026 finals are in Qingdao. World Rowing announced beach sprint rowing for the Olympic program at Los Angeles 2028, making it rowing's newest Olympic event.
