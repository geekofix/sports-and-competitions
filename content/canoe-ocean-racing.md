## Basic Information

Canoe ocean racing is the long-distance downwind paddling discipline in surfski — long, narrow, open-deck ocean kayaks with a rudder, typically 6–7 m long — in which paddlers race several kilometres offshore, catching swells to surf. It is governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF, rebranded Paddle Worldwide in 2026), which has held the ICF Canoe Ocean Racing World Championships every two years since 2013 (ICF, n.d.). The 2026 ICF Canoe Ocean Racing World Championships were held in La Vila Joiosa (Vilajoyosa), Spain, from 9 to 11 October 2026, bringing together more than 750 paddlers from 36 countries racing a roughly 20 km downwind course off the town's central beach, with SS1 singles and SS2 doubles plus a relay; the ICF has confirmed Tahiti as 2027 host and France for 2028 (Todo Alicante, 2026; Costa Blanca Digital, 2026). In the women's SS1, Australia's Jemma Smith won her third world title in 1:21:08.15, ahead of South Africa's Kira Bester and Great Britain's Rosie Edwards; Germany's Gordan Harbrecht won the men's SS1 from the masters 40–44 category, and Smith teamed with Cory Hill for Australia's first mixed SS2 world title (1:07:15.05) (TotalPaddler, 2026; PaddleStats, 2026). Cory Hill of Australia, the 2025 men's champion and a four-time world champion, was among the headline names, alongside Portugal's Bernardo Pereira, France's Pierre Vilella, South Africa's Nicholas Notten and Spain's Walter Bouzán (Todo Alicante, 2026).

## Rules

Under the ICF Canoe Ocean Racing rules, races are point-to-point downwind courses of typically 15–25 km (world championships about 20 km) with a mass start from the beach; categories are SS1 (single surfski) and SS2 (double) in open, under-23 and junior classes for men, women and mixed. Paddlers may catch waves (surfing the swell) and use the rudder to steer; drafting behind another paddler is allowed, and there are mandatory re-start laps only if a restart is called. The first paddler to cross the finish line wins; electronic timing is used at world championships (ICF, n.d.; PaddleStats, 2026). Equipment: surfski must meet ICF length/beam rules per class (e.g., SS1 minimum length about 6.4 m, SS2 longer), with a rudder and footwell; safety equipment (leash, life jacket/PFD in some events, whistle) is mandatory and checked at scrutineering. Fouls: deliberately impeding another paddler or cutting across a buoy; penalties are time additions or relegation (ICF, n.d.). Men's and women's classes use identical courses and rules; the mixed SS2 class pairs one man and one woman (PaddleStats, 2026).


## History

Surfski racing developed in South Africa in the 1950s–1960s — the "surf ski" was created by lifeguards and paddlers such as Oscar Chalupsky-era competitors, and the first Cape Town to Milnerton races date to the late 1940s/1950s; the famous Cape Argus Cycle Tour's aquatic cousin, the Cape Point Challenge and the Durban-to-Metro Water race built the racing culture. The ICF took the discipline under its wing with the first ICF Canoe Ocean Racing World Championships in 2013 (Viana do Castelo, Portugal); editions followed in 2015 (Tahiti), 2017, 2019, 2021 (Sagres, Portugal), 2022 (Viana do Castelo), 2023 (Perth, Australia), 2024 (Durban and Funchal, Madeira) and 2026 (La Vila Joiosa, Spain), with 2027 in Tahiti and 2028 in France (ICF, n.d.; Surfski.tv, n.d.). Legends of the sport include South Africans Oscar Chalupsky, Hank McGregor, Michele Eray (2013 world champion) and Kenny Rice, and Australians Cory Hill and Jemma Smith, who have dominated the 2020s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Ocean racing was added to the program of the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics? — no: it is not on the LA28 Olympic program; it remains an ICF world-championship discipline and was demonstrated at the Olympic program review (ICF, n.d.).
