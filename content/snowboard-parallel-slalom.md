## Basic Information

Snowboard parallel slalom (PSL) is the head-to-head alpine snowboard race in which two riders descend two identical parallel courses side by side and the lower combined time advances; it is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS) and has been an Olympic event from Nagano 1998 through Beijing 2022 — at Milano Cortina 2026 the Olympic programme featured the parallel giant slalom only, held at Livigno Snow Park on 8 February 2026, with Benjamin Karl of Austria defending the men's title at age 40 and Zuzana Maděrová of Czechia winning the women's (FIS, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). PSL remains a full FIS World Cup and World Championship discipline — the 2025–26 World Cup visited the Simonhöhe in Kärnten, Austria, on 23 January 2026, pictured (FIS, n.d.-c).

Among the leading current riders, the women lead: Italy's Elisa Caffont led the 2025–26 FIS Snowboard World Cup standings in parallel slalom, with Japan's Tsubaki Miki leading the parallel giant slalom standings; on the men's side, Bulgaria's Tervel Zamfirov won the 2025 world title in PSL and took bronze in the 2026 Olympic PGS at Livigno (Wikipedia, n.d.-b, n.d.-c).

## Rules

Under FIS snowboard regulations, PSL qualification is a single timed run per rider on one course, with the fastest 16 advancing to the knockout bracket; each head-to-head heat is decided over two runs, one on each of the two parallel courses, and the rider with the lower aggregate time advances, until the final (FIS, n.d.-c). Both courses are set with the same number of gates on a firm, prepared piste, and the red and blue courses are mirror images (FIS, n.d.-c).

Equipment is hard-boot alpine gear: directional board, hard boots and bindings, and a helmet is mandatory; men and women compete in separate classifications on the same courses, and junior categories exist at the FIS Junior World Championships (FIS, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Parallel slalom was introduced to the Olympic programme at Nagano 1998 and was held at every Games through Beijing 2022; Austria's Benjamin Karl, pictured racing the FIS World Cup parallel slalom at Jauerling in 2012, is the discipline's great icon — Olympic champion in parallel giant slalom in 2022 and again at Milano Cortina 2026 at age 40, making him the oldest Olympic snowboard medallist ever, with five world titles and three overall World Cup crowns (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

On the women's side, the Czech Ester Ledecká — double Olympic champion in parallel giant slalom (2018, 2022) — and the German and Austrian women's teams of the 2010s set the early standard in parallel racing (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).
