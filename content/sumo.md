## Basic Information

Sumo is Japan's national sport: a form of full-contact wrestling in which two rikishi meet in a clay-ring dohyo, and the first to force his opponent out of the ring or make him touch the ground with any part of his body other than the soles of his feet loses. Steeped in Shinto ritual and professionalized into six annual grand tournaments, it is one of the world's oldest documented professional sports.

## Rules

A bout begins with the tachi-ai charge after ritual salt-throwing, stamping and pre-match preparation. Wrestlers may push, thrust, slap, grab the belt (mawashi) and throw, while punching with a closed fist, pulling the hair and attacking the eyes are forbidden, alongside an exhaustive list of banned moves enforced by the gyoji (referee). Bouts are fast, often decided in seconds, by techniques such as the push-out, belt throws and dramatic sidesteps. Ranks are strictly meritocratic within the banzuke hierarchy: from the entry divisions to the top makuuchi division and the champion rank of yokozuna, promotion demands winning records across consecutive fifteen-day tournaments in which each wrestler faces a different ranked opponent every day.

## History

Sumo's origins go back well over a thousand years: ritual strength contests appear in Japan's earliest chronicles, the imperial court staged ceremonial sumo by the eighth century, and organized professional wrestling developed through the medieval and Edo periods (1603 to 1868), when the dohyo ring, ranked banzuke and the distinctive topknot-and-mawashi style took their familiar form. The Japan Sumo Association standardized the six annual honbasho in Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya and Fukuoka and the strict rank-and-tournament system that still governs the sport. The showa era produced legendary champions such as Taiho and Chiyonofuji, and from the 1980s foreign-born wrestlers reshaped the professional ranks, from the Hawaiian pioneers to the Mongolian champions who have dominated the top division in recent decades. Today the sport stages sold-out tournaments, overseas exhibitions, amateur world championships under the International Sumo Federation, and a global fanbase that follows every basho.
