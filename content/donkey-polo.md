## Basic Information

Donkey polo is a comedic variant of polo played on donkeys instead of horses, popular in Central Asia and at charity events worldwide. The best-known competitive form is the Mongolian donkey polo organized around the Sarlag Mountain range, where teams of three players ride donkeys and use a smaller ball and shorter mallets. It is governed loosely under polo's international umbrella, the Fédération Internationale de Polo (FIP).

## Rules

The game follows polo rules with adaptations: teams of three players (instead of four), two halves of about 15 minutes (instead of four chukkas of 7 minutes), and donkeys are changed at halftime so no animal plays the whole match. Players use a standard polo ball and a mallet, and goals are scored by hitting the ball between the opponent's goal posts. Because donkeys are smaller and less predictable than horses, the game is slower and more chaotic, and rules on fouls are applied more loosely; safety helmets are mandatory in competitive matches. In the Mongolian variant, matches are played on a full-size polo field in the steppe near the Sarlag mountains.

## History

Polo itself originated in Persia over 2,000 years ago and spread through Central Asia; references to polo-like games played on donkeys and other animals appear in Tang dynasty China, where in 878 a noblewoman was reportedly buried with her donkeys and polo equipment. Modern donkey polo is associated with Mongolia, where herders have played informal matches for generations; the sport gained international attention through travel documentaries and the Sar focally organized tournaments. It is also played at charity events in the United Kingdom, the United States and Australia, often raising money for animal welfare, and is occasionally featured at festivals such as the Naadam games in Mongolia.
