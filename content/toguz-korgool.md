## Basic Information

Toguz korgool (“nine sheep droppings”) is the Kyrgyz national game of the mancala family: a board with two rows of nine holes and two scoring stores (kazans), 162 stones (korgool), nine per hole; a player wins by collecting 82 or more stones in his kazan, and 81 each is a draw. It is played at the World Nomad Games (111 athletes in 2026) and at biennial world championships organized by the Toguz Korgool Federation, founded in Bishkek in 1993; tournaments are also held in London, Schweinfurt, La Tour-de-Peilz, Prague and Pardubice. In 2020 UNESCO inscribed the shared element “Togyzqumalaq, Toguz Korgool, Mangala/Göçürme” for Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Türkiye. Men and women of all ages play; it is a standard part of school and club life in Kyrgyzstan.

## Rules

Players move alternately: take all stones from one of your non-tuz holes and sow them anticlockwise, one per hole, with the first stone dropped into the hole just emptied (a single-stone hole drops its stone into the next hole). If the last stone lands in an opponent's hole that then holds an even number of stones, those stones are captured into your kazan. If it lands in an opponent's hole that then holds exactly three, that hole becomes a tuz (“salt”) — but only one tuz per game, never the opponent's ninth hole, and never symmetric to the opponent's tuz; stones later falling into a tuz belong to its owner, who may move them to his kazan at any time. The game ends when a player has no legal move; the stones remaining on each side go to that side's owner, and the player with more stones in kazan plus tuz wins (82+ wins; 81–81 is a draw).

## History

Mancala-type sowing games have been played on the steppe for centuries — the word korgool means sheep droppings, the traditional counters. The related Kazakh toguzkumalak was first described scientifically by Sarsen Amanzholov in 1936 (with earlier mentions by the ethnographer Boris Kuftin in 1906), and its tournament rules were codified in 1949 by Mukhtar Auezov and Kalibek Kuanyshbaev. The Toguz Korgool Federation was founded in Bishkek in 1993 and holds world championships every two years. The game was a competitive sport at the first World Nomad Games in 2014 (as one of two Kyrgyz national games, with Ordo), appeared again in 2016 and 2018, and was part of the program of the 2024 Astana Games. UNESCO inscribed the shared Turkic tradition in 2020. Nine is a sacred number for the nomadic Turkic peoples, hence the 9×9 layout.
