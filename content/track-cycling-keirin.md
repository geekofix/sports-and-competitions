## Basic Information

The keirin is track cycling's motorcycle-paced sprint: riders start behind a derny pacer that steadily accelerates, the field jostles for position behind it, and with roughly 600 to 700 metres to go the pacer swings off and the riders sprint for the line. Invented in Japan as a betting sport and added to the Olympic programme in 2000, it is one of track cycling's premium spectator events.

## Rules

Six (sometimes seven) riders draw positions behind the pacer and must stay behind its rear wheel for the pacing phase, which accelerates the field up to roughly 50 km/h before pulling off with the final laps running; from that release the race is a pure sprint, governed by strict positional and movement rules (no riding below the measurement line, no dangerous moves in the closing sprint). Races are run over roughly 2,000 total metres with the sprint in the last 600 or so, riders qualify through heats and repechage into finals, and officials penalize interference with relegation or disqualification. The Japanese keirin format is slightly different (longer pacing, strict gate draws and a professional betting league structure) and the rules there underpin the sport's professional heartland.

## History

Keirin was developed in Japan in 1948 as a public betting sport to aid postwar reconstruction: the Japanese leagues built purpose-built velodromes, strict rider academies (with famous rivalry and apprenticeship culture) and pari-mutuel wagering that made its stars household names. The UCI adopted the discipline for world competition (world championships from 1980, Olympic debut at Sydney 2000), and the event's pacing rhythm and field-position tactics have made it a mainstay of the Olympic programme, with Japan's professional format remaining the largest stage: dozens of winning riders and betting daily across the country's velodromes.
