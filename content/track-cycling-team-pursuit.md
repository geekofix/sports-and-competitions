## Basic Information

The team pursuit is track cycling's endurance time trial: two teams of four riders start on opposite sides of the velodrome and race 4,000 metres, clocking the time of the third rider across the line, with the win by time or by catching the opposing team. One of the sport's most precisely drilled disciplines, it has been on the Olympic programme since 1908 in various forms and is contested by national squads worldwide.

## Rules

Teams start on opposite straights and ride in a four-man or four-woman line, taking turns on the front and peeling up the banking to rejoin at the rear; the team's time is taken by its third finisher, so a dropped rider costs nothing as long as three hold the pace, and the discipline rewards perfect formation riding (riders within centimetres of the wheel ahead). Distances are 4,000 metres for elite men and women (junior and category distances vary), qualification rounds are timed to the thousandth, finals are run as direct pairings where catching the opponents ends the race, and equipment rules match the endurance track standard (fixed gear, no brakes, aero frames and discs within the regulations).

## History

The pursuit family grew from the individual time-trial tradition of early track racing, and the team variant became one of the Games' classic events: contested since 1908 for men, joined by women's formats at world level in later decades, and a core world championship event throughout the twentieth century. Records fell century by century as tracks, aerodynamics and training science advanced (the four-minute barrier for 4,000 metres fell in the 2000s, and the modern era has pushed the mark near three-and-a-half minutes), and the discipline remains the shop window of national track programs: the riders who can drill a four-man pursuit train are the endurance core of Olympic track cycling.
