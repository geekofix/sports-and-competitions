## Basic Information

Lacrosse is a fast, full-contact team sport played with a small hard rubber ball and a long-handled stick (crosse) with a netted head, in which players catch, carry and pass the ball to throw it into the opponents' goal. The main formats are men's and women's field lacrosse (10 v 10 and 12 v 12 on a large grass field), box lacrosse (6 v 6 indoors on a hockey rink), and World Lacrosse Sixes (6 v 6), which will make lacrosse's Olympic debut at Los Angeles 2028. The sport is governed worldwide by World Lacrosse, which has more than 90 member nations.

## Rules

In men's field lacrosse, two teams of ten play four 15-minute quarters on a field about 100 m long with goals at each end; play starts with a face-off, and an 80-second shot clock forces constant attacking. Players may carry the ball in the pocket of the crosse, pass and shoot, but body contact is limited to shoulder-level checking; offside, holding and slashing are fouls. Women's field lacrosse is 12 v 12 with modified checking rules and a 90 m field. Box lacrosse is played 6 v 6 on an ice hockey rink with full body checking and boards. In World Lacrosse Sixes, teams of six (five runners plus a goalkeeper) play four 8-minute quarters on a 70 m x 36 m pitch with a 30-second shot clock and no long defensive poles.

## History

Lacrosse originated among the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) peoples of North America, who played a ceremonial game called baggataway as early as the 12th century; it was called "the Creator's game" and is still considered a gift from the Creator. French missionaries in the 17th century named it "la crosse" for the stick. The Canadian dentist William George Beers codified the modern rules in 1867 and championed lacrosse as Canada's national summer game, a status Parliament formally confirmed in 1994. Lacrosse was a medal sport at the 1904 St. Louis and 1908 London Olympics — Canada won both — and a demonstration sport in 1928, 1932 and 1948. After decades of campaigning, the IOC approved lacrosse (sixes format) for the 2028 Los Angeles Games in October 2023. The professional Premier Lacrosse League and the National Lacrosse League (box) are the top North American leagues.
