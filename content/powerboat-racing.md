## Basic Information

Powerboat racing is the motorsport of engine-driven boat racing: classes from small circuit racers to offshore catamarans compete over closed courses on lakes, rivers and open sea, governed worldwide by the Union Internationale Motonautique (UIM) (UIM, n.d.). The headline series is the UIM F1H2O World Championship, the highest class of inshore powerboat racing, in which single-pilot closed-cockpit tunnel boats exceed 250 km/h; the 2025 drivers' title went to Shaun Torrente of the USA with the Victory Team, his fourth world championship, and the 2026 season runs seven rounds from Cagliari to the Sharjah finale in December with Torrente defending (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the leading women in the UIM family, Sweden's Mathilda Wiberg became the first woman to win a UIM F2 world championship in 2025, while the men's elite is headed by Torrente, Jonas Andersson and Stefan Arand; the all-time record is held by Italy's Guido Cappellini with 10 F1H2O titles between 1993 and 2009 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

UIM classes group boats by hull type, engine size and crew: circuit classes — F1H2O tunnel boats, F2, F4 and O-class hydroplanes — race over marked laps on enclosed buoy courses with rolling or pontoon starts, while offshore classes race point-to-point over coastal courses with safety crews (UIM, n.d.). F1H2O boats are catamaran-style tunnel hulls with a closed cockpit and a mandatory harness, reaching 250+ km/h and accelerating from standstill to 100 km/h in under two seconds; races run as a Grand Prix plus sprint races under a points system introduced in 2024, with penalties for hitting course marks or jumping the start (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Championships score points across race meetings toward world and continental titles, with separate drivers' and teams' championships; licence, safety-gear and medical requirements apply, and women compete in the same classes — there are no women's-only classes at world level, but women such as Mathilda Wiberg win world titles outright (UIM, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Powerboat racing began in the early 20th century as engine speed became a spectacle: the Harmsworth Trophy international challenge races started in 1903, and offshore racing developed through the 1920s and 1950s on both sides of the Atlantic (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The OZ and ON classes of the 1960s–1980s were consolidated under UIM sanction into the Formula 1 Powerboat World Championship, which has run since 1981 (not in 1987–1989 or 2020) and was promoted by H2O Racing from the mid-1980s; the 1990 reinstatement mandated driver harnesses after designer Chris Hodges pioneered them (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Offshore classics such as the Miami–Nassau race and the Cowes–Torquay–Cowes remain pillars alongside the circuit series (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
