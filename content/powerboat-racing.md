## Basic Information

Powerboat racing is the motorsport of boat racing: classes of engine-driven craft from small circuit racers to offshore catamarans compete over closed courses on lakes, rivers and open sea, governed worldwide by the Union Internationale Motonautique (UIM). Its headline series include the F1H2O world championship of tunnel-hulled circuit racers, offshore classics and national circuit racing up to O-class hydroplanes.

Machines reach race speeds of 200 km/h and more in offshore and circuit formats, with racing run close to spectators on shore courses and harbour circuits.

## Rules

The UIM classes group boats by hull type, engine size and crew: circuit classes (including the F1H2O tunnel boats and O-class hydroplanes) race over marked laps on enclosed courses with buoys, starts from a rolling formation or pontoon grid; offshore classes race point-to-point or over coastal courses with safety crews covering sea conditions. Racing runs under UIM sporting rules with licence and safety-gear requirements, handicaps and penalties for hitting course marks, and championships score points across race meetings toward world or continental titles.

## History

Powerboat racing began in the early twentieth century as engine speed became a spectacle, with the Harmsworth Trophy international challenges from 1903 and offshore racing developing through the 1920s and 1950s on both sides of the Atlantic. Formula-style circuit world championships grew from the 1980s, and today's UIM sanctions world championships in circuit, offshore and endurance classes, with the F1H2O series racing events from Europe to Asia and the Middle East and attracting star drivers and major sponsors.
