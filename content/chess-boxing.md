## Basic Information

Chess boxing is a hybrid combat sport that alternates rounds of chess and boxing in the same match, governed by the World Chess Boxing Organization (WCBO). It was created in 2003 in Berlin by Dutch artist Iepe Rubingh, inspired by the graphic novel "Froid Équateur" by Enki Bilal, and is contested at world championships in weight classes for men and women.

## Rules

A chess boxing match consists of up to eleven alternating rounds: a chess round (four minutes of chess clock time) followed by a boxing round (three minutes), with one-minute breaks between rounds; the match starts with a chess round. A fighter wins by checkmate, knockout, technical knockout or, if the match goes the distance, by judges' decision; a fighter who runs out of chess time loses, and a boxing round can end early by KO. Both athletes must be proficient in both sports: boxing gloves are put on for the boxing rounds and removed for chess. Referees from both sports officiate their respective rounds.

## History

Iepe Rubingh organized the first official chess boxing event in Berlin in 2003 after the idea from Enki Bilal's 1998 comic. The WCBO was founded to govern the sport and sanctions world championships; notable champions include Russian fighters such as Iepe Rubingh himself (a former kickboxer) and later title holders like "The Joker" and others in the light-heavyweight division. The sport combines the tactical calm of chess with the physical impact of boxing and remains a niche but internationally followed spectacle.
