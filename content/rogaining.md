## Basic Information

Rogaining is the sport of long-distance cross-country navigation in which teams of two to five people visit as many checkpoints as possible within a time limit, on foot and armed only with map and compass. It was invented in Australia in 1976 — the name honours its founders Rod, Gail and Neil Phillips ("RoGaine") — and the sport is governed internationally by the International Rogaining Federation (IRF), based in Melbourne, whose flagship is the World Rogaining Championships, a 24-hour event held since 1992.

## Rules

The championship format is a 24-hour rogaine starting and finishing at noon, but shorter variants of 3, 6, 8, 15 hours are common at regular events, with teams free to choose their own route between checkpoints ("controls") that carry different point values depending on navigational difficulty and distance. The sport's core rules are teamwork and independence: teams must stay together at all times and move only by foot; outside assistance is barred, and each team must be self-sufficient along the way — food and water can be provided only at central event bases, with water drops provided by organisers. Each competitor must carry a whistle, teams record checkpoint visits with electronic punching devices issued by organisers, and maps provided by the event are drawn at a scale of between 1:24,000 and 1:64,000, with an event hash house serving as the base for food and rest. Scoring rewards the highest total of checkpoint points within the time limit; teams lose points for returning late, and the team with the highest score in each gender class (men's, women's and mixed) is crowned champion in championship events, with age categories from youth (under 23) to ultra-veteran (65 and over).

## History

The sport began in Australia in 1976, when Rod, Gail and Neil Phillips of the Melbourne University Mountaineering Club organised the first 24-hour navigational contest and codified the sport's rules and ethos; the name combined their initials into "rogaine". From an Australian base the sport grew via New Zealand and North America in the 1980s and is today established in Europe — Estonia, Latvia, the Czech Republic, Ukraine, Spain, Finland and Russia all field strong rogaining communities — with the International Rogaining Federation, registered in Melbourne, counting ten member associations as of January 2026 and sanctioning championship events. The World Rogaining Championships were first held at Beechworth, Australia, in 1992, then biennially until 2012, annually through 2017, and — excepting the pandemic period — every second year since; the two dominant nations are Australia and Estonia, with Estonian pair Timmo Tammemäe and Sander Linnus among the most successful athletes in recent championship history. A typical world championship draws over 300 teams, with teams covering well beyond 100 kilometres in the terrain over the 24-hour period.
