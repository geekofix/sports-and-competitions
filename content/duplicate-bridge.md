## Basic Information

Duplicate bridge is the competitive form of contract bridge in which the same hands (board) are played by multiple pairs, so that skill rather than luck decides the result — each pair plays the same cards as another pair sitting in the opposite direction, and scores are compared on a board-by-board basis. It is governed by the World Bridge Federation (WBF), which runs the World Championships (Bermuda Bowl for men, Venice Cup for women, and the World Olympiads), and by continental bodies such as the European Bridge League.

## Rules

In a duplicate pairs event, each board is played twice: once by your pair (North-South or East-West) and once by the opposing pair at the other table in the same direction, so both pairs bid and play the same cards from both sides. Raw scores are converted to IMPs (International Match Points) by comparing the two results at each board, then to victory points (VPs) for the match; the pair with more VPs wins the match. Team events (e.g., Bermuda Bowl) use round-robin or knockout formats with boards carried over between matches, and a carry-over scoring system. At the top level, screen play (bidding/play hidden behind screens) is used to prevent unauthorized information, and boards are dealt by computer. The Laws of Duplicate Contract Bridge, maintained by the WBF Laws Committee, govern irregularities, penalties and appeals.

## History

Duplicate bridge was pioneered in the 1920s-1930s, with the first major duplicate events in the United States (the Vanderbilt Cup, 1928) and the first World Championship match in 1932 between the Culbertson and Lenz teams. The World Bridge Federation was founded in 1958 in Turin, merging the International Bridge League (founded 1932) and the European Bridge League, and has organized the Bermuda Bowl (men, since 1950 as an invitational, under WBF since 1960), the Venice Cup (women, since 1974) and the World Bridge Olympiad (since 1960). Bridge was a demonstration sport at the 2002 Salt Lake City Olympics and is recognized by the IOC. Legendary players include Giorgio Belladonna, Benito Garozzo and the Italian Blue Team that dominated the 1960s-70s, and later stars such as Zia Mahmood.
