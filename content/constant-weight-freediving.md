## Basic Information

Constant weight (CWT) is the depth discipline of freediving: the athlete descends and ascends under their own power with fins and a constant weight (no variable buoyancy devices), and the depth of the bottom plate/tag counts (AIDA, n.d.). It belongs to the freediving family and is the most-watched competitive depth event.

The AIDA men’s CWT world record is 136 m, set by Russia’s Alexey Molchanov at the 31st AIDA World Championship in Limassol, Cyprus, on 29 September 2023; in 2025 he equalled Arnaud Jerald’s 126 m in constant weight with bifins (CWTB) at Limassol and then pushed CWTB to 127 m at Deep Dominica on 1 December 2025 — his 41st career world record (Molchanovs, 2025). The women’s CWT record is 123 m by Italy’s Alessia Zecchini (2023), matched under CMAS by Slovenia’s Alenka Artnik (2025); Poland’s Agata Zalecka set the women’s CWTB record of 113 m at the 2025 CMAS World Cup in the Philippines (photos above) (DeeperBlue, 2026).

## Rules

Rules: a bottom plate with tag must be reached, video from the bottom camera and full surface protocol are required for ratification, doping control applies, and no pre-oxygenation (AIDA, n.d.). Depth competitions are held at deep, calm venues — Dean’s Blue Hole (Bahamas), Limassol (Cyprus) and Dominica are the current classics (Freediving Base, 2026). Men and women compete in separate events at the AIDA/CMAS Depth World Championships.

## History

Competitive depth freediving took off after Jacques Mayol and Enzo Maiorca broke the 100 m psychological barrier in the 1970s–80s; AIDA (founded 1992) standardized the tag-and-video protocol in the 1990s, and Herbert Nitsch’s 214 m no-limits dive in 2007 remains the deepest ratified AIDA dive ever (Wikipedia, n.d.). Molchanov has dominated the men’s CWT since 2012, growing the record from 126 m to 136 m.
