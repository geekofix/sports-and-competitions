## Basic Information

Taolu is the choreographed "forms" discipline of competitive wushu, in which a single athlete performs a prescribed sequence of martial techniques — stances, strikes, kicks, jumps and weapon movements — on a carpet while judges score the routine (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). It is governed by the International Wushu Federation (IWUF), founded on 3 October 1990 in Beijing and recognised by the IOC in 2002, and it sits alongside sanda as one of the two halves of every World Wushu Championships, held biennially since 1991 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The major events are changquan (long fist), nanquan (southern fist), taijiquan (tai chi) and combined weapon events such as jianshu/qiangshu and daoshu/gunshu, with equal men's and women's programmes — ten taolu events, five per sex, were contested at the 2008 Beijing Wushu Tournament — and at the end of this section the current leading athletes are the reigning women's and men's world champions in each event, crowned at the 17th World Wushu Championships in Brasília in 2025 (Olympedia, n.d.).

## Rules

A taolu routine is performed on a 14 × 8 m carpet marked with a 5 cm white boundary line, and must last at least about 80 seconds for compulsory events; stepping off the carpet, falling or exceeding the time limit brings deductions (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Judges award points for degree of difficulty, execution of techniques, and overall performance — power, rhythm, coordination and spirit — with the scores combined into a final mark (Wikipedia, n.d.). Athletes compete one at a time in their chosen event: bare-handed styles (changquan, nanquan, taijiquan) or weapon routines (jian/daoshu swords, qiang/gun spears and staffs, nandao/nangun southern weapons and others), and combined events rank the same athlete across two related events (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Men's and women's events are equal in number at senior championships, and junior championships split competitors into age groups — under the current rules 7–11, 12–14 and 15–17 years (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The forms tradition behind taolu stretches back centuries in Chinese martial arts, but modern sport wushu — with standardised compulsory routines — was codified in China after 1949 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Wushu Federation was founded on 3 October 1990 in Beijing during the 11th Asian Games, the first World Wushu Championships were held in Beijing in 1991 and have run biennially since, and wushu entered the Asian Games programme in 1990 (International Wushu Federation, n.d.). Compulsory routines were required from 1993 to 2005 (second set) and again from 2013 to 2015 (third set), with optional routines in between; the IOC-permitted Beijing 2008 Wushu Tournament featured ten taolu events at the Olympic Sports Center Gymnasium, and wushu was a demonstration sport at the Nanjing 2014 Youth Olympics before becoming official at Dakar 2026 (Olympedia, n.d.). Among the discipline's icons are Yuan Xiaochao of China, undefeated in men's changquan internationally since 2005 and Beijing 2008 gold medalist; Willy Wang of the Philippines, nanquan gold at Beijing 2008; and Lin Fan of China, women's nanquan gold at Beijing 2008 and 2011 world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.).
