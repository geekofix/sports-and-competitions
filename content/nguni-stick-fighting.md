## Basic Information

Nguni stick fighting (indlamu) is the traditional stick-and-shield combat of the Zulu, Xhosa, Swazi and Ndebele peoples of southern Africa: fighters armed with a wooden stick (ishova) and a small shield try to strike each other's shield or body while defending (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is practised at cultural festivals and in the modern competitive scene centred on KwaZulu-Natal, with weight and age categories at organised tournaments (Traditional Sports, n.d.). Within the combat sports family on this site, its closest relatives are [Tahtib](tahtib.html), [Langa](langa.html), [Boxing](boxing.html), [Kickboxing](kickboxing.html), [Fencing](fencing.html), [Eskrima](eskrima.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Bouts are fought in a circle; strikes with the stick to the shield, arm or body score; the fight continues until a knockdown, a called stop or points decide at tournaments. Protective headgear and gloves are used in modern competition; traditional bouts use shields of cowhide. Men's and women's divisions exist at festivals (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Stick fighting was part of Nguni warrior training from at least the 19th century — Shaka's regiments drilled with sticks — and survives today as a cultural sport at events like the Reed Dance and at Lesedi Cultural Village (Wikipedia, n.d.).
