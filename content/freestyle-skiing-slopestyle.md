## Basic Information

Slopestyle is the freestyle skiing discipline in which athletes ride a course of rails, boxes and jumps (kickers), performing tricks on each feature, judged on difficulty, execution, amplitude, variety and landings. It is governed by FIS, an Olympic event since Sochi 2014, and is the most popular freestyle skiing format at the X Games.

## Rules

At FIS slopestyle events and the Olympics, each competitor gets two or three runs (two at the Olympics, best counts) down a course with a sequence of features — typically 2-3 jumps and several rails/boxes. Five judges score each run out of 100 on the FIS criteria: amplitude, difficulty, variety, execution and landing. The best run counts; qualifying reduces the field to the final. Riders choose their line and tricks, with variety across different features rewarded. Men's and women's events are held at the Olympics.

## History

Slopestyle developed from skateboarding and snowboarding terrain parks in the 1990s-2000s and was formalized by FIS in the 2000s-2010s, becoming an Olympic sport at Sochi 2014, won by USA's Joss Christensen and Canada's Dara Howell. The X Games slopestyle made stars such as Tanner Hall and Simon Dumont. Olympic champions include Joss Christensen (2014), Dara Howell (2014), Øystein Bråten (Norway, 2018) and, at Beijing 2022, New Zealand's Nico Porteous (men) and China's Eileen Gu (women). Stars also include Sweden's Henrik Harlaut and USA's Alex Hall.
