## Basic Information

Snowboard halfpipe is the judged discipline in which riders perform tricks while travelling wall-to-wall in a U-shaped snow structure; it is governed by the FIS and has been an Olympic event since Nagano 1998 (FIS, n.d.-a). At the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics at Livigno Snow Park — whose superpipe is 198 metres long, 22 metres wide with 7.2-metre walls inclined at 18 degrees — Yuto Totsuka of Japan won the men's gold with 95.00 points, and Choi Ga-on of South Korea won the women's gold with 90.25, becoming at 17 years and 101 days the youngest Olympic snowboard champion, ahead of Chloe Kim of the United States with 88.00 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the current leading women are Choi Ga-on, Chloe Kim, the 2025 world champion with 93.50 at Engadin, and Mitsuki Ono of Japan; among the men, Yuto Totsuka is the 2026 Olympic champion (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Halfpipe runs are judged out of 100 points by a panel on amplitude, difficulty, execution, variety and landing; at the Olympics riders take three runs with the best two counting (FIS, n.d.-b). Tricks include airs above the lip — spins from 360 to 1440 degrees and inverted tricks such as McTwists, double corks and handplants — plus lip tricks like alley-oops; a fall or a hand down on the landing costs heavily (FIS, n.d.-b). The superpipe dimensions are regulated: at Livigno the pipe was 198 m long, 22 m wide, with 7.2 m walls and an 18-degree transition (Wikipedia, n.d.). A qualification round advances the top riders to the final, and World Cup halfpipe events follow the same runs-and-judges format (FIS, n.d.-a). Women's and men's events use the same pipe and identical judging criteria, with separate classifications (FIS, n.d.-b).

## History

Halfpipes evolved from the 1980s freestyle snowboard scene — Tom Sims built early halfpipes in the early 1980s — and ESPN X Games added halfpipe in 1995; the FIS brought halfpipe to the Olympic programme at Nagano 1998, where Switzerland's Gian Simmen and Germany's Nicola Thost won the first gold medals, while Norway's Terje Håkonsen famously boycotted the Games in protest of the Olympic format (Wikipedia, n.d.). Retired icons of the pipe include America's Shaun White, who won three Olympic golds (2006, 2010, 2018) and landed the first double McTwist 1260 in competition, Australia's Torah Bright (Vancouver 2010), America's Hannah Teter (Turin 2006) and Kelly Clark, a four-time X Games champion (Wikipedia, n.d.).
