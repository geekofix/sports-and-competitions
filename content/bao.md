## Basic Information

Bao (laizi) is the traditional mancala of the East African Swahili coast — Zanzibar, coastal Tanzania, Kenya, Mozambique and the Comoros — played on a 4×8 board (four rows of eight pits) with 64 seeds, two per pit, and special “house” pits (nyumba). It is the most complex of the East African mancalas, with women's, boys' and men's rule variants, and it is played by men and women in stone town streets and villages. Related games use the same 4×8 equipment, including omweso of Uganda. Within the mancala and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Omweso](omweso.html), [Kalah](kalah.html), [Oware](oware.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The board has four rows of eight pits; each player controls two rows (32 pits), and the fifth pit of each player's inner row is the nyumba (house). Setup: two seeds per pit (men's variant), with the nyumba holding a larger stock. A move sows the contents of one pit around the player's own two rows (counterclockwise), with relay sowing from occupied pits; captures follow column and house rules (landing the last seed in the opponent's row captures that column's seeds when conditions are met; seeds in a captured nyumba are “killed”/resown). Special moves include the “namua” opening and tactical play around the house. The player who captures all the opponent's seeds — or is last to move — wins; variants for women, boys and men differ in setup and capture detail.

## History

Bao is a centuries-old game of the Swahili coast, played on carved wooden boards in Zanzibar's Stone Town; the name laizi means “custom.” It belongs to the great East African 4×8 mancala family that includes omweso (Uganda), isolo (Tanzania) and gabata (Ethiopia). Boards carved into rock and wood are found along the coast, and the game is a daily sight at bus stops and cafés. Bao was played at the World Nomad Games program family of East African intellectual games.
