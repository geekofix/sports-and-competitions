## Basic Information

Double mini-trampoline (DMT) is a gymnastics discipline in which an athlete runs down a runway, mounts a small double mini-trampoline \u2014 a narrow bed of 92 cm width on a 430 cm frame with a rising and a flat section \u2014 and performs a pass of acrobatic skills before dismounting onto a crash mat (FIG, n.d.-b). It is governed by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) together with trampoline and tumbling, and it is contested at the World Trampoline Gymnastics Championships and at The World Games, though it is not an Olympic discipline (FIG, n.d.-b). The 39th Trampoline and Tumbling World Championships were held in Nanjing, China, 4\u20138 November 2026 at the Yangtze River International Conference Center, with about 400 gymnasts from 41 nations; the United States women defended their double mini-trampoline team title, Russia won the men's team title, and both individual men's world titles went to first-time winners (FIG, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current athletes are the 2026 world champions from Nanjing \u2014 in the women's DMT the U.S. team champions including Kaitlyn McNew, and in the men's DMT the Russian team champions including Ilia Penkin \u2014 while Canada's Gavin Dodd placed fifth in the men's individual final with 29.600 points (FIG, n.d.-a).

## Rules

DMT is conducted under the FIG Technical Regulations (FIG, n.d.-b). Each competitor performs one pass consisting of a mount onto the apparatus, a series of skills on the rising and flat sections \u2014 typically combinations of somersaults and twists such as double back flips \u2014 and a dismount onto the crash mat; a spotter/"mounter" stands beside the apparatus (FIG, n.d.-b). Judges award marks for execution, difficulty and time of flight, and the pass score is the sum of these components; the highest total wins the individual title, and team titles are decided by the combined scores of the three team members (FIG, n.d.-b). The apparatus must be 430 cm long with a bed at least 92 cm wide and 15\u201320 cm high at the flat section, preceded by a runway of at least 25 m; athletes wear competition leotards and compete barefoot (FIG, n.d.-b). Men's and women's events use the same apparatus and judging, and junior age-group competitions run alongside the senior championships (FIG, n.d.-a). A fall from the apparatus or a hand touch on the mat results in a deduction or a zero for the affected element (FIG, n.d.-b).

## History

The double mini-trampoline was invented around 1970 in the United States by Robert F. Bollinger \u2014 the "father of the DMT" \u2014 together with George Nissen, the inventor of the modern trampoline, and it was first demonstrated at the 1976 AAU championships in Tulsa, Oklahoma, which hosted the first DMT world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.). The bed was widened to its current 92 cm in the mid-1990s through the work of German coach Horst Kunze and the Eurotramp company, making multi-somersault skills safer and more spectacular (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FIG has governed the discipline since the 1990s, the World Championships include DMT alongside trampoline and tumbling since 1999, and the discipline is part of The World Games program (FIG, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the discipline has produced enduring stars from the United States, Canada and Russia, with the U.S. women's team successfully defending its world title at Nanjing 2026 (FIG, n.d.-a).
