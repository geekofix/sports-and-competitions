## Basic Information

Sporthocking is a German trick sport played with a specially equipped stool: the name comes from the German verb *hocken* (to squat) and *der Hocker* (the stool), and athletes perform acrobatic tricks — spins, kicks, throws, slides and juggling moves — with the stool, which is lined with rubber and can be flipped onto its side to become two wheels for rolling tricks, blending elements of rhythmic gymnastics, skateboarding and breakdance (Topend Sports, n.d.). The sport was invented in 2007 by the German brothers Michael and Stephen Landschutz, who also run the official Sporthocking team, and it is practiced as an individual freestyle discipline or in teams, with ranked competition categories in Germany (The Local Germany, 2025). Women and men both practice Sporthocking, and mixed teams as well as all-female and all-male crews compete in the ranked categories (Topend Sports, n.d.).

## Rules

Sporthocking has no single international federation yet; the rules are set by the Sporthocking community around the Landschutz brothers' official team and by German event organizers, and competitions are judged on difficulty, execution, variety and showmanship of the trick run (The Local Germany, 2025). The apparatus is a colorful plastic stool with rubber linings; when flipped on its side the seat and legs form two wheels, so the same stool works for sitting, squatting, spinning, kicking, throwing, sliding and juggling tricks (Topend Sports, n.d.). A typical run lasts one to two minutes, tricks are performed standing, sitting or lying, and falls or dropped stools cost points in scored events; individual and team categories exist with ranking lists in Germany. There are no separate rules for women and men — both genders compete together under identical judging — and no official age classes, though youth categories exist at school events (The Local Germany, 2025).


## History

Sporthocking was invented in 2007 in Germany by the brothers Michael and Stephen Landschutz, who first demonstrated stool tricks at juggling conventions and founded the official Sporthocking team; the sport spread through German youth and festival culture, described as a mix of skateboarding and sitting down, and is now practiced in ranked individual and team categories (The Local Germany, 2025; Topend Sports, n.d.). Early adopter Hannes Roth, a member of the official team run by the Landschutz brothers, helped popularize the sport at juggling conventions (Topend Sports, n.d.).
