## Basic Information

Triathle is the modern biathlon-descended sport of the UIPM (the international modern pentathlon federation): a continuous race that alternates laser pistol shooting (five knockdown targets per bout, from a static position) with running and swimming stages, repeated over multiple rounds. The short, spectator-friendly format was created in 2013 to provide a rapid, accessible racing event for pentathlon's constituent disciplines, and it is contested with world championships in age-group and elite classes.

## Rules

A triathle course alternates shoot-run-swim in a continuous flow (typically beginning with a shoot before the first run in the standard format): athletes race a run leg, stop at the static 5-metre or 10-metre (by category) shooting range to hit five knockdown targets with a laser pistol within the 50-second time limit (or a combined relay-style run-shoot), then continue directly into the swim leg and out onto the next run, repeating the sequence over set laps. Time decides the finishing order (with misses adding a time penalty or loop in some formats), and rules govern category distances, equipment (certified laser pistols and targets), transition conduct and safety at open-water venues.

## History

Triathle was introduced by the UIPM in 2013 as part of modern pentathlon's family of accessible racing formats (embracing laser shooting since 2011 as the sport moved from air pistols in its mainstream disciplines): with the shoot-run-swim rhythm making for fast races of roughly 20 minutes, it quickly generated its own world championships and youth circuits, aided the development pipeline of pentathlon and biathle athletes, and demonstrated pentathlon's constituent disciplines to new audiences. The format continues on the UIPM calendar (biathle, triathle and tetrathlon feeding the Olympic modern pentathlon pipeline), with strong participation in the pentathlon heartlands of Europe and Egypt and a growing age-group world championship scene.
