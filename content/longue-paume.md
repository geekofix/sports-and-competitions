## Basic Information

Longue paume is a traditional French outdoor handball game and a direct ancestor of jeu de paume and modern tennis, played on a long open alley (terrain) roughly 90–100 m long, or against a wall in the enlevée form (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Fédération Française de Longue Paume, n.d.). Players hit a cork-and-rubber ball with the open hand — the serve is thrown by hand into the opponent's service box, and the ball may bounce once before being returned (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring follows the tennis-like system of 15, 30, 40 and game, called chasses in longue paume, and matches are played by teams of five (Fédération Française de Longue Paume, n.d.). The game is governed in France by the Fédération Française de Longue Paume and is strongest in Picardy and northern France, where the concours of Saint-Quentin has been held since the 19th century; the champion of France receives the Bouclier de Brennus, a trophy designed in 1892 by Charles Brennus at the request of Pierre de Coubertin (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete in the championnat féminin at club and national level under the same rules as men (Fédération Française de Longue Paume, n.d.). Longue paume is an open-air handball game of the pelota/wall family — [Jeu Provençal](jeu-provencal.html), [Basque Pelota](basque-pelota.html), [American Handball](american-handball.html), [Welsh Handball](welsh-handball.html), [Gaelic Handball](gaelic-handball.html) — and the full list is under Important Links.

## Rules

The terrain (gagne-terrain) is a long, narrow alley about 90–100 m by 9–10 m, marked with service and foul lines; the server throws the ball by hand from behind the service line into the diagonally opposite service box, and a let (faute) is called if the serve lands outside it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Fédération Française de Longue Paume, n.d.). After the serve, the ball may bounce once on each side and must be struck back with the open palm before the second bounce; points are lost for a double bounce, hitting the ball out of the alley, touching the net-cord area or catching the ball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring runs 15, 30, 40, then the game (jeu), with a set won at a two-point margin once 40 is reached — the same counting inherited by tennis (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Matches are team events of five players per side, and the enlevée variant is played against a high wall instead of down an alley (Fédération Française de Longue Paume, n.d.). The ball weighs about 40–50 g with a cork core and rubber covering, and players wear a simple leather or fabric band on the striking hand (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's and women's competitions use identical courts, balls and scoring (Fédération Française de Longue Paume, n.d.).

## History

Longue paume descends from the medieval jeu de paume, the royal handball game of French and English courts, which reached its golden age in the 16th–18th centuries — the famous Jeu de paume oath of 20 June 1789, sworn on a Paris court, launched the French Revolution (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The outdoor long form survived in Picardy and the north of France after the indoor game declined in the 19th century, and it was revived as an organized sport in the early 20th century under Pierre de Coubertin, the founder of the modern Olympics, who also commissioned the Bouclier de Brennus in 1892 as the trophy for the champion of France, designed by Charles Brennus, the rugby union player after whom the rugby trophy is also named (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The concours of Saint-Quentin, documented since 1899, remains the most famous annual open tournament (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
