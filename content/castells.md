## Basic Information

Castells are human towers built at festivals in Catalonia, Spain, by teams called colles castelleres. A colla builds a tower in stages: first the pinya (a dense base of hundreds of people), then the trunk (levels of castellers), often a pom de dalt on top, and finally the enxaneta, a small child who climbs to the top and raises a hand with four fingers (the aleta) to signal success before the tower is dismantled. The Coordinadora de Colles Castelleres de Catalunya (CCCC) unites around 100 colles, and castells were inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2010.

## Rules

In a competition (concurs), each colla attempts one castell; a tower counts as carregat (loaded) when the enxaneta completes the aleta, and descarregat (dismantled) when it is taken down in an orderly way. Juries score towers by difficulty, with higher and wider structures worth more; falls end the attempt. The CCCC's safety rules require the pinya, protective helmets for the enxaneta, and medical cover at events. The biennial Concurs de Castells de Tarragona is the most prestigious competition.

## History

Castells have over 200 years of documented history in southern Catalonia, rooted in folk dances and the ball de castells. On 16 November 2010 in Nairobi, UNESCO inscribed castells on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Milestones include the first 10-level tower attempts in 1998 and the first fully completed 4 de 10 (four people per level, ten levels high) by Castellers de Vilafranca in November 2015. The tradition's motto is "força, equilibri, valor i seny" (strength, balance, courage and common sense).
