## Basic Information

The World Eskimo Indian Olympics (WEIO) is an annual multi-sport event in Fairbanks, Alaska, held each July since 1961, preserving the traditional survival sports of the Inuit, Iñupiat, Yupik and other circumpolar and Native Alaskan peoples — blanket toss, high kicks, ear pull, knuckle hop, four-man carry, seal skinning and maktak eating — alongside dance, storytelling and the Miss WEIO pageant (WEIO, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The 65th edition ran in July 2026 at the Big Dipper Ice Arena with new records in the knuckle hop (152 ft 6 in), ear weight and blanket toss (ICT News, 2026). Women have competed since 1970 and now take part in the arduous events too (Wikipedia, n.d.). Within the multi-sport events and festivals on this site, its closest relatives are [Arctic Winter Games](arctic-winter-games.html), [Arctic Sports](arctic-sports.html), [Dene Games](dene-games.html), [Firefighter Games](firefighter-games.html), [Lumberjack Championships](lumberjack-championships.html), [Wheelbarrow Olympics](wheelbarrow-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Events follow the WEIO technical rules for each discipline (blanket toss, one-foot/two-foot/Alaskan high kick, kneel jump, sledge jump, triple jump, arm pull, airplane, one-hand reach, head pull, knuckle hop, finger/stick pull, pole push, hand games, snow snake, ear pull, ear weight, four-man carry, plus culture events such as fish cutting, seal skinning and maktak eating). Age and gender categories are set per event (WEIO, n.d.; AWG, n.d.).

## History

WEIO began in 1961 on the banks of the Chena River with teams from Utqiaġvik, Unalakleet, Tanana, Fort Yukon and Noorvik; it moved to Anchorage in 2007 and back to Fairbanks, its permanent home. The name gained 'Indian' in 1970 when women's events were introduced (Wikipedia, n.d.).
