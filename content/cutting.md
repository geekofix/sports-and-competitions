## Basic Information

Cutting is a western riding competition in which a horse and rider separate a single cow from a herd and prevent it from returning for a set time (2 minutes 30 seconds), demonstrating the horse's "cow sense". It is governed in the US by the National Cutting Horse Association (NCHA), founded in 1946, which runs the Futurity, Super Stakes and the World Finals; the American Quarter Horse Association also sanctions cutting events. The working cow horse's agility is the essence of the sport.

## Rules

Under NCHA rules, a cutter has 2 minutes 30 seconds to show the cow three times: the horse must work the cow, turn it and hold it away from the herd, and the rider may use the reins only to guide — no roping. Judges start each run at 70 points and add or subtract points for the horse's maneuvers (working the cow, turning, eye appeal) and for errors (losing the cow, the cow returning to the herd, excessive rein use). The highest score wins; ties are broken by a run-off. Classes are by rider experience (open, non-pro, amateur) and horse age.

## History

Cutting developed from the everyday ranch work of separating cattle on horseback in Texas and the American Southwest in the late 19th century, and the first cutting contests were held in the 1930s. The NCHA was founded in 1946 in Fort Worth, Texas, and the NCHA Futurity (for three-year-old horses) became the sport's premier event. Cutting remains central to Quarter Horse competition, with top horses earning millions in purses and famous bloodlines such as those of the Metallic Cat line.
