## Basic Information

The IndyCar Series is the premier class of American open-wheel racing: the 2026 NTT IndyCar Series is the 115th official season of the American championship and the 31st under IndyCar sanction, with a 17-race calendar of five ovals, six street circuits and six road courses (IndyCar, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Cars are Dallara DW12 chassis with the UAK18 aerodynamic kit and the aeroscreen cockpit protection, powered by 2.2-litre twin-turbo V6 hybrid engines from Honda and Chevrolet producing roughly 700 hp — over 800 hp on road and street courses, about 575 hp at Indianapolis — with Firestone as the sole tire supplier through 2030; the new IR-28 chassis with a 2.4-litre V6 hybrid debuts in 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 series champion is Álex Palou of Spain, who won his fourth consecutive and fifth overall title with Chip Ganassi Racing (IndyCar, n.d.-a). The season's centerpiece, the 110th Indianapolis 500 on 24 May 2026, was won by Sweden's Felix Rosenqvist of Meyer Shank Racing, who beat David Malukas to the flag in one of the closest finishes in race history (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the active women in the series, veteran Katherine Legge of the USA remains the most experienced female driver on the 2026 grid, while the women's pioneers of the sport are celebrated in its history (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

IndyCar races run over set distances — typically 200–500 miles (80–200 laps depending on the track) — with a maximum race duration; drivers must make at least one pit stop for fuel and tires, and full-course cautions bunch the field while pit stops under yellow are regulated (IndyCar, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Points are awarded for the race finish, pole position, leading a lap and leading the most laps, and the season champion is the driver with the most points; there is no playoff reset — the leader after the finale is champion (IndyCar, n.d.-a). Qualifying at the Indianapolis 500 uses a two-day format with a 45-minute Last Row Shootout; the field is locked at 33 cars (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Refueling is allowed during pit stops, and cars must carry onboard fire suppression and the aeroscreen; drivers must pass a medical exam and wear a HANS device, fire suit and helmet (IndyCar, n.d.-a). Oval races run counterclockwise with banking, street and road courses run clockwise; overtaking is unrestricted except under yellow flags (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The series fields no separate women's championship, but women have competed and won in the series since 1977, and the current grid regularly includes female drivers such as Katherine Legge (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The Indianapolis 500 was first run on 30 May 1911, won by Ray Harroun in a Marmon Wasp averaging 74.6 mph (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). American open-wheel racing split in 1996 when Indianapolis Motor Speedway owner Tony George founded the Indy Racing League (IRL) in reaction to the CART series; the IRL, later branded the IndyCar Series, ran its first race in 1996 and took over the Indianapolis 500 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Roger Penske's Penske Entertainment acquired the Indianapolis Motor Speedway and IndyCar in January 2020, reuniting the sport's most famous race with the series (Penske Entertainment, n.d.). Among the icons are A. J. Foyt, the first driver to win the Indianapolis 500 four times (1961, 1964, 1967, 1977) and a record seven-time USAC/IndyCar champion; Rick Mears, also a four-time Indy 500 winner (1979, 1984, 1988, 1991); and Mario Andretti, winner of the 1969 Indianapolis 500 and the 1978 Formula 1 World Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The women's pioneers include Shirley Muldowney, the first woman to qualify for and win an IndyCar race (1977), Janet Guthrie, the first woman to start the Indianapolis 500 (1977), Sarah Fisher, the first woman to win an IndyCar pole (2002), and Danica Patrick, the first woman to win an IndyCar race (Motegi, 2008) and the most experienced woman at the Indianapolis 500 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
