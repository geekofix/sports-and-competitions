## Basic Information

Sepak takraw — Southeast Asia's spectacular "kick volleyball" — is a team sport in which two regus (teams) of three players each contest a woven rattan or synthetic ball over a net, using only their feet, knees, chest and head, never their hands. Its modern form was standardised in 1960 in Kuala Lumpur from the traditional circle games of the region (sepak raga in Malay, takraw in Thai), and it is governed internationally by the International Sepaktakraw Federation (ISTAF), founded in 1988. It is a medal sport of the Asian Games and Southeast Asian Games, with Thailand the traditional superpower and Malaysia, Myanmar, Vietnam, Indonesia and the Philippines also strong.

## Rules

Regu matches are played on a court the size of a doubles badminton court, 13.4 by 6.1 metres, with a net 1.52 m high for men and 1.42 m for women; each team fields three players — the tekong (server) and two strikers. The serve is delivered from the serving circle by the tekong, keeping one foot grounded while kicking the ball over the net; thereafter each side may touch the ball up to three times (as in volleyball) before returning it, and rallies end with a fault — the ball touching the ground, net or an illegal contact such as a hand. Points are scored rally-style by the serving side (regu format), sets are played to 21 with a two-point lead or to 25 at 21-21, and matches are best of three sets; a team event matches three regus per side. The ball is a woven sphere with 12 holes and 20 intersections, of rattan or synthetic fibre approved by ISTAF, and the sport's signature acrobatics — the roll spike and the sunback spike, a mid-air somersault overhead kick — are among the most celebrated strikes in world sport. Doubles (two players) and four-a-side variants exist alongside the standard regu game under ISTAF rules.

## History

Kicking a woven ball in a circle is one of Southeast Asia's oldest pastimes — sepak raga in the Malay world, takraw in Thailand, chinlone in Myanmar, sipa in the Philippines — with the net version emerging in the 1940s in Penang (then called sepak raga jaring) and quickly spreading through Malayan schools. In 1960 officials from Malaya, Singapore, Thailand and Myanmar met in Kuala Lumpur, fixed the compromise name "sepak takraw" (Malay "kick" plus Thai "woven ball") and agreed standard rules; the sport debuted as a medal event at the 1965 Southeast Asian Peninsular Games in Kuala Lumpur, entered the Asian Games in 1990 at Beijing, and was internationally federated in 1988 with the founding of ISTAF, which today counts national federations in more than 30 countries. The King's Cup World Championship, first held in Thailand in 1985 under royal patronage, became the sport's most prestigious annual event, and ISTAF subsequently launched the SuperSeries circuit and the World Cup (first staged in 2011 in Kuala Lumpur). Thailand has dominated international competition across regu and doubles, with Malaysia, Myanmar and Vietnam collecting world titles and the sport's Olympic ambition an explicit long-term goal of its federation; sepak takraw is also played — at very high level — in schools and clubs from India to Iran, Japan and the United States.
