## Basic Information

Sepak takraw — nicknamed "kick volleyball" — is a Southeast Asian team sport played with a woven rattan or plastic ball over a net on a court the size of a badminton doubles court, using feet, knees, chest, shoulders and head but never hands or arms; it is governed worldwide by ISTAF, the International Sepaktakraw Federation formed in 1988, which runs the ISTAF World Cup, the SuperSeries and the annual King's Cup World Championship in Bangkok (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ISTAF World Championship 2026 — the 39th King's Cup — is scheduled for 3–11 August 2026 at Terminal 21 in Bangkok, and at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya Japan's Yuki Sato won gold in the men's quadrant event — Japan's first Asian Games sepak takraw gold since the sport joined the programme in 1990 — plus bronze in the men's regu (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the current leading players, the women's game is headed by Asian Games medallists such as India's Khushbu, bronze medallist in the women's regu at the 2022 Asian Games, while the men's elite is led by Japan's Yuki Sato (2026 Asian Games quadrant gold and 2025 King's Cup quadrant gold) and by the powerhouse national teams of Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

 Within the net and court team ball games family on this site, its closest relatives are [Sepak Takraw Doubles](sepak-takraw-doubles.html), [Sepak Takraw Regu](sepak-takraw-regu.html), [Volleyball](volleyball.html), [Beach Volleyball](beach-volleyball.html), [Beach Volleyball 4x4](beach-volleyball-4x4.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The court measures 13.40 m × 6.10 m with a net 1.52 m high for men and 1.42 m for women; each corner of the court has a quarter circle of 0.90 m radius in which the two non-serving players (apit kiri and apit kanan) must stand at the service, and the server (tekong) serves from a centre service circle of 0.60 m diameter whose centre lies 2.45 m from each baseline and 3.05 m from each sideline (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The synthetic ball has 20 panels and 12 holes, a circumference of 42–44 cm and a weight of 170–180 g for men, and 43–45 cm and 150–160 g for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the classic regu format three players per side may touch the ball a maximum of three times before returning it over the net, no player may touch it twice in a row, and hands and arms are forbidden in play — only the server may use the hands to toss the ball for the service; faults include touching the net or posts, more than three touches, carrying or rolling the ball, and serving out of position (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A set is won at 15 points with a two-point margin (15 was adopted in 2011, replacing 21), and a match is best of three sets (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Variants include doubles (two players per side on a smaller court with a lighter ball), team (nine players in three regus), quadrant and hoop takraw, and beach sepak takraw is also contested (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's competitions use the lower net and the lighter, slightly larger ball, and both genders compete at the Asian Games and the SEA Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The game's roots go back to 15th-century Malaya and Thailand, where circle games with a woven rattan ball — sepak raga in Malaysia, takraw in Thailand — were played; the Siam Sports Association wrote the first competitive rules in 1829 and introduced the net with the first public tournament around 1870; in 1945 Hamid Mydin created the net version "sepak raga jaring" in Penang, and in 1960 officials from Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Myanmar met in Kuala Lumpur to standardise the modern game and agree the compromise name sepak takraw — Malay sepak ("kick") plus Thai takraw ("woven ball") (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport became a medal event at the Southeast Asian Peninsular Games in 1965 and at the Asian Games from 1990; the King's Cup World Championship has been held annually in Bangkok since 1985, with Malaysia the inaugural champions, the ISTAF World Cup began in 2011 in Kuala Lumpur, and ISTAF itself was formed in 1988 out of the Asian Sepaktakraw Federation (ASTAF) founded in 1960 (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In Europe the Federation of European Sepaktakraw Associations (FESTA) has organised championships since 2003, with Germany the strongest European nation (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
