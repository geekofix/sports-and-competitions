## Basic Information

Goju-Ryu is one of the four major traditional styles of karate, founded by Chojun Miyagi in Okinawa in the 1930s, combining hard striking techniques with softer, circular movements and breathing exercises (junbi undo, sanchin). It is practiced worldwide under the International Okinawan Goju-Ryu Karate-Do Federation (IOGKF), founded by Miyagi's successor Morio Higaonna in 1987, and by other lineages such as the Jundokan (Eiichi Miyazato).

## Rules

Competition follows the WKF-style rules used by most karate organizations: kumite (sparring) matches are 2-3 minutes, scored on clean, controlled techniques (ippon = 1 point under older rules, or 2/1 points for yuko/ippon under WKF), with light, controlled contact to the head permitted in adult competition and no head contact for juniors; excessive contact, dangerous sweeps and throws are penalized. Kata (forms) competitions are judged on the performance of prescribed sequences; the style's signature katas include Suparinpei, Seienchin, Saifa and Kururunfa. Bunkai (application) demonstrations are also contested. The IOGKF holds its own World Championships every two years with additional traditional rules (goju-specific etiquette, okinawan kobudo weapons optional in some events).

## History

Goju-Ryu traces its lineage to Okinawan martial arts taught by Kanryo Higaonna (1853-1916), who brought Chinese kung fu (from Fujian) to Okinawa; his student Chojun Miyagi (1888-1953) named the style "Goju-Ryu" ("hard-soft style") in the 1920s-30s, after a line from the Taoist text Hakko Sho describing the "eight principles of quanfa" (the way of soft and hard). After Miyagi's death, Morio Higaonna (1938-2012) unified the main lineages and founded the IOGKF in 1987 in Okinawa. Goju-Ryu is one of the styles recognized by the World Karate Federation (WKF) for Olympic-style competition, alongside Shotokan, Wado-Ryu and Shito-Ryu.
