## Basic Information

Gōjū-ryū ("hard-soft style") is one of the main traditional Okinawan styles of karate, founded by Miyagi Chōjun (1888–1953) in Okinawa around 1930, blending Okinawan *te* with Chinese kung-fu influences from his teacher Kanryō Higaonna; its name was coined in 1929 after Miyagi's disciple Jinan Shinzato jokingly called the unnamed style *hankō-ryū* ("half-hard style") at a demonstration in Kyoto (International Okinawan Goju-ryu Karate-do Federation [IOGKF], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The style is practiced worldwide under the IOGKF — founded by Morio Higaonna (born 1938) in July 1979 in Poole, England, today the largest traditional Okinawan organization with 55+ affiliated countries and 75,000+ members, led since 2012 by World Chief Instructor Tetsuji Nakamura — and is recognized by the World Karate Federation (WKF) as one of the main styles alongside Shotokan, Wado-ryu, Shito-ryu, Goju-ryu's rival Goju-kai and others (IOGKF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Competitively, karate — including Gōjū-ryū practitioners — is contested under WKF rules in *kumite* (sparring) and *kata* (forms); the 3rd Okinawa Karate World Tournament was held at the Okinawa Convention Center in Ginowan from 30 July to 2 August 2026 (IOGKF, n.d.-b). Gōjū-ryū's signature kata include Sanchin, Tensho, Saifa, Seienchin, Shisochin, Sanseru, Seisan, Kururunfa and Suparinpei (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

At the end of 2026 the leading women in Gōjū-ryū competition include the top kata and kumite medallists of the 2026 IOGKF and WKF events — the IOGKF's 2026 Kata Challenge ran in autumn 2026 — while on the men's side Tetsuji Nakamura's IOGKF squad (three-time world champion Nakamura himself) and the Goju-kai of Gōgen Yamaguchi's lineage remain the strongest schools (IOGKF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In WKF kumite (the competition format used for Gōjū-ryū at world level), two competitors face each other on an 8 × 8 m tatami for 3 minutes (2 minutes at youth level); scoring techniques are punches (*tsuki*), strikes (*uchi*) and kicks (*geri*) to the head, chest and back, awarding *yuko* (1 point), *waza-ari* (2 points) or *ippon* (3 points); a contestant leading by 8 points wins by *kachi* (victory), and the match ends on 3-minute expiry, knockout or disqualification, with penalties (*shikaku*) for prohibited contact, passivity and evasion (World Karate Federation [WKF], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). In kata competition, a single performer executes a designated or free kata, judged on technique, power, speed, rhythm, breathing, kiai and stances, with scores from 5.0 to 10.0 per judge and flags for the two finalists' *bunkai* application (WKF, n.d.-a). Gōjū-ryū's own training also includes *yakusoku kumite* (pre-arranged sparring), *torite* (joint-lock and throwing techniques) and the hard-soft breathing methods of *ibuki* and *tanden* (IOGKF, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate weight classes (−55 to +84 kg men; −50 to +68 kg women) in kumite, and identical kata rules for both sexes; cadet (U21) and junior (U18) categories exist (WKF, n.d.-a).

## History

Gōjū-ryū traces to Kanryō Higaonna (1853–1915) of Naha, Okinawa, who studied Chinese kung-fu in Fuzhou and taught Miyagi Chōjun (1888–1953), who from the 1920s systematized the art, opened a garden dojo, and named it Gōjū-ryū in 1929 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; IOGKF, n.d.-a). After Miyagi's death on 8 October 1953, his senior students — including Eiichi Miyazato, whom a 1954 committee chose as successor — carried the style worldwide; Gōgen Yamaguchi (1909–1989), the "Cat" of Kumamoto, founded the Goju-kai in 1935 and spread Gōjū-ryū across Japan and abroad (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Morio Higaonna founded the IOGKF in 1979 in Poole, England — recognized by the Nippon Kobudo Kyokai in 1998 with Higaonna as representative master — and after his departure in 2022 the federation, now based in Burlington, Ontario, is led by Tetsuji Nakamura (IOGKF, n.d.-a). Among the style's icons are Chōjun Miyagi himself, Gōgen Yamaguchi, and Eiichi Miyazato (1935–2009), the tenth-dan successor (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
