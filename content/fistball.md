## Basic Information

Fistball (German: *Faustball*) is a team sport in which two teams of five players hit a light leather ball over a net with the fist or forearm — only the fist and forearm may touch the ball — on an outdoor field; it is the direct cousin of [Volleyball](volleyball.html) and [Beach Volleyball](beach-volleyball.html), with a similar net and court but a ball that may bounce once (IFA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed worldwide by the International Fistball Association (IFA), founded in 1960, which organizes the quadrennial Fistball World Championships — men's since 1968, women's since 1994 — the IFA World Tour for clubs, and the sport has been on the World Games program since 1985 (IFA, n.d.-a). See [Fistball Beach](fistball-beach.html) and [Fistball Indoor](fistball-indoor.html) on this site for the variants (International Fistball Association, n.d.-a) (International Fistball Association, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026, the leading women's club side in the IFA Fistball World Tour was Sogipa of Porto Alegre, Brazil, with 2,360 points, while the men's World Tour was won by TV Wohnplan Enns of Austria with 3,910 points; Germany are the reigning men's world champions (2023), and the next men's World Championship is Novo Hamburgo, Brazil, in 2027, and at the 2026 IFA Club World Cup in Llanquihue, Chile, and the U18 World Championship in Reiden, Switzerland, Germany (men) and Austria (women) took the titles (IFA, n.d.-a).

 Within the net and court team ball games family on this site, its closest relatives are [Volleyball](volleyball.html), [Fistball Beach](fistball-beach.html), [Fistball Indoor](fistball-indoor.html), [Beach Volleyball](beach-volleyball.html), [Beach Volleyball 4x4](beach-volleyball-4x4.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Fistball is played 5 against 5 on an outdoor field of 50 m × 20 m divided by a net; the net is 2.00 m high at the top edge for men and 1.90 m for women (IFA, n.d.-b). The ball is a leather-covered ball of 340–360 g with a circumference of 65–68 cm (men; lighter for youth classes), inflated to 0.6–0.7 bar (IFA, n.d.-b). A team may contact the ball at most three times, and the ball may touch the ground at most once between contacts; a player may not hit the ball twice in succession, and catching or holding the ball is a fault (IFA, n.d.-b). The serve is taken from behind the back boundary line, the server may step on the line, and the ball must cross the net into the opponent's court; the attack line is 3 m from the net on each side, and a player may not touch the net or cross the center line under the net (IFA, n.d.-b). A set is won at 11 points with a two-point lead, capped at 15, and a match is best of five sets (IFA, n.d.-b). Fouls — net touch, line crossing, double hit, carry, ball out, or dangerous play — award a point to the opponent, and the referee may caution or exclude players for unsportsmanlike conduct (IFA, n.d.-b). Men and women play under identical rules except the net height and ball weight in youth classes; age classifications U14, U16 and U18 exist alongside the senior game, and the IFA also sanctions beach and indoor variants with adapted courts (IFA, n.d.-b). Substitutions are limited per set, and teams have one timeout per set (IFA, n.d.-b).

## History

Fistball descends from the Italian Renaissance game of *pallone* and the Roman *harpastum*: the earliest references to a fist ball game date to the 3rd century AD, and the Florentine game of *scaino*, precursor of *pallone col bracciale*, was played from the 16th century and spread the net game across Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.). The modern game was brought to Germany around 1870 by the gymnastics teacher Georg Weber, who introduced it as a team game for men; the first public demonstration was at the German Gymnastics Festival in Dresden in 1885, and the first written rules were published in 1894 by Weber and Schnell (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first German men's championship was held in 1913 in Leipzig, where LLB Frankfurt defeated 1879 MTV Munich 114–101 — the famous long rallies of fistball were born there; by 1927 about 12,000 teams played in Germany, and the first women's championship followed in 1921 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game was introduced to the United States in 1911 by Christopher Carlton and spread with German emigrants to Brazil, Argentina, Paraguay and Namibia, where it remains popular today (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Fistball Association (IFA) was founded in 1960; the first men's World Championship took place in 1968 in Linz, Austria, won by West Germany, and the women's World Championship debuted in 1994 in Buenos Aires, won by Germany (IFA, n.d.-a). Fistball has been a World Games sport since 1985 in Duisburg (Wikipedia, n.d.).
