## Basic Information

The elimination race — nicknamed the "Devil take the hindmost" — is a bunch-start track cycling race in which the last rider across the line at each intermediate sprint is eliminated until only the final two or three contest a sprint for the win (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). It is a standalone event at the UCI Track Cycling World Championships and the UEC European Championships and has been part of the Olympic omnium since London 2012, becoming a standalone Olympic event for both women and men at Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Among the current leading riders — women first — are Ireland's Lara Gillespie, who made history for her country by winning the 2025 world title in Santiago, Chile, and Italy's Elia Viviani on the men's side, the 2025 world champion who claimed his third elimination title in four years in the final where he beat home favorite Campbell Stewart (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

All riders start together after a neutralized lap; on a standard 250 m track the riders contest a sprint every two laps, and the last rider of the bunch to cross the line — judged by the position of the rear wheel — is eliminated and must leave the track via the inner lane (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). The bell rings to signal the final lap, the remaining two or three riders then sprint it out for the win, and a photo finish decides which rider is eliminated if two cross together; being a lap down does not itself eliminate a rider — only the placing at each sprint line matters (Wikipedia, n.d.). Elite fields typically number 20–24 starters, so a race on a 250 m track runs about 19 elimination laps, roughly 5 km of continuous high-intensity effort (Wikipedia, n.d.). In the omnium, the elimination race is the third of four events (scratch, tempo, elimination, points) and its placings award 40, 38, 36 points and so on toward the overall title (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). Men and women race the identical format in separate events, and the race is governed by Part 3 of the UCI Cycling Regulations, which require a lap counter and bell at the finish line (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.).

## History

The elimination format descends from the "devil" races of the great indoor six-day meetings of the 1920s and 1930s, where the last rider each lap was eliminated; it entered the omnium at the 2010 UCI Track Cycling World Championships and the Olympic programme with the omnium at London 2012, and was added as a standalone Olympic event for women and men at Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.). New Zealand's Campbell Stewart (b. 1995), a long-time omnium and elimination specialist, retired in 2025 after taking silver in the world final in Santiago — the "perfect retirement gift" for Elia Viviani, who won his third elimination world title in four years there (Wikipedia, n.d.). Ireland's Lara Gillespie won the women's world title at the same 2025 championships in Santiago, 22–26 October 2025, the first Irish rider to do so (Wikipedia, n.d.).
