## Basic Information

WT Taekwondo is the Olympic branch of taekwondo, governed by World Taekwondo (founded 1973 as the World Taekwondo Federation, renamed in 2017) together with the Kukkiwon headquarters in Seoul: the dynamic, sport-oriented form of the art known for its Taegeuk poomsae, electronic body protectors and spectacular head kicks. It is the version of taekwondo contested at the Olympic and Paralympic Games and world championships.

## Rules

WT kyorugi sparring is full-contact on a marked mat over rounds within weight categories: points are scored by kicks and punches to the trunk (recorded by electronic body protectors) and kicks to the head (scored by electronic sensors), with turning kicks and high spinning techniques earning premium points, and penalties enforcing safe competition that limits permitted techniques. Poomsae competition judges the Taegeuk and black-belt forms for accuracy, power and presentation, and the Kukkiwon certifies dan (black belt) rank through its standardized curriculum. Competitions run single-elimination brackets with repechage at the Olympic Games, each round resets the score, and video replay lets officials review scoring disputes.

## History

World Taekwondo was founded in Seoul in 1973 alongside the Kukkiwon, established the year before as the art's central gymnasium and technical authority, and it quickly became the international federation of the sport: world championships began in 1973, the sport entered the Olympic programme as a demonstration in Seoul 1988 and Barcelona 1992, and became a full medal sport at Sydney 2000. The 2010s saw rules and technology change the game, with electronic body protectors, instant video replay and head-sensor scoring improving both spectacle and safety, and the Paralympic debut of para taekwondo at Tokyo 2020 made WT the global flagship of the art, with member national associations across the world. Renamed World Taekwondo in 2017, the federation continues to expand its competition formats, ranking system and youth development worldwide.
