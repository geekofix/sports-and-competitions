## Basic Information

WT taekwondo — the Olympic style — is governed by World Taekwondo (WT, formerly the World Taekwondo Federation, WTF), established on 28 May 1973 at the Kukkiwon in Seoul by 35 representatives, today a federation of more than 210 member nations with president Choue Chung-won since 2004, renamed from WTF to World Taekwondo in June 2017; the IOC recognized it on 17 July 1980, and taekwondo has been an Olympic medal sport since Sydney 2000 (World Taekwondo, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Its two competition disciplines are kyorugi (electronic-scored sparring) and poomsae (forms), and para taekwondo joined the Paralympic programme at Tokyo 2020 (World Taekwondo, n.d.). At the 2025 World Championships in Wuxi, China, Brazil's Maria Clara Pacheco defeated home favorite Kim Yu-jin 2–0 in the women's –57 kg final (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the current top female athletes — named first — are Pacheco (2025 world champion and world No. 1 at –57 kg), Paris 2024 Olympic champions Kim Yu-jin (–57 kg), Viviana Márton of Hungary (–67 kg) and Althéa Laurin of France (+67 kg, pictured below); leading men include Park Tae-joon (–58 kg), Arian Salimi (+80 kg) and Firas Katoussi (World Taekwondo, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.).

## Rules

Kyorugi is fought on a matted octagonal contest area with electronic PSS sensors in the hogu (trunk protector) and headgear: bouts run three rounds of two minutes with one-minute rests; a punch to the trunk scores 1 point, a kick to the trunk 2 points, a kick to the head 3 points, and turning or spinning techniques earn bonus points; an eight-point lead or a knockout ends the match immediately, and ties go to a golden-point round (World Taekwondo, n.d.). World championships and the Olympics are single-elimination with repechage, at eight weight classes per gender at the worlds (men –54, –58, –63, –68, –74, –80, –87, +87 kg; women –46, –49, –53, –57, –62, –67, –73, +73 kg) and four per gender at the Olympics (men –58, –68, –80, +80 kg; women –49, –57, –67, +67 kg); men and women compete under identical rules in separate divisions (World Taekwondo, n.d.). Para taekwondo (K44 class) has been on the Paralympic programme since Tokyo 2020 with head contact and punches prohibited, and poomsae — including the freestyle discipline — is judged under the same WT rules; the mixed team event was added at Paris 2024 (Olympics, n.d.; World Taekwondo, n.d.). The official rule source is the World Taekwondo Competition Rules (World Taekwondo, n.d.).

## History

After Korea's liberation in 1945 the kwan schools rebuilt Korean fighting arts; President Park Chung-hee declared taekwondo the national sport in 1971, the KTA Central Dojang opened in 1972 and became the Kukkiwon, and on 28 May 1973 the World Taekwondo Federation held its inaugural meeting there, staging the first world championships in Seoul with 19 nations; in 1977 the nine kwans recognized the Kukkiwon and WTF (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The IOC recognized the WTF on 17 July 1980, taekwondo was demonstrated at Seoul 1988 and Barcelona 1992 and adopted for Sydney 2000 on 4 September 1994, Kim Un-yong (1931–2017) led the WTF from 1973 to 2004 before Choue Chung-won, a 2014 protocol with the ITF reunited the two styles, and the federation was renamed World Taekwondo in June 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Taekwondo, n.d.). Icons include founder Choi Hong Hi (1918–2002), Hadi Saei of Iran (Olympic gold 2004 and 2008, retired 2012), Steven López of the USA (Olympic gold 2000, five world titles, retired) and Brigitte Yagüe of Spain (six world titles, Olympic silver 2012, retired 2013) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
