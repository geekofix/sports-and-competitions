## Basic Information

Ski cross is the racing discipline of freestyle skiing: groups of four skiers race head-to-head down a specially built course of jumps, rollers, banked turns and straights, in a spectacle part alpine racing, part motocross. Governed by the International Ski Federation (FIS) as one of its freestyle skiing disciplines, it has been an Olympic event \u2014 for men and women \u2014 since the Vancouver 2010 Winter Games.

## Rules

Competitions begin with timed qualification runs to seed the field, after which skiers race in heats of four down the course side by side; the top two of each heat advance through rounds of 16 or 32 to the final, with a small final deciding minor placings. The course features uphill and downhill rollers, gap jumps, step-downs, banked turns and flat sections, testing both speed and technical line choice; intentional contact is forbidden \u2014 pushing, pulling or holding an opponent draws disqualification \u2014 while incidental contact in the racing jumble is accepted, and overtaking must be clean. The first athlete across the line wins the heat, so races are decided on finishing order and not the clock; a racer's tactics of drafting and choosing inside lines often make the fastest qualifier vulnerable to pile-ups. Competitors wear standard alpine-style equipment including chin-guard helmets, back protection and specifically designed ski-cross skis, and the format echoes the snowboard cross discipline introduced to the Olympics two years earlier (2006).

## History

Ski cross grew out of snowboard cross' success as a television-friendly mass-start race on a course of jumps and berms, and out of the older knock-out downhill exhibition events of the 1990s; it was adopted into FIS freestyle skiing early in the 2000s and joined the FIS Freestyle World Cup circuit. The International Olympic Committee added ski cross to the Olympic programme for the 2010 Vancouver Games \u2014 with the FIS adding snowboard cross at Turin 2006 as the model \u2014 and the sport's first Olympic champions were Michael Schmid of Switzerland and Ashleigh McIvor of Canada, before France's Jean-Fr\u00e9d\u00e9ric Chapuis and Canada's Marielle Thompson succeeded them at Sochi 2014. France has become the discipline's powerhouse at Olympic and world level, its national federation churning out racers from its alpine development system; the FIS stages ski cross as part of the freestyle World Cup and world championships, and the discipline remains a fan favourite for its crashes, drafting duels and photo finishes \u2014 qualities that have kept it on the Olympic programme through Milan Cortina 2026.
