## Basic Information

Ski cross is a freestyle skiing race in which up to four athletes start simultaneously and race down a course of jumps, rollers and banked turns, with the first two across the finish line advancing; it has been an Olympic discipline since Vancouver 2010 and is governed by the International Ski Federation (FIS) (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At Milano Cortina 2026, raced at the Livigno Snow Park on 20–21 February, Germany's Daniela Maier won the women's gold — the first German gold medal in Olympic freestyle skiing — 0.14 seconds ahead of Switzerland's Fanny Smith, with the defending champion Sandra Näslund of Sweden taking bronze, while Italy's Simone Deromedis won the men's gold in a home-nation 1–2 ahead of teammate Federico Tomasoni and Switzerland's Alex Fiva (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Among today's elite, the women's field is headed by Germany's Daniela Maier — Olympic champion at Milano Cortina 2026 after bronze at Beijing 2022 — Fanny Smith of Switzerland, the 2025 world champion and 2026 silver medallist, and Sandra Näslund of Sweden, Olympic champion 2022, three-time world champion and winner of five ski cross World Cups, while the men's side is led by Italy's Simone Deromedis, the 2026 Olympic and 2023 world champion, alongside Switzerland's Ryan Regez, the Beijing 2022 Olympic champion (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men and women race identical courses and formats in separate events, with no rule differences between genders (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

 Within the winter ski and snowboard family on this site, its closest relatives are [Skiing](skiing.html), [Snowboarding](snowboarding.html), [Alpine Skiing Downhill](alpine-skiing-downhill.html), [Alpine Skiing Giant Slalom](alpine-skiing-giant-slalom.html), [Alpine Skiing Slalom](alpine-skiing-slalom.html); the full list of 38 related pages is under Important Links.

## Rules

Ski cross is governed by the FIS (https://www.fis-ski.com/) within freestyle skiing. Each competition begins with a timed qualification run — one skier against the clock — and the fastest 32 athletes (the fastest 16 if fewer than 32 compete) advance to the knockout rounds, where athletes race in heats of four and the top two from each heat progress; a big final decides places 1–4 and a small final places 5–8 (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Courses are built from natural and artificial features — jumps, rollers, banked turns and compressions — and, unlike alpine skiing, there are no gates; the racing line itself is the challenge (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The central rule is fair racing: intentional contact with another competitor, such as grabbing or blocking to gain an advantage, is grounds for immediate disqualification, although incidental contact while passing is common and often causes falls (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Athletes wear alpine-style helmets, goggles and dedicated ski cross skis; men and women use the same course specifications and race format, competing in separate events (FIS, n.d.).

## History

The idea of a multi-skier obstacle race was born in the late 1970s at Alyeska Resort in Alaska, where Scott Hunter led races down natural bobsled-like gullies, and again in the late 1980s, when Jim "Too Tall" Essick of Recreational Sports Marketing pitched a motocross-for-skiing concept that became "boardercross" on television in 1991; the skier version, ski cross, was recognized by the FIS in 2003 and joined the FIS Freestyle World Cup in 2004 and the World Championships in 2005 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.). It was a staple of the Winter X Games as SkierX from 1998 to 2012 — Canada's Aleisha Cline won four gold medals between 1999 and 2003 — before the Olympic debut at Vancouver 2010 on Cypress Mountain, where Switzerland's Michael Schmid (born 1982) won the men's event and Canada's Ashleigh McIvor (born 1983, retired 2012) the first women's Olympic gold ahead of Norway's Hedda Berntsen and France's Marion Josserand (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Later Olympic champions include France's clean sweep of the men's podium at Sochi 2014 led by Jean-Frédéric Chapuis, Canada's Marielle Thompson and Kelsey Serwa (women's gold 2014 and 2018), Canada's Brady Leman (men, 2018), and Switzerland's Ryan Regez and Sweden's Sandra Näslund at Beijing 2022, where Fanny Smith and Daniela Maier were both awarded bronze medals after the Court of Arbitration for Sport ruling of 13 December 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
