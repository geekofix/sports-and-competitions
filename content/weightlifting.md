## Basic Information

Weightlifting (Olympic weightlifting) is a strength sport in which athletes lift a barbell loaded with rubber-plates overhead in two specific lifts — the snatch, a single continuous movement from floor to full squat-and-stand lockout, and the clean and jerk, a two-part lift from floor to shoulders and then overhead. Success is measured by the total of the best valid lift in each discipline, contested in bodyweight categories for men and women.

It is one of the oldest sports on the modern Olympic programme, judged by three referees against strict technical rules under the International Weightlifting Federation, and it is practised competitively worldwide, with China, historically strong Eastern European nations, and Iran among its powerhouses.

## Rules

In the snatch the bar is pulled from the platform in one motion to overhead while the lifter drops into a squat and stands to finish arms locked. The clean and jerk moves the bar to the shoulders in one motion, then drives it overhead from a split or squat position; both lifts must finish with straight arms, feet in line and the bar under control until the referee's down signal. Each lifter gets three attempts per lift at meets, weights declared attempt by attempt in a competition of ascending bars, and the heaviest valid snatch plus the heaviest valid clean and jerk form the total. Competition rules cover bodyweight categories, referee majority decisions, timing (one minute, two for consecutive lifts), bar loading errors, and doping controls enforced at all levels.

## History

Weightlifting appeared at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896 and has been on the programme continuously since 1920; the early Olympics even included one-hand and two-hand lifts before the two-lift format settled. The International Weightlifting Federation was founded in 1905 and has governed the sport's world championships since 1891. Women debuted at the Sydney 2000 Olympics, and records have been rewritten as technique shifted from raw pressing to explosive squat-style lifting, while the sport remains a centrepiece of strength culture portrayed in gyms worldwide.
