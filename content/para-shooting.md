## Basic Information

Para shooting is target shooting for athletes with physical or visual impairments, governed by World Shooting Para Sport under the International Shooting Sport Federation umbrella and classified as SH1 (the firearm can be held and operated without a support stand) or SH2 (a support stand is required) (World Shooting Para Sport, n.d.-c; International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.). It has been on the Paralympic programme since Toronto 1976 and was staged at Paris 2024 at the Châteauroux Shooting Centre (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.).

The programme comprises rifle events R1–R9 (10 m air rifle standing/prone SH1 and SH2, 50 m rifle three positions SH1, 50 m rifle prone SH2, men's and women's or mixed) and pistol events P1, P2 (10 m air pistol SH1) and P4 (50 m pistol SH1), with qualification plus eight-finalist finals and decimal scoring (World Shooting Para Sport, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Leading the women today: Avani Lekhara of India (pictured) won the R2 10 m air rifle standing SH1 gold at Tokyo 2020 and again at Paris 2024 and added individual bronze plus women's team gold at the 2026 WSPS World Championships in Changwon, alongside team-mate Mona Agarwal's silver; Slovakia's Veronika Vadovicová won the mixed prone SH1 gold at Paris 2024 (2026, September 16; 2024, September). On the men's side, Korea's Jinho Park added the R7 50 m rifle three positions SH1 world title with a world record 354.7 at Changwon 2026 to his Paris 2024 Paralympic gold (2026, September 13). The 2026 WSPS World Championships at the Changwon International Shooting Range (from 13 September 2026) were the first held there and awarded six LA28 quota places (2026, September 13; World Shooting Para Sport, n.d.-a).

## Rules

ISSF rules apply with WSPS adaptations: 60-shot qualifications at 10 m or 50 m depending on the event, followed by finals with eight shooters and progressively eliminated shots, scored in decimals (World Shooting Para Sport, n.d.-c; International Shooting Sport Federation, n.d.).

SH1 athletes may shoot standing or prone without a stand (a stand is permitted but not required), while SH2 athletes must use an ISSF-approved support stand; visually impaired shooters may be assisted in loading; men's, women's and mixed events are offered, and team events aggregate three athletes' scores (World Shooting Para Sport, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Shooting has been contested at every Paralympics since Toronto 1976, with the modern SH1/SH2 classes introduced in the 2010s; the WSPS World Championships and World Cup circuit now run annually, with the 2026 Worlds in Changwon, South Korea (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Shooting Para Sport, n.d.-a).

Among the icons is Australia's Libby Kosmala, pictured at London 2012, while Iran's Sareh Javanmardi completed a three-peat in the 10 m air pistol SH1 at Paris 2024 (2024, September; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
