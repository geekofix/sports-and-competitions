## Basic Information

Land sailing (sand yachting, land yachting) is racing wind-powered vehicles — land yachts with sails — across beaches, dry lake beds and grass fields. The vehicles are divided into classes by FISLY, the Fédération Internationale de Sand et Land Yachting (International Land and Sandyachting Federation), founded in April 1962; the same federation governs kite buggying (Class 8). FISLY has organized European Championships since 1963 and World Championships since 1975, and top yachts exceed 100 km/h.

## Rules

FISLY's International Sailing and Racing Rules govern construction, sail area, safety equipment and right-of-way. Races are run as time trials, mass-start circuit races or speed records on a marked course, usually on a wide beach or dry lake; the fastest yacht around the course or over a timed section wins. Classes include the one-design Class 5 and Class Standard yachts (about 5.5 m long, open cockpits), the faster Class 2 and 3, and the unlimited "open" classes, plus miniyachts and the Class 8 kite buggy. Pilots wear helmets, and courses are set with turning marks and a start/finish line; protests are decided by a jury under the racing rules.

## History

Wind-powered carriages date back centuries — the Dutch used them on beaches in the 1600s — but modern land yachting organized around the turn of the 20th century in France and Britain. FISLY was founded in 1962 by Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, France and Great Britain and has grown to member associations across Europe, the Americas, Australia, New Zealand, Africa and Asia. Famous venues include the beaches of Hoylake and Saint-Pol-de-Léon in France, Westkapelle in the Netherlands, Ivanpah Dry Lake in Nevada and the beaches of New Zealand's South Island. Land yachts have set world speed records above 200 km/h, and the sport remains a grassroots, low-cost alternative to sailing.
