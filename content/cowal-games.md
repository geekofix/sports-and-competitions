## Basic Information

The Cowal Highland Gathering (Cowal Games) is the annual Highland games held at Dunoon Stadium in Dunoon, Argyll and Bute, over the final weekend of August (27–29 August 2026). First held on 11 August 1894, it is the largest Highland games in Scotland, drawing around 3,500 competitors and some 23,000 visitors from around the world. The programme centres on the World Highland Dancing Championships finals (top dancers from each age category dance for the world title on the Saturday), around 150 pipe bands, and the heavy athletics — shot put with the 16 lb shot and the traditional 34 lb Cowal Stone, caber toss, weight over the bar, hammer throw — plus a 5 km fun run, the Tom Odhar hill race and a Scottish backhold wrestling competition (since 2007). Women have competed in Highland dancing since the first Juvenile World Championship in 1932 and the Adult World Championship since 1948; women's shot put has its own title (Emmerleigh Smith won in 2025). Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [Braemar Gathering](braemar-gathering.html), [World Nomad Games](world-nomad-games.html), [Cheese Rolling](cheese-rolling.html), [World Indigenous Games](world-indigenous-games.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Cowal Gathering, n.d.-a).

## Rules

As a multi-discipline festival, each event follows its own rules: heavy events under SHGA rules (shot put from a stop, caber toss for accuracy and distance, weight over the bar for height); the World Highland Dancing Championships use set dances judged on points, with qualifiers (top 20 per age category from the Scottish Open Championships on the Thursday) and finals on the Saturday — Juvenile (12–15), Junior (16–17) and Adult (18+); pipe bands are graded and judged on music and marching/drill; the hill race runs from the stadium to the top of Tom Odhar and back. Competitors enter in gender and age classes; the 2020 and 2021 editions were cancelled (COVID-19) (Cowal Gathering, n.d.-a).

## History

The first organised games in Dunoon date to 1871; the Cowal Gathering itself was the idea of local man Robert Cameron and was first held on 11 August 1894 with about 2,000 attendees (gate receipts £60). Paddle-steamer access from Glasgow boosted its popularity, and it featured in early BBC television broadcasts. The Argyll Shield for the Grade 1 pipe band competition was donated by Princess Louise, Duchess of Argyll, in 1906; until the World Pipe Band Championships began in Glasgow in 1947, Cowal was regarded as the premier pipe band competition and decided the season's Champion of Champions. The Juvenile World Dancing Championship began in 1932 (first winner Gladys S. Bruce), the Adult in 1948 (May Falconer) and the Junior in 1961; the event became a three-day gathering in 2003 and added a live music tent in 2007 (Cowal Gathering, n.d.-b).
