## Basic Information

Evala is the traditional wrestling of the Kabyè people of Togo, a form of West African lutte traditionnelle: fighters in a sand ring try to throw each other or touch the ground with any part but the feet, without blows (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Evala competitions in the Kabyè region culminate each July in a tournament that is both a sporting and an initiation event for young men, drawing thousands of spectators (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Within the combat sports family on this site, its closest relatives are [Laamb](laamb.html), [Kokawa](kokawa.html), [Wrestling](wrestling.html), [Belt Wrestling](belt-wrestling.html), [Oil Wrestling](oil-wrestling.html), [Beach Wrestling](beach-wrestling.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Bouts take place in a circular sand arena; victory by fall, ground touch or ring exit. The traditional Evala tournament runs over several weeks in July with age-group categories, and winners are celebrated as local heroes; international-style lutte traditionnelle (sans frappe) rules apply at Francophonie and African championship events. Men's and women's categories are organised (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Evala is rooted in Kabyè warrior initiation rites; the modern tournament format dates to the mid-20th century and was revived as a cultural festival. It is inscribed in Togo's intangible heritage alongside other lutte traditionnelle forms (Wikipedia, n.d.).
