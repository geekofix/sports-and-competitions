## Basic Information

Bocce is the Italian boules-style game in which players roll or throw large wooden balls to land as close as possible to a small target ball, the pallino. It is played by teams of two or four (two or four balls each) on a marked court of about 27.5 m length, and is governed internationally by the Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale (CBI) for the punto/raffa/volo specialties and the umbrella World Pétanque & Bowls Federation (WPBF), IOC-recognized since 1986.

## Rules

Under CBI/WPBF rules, each frame starts with a coin toss; one team rolls the pallino into the court between the designated lines, then throws its bocce balls alternately, with the team whose ball is farthest from the pallino throwing until it gets closest. Points are scored at the end of each frame: one point for each ball closer to the pallino than the opponent's closest ball. Matches are played to a set number of points (often 13-16) over multiple frames. Balls are 107 mm and about 920 g in tournament play; the court is a long, narrow lane (about 26.5-27.5 x 4 m) with side and end boards.

## History

Bocce descends from Roman ball games (the Romans played a version around 264 BC) and became standardized in Italy in the 19th century; it spread worldwide with Italian emigration, especially to the Americas. The CBI was founded on 3 September 1983 in Chiasso, Switzerland, by 13 nations, and the World Pétanque & Bowls Federation (WPBF, formerly CMSB) was founded in Monaco on 21 December 1985 to coordinate pétanque, raffa, volo and lawn bowls. Bocce was a demonstration sport at the 1988 Seoul Olympics and is played at the World Games.
