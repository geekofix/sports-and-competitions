## Basic Information

Bocce is the family of boules games in which players roll or throw balls to get as close as possible to a small target ball called the jack or pallino; the three main competitive disciplines are punto (pétanque-style), raffa and volo (lyonnaise). Worldwide governance is split between the Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale (Confederazione Boccistica Internazionale [CBI], n.d.-a), founded in Chiasso, Switzerland, on September 3, 1983, and the World Petanque and Bowls Federation (World Petanque and Bowls Federation [WPBF], n.d.-b), founded in Monaco on December 21, 1985, which groups pétanque, raffa and volo. The 2026 CBI World Seniors Bocce Championship was held in Garibaldi, Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil (October 12–17, 2026), with the United States team competing after Ava Della Croce and Andy Zimmerman won precision-shooting gold at the US nationals (CBI, n.d.-a). The game sits in a wider family of target-ball games that also includes French [Pétanque](petanque.html) and [Boule Lyonnaise](boule-lyonnaise.html), the rolling-bowl games [Lawn Bowls](lawn-bowls.html), [Indoor Bowls](indoor-bowls.html) and [Crown Green Bowls](crown-green-bowls.html), and the throwing and sliding cousins [Irish Road Bowling](irish-road-bowling.html), [Varpa](varpa.html), [Ice Stock Sport](ice-stock-sport.html), [Shuffleboard](shuffleboard.html) and [Table Shuffleboard](table-shuffleboard.html), alongside the Paralympic [Boccia](boccia.html); the full list is under Important Links.

The women's side is active at every level: women's and mixed teams compete at the CBI and WPBF world championships, and the European Championship for Clubs in Bergamo (November 2022) already fielded eight women's club teams alongside ten men's (CBI, n.d.-a).

## Rules

A bocce court is a flat, bounded surface about 27.5 m long and 4 m wide for international play (pétanque/punto courts are typically 15 m × 4 m). Each player or team has a set number of balls (three per player in triples, two in doubles, six in singles) and alternates throwing with the opponent; the team whose ball is closest to the pallino scores one point per ball closer than the opponent's best ball, and ends are played to 13 points in international pétanque or to a set number of points in raffa (CBI, n.d.-a; WPBF, n.d.-b). Balls are resin or synthetic, 106–107 mm in diameter and 900–920 g in weight for raffa; the pallino is much smaller. In raffa the ball must touch the ground at least once after the first bounce and may be played with a "spock" (spin); in volo/lyonnaise the ball must be thrown over a central obstacle string and land as close as possible to the pallino, with a maximum of three throws per end in some formats. Fouls include moving the feet past the throwing circle, throwing the wrong ball and touching the pallino; men's and women's events use identical rules and equipment, and youth categories shorten the court and use lighter balls (WPBF, n.d.-b).

## History

Boules games are ancient — the Greeks and Romans played versions with stone balls — and modern bocce took shape in Italy and Provence in the 19th century. Pétanque was codified in 1910 in La Ciotat, France, when Ernest Pitiot invented the name from the Provençal "pè de tanca" (feet together); raffa and volo developed in northern Italy and Lyon. The painting Il Gioco delle Bocce by Ruggero Focardi (1882) shows the game as a 19th-century Italian pastime (CBI, n.d.-a). The CBI and WPBF (formerly CMSB) were founded in 1983 and 1985 and have run world championships since; the last joint raffa/volo world championship gathered 52 teams from 37 countries in Mersin, Turkey, in 2022 (WPBF, n.d.-b).
