## Basic Information

Gidigbo is a traditional game of West Africa (Nigeria/Benin region) played with stones or seeds on a marked board or ground grid, a member of the widespread sowing-game family that includes mancala variants such as ayoayo and oware (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is played by children and adults at markets and festivals. Within the folk and festival games on this site, its closest relatives are [Ampe](ampe.html), [Asafo Atwele](asafo-atwele.html), [Abula](abula.html), [Langa](langa.html), [Mancala](mancala.html), [Carrom](carrom.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Played on a small board or a grid drawn in the sand, with pebbles or seeds as counters; players sow and capture according to local rules, variants closely resembling mancala/ayo games. No standard international rule set exists; the game is governed by local custom (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Gidigbo belongs to the ancient West African sowing-game tradition documented by ethnographers since the 19th century; it remains a common children's game in Nigeria and Benin (Wikipedia, n.d.).
