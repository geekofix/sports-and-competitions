## Basic Information

Corrida de tora (tora race, "log race") is a relay race of Brazil's indigenous peoples, showcased at the World Indigenous Games: nine or ten runners sprint around a track, taking turns lifting and carrying a heavy tree trunk (tora, around 100 kg) on their shoulders — a test of strength and teamwork rooted in the use of trunks in construction and warfare. It was a highlight of the 2015 World Indigenous Games in Palmas and the 2017 edition in Maskwacis, Canada. Men's and women's teams compete. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [World Indigenous Games](world-indigenous-games.html), [Xikunahati](xikunahati.html), [Rustic Race](rustic-race.html), [Lumberjack Championships](lumberjack-championships.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (LifeGate, 2015).

## Rules

Teams of 9–10 runners line up on a track; the first runner lifts the tora log onto his shoulders and sprints a set leg, then passes it to the next runner, and so on around the course; the team that completes the circuit first wins. Rules at the Games: fixed exchange zones, log must be carried (not rolled), falls are allowed with a restart; women's and men's categories; mixed demonstration relays. Safety: medical checks, clear lanes (LifeGate, 2015).

## History

Carrying heavy trunks is an everyday task in Amazonian villages; the corrida de tora formalizes it as a race. It was filmed at the 2015 World Indigenous Games (Palmas) and contested again at the 2017 Games in Maskwacis, Alberta. The Xakriabá people of Minas Gerais keep the tradition (documented 2023) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
