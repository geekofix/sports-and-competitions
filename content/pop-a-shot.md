## Basic Information

Pop-A-Shot is the original arcade basketball game, an electronic shooting machine on which players score as many baskets as possible within a countdown, typically 30 to 60 seconds, using small scaled basketballs on a sloped return ramp. Invented in 1981, the brand became so dominant that its name is often used generically for all arcade basketball games, and it is now also an organized competitive sport with a national championship televised on ESPN8: The Ocho.

## Rules

A standard arcade basketball unit has one or two hoops side by side, an electronic scoreboard with a countdown clock and infrared sensors that register each made basket; shots score points, with many machines awarding bonus points or extra time when the player reaches a scoring threshold before the clock expires. In tournament play, all competitors use the same machine and settings, and the highest score within the time limit wins; tied scores are broken by a shoot-off. Shots must be released before the buzzer. In the branded national championship format, qualifying rounds at bars and entertainment venues feed a finals field in which players shoot a fixed number of timed rounds, with cumulative score deciding the champion.

## History

The original Pop-A-Shot was invented in 1981 by Ken Cochran, a former college basketball coach in Salina, Kansas, who built the game so that children could practice shooting rhythm, and the coin-operated machine became a fixture of American arcades and bars, spawning an entire industry of imitators. The company pioneered electronic scoring, infrared sensors and steel frames, and later added a successful line of home basketball arcade games. As the coin-op market saturated, Pop-A-Shot largely left that world and licensed its name for home games, while the competitive circuit grew: in 2024 the brand hosted its first national championship in 25 years, with about 250 players at a Chicago qualifier and the finals aired on ESPN8: The Ocho — the channel's annual celebration of obscure sports. A new coin-operated machine, the Pop-A-Shot Elite with a 49-inch screen and QR-code player profiles, marked the brand's return to arcades in 2025.
