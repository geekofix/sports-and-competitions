## Basic Information

Wildwater canoeing (also called whitewater racing or downriver racing) is a paddling discipline in which kayaks and canoes race down fast-flowing whitewater rivers as fast as possible — either in a sprint of a few hundred metres over a single rapid or over a longer classic course of several kilometres — with boats designed for speed and stability in turbulent water. Paddlers race individually against the clock, choosing their own racing line down the river rather than negotiating gates.

It is distinguished from canoe slalom by its point-to-point format, and it is governed by the International Canoe Federation, with world championships for sprint and classic distances.

## Rules

Competitions are held in two formats: the sprint, roughly 200 to 600 metres of continuous whitewater raced in timed runs, and the classic, 3 to 6 kilometres of river run start-to-finish — each paddler's time from the flying start to the finish line deciding placement, usually with two runs and the best counting in sprint. Boats are ICF-specification wildwater kayaks and canoes (long, low-volume, high-speed hulls), classes are split between men and women in kayak, Canadian canoe with one paddle, and masters age groups; racing rules set safety requirements, course homologation, requirement to wear buoyancy aids and helmets, and procedures for restarts after outside interference.

## History

Downriver racing emerged in the mid-twentieth century as competitive paddlers on European rivers tested themselves point-to-point through rapids, building on pre-war river touring and post-war slalom booms; the discipline's growth in equipment matched the innovation of fibreglass and plastic canoes after the Second World War. The International Canoe Federation brought world championships into being in the late twentieth century, with wildwater worlds held from 1959 onward in classic, to 1970s formats standardising classes, and sprint discipline worlds from 1999 along with the classic-distance decline and revival by the 2010s — and European series nations such as France, Italy, Switzerland, Czechia and Great Britain producing champions.
