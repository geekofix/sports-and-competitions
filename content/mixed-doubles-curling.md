## Basic Information

Mixed doubles curling is the two-player version of [curling](curling.html) in which each team consists of exactly one woman and one man, who alternate delivering the six stones per end (World Curling Federation, n.d.-a). It is governed by the World Curling Federation (WCF) and played on the same ice sheet, with the same stones and house, as the four-player game, but a match lasts eight ends instead of ten (World Curling Federation, n.d.-b). The event made its Olympic debut at PyeongChang 2018, where Canada won the first gold medal, and at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics (4–10 February, Cortina Olympic Stadium) ten nations played a round robin followed by semifinals and medal games: Sweden's Isabella Wranå and Rasmus Wranå won gold 6–5 over the United States, Cory Thiesse and Korey Dropkin took silver — the first US medal in the event and the first Olympic curling medal for an American woman — and Italy's Stefania Constantini and Amos Mosaner won bronze (Olympics.com, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women in the discipline include Olympic champion Isabella Wranå of Sweden and Olympic silver medalist Cory Thiesse of the United States, who also won the World Mixed Doubles Championship together with their partners (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Mixed doubles follows the WCF Rules of Curling: each team delivers six stones per end over eight ends, and one team per game may use the power play — pre-placed stones (one in play, one guard) that may be swept — announced at the start of the sixth end (World Curling Federation, n.d.-b). Scoring is identical to four-player curling: after each end, the team whose stone lies closest to the button scores one point plus one additional point for every stone closer than the opponent's best stone; an end with no scoring stones is a blank end and the hammer alternates. Fouls — burning a stone before the hog line, delivering across the hog line, or disturbing a stone in motion — are called by the umpires and penalized under the Rules of Curling. There are no separate classes by gender beyond the mixed pairing itself: the Olympic and World Mixed Doubles Championship events are single open competitions in which every team fields one woman and one man, and the two players alternate shots within the team (World Curling Federation, n.d.-a).

## History

Mixed doubles was devised in 2001 by Warren Hansen of Curling Canada as a fast, television-friendly two-player format for the Continental Cup, and the World Curling Federation formally added it to its competition rules in 2008 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The format reached the Olympic stage at the PyeongChang 2018 Winter Games, where the inaugural mixed doubles final was won by Canada (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Since then the World Mixed Doubles Curling Championship has been held annually alongside the men's and women's world championships, and mixed doubles has been a permanent Olympic medal event since 2018 — culminating at Milano Cortina 2026 in Cortina d'Ampezzo, where the sibling pair from Sweden took their nation's first gold in the discipline (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
