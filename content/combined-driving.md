## Basic Information

Combined driving is the equestrian discipline in which a single driver (the "driver" or *cocher*) controls a carriage drawn by one, two or four horses through three phases — dressage, marathon and cones — and the combination with the lowest penalty total wins; it is governed worldwide by the Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI), founded in Lausanne in 1921 (FEI, n.d.). The highlight of the 2026 season was the FEI World Championships in Aachen (Soers), Germany, from 11 to 23 August 2026, which included the Driving World Championship for four-in-hand from 19 to 23 August: Australia's Boyd Exell (born 29 July 1972) won his eighth consecutive individual four-in-hand world title with 142.64 penalty points, ahead of Dries Degrieck of Belgium (148.85) and Chester Weber of the USA; in the team classification the Netherlands took gold (315.13) with Bram Chardon, Ijsbrand Chardon and Koos de Ronde, Australia silver (317.25) and Belgium bronze (325.52) (FEI, 2026). Exell was also world No. 1 in the four-in-hand world ranking in August 2026 and has won 11 World Cup finals (FEI, 2026). The category is open to all genders — leading women drivers compete at the very top of the team and individual events — and Boyd Exell is pictured below at the CHI Genève 2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Combined driving is governed by the FEI Driving Rules, the official rulebook of the world federation FEI (fei.org) (FEI, n.d.). A competition consists of three phases: the dressage phase, where the horse-carriage combination performs a prescribed test of paces and figures judged by marks; the marathon phase, a cross-country test with up to eight obstacle complexes (the "cones" of the marathon) plus a compulsory trot section, timed and penalized; and the cones phase, a timed precision test through a course of double cones in the arena where knocking a ball off a cone adds penalty seconds (FEI, n.d.). Penalty points from all three phases are added, and the individual or team with the lowest total wins — at Aachen 2026 Boyd Exell's 142.64 points were the best combined total (FEI, 2026). The categories are single horse, pair and four-in-hand, each with individual and team (national teams of three drivers) classifications; team scores sum the best two or three individuals as the rules prescribe (FEI, n.d.). There are no separate men's and women's categories — the sport is open, and men and women compete together in the same classes, which is the explicit gender rule of combined driving; age classes are senior, juniors, young drivers and para-driving (FEI, n.d.). The rule source is the FEI (fei.org), the world governing body founded in 1921 (FEI, n.d.).

## History

Horse driving as a sport formalized in the 19th century with the coaching clubs of Britain and the continent, and the FEI — founded on 24 May 1921 in Lausanne by ten national federations — made combined driving one of its seven disciplines (FEI, n.d.). The first FEI World Championship for four-in-hand was held in 1972, and the biennial world championships became the sport's pinnacle; the 2026 edition in Aachen's Soers arena was the latest, run alongside the other FEI world championships (FEI, 2026). The most iconic figure of the modern era is Boyd Exell of Australia (born 29 July 1972), who between 2008 and 2026 won eight consecutive individual four-in-hand world titles — a record streak — plus Olympic team bronze medals at London 2012 and Tokyo 2020 and 11 World Cup finals; his Belgian rival Dries Degrieck and the American Chester Weber, silver and bronze in Aachen 2026, complete the podium dynasty (FEI, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, drivers such as the members of the Swiss and Dutch championship teams have medalled at world level, and the open category means women's achievements are recorded in the same rankings as the men's (FEI, 2026). The Chardon family of the Netherlands — Bram, Ijsbrand and Koos de Ronde's teammates — are the dynasty of team driving, gold in Aachen 2026 (FEI, 2026).
