## Basic Information

Rowing is the sport of propelling a racing boat (a "shell") on water using oars, with athletes seated facing the stern and rowing in sweep (one oar each) or sculling (two oars each) in crews of one to eight. The sport's world governing body is World Rowing, founded on 25 June 1892 in Turin as the Fédération Internationale des Sociétés d'Aviron (FISA) — the oldest international sports federation in the Olympic movement — and its premier events are the Olympic regatta, the annual World Rowing Championships and the World Rowing Cup series, raced over 2,000 metres on buoyed courses.

## Rules

Races are conducted side by side on straight, buoyed courses over the elite standard distance of 2,000 metres (1,000 metres for some para-rowing and masters events), with six lanes at World Rowing events, crews seeded through heats, repechages, semi-finals and finals set out in the World Rowing Rules of Racing. Boat classes range from the single scull (1x) through pairs, doubles and quads to the coxed and coxless fours and the flagship eight (8+), with a coxswain steering and commanding in the coxed boats; lightweight events impose crew average weight limits (70 kg with no rower over 72.5 kg for men, 57 kg with no rower over 59 kg for women). False starts, crew-member changes during a regatta, use of illegal equipment and improper conduct attract the penalties defined in the rule book, with boats and oars — now built from carbon fibre composites rather than wood — subject to strict design regulations and umpires following each race by launch. The World Rowing Championships programme has been gender equal in boat classes since 2018, and para-rowing (integrated into the world championships in 2002, raced over 2,000 metres since 2017) and coastal rowing have joined the federation's championship portfolio.

## History

Rowing's roots as an organised sport lie in 18th- and 19th-century England: boat clubs formed at British public schools (Westminster School's records date to 1813) and at Oxford and Cambridge, whose universities staged the first Boat Race in 1829, while professional scullers drew huge crowds on the Thames and clubs formed in Germany, the United States (Yale's club founded in 1843) and beyond. International governance came early: FISA was founded in Turin in June 1892 — by founding members from Belgium, France, Italy, Switzerland and the Adriatica clubs of Trieste — and organised the first European Rowing Championships in 1893 at Orta, Italy; rowing was on the programme of the first modern Olympic Games in 1896 (cancelled by weather) and has been raced at every Games since 1900, with the United States dominating the early Olympic era, the Soviet Union and above all East Germany (the GDR) in the 1970s and 1980s, and Germany, the USA, Britain, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Romania and the Netherlands prominent since. Women's rowing was trialled by FISA from 1950, contested at the European Championships from 1954, added to the Olympic Games in 1976 (at 1,000 m, extended to 2,000 m from 1984 world and 1988 Olympic), with lightweight events added to the worlds in 1974 (men) and 1985 (women) and to the Olympics in 1996; the annual World Rowing Championships in all classes began in 1962 and the World Cup in 1997, and in 2017 FISA voted to make the Olympic programme gender equal from 2020. Iconic events such as the Boat Race, Henley Royal Regatta (founded 1839) and the Harvard–Yale race (1852) continue alongside the professional world circuit, and para-rowing has been on the Paralympic programme since 2008.
