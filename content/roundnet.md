## Basic Information

Roundnet — popularly known as Spikeball — is a ball game invented in 1989 by Jeff Knurek, a Hawaiian toymaker, inspired by volleyball: two teams of two (or three) players stand around a small trampoline-like net on the ground and hit a ball onto the net so that the rebounding ball is hard for the opponents to return (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game was revived in 2008 when Chris Ruder's company Spikeball Inc., founded in Chicago in 2007, bought the rights, started manufacturing sets, got a US patent in August 2014 and built a professional tour that aired on ESPN2 from May 2018 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Roundnet Federation counted 44 member nations as of July 2025 and has staged a world championship every year since 2019 — the first was held in 2021 in Houthalen, Belgium, after the 2020 edition was postponed by the pandemic (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Games are played in open, women's and mixed divisions, so women and men compete both separately and together (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Roundnet is played 2v2 (3v3 variants exist) around a round net about 3 ft (0.9 m) high, with a ball of 12-inch (30 cm) circumference; players take positions at 90-degree intervals around the net, each directly across from an opponent (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Spikeball, n.d.). Each point begins with a serve: the server stands in a serving circle 15 ft (4.6 m) in diameter around the net and hits the ball onto the net toward the opponents (Spikeball, n.d.). The receiving team then has up to three hits to return the ball onto the net — possession flips each time the ball hits the net — and a team may not touch the ball twice in a row (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After the serve there are no boundaries: play is 360° around the net and players may move anywhere to hit the ball (Spikeball, n.d.). A point is scored when the opposing team fails to return the ball, hits the rim instead of the net, lets the ball bounce twice on the net, or commits a pocket or double hit (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Matches use rally scoring to 21 points, win by two, and serving positions rotate 90° every five points (Spikeball, n.d.). Women's, mixed and open divisions are offered at tournaments and world championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Jeff Knurek invented roundnet in 1989 in Hawaii but did not patent it; the game briefly sold in the 1980s and then faded until Chris Ruder bought the product and trademark rights in 2008, founding Spikeball Inc. in Chicago in 2007 with $100,000 of seed money from him and five friends (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Ruder appeared on ABC's Shark Tank in 2014, retail chains picked up the game, the company sold its millionth net in fall 2017, and the Spikeball Roundnet Association launched a ranking system in 2016 with the first college national championship in 2017 at Clemson (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first Roundnet World Championship was planned for Belgium in September 2020, postponed to 2021 because of COVID-19, and held in Houthalen in September 2021; it has run annually since 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Roundnet Federation now counts 44 nations (24 in Europe, 9 in the Americas, 7 in Asia, 2 in Africa, 1 in Oceania as of July 2025) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
