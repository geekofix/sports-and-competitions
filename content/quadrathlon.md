## Basic Information

Quadrathlon (also quadriathlon or tetrathlon) is an endurance multisport of four disciplines completed in succession — swimming, kayaking, cycling and running — with the lowest overall time winning; it adds kayaking to triathlon for a balanced test of upper and lower body (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed internationally by the World Quadrathlon Federation (WQF), founded in 1990, which stages world championships and a World Cup series since 2001, and in Britain by the British Quadrathlon Association (World Quadrathlon Federation, n.d.). The WQF World Cup awards separate men's and women's titles — Germany's Stefan Teichert and Lisa Maria Hirschfelder won the 2013 series — and world championships run at sprint, middle and long distances with women's and men's fields (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

WQF standard distances range from sprint (750 m swim, 4 km kayak, 20 km bike, 5 km run) through middle (1.5 km / 8 km / 40 km / 10 km) and long (4 km / 20 km / 100 km / 21–25 km) up to ultra, usually in the order swim–kayak–bike–run (World Quadrathlon Federation, n.d.). Competitors race without outside assistance — no drafting beyond permitted gaps, no flippers or snorkels in the swim — and kayaks/canoes must be non-mechanical and over 8 kg; race numbers must be visible on the run, the upper body covered, and gear deposited in transition; penalties range from warnings to disqualification (Wikipedia, n.d.). At world championship level athletes race as individuals or national-team relays, and winter variants replace the swim and run with cross-country skiing and snowshoeing; men and women race in separate categories over identical courses (World Quadrathlon Federation, n.d.).


## History

After failed attempts to launch a four-sport formula in the early 1980s, the Italian Sergio Ferrero staged the first quadrathlon in 1987 on Ibiza under the name "Diamond Man" — 5 km open-water swim, 20 km kayak, 100 km bike and 21.1 km run, won in just under seven hours by Australia's Philip Gabel — and founded the WQF in 1990; the European Quadrathlon Federation followed in 1997 under the Czech Václav Marek (Wikipedia, n.d.). The World Cup has run since 2001 as a multi-country series in which each athlete's best four races count, and Germany, Great Britain and the Czech Republic have produced many champions, including Stefan Teichert and Lisa Maria Hirschfelder of Germany (Wikipedia, n.d.).
