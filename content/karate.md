## Basic Information

Karate is a striking martial art that originated on the island of Okinawa, where indigenous fighting methods (*te*) merged with Chinese kung fu, and was introduced to mainland Japan in 1922 by Gichin Funakoshi (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Today it is practiced worldwide as a sport, a self-defense system and a character-building discipline with two Olympic competition disciplines: kata (pre-arranged forms) and kumite (sparring) (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a). The World Karate Federation (WKF), founded in 1970, governs the sport internationally and runs the World Championships, the Karate 1 series and the official WKF World Ranking (World Karate Federation, n.d.-b); karate made its Olympic debut at Tokyo 2020 (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). At the top of the women's game stand Grace Lau of Hong Kong, the 2025 World Kata Champion, and Maho Ono of Japan, who won kata gold at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, while the 2025 World Kumite Champions included Ahlam Youssef (EGY, −55 kg), Atousa Golshadnezhad (IRI, −61 kg), Thalya Sombe (FRA, −68 kg) and Johanna Kneer (GER, +68 kg); on the men's side Eray Şamdan of Türkiye won −60 kg at the 2025 World Championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2026 season featured the World Senior Championships in Cairo (27–30 November 2025), the Karate World Cup team event in Hangzhou, China (20–22 November 2026), and the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Karate competition is governed by the World Karate Federation (WKF), whose Competition Rules in force from 1 January 2026 (version 2026.0) cover kata, kumite and para karate (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a). In kumite, a senior bout lasts three minutes and is fought on an 8 × 8 m tatami; scoring is yuko (1 point) for a punch to the trunk, waza-ari (2 points) for a kick to the trunk, and ippon (3 points) for a kick to the head or a technique against a fallen opponent, and a lead of eight points ends the bout immediately (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Senior weight categories are −60, −67, −75, −84 and +84 kg for men and −50, −55, −61, −68 and +68 kg for women, with junior and cadet divisions and para karate classes for athletes with impairments; video review assists referees, and prohibited contact is penalized up to hansoku (disqualification) (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a). In kata, the competitor performs a form from the official WKF list of about 102 recognized kata before a panel of judges — seven in round-robin events, five otherwise — who award scores of which the highest and lowest are eliminated; only kata that demonstrate realistic combat application are permitted, and theatrical elements are penalized (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate divisions under identical rules; age classes run from cadets (under 16) through juniors to seniors (18 and over) (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a). See [karate kata](karate-kata.html) and [karate kumite](karate-kumite.html) for the individual disciplines. Rule source: World Karate Federation, https://www.wkf.net/ (World Karate Federation, n.d.-a).

## History

Karate's roots lie in the indigenous fighting methods of the Ryukyu Kingdom, organized into the Shuri-te, Naha-te and Tomari-te lineages, with Chinese influence through trade and envoys (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Key masters of the 19th century include Kanga Sakugawa, Matsumura Sōkon, Arakaki Seishō and Higaonna Kanryō; in 1901 Ankō Itosu introduced karate into the Okinawan school system and simplified forms for physical education (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Gichin Funakoshi (1868–1957), called the "father of modern karate," demonstrated the art at the Ministry of Education in Tokyo in 1922, opened the first Shotokan dojo at Mejiro in 1936, and in the same year the name was changed from "Chinese hand" to "empty hand" at a roundtable in Naha on 25 October 1936 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The four major styles that followed were Shotokan (Funakoshi), Goju-ryu (founded by Chōjun Miyagi, 1888–1953), Shito-ryu (Kenwa Mabuni, 1889–1952) and Wado-ryu (Hironori Otsuka, 1892–1982); Masutatsu Oyama (1923–1994) founded the full-contact Kyokushin style in 1953 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The World Union of Karate-do Organizations was founded in 1970 with Jacques Delcourt as first president and became the World Karate Federation, which held the first World Championships in Tokyo in 1970 (World Karate Federation, n.d.-b). Karate debuted at the Olympic Games at Tokyo 2020, where Sandra Sánchez of Spain won the women's kata gold (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Icons of the art include Funakoshi, Miyagi, Mabuni, Otsuka, Oyama and the Olympic champion Sandra Sánchez (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
