## Basic Information

Pelota Mixteca is the traditional handball game of the Mixtec region of Oaxaca and Guerrero, Mexico: two teams of five on a long narrow court (~100 × 11 m of compacted soil) strike a small solid ball over the opponent's side with a heavy leather glove (3–6 kg, studded with nails) on the hand; the serve bounces the ball off a flat stone (botadero) first. Scoring is tennis-like (complex local variations). Played also in Mexico City and by Oaxacan migrants in Los Angeles and Fresno; the Oaxacan Association of Pelota Mixteca organizes tournaments. Men and (increasingly) women play. Within the pelota, wall-ball and handball games on this site, its closest relatives are [Pelota Tarasca](pelota-tarasca.html), [Valencian Pilota](valencian-pilota.html), [Basque Pelota](basque-pelota.html), [Pitz](pitz.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Berger, M. E, 2016).

## Rules

Court ~100 m long, 11 m wide, marked on compacted earth, divided by a centre line; five players per side (more or less allowed). Serve: bounce the ball on the flat stone, then strike it on the rebound over the court so it bounces on the opponent's side. A point is lost if the return fails, goes out, or is hit with the bare hand in glove zones (Guerrero "cold hands" variant uses cloth). Variants: de forro ball (300–330 g, wool core, suede cover), de hule (900 g rubber), plastic ball (1–1.5 kg) in Los Angeles, del valle (light sponge ball with wooden paddle). Scoring is tennis-like with local variants (Cambridge University Press — Ancient Mesoamerica, 2021).

## History

Players trace the game to the pre-Columbian era (14th–15th centuries per the Asociación Oaxaqueña de Pelota Mixteca); the Dainzú (Oaxaca) stone reliefs show gloved handball players (c. 200 BC–200 AD), and some scholars see it as a descendant of the Mesoamerican ballgame — others (Gillmeister) argue a Franco-Flemish tennis ancestry via Spain. A 1947 reglamento by Raúl Bolaños Cacho codified modern rules. Mexico City announced a traditional-sports centre with courts for pelota mixteca and tarasca (2000s) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
