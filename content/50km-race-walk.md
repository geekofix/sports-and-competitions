## Basic Information

The 50 kilometres race walk was the longest Olympic road race of athletics: 50 km on a measured loop under full race walking judging. It was an Olympic event for men from 1932 to 2020 (last staged in Tokyo 2021) and appeared at the 2017 and 2019 World Championships for women; after Tokyo 2021 it was replaced by the marathon race walk mixed relay, and from 2026 the championship long walk is the 42.195 km marathon race walk (Wikipedia, n.d.; Xinhua, 2025). The world records are 3:32:33 for men, set by Yohann Diniz of France in Zürich on 15 August 2014, and 3:59:15 for women, set by Liu Hong of China in Huangshan on 9 March 2019 (World Athletics, n.d.-f).

Inês Henriques of Portugal (pictured), the 2017 world champion and a former world record holder over 50 km, is one of the icons of the women's event alongside Liu Hong (pictured); on the men's side Diniz (pictured) remains the standard, with Massimo Stano, Evan Dunfee and Caio Bonfim among the leading 2020s walkers (World Athletics, n.d.-f).

## Rules

Race walking is defined by two visible infringements: loss of contact with the ground — the advancing leg must remain straightened from the moment of first contact with the ground until it passes under the body — and any visible loss of contact, each shown with a yellow warning paddle, a red paddle for loss of contact and a red card for a bent knee; three red cards from different judges disqualify the athlete, and the chief judge may award a fourth red card (World Athletics, n.d.-b). Road races are held on closed loops with refreshment stations; at World Athletics and Olympic competitions a panel of race walking judges including the chief judge officiates (World Athletics, n.d.-d). Since 1 January 2026 the 20 km and 35 km have been replaced by the half marathon (21.0975 km) and the marathon (42.195 km) race walk at World Athletics championships — first staged at the 2026 World Race Walking Team Championships in Brasília on 12 April 2026 — and Los Angeles 2028 will stage the half marathon as the only individual walk plus the marathon mixed relay (Xinhua, 2025; World Athletics, n.d.-d).

The 50 km was never staged for women at the Olympics; men and women raced under identical rules at championship level where the distance was offered, junior events use 10 km, and para athletics offers the distance in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-b).

## History

The 50 km walk joined the Olympic programme for men at Los Angeles 1932, after the 10-mile walk of 1908 and the 10 km of 1912, and was dropped after Tokyo 2021 to make the road programme gender-equal with the marathon mixed relay (Wikipedia, n.d.). Denis Nizhegorodov's 3:34:14 of 2008 stood until Yohann Diniz, pictured, walked 3:32:33 at the 2014 European Championships in Zürich (World Athletics, n.d.-f). On the women's side the distance was staged at the 2017 and 2019 World Championships, where Inês Henriques of Portugal, pictured, won gold in London 2017 with a then world record, before Liu Hong, pictured, set the current world record of 3:59:15 in Huangshan in 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-f).
