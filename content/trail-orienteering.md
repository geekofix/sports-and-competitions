## Basic Information

Trail orienteering (TrailO) is an orienteering discipline built around precise map reading: competitors stay on mapped trails and identify, from a marked decision point, which of several control flags in the terrain matches the control shown on the map — with no point-to-point racing and little route choice (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Because the objective is accuracy rather than speed, athletes with limited mobility compete on equal terms with able-bodied athletes, which is why the sport was developed; it has been an official discipline of the International Orienteering Federation (IOF) since 1992 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The IOF runs a World Cup (since 1999) and the World Trail Orienteering Championships (since 2004, biennial from 2023), plus European Championships since 1994, in the Open and Paralympic classes with women and men competing in the same classes (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.).

## Rules

In PreO (precision orienteering), the traditional format, competitors receive the map at the start; controls, start and finish are marked on it, and at each control site several flags are set out labeled A, B, C… from left to right at the decision point — but only one, or none, correctly represents the map control (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Competitors must stay on the trails shown on the map, choose their answer at the decision point, and score one point per correct answer; a few timed controls are used only to break ties (since the 2014 rules) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In TempO, all controls are timed stations: riders sit at a designated spot where all flags are visible, receive one map per question, and answer A–Z (Z = no flag) as fast as possible — 30 seconds are added for each wrong, blank or multiple answer, and the lowest total time wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The TrailO relay fields three-person teams that share a precision course and then run timed legs, and night and virtual (E-TrailO) formats also exist (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). Classes are Open and Paralympic (P), and men and women compete in the same classes on identical terms (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). The official rulebook is the IOF Trail Orienteering Competition Rules (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.).

## History

Orienteering itself originated in Sweden, where the term *orientering* was first used in 1886 at the Military Academy Karlberg and the first public competition was held in Norway in 1897; the International Orienteering Federation was founded in Sweden in 1961 after a 1959 conference of 12 nations (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Trail orienteering was developed in Scandinavia — Sweden in particular — in the 1980s and early 1990s to give people with locomotor disabilities a meaningful competition, and the IOF recognized it as an official discipline in 1992 (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.). The first World Cup was held in 1999, replaced by the inaugural World Trail Orienteering Championships in 2004, which ran annually until 2019 and biennially from 2023 onward; European Championships have been held every year since 1994 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
