## Basic Information

Reining is a western riding competition in which horse and rider perform one of a set of prescribed patterns — small slow and large fast circles, flying lead changes, rollbacks over the hocks, 360-degree spins in place and the hallmark sliding stop — judged on the precision, attitude and athleticism of the horse; it is often called the western equivalent of [dressage](dressage.html) (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed worldwide by the National Reining Horse Association (NRHA), founded in 1966 in Coshocton, Ohio and now headquartered in Oklahoma City, became an FEI-recognized discipline in 2000 — the first western discipline at the World Equestrian Games — and is overseen in the US high-performance structure by USA Reining (founded 2011) under USEF (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport is open to women and men alike, and women's elite stars include Kaci J. O'Rourke of Texas, who in 2024 became the first woman ever to win the NRHA Futurity (Level 4 Open, aboard The Firemen) and in January 2026 the association's newest Million Dollar Rider (NRHA, n.d.-a). Current major champions include Casey Deary, 2025 NRHA Futurity Level 4 Open champion with Belissimo (231.5), and Andrea Fappani, 2026 6666 NRHA Derby Level 4 Open champion with Alpha Viking (236) and the first $10 million rider in NRHA history; the 60th Anniversary NRHA Futurity runs 23 November–5 December 2026 at the OKC Fairgrounds (NRHA, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the NRHA Handbook, every run starts from a base score of 70, each maneuver is judged on execution and attitude with plus and minus marks (maneuver scores from +2 down to −2), and penalties are added for visible disobedience: half-point and one-point deductions for incorrect or missing maneuver parts, two-point penalties for a frozen spin or rollback, and five-point penalties for spurring in front of the cinch, holding the saddle or blatant disobedience; a score of 70 represents an average, correct, workmanlike run on a pattern of average difficulty (Wikipedia, n.d.). Contestants ride one of the NRHA's published patterns — the pattern book is updated regularly — whose required elements include the small slow circle, large fast circle, flying lead changes at the lope, rollback, 360-degree spins and the sliding stop (NRHA, n.d.-a). Classes span Open, Non Pro, Intermediate and Limited divisions with levels from L1 (easiest) to L4 Open, plus Youth (by rider age), Rookie, Snaffle Bit and horse-age divisions (Junior horse 5 and under, Senior 6 and over), the aged events being the NRHA Futurity for three-year-olds and the NRHA Derby for four-to-six-year-olds, both in Oklahoma City; freestyle reining, ridden to music with a compulsory maneuver list, is also offered (Wikipedia, n.d.). Horses work in western tack with reining sliding plates, and both stallions and mares — and riders of any gender — compete side by side in the same classes (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Reining grew out of the maneuvers a working cow horse needed on the American ranch — fast circles to rate cattle, the rollback to turn a herd and the hard sliding stop — and formal competition developed from stock-horse shows, with the American Quarter Horse Association recognizing reining in 1949 and the USEF adding it to its western division (Wikipedia, n.d.). The NRHA was founded in 1966 and soon staged its first Futurity, modeled on cutting's aged events, and the NRHA Hall of Fame, founded in 1986 and maintained by the Reining Horse Foundation, honored Dale Wilkinson as its first inductee — the Hall of Fame award is named for him (NRHA, n.d.-b). Icons of the sport include Tom McCutcheon (born 27 March 1967 in Wisconsin), WEG individual gold medalist in Lexington 2010 and two-time team gold medalist, inducted into the 2026 Hall of Fame class alongside Mike Hancock and Bob Santagata and the legendary horses Americasnextgunmodel and This Chics Dundreamin; Tim McQuay was the first $2 million rider in NRHA history (Hall of Fame 2000); and horses such as Smart Chic Olena, Boomernic — the 1992 Futurity winner who introduced the West Coast reining style — and Wimpys Little Step shaped the modern sport (NRHA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.).
