## Basic Information

Brännboll (Swedish for "burning ball") is a Scandinavian bat-and-ball game played mainly in Sweden: the batter throws the ball up into the air herself and hits it with a bat, then runs around four bases while the fielding team tries to put her out — with the special twist that a fielder may "burn" a runner by hitting them with the ball. The biggest event is the Brännbollscupen at the Brännbollsyran festival in Umeå, which has drawn more than 300 teams and over 3,000 players since 1974 and is nicknamed the "world championship of brännboll" (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-a). The 2026 Brännbollscupen took place at Mariehemsängarna in Umeå on May 29–30, 2026, with the final on Saturday at 16:00 between BK Rel and Nucken; the 2025 edition was won by High Rise for the fourth consecutive year. The accompanying Brännbollsyran festival ran June 5–7, 2026, organized together with the Homerun Festivals music events (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-a) (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-b). There is no international world federation; the sport is organized locally by clubs and the Brännbollsyran committee in Umeå (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-b).

On the gender side, brännboll is played by women's, men's and mixed teams at Brännbollsyran and in local leagues — the tournament explicitly includes dam (women's) and mixed classes alongside the open competition, and girls' and women's teams are a normal part of Swedish club sport (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Brännboll is played on a field with four bases (first, second, third and home) arranged in a diamond. There is no pitcher: the batter stands at home, throws the ball vertically into the air and hits it with the bat on the way down — a hit that is caught on the fly is an out. After a fair hit, the batter runs to first base and may continue around the bases; the fielding team tries to get her out by catching the ball before it touches the ground, tagging her with the ball, or touching a base with the ball before she reaches it. The signature rule is the burn (bränna): a fielder may throw the ball at a runner, and a runner hit by the ball while not on a base is out (burned) (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-b).

A runner who reaches home scores a point for her team; teams alternate between batting and fielding, and the team with the most points after the agreed number of innings wins. Special rules include the frivarv — a free lap — awarded in some rule sets when the batter hits the ball a set distance or when play is stopped, letting runners advance one base safely. Equipment: a wooden or plastic bat, a soft tennis-style or rubber ball, and four base mats or posts; fielders do not wear gloves in traditional play. Formats: pickup games in parks, school play, club leagues and the big tournament Brännbollscupen, which plays pool stages followed by knockout rounds and a final. Men's, women's and mixed teams all play the same rules; youth teams often use a softer ball and shorter distances (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-a) (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-b).

## History

Brännboll is a traditional Swedish folk game whose name means "burning ball", referring to the burn rule; it has been played in Sweden since at least the 19th century and is strongly associated with the city of Umeå in northern Sweden. The Brännbollsyran ("brännboll frenzy") festival in Umeå has been organized since 1974 and has grown into the Brännbollscupen, with more than 300 teams and over 3,000 players at recent editions — widely described as the unofficial world championship of the game (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-a) (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-b). Since the festival is combined with music events organized by Homerun Festivals, brännboll today sits at the intersection of sport and youth culture in Sweden; the 2026 festival weekend ran June 5–7, 2026 (Brännbollsyran, n.d.-b).
