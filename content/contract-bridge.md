## Basic Information

Contract bridge is the competitive form of the card game bridge, played by four players in two fixed partnerships. After an auction (the bidding), one partnership becomes the declaring side and must win a stated number of tricks with a named trump suit or notrump; the game is governed worldwide by the World Bridge Federation (WBF), which publishes the Laws of Contract Bridge and organizes the World Championships, the Bridge Olympiad and the World Bridge Series (World Bridge Federation [WBF], n.d.-b). The WBF was founded in August 1958 and today unites more than 120 national bridge organizations in eight zones, with Franck Riehm re-elected as president for 2026–2030 (WBF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The elite calendar in 2026 is anchored by the 17th World Bridge Series in Katowice, Poland, 20 August–3 September 2026, where the open teams title (Rosenblum Cup) was contested by 137 teams, the women's teams title (McConnell Cup) was won by Lynn Baker's team over Jiangsu Runtai 105–104, and the senior teams title (Rand Cup) went to Simson's team over Milner 134–113; the 2026 MacRobertson Shield — the oldest international teams trophy in bridge, contested every four years since 1925 — was won by England, captained by Samir Patel, from 24 July to 9 August 2026 at venues including Roehampton, Hurlingham, Sussex and Surbiton (WBF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2027 World Teams Championships (Bermuda Bowl, Venice Cup, d'Orsi Seniors and the mixed Wuhan Trophy) will be held in Barbados (WBF, n.d.-b). Bridge is played at the highest level in open, women's, senior and mixed team events; the laws themselves are identical for all — there are no gender- or age-specific rules, only separate competitions, most prominently the Venice Cup for women's national teams, first held in 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading figures of the current elite are the 2026 World Bridge Series champions — Samir Patel's England open team and Lynn Baker's McConnell Cup-winning women's team — alongside record-winning national squads such as Italy's (WBF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Contract bridge is played under the Laws of Contract Bridge (2017 edition), the official rulebook published and maintained by the WBF (WBF, n.d.-a). A standard 52-card deck is dealt clockwise, 13 cards to each player; the dealer rotates each board. Play proceeds in phases: the auction, in which players bid levels (1–7) and strains (clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades or notrump) in a prescribed order, with each bid obliging the partnership to win six plus the level number of tricks; the play of the hand, in which the declarer plays both their own cards and the dummy's cards after the opening lead; and scoring (WBF, n.d.-a). During the play, partnerships follow suit if able; trick-taking order and the rules for establishing and correcting revokes are enforced by tournament directors, who also adjudicate adjusted scores (WBF, n.d.-a). Scoring distinguishes trick points below the line (20 per trick in clubs and diamonds, 30 in hearts and spades, 40 for the first notrump trick), overtricks, honors, game and part-score bonuses, vulnerability, and slam bonuses for small and grand slams; in rubber bridge the first side to win two games scores a game bonus, while duplicate bridge — the standard tournament format — compares the same boards across tables and converts results to IMPs (International Match Point scale) or, in team matches, to victory points (WBF, n.d.-a). Formats include pairs, teams of four (the Bermuda Bowl open, the Venice Cup women's, the d'Orsi Seniors 60+ and the mixed Wuhan Trophy) and the quadrennial Bridge Olympiad with open and women's sections; classifications are open, women's, senior and mixed, with no differences in the playing laws themselves — the women's and senior events simply field separate national teams, and junior (U21/U25) events exist alongside (WBF, n.d.-a; WBF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Bridge descends from the English game of whist, which it replaced in the late 19th century; the decisive step was auction bridge, codified by Harold Stirling Vanderbilt (1884–1960) around 1925, after which contract bridge's modern laws were settled in the late 1920s by the Portland Club in London and the American Contract Bridge League (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The world governing body followed in August 1958 with the founding of the WBF, and the first Bridge Olympiad was held in Turin in 1960 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The first Bermuda Bowl — the open world teams championship — was played in Hamilton, Bermuda, in 1950 at the initiative of American publisher Norman Bach, and the women's Venice Cup followed in 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The most iconic players of the 20th century were the Italians Benito Garozzo (1927–2014) and Giorgio Belladonna (1923–1995) of the legendary "Blue Team", which dominated world bridge from the late 1950s through the 1970s and won the Bermuda Bowl a record number of times; other defining figures include the American bidding theorist Ely Culbertson (1891–1955) and Harold Vanderbilt himself (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the Venice Cup established an international stage for women's teams from 1974 onward, with nations such as the United States and Great Britain winning the early editions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A painting of a bridge party from around 1911 by the French Nabis artist Édouard Vuillard shows the game as a fixture of social life a century ago.
