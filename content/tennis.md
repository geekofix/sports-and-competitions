## Basic Information

Tennis is a racket sport played over a net in singles or doubles, in which players strike a ball to land it in the opponent's court; it is governed worldwide by the International Tennis Federation (ITF), which owns the Rules of Tennis and runs the Davis Cup, the Billie Jean King Cup, the Olympic tennis tournament and the four Grand Slams together with their organizers (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). The professional tours are the ATP for men and the WTA for women, each with official rankings (Association of Tennis Professionals, n.d.; Women's Tennis Association, n.d.). The 2025 Grand Slam season was shared by — women first — Madison Keys (Australian Open), Coco Gauff (French Open), Iga Świątek (Wimbledon) and Aryna Sabalenka (US Open), and by Jannik Sinner (Australian Open, Wimbledon) and Carlos Alcaraz (French Open, US Open) on the men's side (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the current leading players at the end of the 2026 season are the women's world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka, Iga Świątek, Coco Gauff and Naomi Osaka, and the men's world No. 1 Jannik Sinner alongside Carlos Alcaraz (Association of Tennis Professionals, n.d.; Women's Tennis Association, n.d.). Tennis is the parent of a large racket-sports family on this site — [Real Tennis](real-tennis.html) and [Jeu de Paume](jeu-de-paume.html), the walled-court games [Rackets](rackets.html), [Squash](squash.html), [Squash Tennis](squash-tennis.html), [Racquetball](racquetball.html) and [Hardball Squash](hardball-squash.html), the net games [Table Tennis](table-tennis.html), [Badminton](badminton.html) and [Crossminton](crossminton.html), the paddle games [Pickleball](pickleball.html), [Padel](padel.html), [Platform Tennis](platform-tennis.html), [Paddle Tennis](paddle-tennis.html), [POP Tennis](pop-tennis.html), [Beach Tennis](beach-tennis.html), [Soft Tennis](soft-tennis.html), [Mini Tennis](mini-tennis.html) and [Touchtennis](touchtennis.html), the combination sport [Racketlon](racketlon.html), and the adaptive formats [Para Table Tennis](para-table-tennis.html), [Para Badminton](para-badminton.html), [Wheelchair Tennis](wheelchair-tennis.html), [Blind Tennis](blind-tennis.html) and [Showdown](showdown.html).

## Rules

The ITF Rules of Tennis define the court — 23.77 m long, 8.23 m wide for singles and 10.97 m for doubles, with a net 0.914 m high at the center — and the ball (6.54–6.86 cm, 56.0–59.4 g) (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). The server stands behind the baseline and must land the ball in the diagonally opposite service box; two serves are allowed and a let is replayed (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). Points run 15–30–40–game, with deuce requiring a two-point advantage; a set is won at six games with a two-point margin, a tiebreak at 6–6 (first to 7 points, win by 2) (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). Matches are best of three sets at most tour and Olympic events, and best of five sets for men at the four Grand Slams and in the Davis Cup (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). Hawk-Eye electronic line calling is standard at tour and Grand Slam events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play under identical rules, in separate ATP and WTA tours plus mixed doubles, and the Olympic tournament has been a full medal event since Seoul 1988 (Olympics are not needed; International Tennis Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Tennis descends from the 12th-century French jeu de paume (real tennis); lawn tennis was codified in 1873–74, with Major Walter Clopton Wingfield patenting "Sphairistikè" in 1874 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first Wimbledon championship was held in 1877, won by Spencer Gore, and the first U.S. Championships in 1881, won by Richard Sears; the International Lawn Tennis Federation (today the ITF) was founded in Paris in 1913, the Davis Cup began in 1900 through Dwight F. Davis, and tennis was on the Olympic programme from 1896 to 1924 before returning as a full medal sport at Seoul 1988 (International Tennis Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Open Era began in 1968 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the game include Rod Laver (b. 1938), the only player to win the calendar Grand Slam twice (1962 and 1969); Björn Borg (11 major titles, retired 1983); John McEnroe, Jimmy Connors and Pete Sampras (14 majors, retired 2002); and on the women's side Steffi Graf (Golden Slam 1988, retired 1999), Martina Navratilova (18 Grand Slam singles titles), Chris Evert, Billie Jean King of the 1973 "Battle of the Sexes", and Serena Williams (23 major singles titles, retired 2022), pictured below (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
