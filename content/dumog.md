## Basic Information

Dumog (buno in Luzon) is the Filipino art of standing grappling and wrestling, the close-quarters component of Filipino martial arts: pushes, pulls, weight shifts, joint locks, chokes, takedowns and throws based on "control points" on the body, plus environmental use (walls, posts) and close-range striking. The Visayan form from Antique, Iloilo and Capiz on Panay island is a distinct traditional sport whose practitioners have been recruited to the Philippine national wrestling team; tribal variants include Ifugao bultong, Maranao purgos and Samal dama. Men and women practice; modern Dumog competitions exist in the Visayas. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Pekiti-Tirsia](pekiti-tirsia.html), [Arnis de Mano](arnis-de-mano.html), [Eskrima](eskrima.html), [Wrestling](wrestling.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Combatpit, n.d.).

## Rules

Dumog is taught as part of FMA systems (Harimaw Buno, Pekiti-Tirsia, Lameco Eskrima) and has informal sporting rules in the Visayas: standing start, first to throw the opponent cleanly to the ground or to submit wins; joint locks and chokes are allowed in the martial context but barred in sport sparring; pain-compliance and nerve-point attacks (kino-mutai) appear in the combat curriculum. Weight classes are not used; bouts are short and decided by fall or submission (Combatpit, n.d.).

## History

Dumog predates Spanish colonization (1521–1898), when martial arts were banned and practiced covertly; its name derives from the Hiligaynon "dumo" (to immerse oneself in search of metaphysical power), and farmers of Negros and Panay used grappling to control carabaos (water buffalo). After WWII it merged with judo and jujitsu training among FMA masters; notable teachers include Edgar Sulite (Lameco Eskrima), Leo Gaje and Rodel Dagooc of Cebu, a Dumog champion who trained military and police units (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
