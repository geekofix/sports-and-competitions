## Basic Information

The mountain bike marathon (XCM) is the long-distance mass-start discipline of mountain biking, raced point to point over roughly 60 to 160 km of natural terrain — forest roads, singletrack and climbs — on a mountain bike; it is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale under Part 4 of its regulations and has had its own UCI Mountain Bike Marathon World Championships since 2003 (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 world championships were held in Primiero San Martino di Castrozza in Trentino, Italy, on 12–13 September 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Among the 2026 champions, the women lead: Anna Weinbeer of Switzerland, who also won the 2026 HERO XCM World Cup series with four of five rounds and took silver at the 2025 worlds behind Kate Courtney — who moved to road racing for 2026 — while Andreas Seewald of Germany won the men's title in 4:33:47, his second after Capoliveri 2021, ahead of David Valero of Spain; other leading men include Wout Alleman of Belgium and Héctor Leonardo Páez of Colombia (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under UCI mountain bike regulations, XCM races are mass-start events over 60–160 km (typically 70–100 km at the World Championships) with large elevation profiles; the first rider across the line wins, and the winner wears the rainbow jersey until the next World Championships (UCI, n.d.-b). Riders may receive neutral technical assistance and food only in designated feed and technical zones; outside assistance leads to disqualification (UCI, n.d.-b).

Bikes are hardtail or full-suspension mountain bikes meeting the UCI equipment rules, and helmets are mandatory; men and women race separate elite events over the same or slightly shortened course, with under-23 and junior categories at the World Championships; the Olympic mountain bike event is the shorter cross-country (XCO) format, linked below (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Marathon mountain biking grew out of the North American NORBA marathon and 24-hour scene of the early 1990s; the UCI introduced the XCM World Championships in 2003 in Lugano, Switzerland, and a World Cup series followed, today headlined by events such as the HERO series (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

France's Julien Absalon, pictured as French champion in 2003, is one of the icons of the sport — Olympic cross-country champion in Athens 2004 and Beijing 2008, four-time world champion and a dominant marathon racer; on the women's side, Norway's Gunn-Rita Dahle Flesjå won the marathon world title five times between 2004 and 2008 and took Olympic cross-country gold in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
