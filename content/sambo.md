## Basic Information

Sambo is a martial art and combat sport developed in the Soviet Union in the 1920s and 1930s — the name is the Russian acronym for "self-defence without weapons" (SAMozashchita Bez Oruzhiya) — combining judo and jujutsu with the folk wrestling styles of the former Russian Empire; it is practised in three competitive forms, sport sambo (jacket wrestling), combat sambo (which adds strikes) and beach sambo, and is governed worldwide by the International SAMBO Federation (FIAS), which received full International Olympic Committee recognition on 20 July 2021 at the IOC session in Tokyo (FIAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2025 World SAMBO Championships in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, 7–9 November 2025, crowned champions in 31 medal sets across sport sambo, combat sambo and blind sambo, with women's titles including Margarita Barneva (FIAS 1, 50 kg) and Taisia Kireeva (FIAS 1, 72 kg) and Ramed Gukev (FIAS 1) winning his second consecutive men's 79 kg title; sambo was also on the programme of the 2025 World Games in Chengdu, China (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the current leading athletes, the women's field is headed by the 2025 world champions Margarita Barneva and Taisia Kireeva, while on the men's side Ramed Gukev (79 kg), Seyedmaysam Banitaba Khoramanadi of Iran — winner of Iran's first world gold medal at a SAMBO World Championships (64 kg, 2025) — and Mongolian blind-sambo champion Chuluunsukh Galbadrakh (79 kg) headline the FIAS results lists (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Sport sambo bouts are contested on a mat: competitors wear a kurtka (a short jacket tied with a belt, similar to a judogi top), matching shorts and soft sambo shoes, red or blue for the two sides, and a bout is won by total victory (an immediate, clean throw or submission), by technical points for throws, pins and holds, or on points at the final whistle, with warnings, penalties and disqualification for prohibited holds such as neck cranks (FIAS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Sport sambo permits leg locks and armlocks but bans chokes and strangles; combat sambo adds punches, kicks, knees and elbows to the grappling under full-contact rules; beach sambo is fought for three minutes without penalties until the first scored action; and blind sambo adapts the rules for visually impaired athletes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Since 1 January 2021, FIAS contests seven weight categories per gender at senior level — down from the traditional nine, with the lightest limits raised and the men's heavyweight ceiling lowered — and all adult and junior bouts, for men and women alike, last five minutes, while youth and cadet bouts last four minutes (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women compete under the same rules and in their own weight categories, with separate women's world championships staged since 1984, and junior, youth, cadet and blind-sambo categories exist alongside the seniors (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Sambo's development began in the early 1920s in the Red Army's hand-to-hand combat programme through two pioneers working largely independently: Viktor Spiridonov, a World War I veteran who drew on regional wrestling styles, and Vasili Oshchepkov, who had studied judo at the Kodokan under Jigoro Kano and taught it to Red Army soldiers — Oshchepkov was executed in the Great Purge in 1937 (USA Sambo, n.d.). Anatoly Kharlampiev, a student of Oshchepkov, systematised the art, and official recognition arrived with the USSR sports committee order no. 633 of 16 November 1938 — the recognised birth date of sambo; his 1940 book on sambo wrestling became the standard reference (USA Sambo, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). United World Wrestling recognised sambo as an international wrestling style in 1968, it was a demonstration sport at the 1980 Moscow Olympics, the first World Cup was held in 1977 in Oviedo, Spain, and the first women's world championships in 1984 in Madrid (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). FIAS was founded on 13 June 1984 at a constitutive assembly in Madrid with delegates from 56 nations — Fernando Compte was elected its first president — after a 1993 East–West split the federation was reunified, and FIAS received provisional IOC recognition on 30 November 2018 and full recognition on 20 July 2021 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Retired icons of the sport include Fedor Emelianenko, the legendary MMA heavyweight champion, Khabib Nurmagomedov, undefeated UFC lightweight champion who retired in 2020, Marina Mokhnatkina, six-time sambo world champion, Dorjsürengiin Sumiyaa, three-time sambo world champion and 2016 Olympic judo silver medallist, and FIAS president Vasily Shestakov, a former world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
