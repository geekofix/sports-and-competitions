## Basic Information

Congkak (dakon) is the mancala of Malaysia and Indonesia, played with cowrie shells on a boat-shaped wooden board with two rows of seven pits and two end stores (rumah): 98 shells, seven per pit. It is traditionally played by women and girls — often in longhouse verandas — but men and boys play too, and congkak tournaments are held in Malaysia and in the diaspora. The name dakon refers to the pebble counters. Within the mancala and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Sungka](sungka.html), [Mancala](mancala.html), [Kalah](kalah.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Players sow counterclockwise from one pit, skipping the opponent's store; the last shell in the player's own rumah earns another turn. Landing the last shell in an occupied pit on the player's own side triggers relay sowing; landing in an empty pit on the player's side captures that shell plus the opposite pit's shells (if any) into the store. Special rules cover the opening “datou” moves and the endgame sweep. The player with more shells in the rumah when one side is empty wins.

## History

Congkak belongs to the Southeast Asian mancala family spread by trade — cognate with sungka in the Philippines (from Sanskrit sankhya) and pallanguzhi in South India. Boat-shaped boards carved from hardwood are a craft tradition of the Malay archipelago, and congkak was historically one of the games played by women in the home; today it is played by all genders and featured in Malaysian cultural festivals and school programs.
