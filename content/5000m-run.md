## Basic Information

The 5000 metres — twelve and a half laps from a standing start — is the classic long-distance track race, governed by World Athletics and on the Olympic programme since 1912 for men and 1984 for women (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 12:35.36 for men, set by Joshua Cheptegei of Uganda in Monaco 2020, and 13:58.06 for women, set by Beatrice Chebet of Kenya at the 2025 Prefontaine Classic as the first woman under 14 minutes (World Athletics, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.). At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by Chebet (pictured), Olympic champion in 2024 and double world champion in 2025, alongside Faith Kipyegon, while the men's race was headed by Olympic champion Jakob Ingebrigtsen and Grant Fisher after Cheptegei's move to the roads (World Athletics, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The 5000 m starts from a standing start and is run as a pack race without lanes; athletes may be lapped, and the lap counter shows the laps remaining. Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

Paavo Nurmi of Finland, the Flying Finn (pictured), won nine Olympic gold medals in the 1920s including the 5000 m at Paris 1924, where he also won the 10,000 m and the cross country (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Kenenisa Bekele of Ethiopia set the men's world record of 12:37.35 in 2005, which stood for 15 years until Cheptegei broke it in 2020; Mo Farah of Great Britain won back-to-back Olympic 5000 m and 10,000 m doubles in 2012 and 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, Tirunesh Dibaba and Letesenbet Gidey of Ethiopia set the pace in the 2010s before Chebet's 13:58.06 in 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
