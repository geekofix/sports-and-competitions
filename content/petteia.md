## Basic Information

Petteia ('lines game') is the ancient Greek board game played on a grid (8x8 or larger) where pieces move along lines and are captured by custodial enclosure (flanking on opposite sides); it is a candidate ancestor of latrunculi and chess. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Latrunculi](latrunculi.html), [Alquerque](alquerque.html), [Nine Men's Morris](nine-men-s-morris.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology, n.d.).

## Rules

Grid board; pieces move orthogonally along lines until blocked; capture by surrounding an enemy on two opposite sides. Different Greek cities used different board sizes (8x8 and larger) (Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology, n.d.).

## History

Petteia was played across ancient Greece (Athens, Sparta, Thessaly) from the 8th century BC; reconstructions at the Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology (2022/2025 photos) show the 'enneas' (nine lines) variant; it influenced Roman latrunculi (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
