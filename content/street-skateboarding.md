## Basic Information

Street skateboarding is the Olympic skateboarding discipline contested on a course of stairs, handrails, benches, ledges and slopes; since Tokyo 2020 it is one of two Olympic skateboarding events alongside park (Olympics.com, n.d.-a). In competition each skater completes two 45-second runs plus five single-trick attempts; every run and trick is scored 0–100 by five judges, and the final score is the best run plus the best two tricks — maximum 300 points (Olympics.com, n.d.-b).

Tokyo 2020 made history with the youngest podiums ever: in the women's event 13-year-old Momiji Nishiya of Japan won gold ahead of 13-year-old Rayssa Leal of Brazil and Funa Nakayama; the men's title went to Yuto Horigome (Olympics.com, n.d.-b). At Paris 2024 Horigome defended the men's gold (Jagger Eaton silver, Nyjah Huston bronze) and 15-year-old Coco Yoshizawa won the women's gold ahead of Liz Akama and Rayssa Leal (Olympedia, n.d.).

Today's top women are exactly these Olympic champions — Momiji Nishiya (pictured), Coco Yoshizawa and Rayssa Leal — alongside six-time world champion Nyjah Huston on the men's side; the 2026 World Skateboarding Championships in Asunción (2–18 October 2026, 10,000+ athletes from 100+ countries) gave street and park full world-championship status as a key LA 2028 qualifier, and the Street League Skateboarding tour (Cleveland, 9 August 2025) remains the biggest pro series (World Skate, n.d.; SLS, n.d.).

## Rules

The street course contains stairs with handrails, ledges, benches, manual pads, flat rails and gaps/Hubbas, plus slope sections. Format: two 45-second runs and five best-trick attempts; each is scored 0–100 by five judges on difficulty, execution, variety, use of the whole course and flow; the final score = best run + best two tricks (max 300) — the format used at Paris 2024 (Olympics.com, n.d.-b; Olympedia, n.d.). In prelims and finals the same structure applies with best-of-three runs in park; street keeps two runs + five tricks (Olympics.com, n.d.-a).

Equipment: a standard street skateboard, no restrictions on trucks/wheels beyond safety inspection; helmets are mandatory at the Olympics and most pros (Olympics.com, n.d.-a). Men's and women's events are separate but identical in format and scoring (Olympics.com, n.d.-b).

## History

Street skating grew out of pool and vert skating in 1970s Southern California; Rodney Mullen — profiled here at PopTech 2013 — invented the flatground ollie and most of the fundamental street tricks (kickflip, heelflip, 360 flip) in the late 1970s and 1980s, turning skateboarding from a ramp sport into a street sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Skateboarding debuted as an Olympic sport at Tokyo 2020 with street and park, won on the men's street side by Yuto Horigome and on the women's side by 13-year-old Momiji Nishiya (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The World Skateboarding Championships run since 2019 under World Skate; Tokyo hosted the 2023 edition (where Nishiya took bronze), and the 2026 edition in Asunción, Paraguay, was the first with full world-championship status for street and park (World Skate, n.d.). The Street League Skateboarding (SLS) tour, founded 2010, remains the premier invitational pro series (SLS, n.d.).
