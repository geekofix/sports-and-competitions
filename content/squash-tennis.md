## Basic Information

Squash tennis is an American variant of squash played with the equipment of lawn tennis — junior tennis racquets and a standard tennis ball — on a four-walled court, combining the complexity of squash with the speed of racquetball (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The National Squash Tennis Association (NSTA) governed the game and adopted a standard court size of 17 by 32.5 feet (5.2 × 9.9 m) in 1910 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The game is played by two players (singles only) on courts that today are usually North American squash courts (18.5 × 32 ft) or international squash courts (21 × 32 ft) with added tape lines — the dedicated 17 × 32.5 ft squash tennis courts of the New York area largely disappeared once play was authorized on standard squash courts in the early 1930s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Women play on exactly the same terms — the NSTA's 1911–12 official handbook already covered all club play — and historic estate courts such as Plum Orchard on Cumberland Island, Georgia (built winter 1903/04 by the Carnegie family, restored 2008, now run by the National Park Service) still host the game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Court markings: a tin (telltale) at the bottom of the front wall, a service line about 6 ft (1.8 m) up the front wall, an out-of-bounds line about 16 ft (4.9 m) up the front wall and 4.5 ft (1.4 m) up the back wall, a floor service line 10 ft (3 m) from the back wall and a center court line from the front wall to the service line; unlike squash there are no service boxes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Play is singles: the serve must hit the front wall above the service line and land beyond the floor service line; rallies are fast, with the ball allowed to bounce once, and points are scored on every rally (rally scoring) in modern club play. Games are typically to 11 or 15 points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Equipment is simple: junior (25-inch) tennis racquets and a standard lawn tennis ball — historically higher-pressure modified balls, the last specially made ball being green for visibility against dark walls; players today darken a yellow ball or use colored balls (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play the same rules on the same courts (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Squash tennis was originally played on racquets courts and then on fives courts; before 1911 there were no court standards, and the game was played at private estates and clubs. One of the oldest surviving courts is at Plum Orchard, the Carnegie mansion on Cumberland Island, Georgia, built in the winter of 1903/04 and fully restored in 2008 with working tins and electric lights; the National Park Service now owns the estate (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

In 1910 the NSTA adopted the standard 17 × 32.5 ft court, many built in the New York area; after the NSTA authorized play on North American squash courts in the early 1930s the dedicated courts disappeared as economically unviable. The NSTA published an illustrated playing guide with rules in 1968 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
