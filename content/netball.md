## Basic Information

Netball is a fast team ball sport played by two teams of seven players on a rectangular court 30.5 m long and 15.25 m wide, divided into thirds, with a raised goal ring (no backboard) at each end; the aim is to pass the ball down the court and shoot it through the opponent's ring from within the 4.9 m shooting circle (World Netball, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game was created for women in England in the 1890s as an adaptation of basketball and is today played in more than 80 countries, especially across the Commonwealth; at the top level it is a women's sport, while mixed netball is played recreationally and at junior level (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The world governing body is World Netball (formerly the International Federation of Netball Associations, renamed 2020), IOC-recognized since 1995, and it publishes the official world rankings of national teams (World Netball, n.d.). ## Rules

Each team has seven players in fixed positions with restricted court areas: Goal Shooter (GS) and Goal Attack (GA) may shoot from the goal circle, the Centre (C) plays in the centre and goal thirds, and Wing Attack (WA), Wing Defence (WD), Goal Defence (GD) and Goal Keeper (GK) are restricted to their thirds (World Netball, n.d.). A player in possession may take no more than one step and must pass or shoot within three seconds; dribbling and running with the ball are prohibited (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A goal is scored only from within the 4.9 m shooting circle and counts one point under standard World Netball rules; a match lasts 60 minutes in four 15-minute quarters, ends change each quarter, and the centre pass alternates (World Netball, n.d.). Some domestic leagues modify scoring — Australia's Suncorp Super Netball awards two points for a "Super Shot" from outside the goal circle, and the Fast5 format scores one, two or three points depending on the shooting zone (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competition is organized for women at elite level, with open/mixed formats at grassroots and junior level; age groups (under-15 to seniors) follow the same rules for girls and boys until the senior women's game (World Netball, n.d.).

## History

Netball was devised in 1895 by Clara Gregory Baer, a physical education instructor at Sophie Newcomb College of Tulane University in New Orleans, while she was waiting for basketball baskets to arrive; it became the second team sport played by women in the United States after basketball and rivaled [volleyball](volleyball.html) in popularity through the 1920s, possibly influencing volleyball's development (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game spread to England, where rules were standardized, and to the Commonwealth through British influence; the Swedish pioneer Martina Bergman-Österberg introduced it to Sweden in the 1920s and helped found the international movement (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Federation of Netball Associations was founded in 1960, and the Netball World Cup has been held every four years since 1967; Australia won the 2023 edition in Cape Town (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The federation was renamed World Netball in 2020, and netball made its Commonwealth Games debut in 1998 and is bidding for Olympic inclusion (World Netball, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
