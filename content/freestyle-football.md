## Basic Information

Freestyle football is the art of juggling, controlling and performing tricks with a football using any part of the body except the hands below the elbows; in competition it is judged on difficulty, execution, style, variety and musicality (WFFA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed by the World Freestyle Football Association (WFFA), founded in 2017 in Ontario with headquarters in Barcelona, which counts 119 member countries (as of January 2026), maintains the Elo-based world rankings and runs the main events: the WFFC (World Freestyle Football Championship, the successor of Red Bull Street Style, held since 2008), the Super Ball World Open in Prague (since 2009, World Open format since 2012) and the Pulse Series of continental qualifiers (since 2023), plus NextGen U16 and World Freestyle Day on September 1; the WFFA rules were last updated in 2024 (WFFA, n.d.-a; WFFA, n.d.-b). The 2025 World Championship in Sopot went to Jesse Marlet of the Netherlands in men's and Isabel Wilkins of England in women's, with Aguska Mnich of Poland and Mathilde Fortier among the runners-up, while Super Ball 2026 runs August 17–24, 2026 (WFFA, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Isabel Wilkins (ENG), the 2025 world champion, Aguska Mnich (POL), a multiple-time champion and runner-up, and Mélody Donchet (FRA) and Kitti Szász (HUN), the first world champions of 2011, while on the men's side Jesse Marlet (NED), champion of 2024 and 2025, heads the Elo world rankings ahead of Poland's Michał Rycaj "MichRyc" and other European and Asian stars (WFFA, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the WFFA competition rules (last updated 2024), the premier format is the battle: two freestylers face off head-to-head in alternating rounds (typically 30–60 seconds each), and a panel of judges awards the round on difficulty, execution, style, variety and musicality — the player with the majority of round wins takes the battle (WFFA, n.d.-a). Other formats include routines (a choreographed 2–3 minute performance to music), the circle contest (a group of players in a circle, each showing tricks, with elimination votes), request/battle hybrids and the "kill the beat" format; at the Super Ball World Open these formats are combined in a knockout bracket (WFFA, n.d.-a; WFFA, n.d.-b). The fundamental rule is that the ball may be played with any body part except the hands and arms below the elbow; dropping the ball ends a round in battles and costs points in routines (WFFA, n.d.-a). Competitions are divided into men's and women's categories (plus junior/NextGen U16), scored by accredited judges, and the results feed the WFFA Elo world ranking, which is published for men and women separately (WFFA, n.d.-b). Ronaldinho has served as a WFFA ambassador since 2015 (WFFA, n.d.-a).

## History

Freestyle football grew out of the street football culture of Brazil and Europe in the 1990s–2000s, with pioneers such as the Frenchman Souheil Jahjah popularizing tricks in videos; the first world-level competitions were the Red Bull Street Style events from 2008, won early on by Kitti Szász of Hungary and Mélody Donchet of France in 2011 and again by Szász and Algeria's Lisa Zimouche in 2012 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; WFFA, n.d.-b). The Prague-based Super Ball, created by Lukáš "Lucaso" Škoda in 2009, became the World Open format in 2012 and remains the season's showpiece alongside the WFFC (WFFA, n.d.-b). The WFFA was founded in 2017 in Ontario (Canada) and moved its headquarters to Barcelona, formalizing the rules (updated 2024), the Elo rankings and the Pulse Series of five continental qualifiers introduced in 2023 (WFFA, n.d.-a). Ronaldinho became a WFFA ambassador in 2015, bringing global attention to the sport (WFFA, n.d.-a).
