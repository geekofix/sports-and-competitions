## Basic Information

Dog sledding (mushing) is a winter sport and mode of transport in which a team of harnessed sled dogs pulls a sled and musher over snow. Competitive racing is governed by the International Federation of Sleddog Sports (IFSS), which sanctions World Championships in sprint, middle-distance and long-distance (stage race) formats. The most famous races are the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race (Anchorage to Nome, Alaska, about 1,000 miles) and the Yukon Quest (1,000 miles between Alaska and Canada's Yukon).

## Rules

At IFSS World Championships, teams are classified by the number of dogs (from 2-dog skijoring-linked classes up to unlimited open-class teams of 12-16 dogs). Sprint races run 4-12 miles per day over several days on marked trails with mass starts or interval starts; long-distance races such as the Iditarod cover roughly 1,000 miles in 8-15 days with mandatory rests at checkpoints. The musher stands on the runners or toboggan and may push or brake; dogs must be vaccinated, microchipped and in good health, with veterinary checks at checkpoints and a mandatory rest at the halfway point. Drafting, outside assistance and doping are prohibited under IFSS anti-doping and animal-welfare regulations. Races are started with a staggered start procedure, and the first team to cross the finish line wins.

## History

Sled dogs have pulled loads across Arctic regions for thousands of years, used by Indigenous peoples of Alaska, Canada, Greenland and Siberia. Competitive racing began in the early 1900s in Nome, Alaska, and the first Iditarod was run in 1973 (won by Dick Wilmarth in about 20 days) to commemorate the historic mail trail; the Yukon Quest started in 1984. The IFSS was founded in 1960 (as the International Sled Dog Federation) and organizes World Championships since 1975 in sprint and distance formats. Legendary mushers include Joe Redington Sr. ("the father of modern sled dog racing"), Rick Swenson (five-time Iditarod winner), and Susan Butcher (four-time Iditarod champion, 1986-1990). The 1925 serum run to Nome, when sled teams delivered diphtheria antitoxin, made sled dogs national heroes and is commemorated by the Iditarod.
