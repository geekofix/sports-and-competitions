## Basic Information

Varpa is the traditional throwing game of the Swedish island of Gotland, allegedly dating back to the Viking Age: players hurl a flat, heavy disc — traditionally a suitably shaped stone, today usually a purpose-made metal or rubber disc weighing 0.5–5 kg — to land as close as possible to a wooden peg (the pinne) driven into the ground (Svenska Varpaförbundet, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Women and men both play, in separate classes and in mixed pairs: women throw from 15 metres, men from 20 metres (senior men from 18 metres), and the mixed format pairs one woman with one man on the same team (Svenska Varpaförbundet, n.d.). The game is organized by Svenska Varpaförbundet, a member of the Swedish Sports Confederation, and its highlight is the annual Stångaspelen (pole games) on Gotland, where varpa is contested alongside the island's other traditional games (Wikipedia, n.d.). Throwing at a peg instead of a ball makes varpa a cousin of the jack games [Pétanque](petanque.html) and [Bocce](bocce.html) and of the bowls [Lawn Bowls](lawn-bowls.html), [Indoor Bowls](indoor-bowls.html) and [Crown Green Bowls](crown-green-bowls.html), as well as [Irish Road Bowling](irish-road-bowling.html), [Ice Stock Sport](ice-stock-sport.html), [Shuffleboard](shuffleboard.html) and [Table Shuffleboard](table-shuffleboard.html) — all listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The wooden pin is planted at the set throwing distance — 20 m for men, 18 m for senior men and 15 m for women — and each player throws the varpa aiming to land closest to it; a stone varpa also scores when it knocks the pin over (Svenska Varpaförbundet, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Formats include the individual kula — a match played to 12 points, one point for the closest throw of each round — the team kula with three players per team, mixed pairs (one man and one woman), and the Centimeter competition, in which each of 36 throws (108 in the team version) scores the distance to the pin in centimetres, with any throw within one metre counting as 100 (Svenska Varpaförbundet, n.d.). Teams alternate throws in a fixed order, and the rules fix the disc's material and weight range (0.5–5 kg), the pin's dimensions and the measuring method (Svenska Varpaförbundet, n.d.).

## History

Varpa is said to have been played on Gotland since the Viking Age, when warriors allegedly threw stones at a peg to settle disputes and sharpen their aim (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game was demonstrated at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, where the Gotland games — varpa together with pärm (pole vaulting over a horizontal bar) and stångstörtning (pole throwing) — were shown as living Swedish folk sport (Wikipedia, n.d.). Through the twentieth century it became organized in clubs and leagues on Gotland and the Swedish mainland under Svenska Varpaförbundet, and it remains a centerpiece of the annual Stångaspelen at Hemse and other Gotland summer festivals, where women and men compete in their own classes and in mixed pairs (Svenska Varpaförbundet, n.d.).
