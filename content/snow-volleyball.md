## Basic Information

Snow volleyball is the winter counterpart of beach volleyball: teams contest a ball over a net on a snow-covered court, governed internationally by the FIVB, which with the European Volleyball Confederation (CEV) runs the Snow Volleyball World Tour and campaigns for a Winter Olympic place (FIVB, n.d.-b). The original format was two teams of two on a small court with sets to 21; the current official international game is three players per side plus one substitute (FIVB, n.d.-a). The inaugural FIVB Snow Volleyball World Tour opened in 2019 with stops in Wagrain, Austria, Kronplatz, Italy, and Bariloche, Argentina, and the 2026 tour includes a stop at Uludağ near Bursa, Türkiye; the first European women's champions were Monika Povilaitytė and Ieva Dumbauskaitė of Lithuania at Wagrain-Kleinarl in 2018 (FIVB, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the FIVB Snow Volleyball Rules, each team fields three starters and one substitute, and a match is the best of three sets to 15 points, won by two (FIVB, n.d.-a). Each side may contact the ball at most three times per rally, a block counts as a team touch but the blocker may play the ball again, and no player may touch the ball twice consecutively — the same contact rules as beach volleyball; each team may make two substitutions and take one timeout per set (FIVB, n.d.-a). The court is a rectangle dug into packed snow, smaller than a beach court, with the net at the official height; players may wear gloves, thermal clothing and traction footwear. Fouls — catching or holding the ball, a fourth contact, a double hit, or touching the net — award a point and the serve to the opponent under rally scoring (FIVB, n.d.-a). Women's and men's teams compete in parallel categories on identical courts under identical rules, with no senior classification differences; the official international programme is exclusively 3x3 (FIVB, n.d.-b).

## History

Informal snow volleyball was played for decades in Alpine countries, but the modern sport crystallised around the Austrian promoter Martin Kaswurm, whose 2008 initiative in Wagrain led to a local Snow Volleyball Tour in 2009 and recognition by the Austrian Volleyball Federation in 2011 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The CEV adopted snow volleyball in October 2015, launched the CEV Snow Volleyball European Tour in 2016, and staged the first European Championships in March 2018 at Wagrain-Kleinarl, Austria; the sport was showcased at the PyeongChang 2018 Winter Olympics at the Austria House, and the FIVB launched the inaugural Snow Volleyball World Tour in 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Kaswurm remains the founding promoter of the discipline (Wikipedia, n.d.).
