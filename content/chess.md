## Basic Information

Chess is a two-player strategy board game played on an 8×8 board with 16 pieces per side, governed worldwide by the International Chess Federation (FIDE), founded in Paris in 1924 (FIDE, n.d.-b). The elite calendar in 2026 is dominated by the World Chess Championship match between defending champion Gukesh Dommaraju of India and challenger Javokhir Sindarov of Uzbekistan — the winner of the 2026 Candidates Tournament in Paphos, Cyprus, who went through it unbeaten with 10/14 — played over 14 classical games from 24 November to 12 December 2026 at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny near Geneva, the youngest-ever title clash, both players being 20 during the event (FIDE, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). On the November 2026 FIDE rating list, Magnus Carlsen of Norway remains world No. 1 with 2823 points, followed by Hikaru Nakamura (USA, 2792), with Sindarov fourth at 2782 and Gukesh at 2710 (FIDE, n.d.-a). The 46th Chess Olympiad was held in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, in 2026, with the hosts winning the open section and China the women's section, and the first Freestyle Chess World Championship took place in February 2026 at Weissenhaus, Germany (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In women's chess, Ju Wenjun of China — four-time Women's World Champion and the leading name of the 2020s — is pictured below; she will be challenged for the 2027 Women's World Championship by Vaishali Rameshbabu of India, winner of the 2026 Women's Candidates, with Divya Deshmukh among the new generation of Indian stars (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). On the men's side the current leading players at the end of 2026 are Magnus Carlsen (No. 1), Hikaru Nakamura (No. 2), the 2026 challenger Javokhir Sindarov and defending champion Gukesh Dommaraju (FIDE, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Chess is played under the FIDE Laws of Chess, the official rulebook published and maintained by FIDE (FIDE, n.d.-b). The board has 64 squares of alternating colors; each player commands 16 pieces — king, queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, eight pawns — with the objective of checkmating the opponent's king, i.e., attacking it so that it cannot escape capture (FIDE, n.d.-b). Special rules govern castling, en passant captures and pawn promotion (a pawn reaching the far rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight); a player who touches a piece must move it if a legal move exists ("touch-move") (FIDE, n.d.-b). Draws occur by stalemate, insufficient mating material, agreement between the players, the fifty-move rule and threefold repetition of the same position (FIDE, n.d.-b). Time controls define the formats: classical (standard) chess uses more than 60 minutes per player, rapid 10–60 minutes, and blitz under 10 minutes, with increment rules; FIDE also sanctions Chess960 (Fischer random) and online/arbitrated variants (FIDE, n.d.-b). Titles and classifications differ by gender and age: open titles (GM, IM, FM) are available to all, while women-only titles (WGM, WIM, WFM) exist alongside the Women's World Championship, and separate World Junior (U20) and Senior (50+/65+) championships are organized — the Olympiad, FIDE's flagship team event, is likewise split into an open and a women's section with national teams of five playing four boards per round (FIDE, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Chess descends from the Indian game *chaturanga* (6th century AD) via the Persian *shatranj*, reached Europe by the 10th century, and took its modern form around 1475 in Spain with the empowered queen and bishop (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first official World Chess Championship was played in 1886 in New York, when Wilhelm Steinitz defeated Johannes Zukertort (FIDE, n.d.-b). The 20th century produced the iconic champions: Paul Morphy (1837–1884), the unofficial champion of the 1850s; José Raúl Capablanca (1888–1942); Alexander Alekhine (1892–1946); Bobby Fischer (1943–2008), whose 1972 victory over Boris Spassky in Reykjavík became a Cold War symbol; and Garry Kasparov (b. 1963), world champion 1985–2000, who lost the 1997 rematch to IBM's Deep Blue, the first computer victory over a reigning world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Magnus Carlsen (b. 1990) dominated the 2010s and early 2020s before Gukesh Dommaraju dethroned him in 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The women's game has its own pantheon: the Polgár sisters of Hungary — above all Judit Polgár (b. 1976), pictured below, who reached the world top 10 and a peak rating of 2735, the highest ever by a woman — Vera Menchik (1906–1944), the first Women's World Champion, Hou Yifan (b. 1994) and Ju Wenjun (b. 1991), four-time Women's World Champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
