## Basic Information

Field handball (team handball) is a fast indoor/outdoor team sport in which two teams of seven players (six court players plus a goalkeeper) pass a ball to throw it into the opponent's goal, played on a 40 m x 20 m court with a 3 m x 2 m goal. It is governed by the International Handball Federation (IHF), which runs the IHF World Men's and Women's Handball Championships, and is an Olympic sport since 1972 (men) and 1976 (women).

## Rules

At IHF World Championship level, each team fields seven players, and a match is two 30-minute halves. Players may dribble, pass and run with the ball but must not take more than three steps while holding it; contact is allowed only in a limited way (no holding, pushing or tackling from behind), and fouls are punished with free throws, 7-meter penalties (for clear scoring chances) and yellow/red cards (two-minute suspensions, three suspensions = disqualification). A goal counts when the whole ball crosses the goal line. Throw-offs restart play after goals; the goalkeeper may use the whole body inside the 6 m goal area but may not hold the ball more than 3 seconds. Extra time (two 5-minute periods) decides draws at knockout stages. The ball is 58-60 cm in circumference and 425-475 g (men), smaller and lighter for women.

## History

Modern handball was codified in northern Europe at the end of the 19th century — Danish teacher Holger Nielsen and German gymnastics teacher Karl Schelenz formalized rules around 1906-1917 — combining elements of football and basketball. The IHF was founded in 1946 in Copenhagen and has organized World Championships since 1938 (men, outdoor) and 1957 (women). Handball entered the Olympics in Berlin 1936 (outdoor, 11-a-side) and became an indoor 7-a-side Olympic sport in Munich 1972. Denmark, France, Sweden, Germany, Norway, Spain and Egypt are traditional powers; stars include Mikkel Hansen, Nikola Karabatić and Stine Bredal Oftedal.
