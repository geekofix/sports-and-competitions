## Basic Information

Motoball (also called motorcycle polo) is a team sport that combines association football with motorcycling: four players per team ride motorcycles on a football pitch while a fifth player, the goalkeeper, stands on foot in the goal. The aim is to drive a large ball into the opponent's goal. The ball is much bigger than a football, about 40 cm in diameter, so it can be controlled with the feet and pinned between bike and leg. Motoball is organized internationally under FIM Europe, with France, Germany and other European countries as the heartland of the sport.

## Rules

A match is played 5 against 5 on a standard football field about 100 m long, in four periods of 20 minutes each. The only area motorcycles may not enter is the semicircle in front of each goal, and goals may only be scored by kicking the ball with the legs from outside that restricted zone. Riders wear helmets and shin guards, and the bikes are specially prepared machines with engines limited to 250cc (two- or four-stroke) at senior level, with lower limits (85cc two-stroke, 150cc four-stroke) in youth classes; special frames are mounted at the sides of the bike so riders can pinch the ball between machine and leg. The FIM Europe Motoball Rules (Appendix 075) govern international matches, including the annual European Championship for national teams.

## History

Motoball began as an officially organized sport in the mid-1920s to mid-1930s in France and spread across Europe, with France remaining its heartland. It was included in the program of the inaugural Goodwill Games in Moscow in 1986. The FIM Europe Motoball Commission, chaired since 2012 by Pascale Reschko Jacquot of the French Motoball Commission, sets the international rule changes and appoints referees, and the Motoball European Championship has been held annually since 1986, traditionally dominated by France, Germany, Russia and other Eastern European teams.
