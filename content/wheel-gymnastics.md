## Basic Information

Wheel gymnastics — German *Rhönradturnen*, performed on the Rhönrad, gym wheel or German wheel — is a gymnastics discipline in which athletes execute rolls, balances and acrobatic elements inside a large wheel of two parallel rims joined by six spokes, sized between 130 and 245 cm in diameter and weighing 40 to 60 kg (Wikipedia, n.d.). The three competition disciplines are straight line (rolling along the floor, with or without music), spiral (the wheel rolling on its rim in small, big or vertical spirals) and vault (the wheel used as a vaulting table for somersaults), scored under the IRV Code of Points with difficulty and execution marks combining to a maximum of 20 points (International Wheel Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). The sport is governed by the International Wheel Gymnastics Federation (IRV), founded in 1995 and headquartered in Bern, Switzerland, which runs biennial World Championships — first held in Den Helder in 1995 and most recently in Almere in 2024 — with heartlands in Germany, Switzerland, the Netherlands, the United States and Japan. Women and men compete at the highest level, and the leading women of the current era are Switzerland's Cheyenne Rechsteiner — straight-line world champion 2015 and spiral world champion 2018 and 2024 — and her compatriots who have taken straight-line and all-around titles at the recent championships (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Straight line is performed along a marked runway as rolling exercises with or without music; spiral comprises rotations of the wheel on its own rim — small spiral, big spiral and vertical spiral; vault uses the stationary wheel as a springboard for acrobatic leaps and somersaults (International Wheel Gymnastics Federation, n.d.). Routines are judged under the IRV Code of Points: a difficulty value of up to 10 points plus an execution score of up to 10 points, minus deductions, give a maximum of 20 points, and the all-around title combines the three disciplines (Wikipedia, n.d.). National federations run graded competition levels from basic to elite, and at world championships women and men compete in their own events, with junior age-group categories at youth level.

## History

The Rhönrad was invented by Otto Feick (4 July 1890, Reichenbach – 17 October 1959, Schönau an der Brend), a German locksmith and railwayman who worked for Deutsche Reichsbahn in Ludwigshafen from 1914 to 1923 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Recalling how as a child he had tied two iron hoops together and rolled them down a hill — a memory that returned while he was jailed in Mainz in 1921 — he built the first prototype in Ludwigshafen around 1920–1922 on the grounds of the sports club VSK Germania, which he had founded in 1919. After moving to Schönau an der Brend in 1923 he applied for a patent, granted on 8 November 1925 (no. 442057, wheel-gymnastic and sports equipment), and the name Rhönrad — after his new home in the Rhön mountains — was protected from 1926 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Feick toured Germany with a show group from 1926, the wheel was demonstrated at the GeSoLei trade fair in Düsseldorf in 1926 and by 120 gymnasts at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, and the German Gymnastics Federation recognised the discipline in 1959, with the first German championships in Hanover in 1960 (Wikipedia, n.d.). International competition began with a European championship in Liestal in 1992, and the Internationaler Rhönradturnverband was founded in 1995, staging the first World Championships in Den Helder that same year; on the women's side, Switzerland's Cécilia Meschberger won the straight-line world title in 2007 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
