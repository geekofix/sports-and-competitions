## Basic Information

Sailing is the sport of racing wind-powered boats — dinghies, skiffs, multihulls, keelboats and boards — on marked courses, governed worldwide by World Sailing (founded in 1907 as the International Yacht Racing Union, renamed World Sailing in 2015) (World Sailing, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It has been on the Olympic programme since the 1900 Paris Games; at Paris 2024 the ten events were raced in Marseille, with Italy's Ruggero Tita and Caterina Banti winning the mixed Nacra 17 gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). For Los Angeles 2028, confirmed for 16–28 July 2028, ten events and 330 sailors — an equal 165 men and 165 women — will race at two venues: the board events (Formula Kite and iQFoil, men's and women's) at Belmont Shore in Long Beach and the boat events (ILCA 6/7, 470, 49er, 49erFX, Nacra 17) at the Port of Los Angeles in San Pedro (World Sailing, 2025; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Men's and women's events are fully equal, with mixed classes for the 470 and Nacra 17 (World Sailing, n.d.).

Among current leading sailors, the women's fleet includes Paris 2024 gold medalists Marit Bouwmeester (Netherlands, ILCA 6, her third Olympic title), Odile van Aanholt and Annette Duetz (Netherlands, 49erFX), Sharon Kantor (Israel, women's iQFoil) and Ellie Aldridge (Great Britain, women's Formula Kite); the men's side includes Matt Wearn (Australia, ILCA 7), Tom Reuveny (Israel, men's iQFoil), Toni Vodisek (Slovenia, men's Formula Kite) and Spain's Diego Botín and Florián Trittel (49er), alongside the mixed Nacra 17 champions Tita and Banti (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Racing is governed by the Racing Rules of Sailing (RRS), published every four years by World Sailing: boats race a course of legs between marks, with fleet races scored by low-point system — one point per place, discards after a set number of races — followed by a medal race for the top ten (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Starting sequences are controlled by flags (Z, I, U, black-flag disqualifications for premature starters), and right-of-way rules (port/starboard, windward/leeward, mark-room) decide overlaps; protests are heard by a jury (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Olympic classes are one-design or tightly controlled one-design/box-rule classes so equipment is equal; beyond the Olympics, World Sailing recognizes dozens of classes from the Optimist dinghy for children to maxi yachts and offshore classes raced around the world (World Sailing, n.d.). Match racing — one-on-one duels — decides the America's Cup, the oldest trophy in sport, first raced in 1851 (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

## History

Organized yacht racing began in 1815 with the founding of the New York Yacht Club, and the America's Cup was first contested in 1851 around the Isle of Wight, won by the schooner *America* (Wikipedia, n.d.-d). Sailing has been an Olympic sport since 1900 (with women first competing in 1900 as crew). The International Yacht Racing Union was founded in London in 1907 by 14 nations and became World Sailing in 2015 (World Sailing, n.d.). The 2023 Sailing World Championships in The Hague combined all Olympic classes and qualified nations for Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Retired icons include the American Dennis Conner, the first sailor to win and lose the America's Cup; New Zealand's Peter Burling, who with Blair Tuke won 49er gold in 2016 and silver in 2020 and 2024; and Britain's Ben Ainslie, the most decorated Olympic sailor of all time with five medals across five Games from 1996 to 2016 (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
