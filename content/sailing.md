## Basic Information

Sailing as a competitive sport is the racing of sail-driven craft — dinghies, skiffs, catamarans, windsurfers, kiteboards, keelboats and yachts — around courses marked by buoys, governed worldwide by World Sailing, founded in 1907 as the International Yacht Racing Union. Sailing has been on the Olympic programme since 1900 (its 1896 debut was cancelled by weather), with ten events at Paris 2024 ranging from the ILCA dinghies to the foiling windsurfer (iQFOiL) and kiteboarding (Formula Kite). Beyond the Games, the sport spans world championship classes, the America's Cup match racing, offshore and ocean racing, and club fleets worldwide.

## Rules

Racing is governed by World Sailing's Racing Rules of Sailing (RRS), updated on a four-year cycle aligned with the Olympic quadrennium, whose core is the right-of-way rules between boats on opposite tacks and at marks, plus protest procedures by which a wronged boat may seek redress through on-water flags and post-race hearings. Olympic-class racing uses fleet races around windward-leeward or trapezoid courses: typically ten or twelve opening-series races, whose low-point scores qualify the top ten boats for a double-points medal race. Equipment is controlled by strict one-design "class" rules — the Olympic events each specify their equipment, such as the single-handed ILCA 6 and ILCA 7 dinghies, the two-person skiffs (49er and 49erFX), the mixed 470 dinghy and Nacra 17 catamaran, the iQFOiL windsurfer and the Formula Kite — while para competition runs in classes such as the 2.4mR and Hansa 303 under World Sailing's para framework. Handicap and rating systems (IRC, ORC) govern mixed-fleet offshore racing, and events are subject to equipment inspection and event classification from club level up to world championships.

## History

Yacht racing institutionalised in the 19th century — the America's Cup began in 1851 and remains sport's oldest international trophy, while national authorities and the IYRU (founded 1907, later ISAF, renamed World Sailing in 2015) codified measurement and racing rules. Sailing appeared at the first modern Olympics in 1896 (cancelled) and has been ever-present since 1900, its Olympic classes changing constantly: 46 different classes have been used, from the 12-metre boats of early Games to today's foiling craft, with windsurfing added in 1984, the 470 introduced in 1976, the 49er skiff in 2000, the first mixed events arriving in the 2020s, kiteboarding joining at Paris 2024 — where a planned mixed offshore event was dropped from the programme — and the number of events stabilised at ten. World championships are staged annually in dozens of classes, from the Optimist junior dinghy worldwide to keelboat classes, while offshore and ocean racing grew from races such as the Fastnet (1925), the Sydney–Hobart (1945) and the Volvo/Ocean Race round-the-world events; dinghy classes blossomed worldwide after 1950 under class associations, and the modern era has seen foiling technology — hydrofoils lifting boards and boats above the water — transform elite racing from the America's Cup's foiling monohulls to Olympic windsurfing's iQFOiL.
