## Basic Information

Ball hockey is the off-ice version of ice hockey: teams play in gym shoes on a dry court with a ball instead of a puck, using curved sticks. It is governed internationally by the International Street and Ball Hockey Federation (ISBHF), which is recognized by the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) and organizes the Ball Hockey World Championship, first held in 1996.

## Rules

Under ISBHF rules, a team fields four skaters plus a goalkeeper (some tournaments use five skaters), playing two 20-minute halves (or four 12-minute quarters in some leagues) with rolling substitutions. Goals are scored by hitting the ball into the opponent's net with the stick; the ball may be played with the stick, feet or body but not above shoulder height with the stick (high-stick rule). Offside and icing apply as in ice hockey, body checking is prohibited, and infractions lead to free hits or penalty shots. At the World Championship, men's and women's national teams compete in a round-robin followed by playoffs.

## History

Ball hockey developed in Canada and Europe in the 1960s as a cheap alternative to ice hockey, played in streets, gyms and parking lots. The ISBHF was founded in 1993 and held the first Ball Hockey World Championship in 1996; since then the tournament has grown into a biennial event with dozens of nations. Street hockey (the related inline variant) became especially popular in North America and Russia.
