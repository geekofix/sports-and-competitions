## Basic Information

Ninja sport (ninja-style obstacle course racing) is a competitive format in which athletes race against the clock over short courses with closely spaced technical obstacles — the warped wall, salmon ladder, balance elements, climbing and swinging structures — inspired by the Japanese TV show SASUKE (1997) and its international adaptations such as American Ninja Warrior (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is a discipline of the obstacle sports family alongside [obstacle course racing](obstacle-course-racing.html), and it was governed internationally by World Obstacle (FISO), whose structure has been integrating into the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM) since 2026; ninja-style obstacle racing will also become the fifth discipline of [modern pentathlon](modern-pentathlon.html) at Los Angeles 2028 (World Obstacle, n.d.-a). Women and men compete in separate elite categories in the Ninja World Championships and in league formats such as the World Ninja League (World Obstacle, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In Ninja Speed, the athlete runs a linear course of about 12 obstacles (FISO competition rules) from start line to finish line in the fastest possible time; falling or failing an obstacle ends the run or costs time depending on the ruleset (World Obstacle, n.d.-b). In league formats such as the World Ninja League and the Ultimate Ninja Athlete Association, courses have 10 to 20 obstacles in a predetermined order, athletes get one attempt per obstacle (except the warped wall), and scoring combines points for completed obstacles with the clock time — most points wins, fastest time breaks ties; UNAA allows a maximum of three fails per course (Ultimate Ninja Athlete Association, n.d.). Courses must contain a mix of upper-body, lower-body and balance obstacles, and athletes compete in age divisions from children (as young as 6 in some leagues) to adults, with men's and women's categories at elite level (World Obstacle, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The format originated with SASUKE, first broadcast in Japan in 1997, where 100 competitors faced a four-stage obstacle course; the franchise was exported as Ninja Warrior and aired in more than 160 countries (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Outside television, ninja gyms and leagues emerged in the 2010s: the National Ninja League (now World Ninja League) and the Ultimate Ninja Athlete Association were both founded in 2015, each running season-long series that culminate in world finals (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). World Obstacle (FISO), founded in Lausanne in the 2010s as the international federation for obstacle sports, recognized Ninja alongside OCR, gained GAISF observer status in 2021, and staged World Championships; in 2026 its members voted to integrate into the UIPM, which in May 2026 also agreed with SASUKE producer TBS to use ninja-style obstacles in the LA 2028 modern pentathlon event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Obstacle, n.d.-a).
