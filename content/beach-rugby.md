## Basic Information

Beach rugby is rugby union played on sand with fewer players — typically five per side (Beach Rugby 5s) or, in the classic variant, smaller teams on a reduced pitch — with continuous, high-scoring play and diving tries. It is governed by World Rugby and is contested at the World Rugby Beach Rugby Championships and at multi-sport beach events such as the World Beach Games.

## Rules

Under World Rugby beach rugby rules, teams of five players (four on the field plus one substitute in some formats) play on a 50 x 35 m sand pitch with goals at each end. Matches last two halves of 5-7 minutes with a running clock. After a tackle, play continues with quick rucks and mauls on the sand; the ball must be passed within 5 seconds of a tackle. Tries are worth 1 point (no conversions), and the team with the most tries wins. At the Beach Rugby World Championships, national teams play group stages followed by knockout rounds.

## History

Beach rugby grew out of rugby sevens and tag rugby in coastal communities in the 1990s, with early tournaments in France, Italy and South Africa. World Rugby codified beach rugby formats and sanctioned international events, including the Beach Rugby World Championships, and the sport appeared at the 2019 World Beach Games in Qatar and the 2023 European Games. It remains especially popular in Europe, South America and Oceania.
