## Basic Information

Asafo atwele is a Ghanaian rhythm game of the Fante asafo companies (warrior guilds): players clap and chant in call-and-response patterns, competing between asafo groups at festivals and funerals (Wikipedia, n.d.). Atwele combines clapping games with dance and is a signature element of Fante cultural life around Cape Coast and Accra. Within the folk and festival games on this site, its closest relatives are [Ampe](ampe.html), [Gidigbo](gidigbo.html), [Abula](abula.html), [Langa](langa.html), [Rock Paper Scissors](rock-paper-scissors.html), [Hand Clapping Games](hand-clapping games.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Two groups face each other and alternate clapping/chanting verses; the opposing group must answer correctly and in rhythm; missed or off-beat answers lose points. Performed at asafo festivals, funerals and competitions, with both adult companies and school groups (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The asafo companies of the Fante people date back centuries as town defence and mutual-aid societies; their atwele clapping contests remain a living tradition (Wikipedia, n.d.).
