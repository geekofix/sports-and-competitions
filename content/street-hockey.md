## Basic Information

Street hockey is the collective name for outdoor hockey variants played on asphalt, concrete or sport court — on foot with a ball (ball hockey, dek hockey, road hockey) or on inline skates (inline/roller hockey) — with a puck or ball propelled into a goal by a hockey stick; pickup games use minimal equipment and locally agreed rules such as no slapshots and no body checking, while organized play is governed by the International Street and Ball Hockey Federation (ISBHF) for ball hockey and the International Inline Hockey Federation (IIHF-affiliated bodies) for inline hockey (ISBHF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The ISBHF, based in the Czech Republic and recognized by the IIHF, has 41 member countries as of 2026 and runs biannual Ball Hockey World Championships — the 2026 men's and women's championships were held in Ostrava, Czech Republic, 20–28 June 2026, with the women's event in its 10th edition; the first world championship took place in Bratislava in 1996 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Both men's and women's divisions are played at every world championship, and mixed pickup games are open to all genders (ISBHF, n.d.).

## Rules

In ISBHF ball hockey, teams field five runners plus a goaltender on a court of roughly 40 x 20 m (minimum 26 x 13 m for smaller venues), using hockey sticks and a specially designed orange ball instead of a puck; games run in two 20-minute halves (halftime 10 minutes), with goals scored by shooting the ball into the 1.83 x 1.22 m net, and a match tied after regulation decided by overtime and then a shootout (ISBHF, n.d.). The ISBHF Rulebook and Regulations cover offside via a floating blue line, icing, fouls (hooking, tripping, slashing, high-sticking, roughing), penalties (2-minute minors, 5-minute majors, misconducts) served in a penalty box, and goalkeeper equipment — helmet with full mask, chest protector, leg pads, blocker and trapper — while runners must wear helmets, gloves and shin guards at international tournaments (ISBHF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Pickup street hockey on asphalt usually drops protective gear to gloves and helmet, bans slapshots, and plays 4-on-4 or 5-on-5 with a goalie, two 20-minute halves and running clock; inline hockey follows IIHF rules on a rink of 61 x 26 m with four 15-minute periods and body checking allowed in adult leagues. Men and women play under the same ISBHF rules in separate divisions at world championships, and youth age groups run at national tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Organized ball hockey began in Canada in the 1960s — Native peoples had played variants as early as 1572 — followed by the United States in the 1970s and Europe in the 1980s and 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first Ball Hockey World Championship was held in Bratislava, Slovakia, in 1996, and the ISBHF has staged men's championships biennially since, with a women's championship added and the 2026 edition in Ostrava, Czech Republic, 20–28 June 2026 (ISBHF, n.d.). Street hockey pickup culture on asphalt grew in North American cities in the 1950s–1970s, with the "boot hockey" tradition of Canadian prairie kids using snow chunks as goalposts (Wikipedia, n.d.).
