## Basic Information

The 10,000 metres — 25 laps of the track from a standing start — is the longest race on the Olympic track programme, governed by World Athletics (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 26:11.00 for men, set by Joshua Cheptegei of Uganda in Valencia in 2020, and 28:54.14 for women, set by Beatrice Chebet of Kenya at the 2024 Prefontaine Classic as the first woman under 29 minutes (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by Chebet, pictured at the 2023 Budapest final, and Agnes Ngetich, while the men's race was headed by Cheptegei (pictured), who has since focused on road racing, and Grant Fisher of the USA (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

## Rules

The 10,000 m starts from a standing start and is run as a pack race without lanes, with a lap counter; lapping applies. Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

The 10,000 m has been an Olympic event since 1912 for men and 1988 for women, after women first ran it at the 1984 Los Angeles Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Emil Zátopek of Czechoslovakia won the 5000 m, 10,000 m and marathon hat-trick at Helsinki 1952; Kenenisa Bekele set 26:17.53 in 2005 before Cheptegei's 26:11.00 in 2020; Haile Gebreselassie of Ethiopia and Mo Farah of Great Britain were the dominant champions of the 2000s and 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, Almaz Ayana of Ethiopia ran 29:17.45 in Rio 2016 before Chebet lowered the record to 28:54.14 in 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
