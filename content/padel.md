## Basic Information

Padel is a racket sport that combines elements of [tennis](tennis.html) and [squash](squash.html), played almost always in doubles on a small enclosed court of 20 by 10 meters whose glass and mesh walls are part of the game. It was invented in 1969 by the Mexican businessman Enrique Corcuera at his home Las Brisas in Acapulco, originally called "Paddle Corcuera", after he walled in a space too small for a tennis court. The International Padel Federation (FIP), founded in Madrid on 12 July 1991, governs the sport, and the Padel World Championship has been held every two years since 1992. According to the FIP, more than 35 million people play padel in over 150 countries, and the sport is targeting Olympic inclusion at Brisbane 2032.

## Rules

The court measures 20 by 10 meters with a net across the middle, and is enclosed by walls about 3 meters high in glass and 4 meters at the ends in glass plus mesh. Players use a solid, stringless, perforated racket and a tennis-like ball. The serve is underhand, struck at or below waist level into the diagonally opposite service box. Scoring is identical to tennis: 15, 30, 40, game; six games win a set, with a tiebreak at 6 all; matches are best of three sets, and from 2026 an optional "star point" at deuce may be used. The defining feature is the walls: after the ball has bounced once in a player's court, it may be played directly or off the side or back wall, and on the opponent's side the ball may be played off the back glass after the bounce, so rallies continue long after the ball would be out in tennis. A ball that bounces twice, or that is hit into the net or over the walls without bouncing, loses the point.

## History

Enrique Corcuera built the first padel court in 1969 at his home in Acapulco and, according to the FIP, his wife Viviana wrote the first set of rules. Prince Alfonso de Hohenlohe-Langenburg, who summered at Corcuera's house, built the first courts in Spain at the Marbella Club Hotel in 1974, and the Argentine tennis player Julio Menditeguy brought the sport to Argentina in the mid-1970s, where it exploded in popularity; Argentina also pioneered the all-glass court in 1989. The International Padel Federation was founded in Madrid in 1991 by the Argentine, Spanish and Uruguayan associations, and the first Padel World Championship was held in Madrid and Seville in 1992, with every final since featuring Argentina against Spain or Brazil. The professional circuit evolved from the Padel Pro Tour (2005) through the World Padel Tour (2013) to Premier Padel, launched in 2022 with Qatar Sports Investments and unified with the WPT from 2024; the four Majors are held in Doha, Rome, Paris and Acapulco. Padel was included in the 2023 European Games in Kraków, and stars of the professional game include Alejandro Galán, Juan Lebrón, Arturo Coello, Gemma Triay and Ariana Sánchez, alongside former tennis professionals such as Roberta Vinci and Lara Arruabarrena.
