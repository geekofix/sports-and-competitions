## Basic Information

Wheelchair rugby (known widely by its former nickname murderball) is a coed Paralympic team contact sport for athletes with impairments affecting both arms and legs, combining elements of rugby, basketball and handball — played indoors on a basketball-size court by teams of four, with players carrying a volleyball across a goal line to score while colliding in purpose-built defensive chairs.

It is the only mixed-team full-contact Paralympic sport, with men and women playing on the same teams, and is governed by World Wheelchair Rugby with world championships and an international club circuit.

## Rules

Each team fields four players whose classification values (0.5 to 3.5 points by functional ability, including arm and hand function) may not sum above 8.0 points on court. Players carry the ball across the opponent's goal line to score, in games of four eight-minute quarters; possession lasts 10 seconds per player (12 seconds to advance it into the attacking half) with the 40-second shot clock forcing an attempt. Full chair contact is allowed between players who are competing for the ball, personal fouls lead to penalties, and the ball may be passed forward without limitation. Players must remain seated in their chairs and dribble with the ball on their lap, with the sport using a standard volleyball rather than a rugby ball.

## History

The sport was invented in Canada in 1977 by a group of quadriplegic athletes in Winnipeg seeking a full-contact alternative to wheelchair basketball, and was first called murderball. It spread through North America in the 1980s under the name quad rugby, reached international level with the first world championship in 1996 (after three trial world tournaments in Toronto in 1989 through 1993) and the wheel-rugby organisations uniting into an international body, and after a demonstration appearance at Atlanta 1996 it became a full Paralympic medal sport at Sydney 2000. The 2005 documentary Murderball raised its global profile considerably, and the sport now has world championships every four years plus a Champions Cup club circuit in North America.
