## Basic Information

Playboating is freestyle kayaking performed by surfing and doing tricks on river waves and hydraulics (stoppers, eddies, holes) rather than in a judged arena — though judged playboating competitions use the ICF freestyle rules on a feature (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is the most widely practised form of freestyle kayaking, with a global community and iconic venues such as the Eiskanal in Augsburg, Germany — an Olympic whitewater venue turned playboating mecca (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are [Freestyle Kayak](freestyle-kayak.html), [Canoe Freestyle](canoe-freestyle.html), [Kayak Cross](kayak-cross.html), [Canoe Slalom](canoe-slalom.html), [Hydrospeed](hydrospeed.html), [Whitewater Rafting Race](whitewater-rafting-race.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

In judged competition, playboating follows ICF freestyle rules: short runs on a single hydraulic, panel-scored moves and combinations. Recreational playboating has no formal rules beyond river safety (helmet, PFD, never paddle alone). Equipment is a short playboat (about 1.7–2.0 m) with a planing hull; men and women compete in the same classes (ICF, n.d.).

## History

Playboating emerged in the late 1980s–1990s as kayakers discovered surfable river features; the sport split from competitive rodeo as the freer, surf-oriented discipline. The Eiskanal in Augsburg and the Ottawa River are iconic venues (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
