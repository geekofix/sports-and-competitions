## Basic Information

Natural track luge (German: Naturbahn) is the original form of [luge](luge.html), raced on tracks adapted from existing mountain roads and paths, packed with snow and naturally iced, with no artificial banking or refrigeration. It is governed by the International Luge Federation (FIL), which also governs artificial-track luge, and it is not an Olympic discipline — at the Winter Games only artificial tracks are used. Natural track racing is slower than kunstbahn luge but more physical and technical, because riders must brake hard and steer actively over rough, unbanked curves.

## Rules

Natural tracks are typically 400 to 1,200 m long, with a level (unbanked) cross-section, a maximum average gradient of about 13 percent and lane widths of at least 3 m; curves have a minimum radius of about 7 m and are delimited with wooden bands or foam mats. Competitions are held in men's singles, women's singles, men's doubles and a mixed team relay, usually over two runs with the combined time deciding the result (doubles and the team event race once). Sleds are simpler than artificial-track sleds, with runners and a seat, and athletes wear helmets and aerodynamic suits. The FIL World Luge Natural Track Championships have been held almost biennially since 1979, and European Championships since 1970.

## History

Natural track luge is the ancient form of the sport: sledding on natural mountain tracks existed for centuries before artificial tracks were built in the 1960s. The first natural track European Championships took place in Kapfenberg, Austria in 1970, and the first World Championships in Inzing, Austria in 1979. The sport remains centered in the Alpine countries — Austria, Italy, Germany — but also in Poland, Russia, Canada and the United States, with more than 60 natural tracks in FIL member countries. Dominant champions include Austria's Gerhard Pilz (five straight men's world titles) and Italy's Patrick Pigneter (multiple men's singles and doubles titles), while the FIL has campaigned for natural track luge's inclusion in future Winter Games.
