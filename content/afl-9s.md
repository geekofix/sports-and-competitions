## Basic Information

AFL 9s is the Australian Football League's official nine-a-side, social touch variant of Australian rules football, run mainly as a schools and community program for ages 13 and up in male, female and mixed competitions (Australian Football League [AFL], n.d.-a). It is played on a smaller field with a smaller ball and modified rules so that more players get involved in every play. Participation is strongly gender-balanced compared with the elite game: in 2024 more than 6,600 AFL 9s teams were registered — around 35% of players were women and girls, and in New South Wales the share reached 51% — and across all AFL programs a record 625,000 Australians participated in 2025, with women's and girls' participation up 45% from 2022 to 2025 to more than 144,000 (AFL, n.d.-b; AFL, n.d.-c). See also [Australian Rules Football](australian-rules-football.html) and [AFLX](aflx.html) on this site (Australian Football League, n.d.-a) (Australian Football League, n.d.-b) (Australian Football League, n.d.-c).

## Rules

AFL 9s is played with nine players per side plus interchanges on a reduced-size oval, with a smaller ball and shorter quarters than the full game. The key modification is that tackling is replaced by a one-handed touch: after being touched, a player must dispose of the ball by handball or kick, keeping the game continuous and safe (AFL, n.d.-a). In mixed-gender games, a goal kicked by a female forward is worth 9 points — three more than any other goal — to reward the women's contribution. Standard Australian rules otherwise apply in adapted form: marks, free kicks, 50-metre penalties and the behind (1 point) and goal (6 points) scoring system. Games are typically played weekly within seasons of no more than about ten weeks, making the format ideal for schools, workplaces and community groups (AFL, 2023).

## History

The roots of AFL 9s lie in "Rec Footy", a recreational nine-a-side touch format codified in 2003, which the AFL branded as AFL 9s in 2011 and relaunched as AFL Nines in 2016 (AFL, n.d.-a). Since then the program has grown steadily: by 2024 more than 6,600 teams were registered nationwide with around 35% women and girls, and in 2025 Victoria's Nines participation grew by a further 27%, contributing to the record 625,000 total participants across all AFL programs (AFL, n.d.-b; AFL, n.d.-c). The format has become one of the AFL's main participation pathways, alongside school programs and Auskick (AFL, n.d.-a).
