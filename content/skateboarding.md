## Basic Information

Skateboarding is the sport and culture of riding and performing tricks on a skateboard, which grew from a Californian sidewalk pastime in the 1940s\u201350s into a global youth movement and, since the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, an Olympic sport with men's and women's events. It is governed internationally by World Skate, which recognises two Olympic disciplines \u2014 street and park \u2014 alongside a vast non-competitive culture of video parts, DIY spots and skateparks.

## Rules

In Olympic-format competition, the two disciplines are judged on style, difficulty, consistency and use of the course. Street contests are held on a flat course of rails, ledges, stairs and banks mimicking urban architecture: skaters perform two timed 45-second runs (best scored) and then five single "best trick" attempts, with the best run and best two tricks counting and judged on a 0\u2013100 scale. Park contests take place in a bowl-shaped course of ramps, bumps and quarter-pipes resembling an empty swimming pool, where skaters get three 45-second runs, with the highest-scoring run deciding the result and ties broken by the second-highest run. Judging panels score on criteria including difficulty and originality of tricks, speed, flow and use of the whole course; falls reduce run scores, and the design of the park course is withheld until shortly before competition. Leading professional circuits include the Street League Skateboarding series and the World Skate tour, whose rankings feed Olympic qualification.

## History

Skateboarding emerged in California in the 1940s\u201350s as "sidewalk surfing", surfers improvising wheeled boards for flat days, and the first skate contest took place at Hermosa Beach in 1963, sponsored by Makaha; the 1975 Bahne-Cadillac Championships at Del Mar \u2014 where the Zephyr "Z-Boys" of Venice and Santa Monica changed skateboarding's aesthetic forever \u2014 and the 1970s urethane-wheel and Dogtown revolutions turned it into a technical sport. The first skateparks, halfpipes and street eras followed, with Tony Hawk (first pro contest win at 11, and later the first documented 900) and street pioneers such as Tommy Guerrero and Mark Gonzales defining the modern discipline. Governing bodies formed and fractured through the 1990s and 2000s, with World Skate (formed from a merger of skateboarding and roller-sport bodies) recognised by the IOC; after a debut at the Nanjing 2014 Youth Olympics, skateboarding entered the Olympic programme at Tokyo 2020 (held 2021) with street and park events \u2014 where Yuto Horigome and Momiji Nishiya won the first street golds and young stars such as Rayssa Leal became global celebrities \u2014 and remained on the programme at Paris 2024, where it became mandatory from Los Angeles 2028. Japan has emerged as the sport's unexpected Olympic superpower.
