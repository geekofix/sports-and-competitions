## Basic Information

Duathlon is a multisport race in continuous run–cycle–run format — a first running leg, a cycling leg and a second running leg, essentially triathlon without the swim — governed worldwide by World Triathlon (formerly the International Triathlon Union, ITU) (World Triathlon, n.d.-a). At the 2026 World Triathlon Duathlon Championships in Abu Dhabi on 14 November 2026, Germany's Lisa Tertsch won the women's title five seconds ahead of Diana Isakova, with Bakker taking bronze, while Italy's Davide Menichelli won the men's title in a 5–30–5 km race (World Triathlon, n.d.-b). At the end of 2026, the leading current duathletes are — in the women's race — 2026 world champion Lisa Tertsch of Germany and her compatriot Bakker, and — in the men's race — 2026 world champion Davide Menichelli of Italy and 2025 world champion Benjamin Choquert of France (World Triathlon, n.d.-b).

 Within the multisport and endurance race family on this site, its closest relatives are [Triathlon](triathlon.html), [Aquathlon](aquathlon.html), [Winter Triathlon](winter-triathlon.html), [Cross Triathlon](cross-triathlon.html), [Swimrun](swimrun.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Duathlon is played under the World Triathlon Competition Rules, the official rulebook that also covers triathlon (World Triathlon, n.d.-a). Standard elite distances are offered in several formats: sprint (5 km run, 20 km bike, 2.5 km run), standard (10 km run, 40 km bike, 5 km run), middle distance (10 km run, 60 km bike, 10 km run) and long distance (10 km run, 150 km bike, 30 km run), with the exact course set by the organizer (World Triathlon, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Races are continuous: after the first run the athletes change in the transition area and ride the bike leg — duathlon is draft-legal, so riders may sit in one another's slipstream, unlike Olympic triathlon (World Triathlon, n.d.-a). Dangerous riding, obstruction, outside assistance and other infringements draw time penalties, yellow or red cards and, for serious offences, disqualification (World Triathlon, n.d.-a). World Triathlon also sanctions off-road duathlons (trail run, mountain bike, trail run), winter duathlons and the long-distance championship held with Powerman Zofingen in Switzerland (World Triathlon, n.d.-a). Age-group, under-23, junior and paraduathlon categories run alongside the elite races, and men's and women's elite fields race the same course in separate classifications with identical rules (World Triathlon, n.d.-a).

## History

Duathlon developed in the 1980s when multisport athletes began dropping the swimming leg to create run–bike–run races, and the first World Duathlon Championships were held in 1990, with the American Kenny Souza taking the first men's world title and a women's world title awarded from the same first edition (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The governing body, the International Triathlon Union, was founded in 1989 in The Hague under its first president Les McDonald of Canada — credited with bringing triathlon to the Olympic program at Sydney 2000 — and it renamed itself World Triathlon in 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The long-distance world championship has been held since 1997, mostly jointly with Powerman Zofingen in Switzerland, billed as the toughest duathlon in the world (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the sport include Belgium's Benny Vansteelant (1964–2007), winner of world, European and multiple Powerman long-distance titles; New Zealand's Erin Baker (born 23 May 1961 in Kaiapoi), the first ITU world champion of 1989 in triathlon, who also won the 1991 duathlon world title and paved the way for women in multisport; and Great Britain's Emma Pooley, four-time long-distance duathlon world champion from 2014 to 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
