## Basic Information

The "electrician games" are competitive skills championships for electricians, best known in the United States as the IDEAL Electrician National Championship (part of the Elite Trades Championship Series since 2016), broadcast on ESPN, and internationally as the electrical-installation competitions at WorldSkills. Contestants race through practical wiring and troubleshooting tasks, judged on speed, accuracy and compliance with the National Electrical Code (NEC).

## Rules

At the IDEAL Nationals, competitors complete a series of hands-on tasks — bending conduit, installing outlets, switches, panels and lighting, wiring a motor control circuit and troubleshooting a faulty circuit — within a set time. Judges score each task against the NEC and the competition's technical standards; mistakes such as code violations, loose connections or incorrect wire sizing cost points, and unsafe work can disqualify. The event uses a bracket/heat format with individual and team categories (apprentice, journeyman), and the top performers advance to the finals. At WorldSkills competitions, electricians wire a complete installation (lighting, sockets, distribution board, motor circuit) in a mock-up apartment over several days, judged on international standards (IEC) with strict safety and time rules.

## History

Skilled-trades competitions have a long tradition in the building industry, from medieval guild contests to the modern WorldSkills movement, founded in 1950 in Spain, which has included electrical installations for decades (the 2024 WorldSkills competition was held in Lyon, France). In the United States, the ABC (Associated Builders and Contractors) National Craft Championships have run electrician competitions for decades. IDEAL National Championship, founded as a standalone event, joined the Elite Trades Championship Series in 2016 and became a televised spectacle on ESPN, raising the profile of the electrical trade and offering large prizes to winners. The competitions highlight vocational excellence and help address the skilled-labor shortage.
