## Basic Information

Electrician games are trade-skills competitions for electricians and electrical apprentices in which competitors install, wire, test and fault-find electrical systems against the clock, judged on safety, compliance with wiring standards and workmanship (WorldSkills International, n.d.). At the international level, electrical installations is one of the core skills at the WorldSkills Competition, the world championship of trades held every two years by WorldSkills International; nationally, contractors' associations and training bodies run their own electrician games — for example apprenticeship contests organized by electrical contractors' associations in the United States, and comparable vocational competitions (Berufswettbewerbe) in German-speaking countries (WorldSkills International, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the current scene is centered on national electrician games and the WorldSkills skill competitions, which select the world's best young electricians (WorldSkills International, n.d.). Men and women compete together in the same classes under identical rules (WorldSkills International, n.d.).

## Rules

Electrician games are conducted under the rules of the organizing body — the WorldSkills Competition rules internationally, national regulations such as the NEC in the United States or VDE standards in Germany locally (WorldSkills International, n.d.). A typical task gives competitors a fixed time — from a few hours to several days — to complete a practical installation: mounting and wiring distribution boards, conduit and cable runs, socket and lighting circuits, motor controls or building-automation components, followed by testing, measuring and fault-finding (WorldSkills International, n.d.). Judges score against a detailed marking scheme that weights safety (PPE, lockout/tagout, safe isolation), compliance with the applicable wiring code, functionality and workmanship; dangerous work stops the task immediately (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitions run in individual and team formats, with junior and senior age classes, and competitors must hold the qualifications required in their country (WorldSkills International, n.d.). There are no separate men's and women's classes — the competition is fully mixed (WorldSkills International, n.d.).


## History

Skilled trades have been tested in public competitions since the guilds of the Middle Ages, when journeymen demonstrated masterpieces to earn the right to work (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The modern world skills movement was founded in 1950 in Spain as the International Vocational Training Organisation (IVTO), later renamed WorldSkills International, and has included electrical installations among its skills since the early competitions (WorldSkills International, n.d.). National electrician games grew out of apprenticeship systems in the twentieth century: in the United States, electrical contractors' associations began organizing apprentice competitions that feed national championships, while European countries formalized their vocational contests alongside dual vocational training (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women have taken part in the electrical skills competitions since the trades opened to them, and women now compete at the highest level of the world championships (WorldSkills International, n.d.).
