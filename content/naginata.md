## Basic Information

Naginata is the modern competitive Japanese martial art of the naginata, a polearm with a curved blade similar to a European glaive or halberd. The sport form is called Atarashii Naginata ("new naginata"), codified after World War II by the All Japan Naginata Federation, and it uses kendo-style protective armor (bogu) with additional shin guards (sune-ate) and a practice weapon with an oak shaft and bamboo blade. Internationally the art is governed by the International Naginata Federation, which organizes World Championships every four years. It is closely related to [kendo](kendo.html) and [jodo](jodo.html).

## Rules

Competition has two forms: shiai (free sparring) and engi (forms performed by pairs, judged on execution). In shiai, two competitors in full bogu fight for a set time (three minutes at international events), and the first to score two valid points wins; otherwise judges decide on overall merit. Valid target areas are men (head), kote (wrists/forearms), do (trunk), sune (shins) and tsuki (throat thrust); a point requires a decisive strike with the correct part of the blade, correct posture, spirit and a kiai shout, confirmed by the three referees. In engi competition, pairs perform preset shikake-oji techniques or kata, and judges award the win to the better-executed team.

## History

The naginata was used by samurai and ashigaru infantry in Japan from the medieval period onward, with classical schools (koryu) such as Tendo-ryu and Jikishinkage-ryu preserving the art. In the early 20th century naginata became part of girls' school education in Japan, and after 1945 the classical styles were unified into the standardized Atarashii Naginata, codified in 1953 by the All Japan Naginata Federation (Zen Nihon Naginata Renmei, founded 1955). The International Naginata Federation subsequently organized the first World Naginata Championships in Osaka, and world championships have been held every four years since (Brussels 2007, Himeji 2011, Montreal 2015 and later editions). In Japan the art is most strongly associated with women's education, though men compete worldwide.
