## Basic Information

Rallycross is a circuit motorsport on mixed-surface tracks of about 1 km, mostly asphalt with a gravel section, where short, wheel-to-wheel races are decided in heats, semifinals and a final, with every driver required to take a mandatory "joker lap" through a longer alternate route once per race (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The top class since 2022 is RX1e: all-electric supercars with two motors producing a combined 680 hp (500 kW), 0–60 mph in about 1.8 seconds and a minimum weight of 1,300 kg (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIA World Rallycross Championship ran 12 seasons from 2014 to 2025 and was then replaced — for 2026 — by the FIA European Rallycross Championship plus a standalone FIA Rallycross World Cup in Jakarta in December 2026, with a full world championship relaunch planned for 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; CNMOTOS, 2026).

Sweden's Johan Kristoffersson is the most decorated driver in the sport's history: eight world titles (2017, 2018 and 2020–2025, six in a row, including a record 11 wins from 12 rounds in 2018), and in 2026 he swept Lousada to win the first European Rallycross title (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Women compete in the championship alongside men in the same classes (FIA, n.d.).

## Rules

A rallycross circuit is roughly 1 km long, at least 65% paved, with a mandatory joker lap — a detour through a gravel loop — that each driver must take exactly once per race; missing it means exclusion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The 2025 regulations reverted to a time-based heat system: four-lap heats (grids drawn randomly for heat 1, then by previous-heat position), five-lap semifinals and a six-lap final with six cars each on a staggered grid; championship points go to the top finishers of the heat ranking and to the top 15 of the weekend overall (Wikipedia, n.d.-d). SuperPole qualifying awards the pole position (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Cars: RX1e electric supercars (680 hp, 880 N·m, 0–60 mph in 1.8 s, min 1,300 kg) in the top class; support classes include RX2e (335 hp) and RX3; previously combustion Supercars with 2.0 L turbo engines of about 600 hp (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-d). All classes race together at European events with separate classifications; men's and women's drivers compete in the same heats (FIA, n.d.).

## History

The first rallycross race was held at Lydden Hill, England, on 4 February 1967, won by Vic Elford in a Porsche 911; the sport grew through the 1970s and the spectacular Group B era of the 1980s, and the FIA European Rallycross Championship has run since 1976 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The FIA World Rallycross Championship launched in 2014 above the European series. Petter Solberg won the first two titles (2014, 2015), Mattias Ekström took 2016, and Johan Kristoffersson won eight titles from 2017 to 2025, including six consecutively from 2020; Sébastien Loeb and the Hansen brothers were among his rivals (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). After twelve seasons the world championship was discontinued: 2026 runs as the FIA European Rallycross Championship plus a one-off FIA Rallycross World Cup in Jakarta (December 2026), with a new world series planned from 2028 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; CNMOTOS, 2026).
