## Basic Information

Deck tennis is a game played by mariners on the decks of ships: a hybrid of tennis and quoits, played with a rubber ring (or disk) that is thrown over a net, caught with one hand and immediately returned from the point of the catch (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It has been standardized in several countries as tennikoit, ring tennis or tenniquoits (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The court is roughly 40–50 ft (11–14 m) long and 15–20 ft (5–7 m) wide, with the midcourt net at least as high as a tennis net, and singles or doubles are both played (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring is usually the tennis system — love, 15, 30, 40, deuce, advantage, game — while the official tennikoit form plays sets to 21 points, badminton-style (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The World Tenniquoits Federation (WTF) publishes the rules of tenniquoits with official court measurements (World Tenniquoits Federation, n.d.).

American sources from the 1930s and 1940s attribute the origin, or at least the formal establishment, of deck tennis to Cleve F. Schaffer (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first and only Deck Tennis World Championship was celebrated in 1981 on the beach in Punta del Este, Uruguay, won by Mariano Herrera and Alejandro Nougues of Argentina (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Ring tennis has since grown world championships — the Ringtennis world championships were held in Germany in 2006 and 2010, and a Tenniquoits World Cup has been staged — with national federations such as the Tennikoit Federation of India (TKFI) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Tenniquoits Federation, n.d.). The game appeared in the 1936 film *Piccadilly Jim* and Roald Dahl describes playing it in *Boy: Tales of Childhood* (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in singles, doubles and mixed doubles (World Tenniquoits Federation, n.d.).

## Rules

In the standard form, the ring is served (thrown) into the opponent’s court; the receiver tries to catch it before it falls and immediately throws it back from the same spot (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A point is scored when the server lands the ring on the opponent’s side of the court; if the ring lands in the neutral area or outside the lines, the throwing side loses the point, and play continues until the ring hits the ground (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring follows tennis (15–30–40, deuce, advantage, game) or, in official tennikoit, sets to 21 points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Tenniquoits Federation, n.d.). The WTF rulebook fixes the court measurements and net height for international ring tennis (World Tenniquoits Federation, n.d.). Both men’s and women’s events are played (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Deck tennis was played on ocean liners in the 1930s and 1940s — men aboard HMS *Indomitable* during World War II are photographed with the game — and American sources credit Cleve F. Schaffer with formalizing it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 1936 film *Piccadilly Jim* shows it on a ship deck, and it also became a garden game (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Britannica, n.d.). The competitive form evolved into tennikoit/ring tennis: the first Deck Tennis World Championship was held in 1981 in Punta del Este, Uruguay, won by the Argentines Herrera and Nougues (Wikipedia, n.d.-a), and since then ring-tennis world championships have been held in Germany (2006, 2010) and tenniquoits world cups internationally, organized by the World Tenniquoits Federation and national bodies such as India’s TKFI (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Tenniquoits Federation, n.d.).
