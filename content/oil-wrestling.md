## Basic Information

Oil wrestling (yağlı güreş) is the national sport of Türkiye, a form of [wrestling](wrestling.html) in which two pehlivan (wrestlers) wearing kispet — thick leather trousers soaked in olive oil — grapple until one brings the other's back or belly to the ground (UNESCO, n.d.). The most famous event is the annual Kırkpınar tournament in Edirne, held since 1362 and recognized by Guinness as the oldest continuously sanctioned sporting event in the world, and the sport was inscribed on the UNESCO list of intangible cultural heritage in 2010 (UNESCO, n.d.). Matches are sanctioned by the Turkish Wrestling Federation (tgf.gov.tr). Oil wrestling is traditionally a men's discipline; women compete in the equivalent Olympic style of wrestling, where women's freestyle has been an Olympic event since Athens 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Before a bout the wrestlers are anointed with olive oil, which makes gripping difficult, and they wear the kispet, leather trousers weighing about 13 kg when dry (Kırkpınar Tournament, n.d.). A match is won when one wrestler pins the opponent so that the back or belly touches the ground, or by lifting the opponent and controlling him with a grip on the kispet — classically carrying him three steps or spinning him around, a winning hold known as paça kazık (World Ethnosport Organization, n.d.). Since 1975 bouts have time limits: up to 40 minutes in the başpehlivan (head-wrestler) category and 30 minutes in the pehlivan category, with score overtime of 15 and 10 minutes if no winner emerges; in overtime the first point or successful hold wins (Kırkpınar Tournament, n.d.). Tournaments run over several days with hundreds of wrestlers in ranked categories from boyhood (er Meydanı) up to başpehlivan; a wrestler who wins the Kırkpınar title three years in a row is awarded the Golden Belt (kemer) and becomes başpehlivan for life (Kırkpınar Tournament, n.d.). The rules are the same at all Turkish oil wrestling events; women's wrestling at the Olympics follows freestyle rules in weight classes instead (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Oil wrestling has been practiced in Anatolia and Thrace for centuries, with organized festivals documented from the Ottoman period; according to tradition, Ottoman soldiers held the first tournament in 1362 in the meadow of Kırkpınar ("Forty Springs") near Edirne to commemorate fallen comrades (World Ethnosport Organization, n.d.). The Kırkpınar tournament has been held annually since, traditionally in early summer, and is listed by Guinness World Records as the world's oldest continuously licensed sporting event (Kırkpınar Tournament, n.d.). The sport was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2010 (UNESCO, n.d.). Famous pehlivan include Kızılcıklı Mahmut, and today around a thousand wrestlers compete at Kırkpınar over three days; similar oil-wrestling festivals are also held in Bulgaria and Greece among Balkan Turkic communities (Wikipedia, n.d.).
