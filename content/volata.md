## Basic Information

Volata is the traditional game of the Aosta Valley in northwestern Italy: a team ball game played on a snowy or grassy field, in which players hit a small ball (the volata, launched from a spring or by hand) with the hand or a wooden implement up-field and toward the opponents' goal, a fast historical cousin of the region's folk football and Alpine ball games. Played in village form in the mountain communities of the valley, it survives as part of the region's heritage game culture.

## Rules

The specific village rules for this simple historical game vary by community : in the general form players divide into two teams on a field, the ball is put in play (served) and advanced by striking it with the hand or an agreed wooden stick, with the aim of sending it across the opponents' line or into a goal area while defending one's own, in continuous play without boundaries in the medieval folk-football style. Local rules govern team sizes, restart procedures (hand or implement hit) and winning conditions (crossings of lines or goals), and the game is played in festival matches rather than in standardized league sport, so the community's own conventions define the details of each match.

## History

Volata belongs to the family of traditional Alpine ball games of the Aosta Valley that share ancestry with the great medieval ball games of Europe; the valley's communities kept folk ball games alive alongside their more famous cousins (the gioco del pallone elastico of Piedmont and the street football traditions of the Alps), and volata has been carried forward as part of the region's heritage-festival culture. Today the game is featured in regional traditional-sport gatherings and demonstrations celebrating Aosta Valley identity, played in an atmosphere of village heritage rather than professional competition, and it remains one of the small local games of the Alps documented as part of Italy's traditional sport inheritance.
