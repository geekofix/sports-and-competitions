## Basic Information

Artistic cycling — German *Kunstradfahren* — is a gymnastics-on-bikes discipline governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI): athletes perform choreographed five-minute routines on a special fixed-gear bicycle in a hall, judged on difficulty, execution and artistic impression (Union Cycliste Internationale [UCI], n.d.-a). The categories are men's and women's singles, women's pairs, open pairs and four-rider teams (ACT 4); the bike has a roughly 1:1 gear ratio, a spinning handlebar and cranks of 130–170 mm, and the competition area measures at most 14 x 11 m with marked circles of 0.5, 4 and 8 m and a 2 m clear border (UCI, n.d.-a) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-a) (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.-b).

Among today's leading athletes, the women come first: at the 2026 UCI Indoor Cycling World Championships — moved from Derby to the MHP-Arena in Ludwigsburg, Germany, October 30 – November 1, 2026, in front of about 8,000 spectators — Germany swept all five artistic titles: Lara Füller won women's singles with a world-record 196 points (taking the record from compatriot Jana Pfann), Ramona Dandl took silver and Switzerland's Alessa Hotz bronze; Philipp-Thies Rapp defended the men's title, the women's pair Neele Jodeleit and Kim Leah Schlüter won for Germany, and the open pair Styber/Rödiger took their third consecutive title; Austria won the men's cycle-ball title and Germany the women's (UCI, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Riders perform a five-minute routine to music containing required elements — riding the marked circles, balance tricks, jumps and, in pairs, partner lifts — and the jury awards points for difficulty and execution with deductions for falls or touching the floor; the highest score wins. The fixed-gear artistic bicycle must meet UCI specifications, and the men's, women's, pairs and team categories are judged separately (UCI, n.d.-a).

## History

The first unofficial world championships were organized in 1888 by impresario Nicholas Edward Kaufmann, whose all-female troupe "Kaufmann's Cycling Beauties" toured Europe and America performing artistic cycling; official men's world championships began in 1956 and women's in 1970, and the discipline has been part of the UCI Indoor Cycling World Championships together with cycle-ball. Today the UCI also runs an Artistic Cycling World Cup — the 2026 edition was held in Dorlisheim, France, on November 20, 2026 (UCI, n.d.-b).
