## Basic Information

Gravel cycling (gravel racing) is the discipline of riding drop-bar bikes on unpaved gravel roads and trails, combining road racing and light off-road terrain. The UCI has sanctioned a Gravel World Cup and Gravel World Championships since 2022, adding gravel as an official UCI discipline; popular mass events include Unbound Gravel (formerly Dirty Kanza) in Kansas and the Belgian Waffle Ride.

## Rules

At the UCI Gravel World Championships (since 2022 in Veneto, Italy), races are mass-start over roughly 130-150 km on mixed gravel, dirt and tarmac roads, with feed zones and technical assistance only in designated areas. Riders must be UCI-licensed elite or national federation-licensed; bikes are gravel or road bikes subject to UCI technical guidelines, including a tire-width cap. Points for the UCI Gravel World Cup ranking decide the world champion. Safety rules: helmet mandatory, with standard UCI equipment and safety regulations applying.

## History

Gravel riding grew out of American Midwest "gravel grinder" events in the 2000s, most famously the Dirty Kanza 200 (founded 2006 in Emporia, Kansas, renamed Unbound Gravel), which grew to thousands of riders. The COVID-era boom in drop-bar off-pavement riding pushed bike makers to develop dedicated gravel bikes, and the UCI created the Gravel World Cup in 2022 and the first Gravel World Championship in 2022 in Italy, won by Gianni Vermeersch (Belgium) and Pauline Ferrand-Prévot (France). European events such as the Belgian Waffle Ride and the Swiss Gravel Race grew rapidly. The discipline now has a professional peloton with dedicated riders alongside amateurs.
