## Basic Information

The World Hen Racing Championships is an annual hen race held at a country pub in England, in which chickens race along a short track to decide the fastest hen in the world over a run of roughly 20 metres. A comic fixture of the village pub calendar since the 1980s, the event draws hen owners and spectators for an afternoon of avian sprinting, fund-raising and laughter.

Hens run in qualifying heats with a final deciding the champion, and the unpredictable nature of racing fowl — some sprint, some wander, some refuse to move — is the event's enduring charm.

## Rules

Hens are entered by their owners and race in a marked lane, in heats of several birds, released from the start line and encouraged toward the finish by their owners with calls and food. The first hen across the line wins the heat, and heat winners advance to a final that decides the world champion hen, with rules on fair treatment — no grabbing, throwing or chasing of birds — rosette prizes for the owners, and proceeds going to charity.

## History

The contest began in the 1980s at a village pub as a novelty to draw regulars and raise money for charity, and it grew into an annual institution with world championship status in the eyes of its organisers and fans. Local press and later national coverage of the sport's inevitable comic turns — hens stopping mid-race, pecking at the track or wandering off altogether — made it a beloved small-scale event, and the championships continue to be run each summer by the pub and its village community.
