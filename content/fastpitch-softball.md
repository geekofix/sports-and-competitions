## Basic Information

Fastpitch softball is the fast, competitive form of softball in which the pitcher throws overhand from a raised circle at speeds up to 70 mph (about 113 km/h), and games are typically seven innings. It is governed worldwide by the World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC), which runs the Softball World Cup (women's and men's), and in the United States by USA Softball, which selects the national teams and runs the USA Softball Hall of Fame.

## Rules

At WBSC World Cup and Olympic level (softball returned to the Olympics at Tokyo 2020/2021), two teams of nine play seven innings; the visiting team bats in the top half. The pitcher must deliver the ball underhand (fastpitch style: windmill motion) from the 43 ft (13.1 m, women) pitching circle, and the ball must pass over home plate between the batter's knees and shoulders (the strike zone). Batters get three strikes or four balls; stealing is allowed but with a "leaving early" rule — the runner must keep contact with the base until the ball is released. Fielding positions follow baseball/softball conventions on a diamond with 60 ft (18.3 m) base paths (women). Games are timed if tied after seven innings, with international tiebreaker rules placing a runner on second base. Olympic/WBSC games use the international tiebreaker from the eighth inning.

## History

Softball was invented in 1887 in Chicago by George Hancock as an indoor version of baseball; fastpitch evolved in the 1930s-40s in the United States, particularly in Texas and Oklahoma, and became organized with the Amateur Softball Association (ASA, founded 1933). The International Softball Federation (ISF, founded 1952) ran world championships until merging with the IBAF in 2013 to form the WBSC. The United States dominated Olympic softball (gold in 1996, 2000, 2004, 2008) before the sport was dropped after 2008 and reinstated for Tokyo 2020, where Japan won gold. Legendary pitchers include Jennie Finch, Monica Abbott (who holds the recorded fastest pitch at 69 mph) and Cat Osterman.
