## Basic Information

Ayoayo (ayo, ayo) is the Yoruba mancala of southwestern Nigeria and Benin, played on a carved wooden board of two rows of six pits with 48 cowrie shells — one of the most widespread mancala variants of West Africa and the closest relative of oware. It is a staple of Nigerian markets and festivals, played by men, women and children, with national championships organized in Nigeria; the game also travelled with the Yoruba diaspora. Within the mancala and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Oware](oware.html), [Dara](dara.html), [Mancala](mancala.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

The rules follow the oware family: players sow counterclockwise from one of their pits, and if the last shell lands in an opponent's pit making it two or three shells, those are captured, with chain captures continuing backward. Regional ayo rule sets differ on details such as whether a grand slam that would starve the opponent is forbidden or must be followed by a feeding move. The game ends when one side is empty; the player with more than half the shells (more than 24) wins.

## History

Ayo is played by the Yoruba of Nigeria, Benin and Togo — the name simply means the seeds used as counters. Like other West African sowing games it spread through trade and the slave trade to the Americas. Stewart Culin documented mancala games of Africa and the diaspora in his early-20th-century ethnographic work; modern tournament play in Nigeria is organized under the Oware Society's abapa rules, which ayo follows closely.
