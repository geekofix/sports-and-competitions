## Basic Information

The high jump is the vertical jump of athletics: an unaided athlete takes off from one foot after a curved approach and clears a horizontal bar set between two uprights, landing on a crash mat. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic programme since 1896 for men and since 1928 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world records are 2.45 m for men, set by Javier Sotomayor of Cuba in Salamanca on 27 July 1993 — the longest-standing men's world record in athletics — and 2.10 m for women, set by Yaroslava Mahuchikh of Ukraine at the 2024 Meeting de Paris on 7 July 2024, breaking Stefka Kostadinova's 2.09 m of 1987 that had stood for 37 years (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was headlined by world record holder and 2024 Olympic champion Yaroslava Mahuchikh (pictured), who competed at the 2026 Weltklasse Zürich; the men's event was led by JuVaughn Harrison of the USA, whose 2.34 m in Monaco on 5 August 2026 was the 2026 world lead, and by Gianmarco Tamberi of Italy, who won the inaugural 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship in Budapest with a world-leading 2.37 m, ahead of Harrison and Erick Portillo of Mexico. The reigning Olympic and world champion among the men is Hamish Kerr of New Zealand (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

## Rules

The bar is set at a starting height chosen by the officials and raised after each round by at least 2 cm outdoors; each competitor has one attempt at each height and is eliminated after three consecutive fouls, and may pass an attempt to save it for a later height (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Ties are decided by count-back: the jumper with the fewest misses at the height at which the tie occurred wins, and if still tied, the one with the fewest total misses (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The take-off must be from one foot, the approach runway is at least 15 m long and the landing area a mat of at least 5 m by 3 m; the result is measured vertically to the nearest centimetre from the bar's upper edge (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete separately under identical rules; junior competitions use the same heights, and para athletics offers the high jump in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

Jumping for height is ancient, but the modern high jump emerged in 19th-century Scotland and Ireland with the 'Scissors' technique; the Fosbury Flop — jumping backwards with an arched back — revolutionized the event when Dick Fosbury, pictured, won the 1968 Mexico City Olympic gold with 2.24 m (Wikipedia, n.d.). Valeriy Brumel of the USSR raised the world record to 2.28 m in 1963, and Bohdan Bondarenko of Ukraine set the championship record of 2.41 m at Moscow 2013; Mutaz Essa Barshim of Qatar shared the Tokyo 2020 Olympic gold with Gianmarco Tamberi in one of the sport's most famous moments (Olympics, n.d.). Stefka Kostadinova's 2.09 m at the 1987 World Championships in Rome stood for 37 years until Mahuchikh's 2.10 in Paris 2024, while Javier Sotomayor, pictured, set the men's 2.45 m in Salamanca 1993, unbroken in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).
