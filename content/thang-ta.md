## Basic Information

Thang ta is the traditional Meitei martial art of Manipur in northeast India: a weapons system whose very name pairs its two implements, thang (sword) and ta (spear), practiced with spear dances, sword drills, stick-and-shield sparring and unarmed techniques. Part of the broader Huyen Langlon tradition and a recognized sport in India, it is both a living cultural practice of the Meitei people and a competitive martial art with national championships.

## Rules

Sport thang ta is contested in standardized events: the spear dance and sword-fighting demonstration segments (judged kata-like on precision and form), and the armed sparring of khutlai paiba, fought with the phunachei stick and chungoi shield under point scoring for controlled strikes and technique. Competition rules set weight categories and age groups, define legal target areas, penalize excessive contact, and referee bouts on a marked arena in the manner of combative martial sports; the tradition's unarmed branch (sarit sarak) and Manipuri wrestling (mukna) sit alongside the weapons forms in the wider system. Federation rules govern equipment (blunt training weapons, protective gear), tournament format and certification for coaches and judges.

## History

Meitei martial tradition traces its golden age to the reign of King Khagemba (1597 to 1652) in the old kingdom of Kangleipak, and the martial culture of Manipur carries its own war-ethics manuscripts (the Chainarol) describing duel conventions of the era. After the Anglo-Manipuri War of 1891 the British banned weapons and the martial arts of Manipur, driving thang ta underground where a few masters preserved it; independence and Manipur's statehood (1972) allowed a revival, with Thang-Ta federations organizing the art as a national sport of India. Today thang ta features in the national school games and Khelo India festivals, in international cultural showcases, and in the daily practice of Manipuri communities at home and abroad as a symbol of Meitei heritage.
