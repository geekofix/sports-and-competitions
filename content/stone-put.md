## Basic Information

The stone put is the traditional Swiss throwing event in which athletes put an extremely heavy natural stone "+E+" the most famous weighing 83.5 kilograms "+E+" for distance. The signature test of the Swiss national festival Unspunnen and a cousin of the Highland games stone throw, it is one of the classic festival strength sports of the Alpine world.

## Rules

Each athlete puts the stone from behind a throwing line with a style derived from the shot put: the stone is balanced in one hand at the neck or shoulder, and the competitor uses body rotation and leg drive before releasing at full extension, adapting technique to the stone's size and shape. A panel of festival judges adjudicates foul lines and measurement, and the winner is the thrower with the greatest valid mark from the agreed number of attempts, typically three. Variations in stone size matter: smaller stones are thrown in lighter classes at regional festivals and youth events, while Unspunnen's iconic 83.5-kilogram stone is reserved for the festival's champion putters.

## History

Stone throwing contests predate recorded Swiss history, with early references to putting stones appearing in the chronicles of the old Swiss Confederation alongside Schwingen (Swiss wrestling) and Hornussen as classic national sports. The Unspunnen festival "+E+" first held near Interlaken in 1805 near the ruins of Unspunnen castle "+E+" made celebrated stone contests its centrepiece and gave the sport its iconic stone (the Unspunnenstein); the festival is now held roughly every three years and continues to crown stone champions in front of international audiences. Stone putting also stands alongside the Highland games' stone throw and other European stone traditions, and it regained a modern following alongside the wider revival of traditional Alpine festival sports in the twentieth century.
