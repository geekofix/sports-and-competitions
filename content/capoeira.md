## Basic Information

Capoeira is an Afro-Brazilian martial art, game and cultural practice that combines dance, acrobatics, music and fighting. Two capoeiristas play inside a circle of participants (the roda) while others play instruments (berimbau, atabaque, pandeiro) and sing. The two main traditions are Capoeira Regional, formalized by Mestre Bimba, and the older Capoeira Angola, preserved by Mestre Pastinha. The capoeira circle was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2014.

## Rules

In the roda there is no formal winner; the game (jogo) between two players is judged by the mestres and the community. Players move in the ginga (a constant swaying step) and use kicks, sweeps, takedowns, hand strikes and acrobatic floreios; the berimbau's rhythm sets the pace and character of the game. Traditional rules of respect govern the roda: players greet each other, the mestre controls the circle, and knowledge is passed by observation and imitation. In competitive capoeira (campeonatos), athletes are scored on technique, creativity and control in timed bouts.

## History

Capoeira developed among enslaved Africans and their descendants in Brazil from the 16th century, with roots in the Angolan martial art engolo; it was first mentioned in an official document in 1789 as a criminal activity. In the 1930s Mestre Bimba (Manoel dos Reis Machado) opened the first official academy in Salvador and created Capoeira Regional with codified sequences, and in 1941 Mestre Pastinha founded his Angola school. Brazil legalized capoeira in 1937 and registered the roda as national cultural heritage in 2008; UNESCO inscribed the capoeira circle in 2014. Today it is practiced in more than 160 countries.
