## Basic Information

The one-foot high kick is a traditional Inuit jumping event: the athlete stands on one foot, jumps, kicks a suspended target (a ring of seal skin or a ball) with the other foot, and must land on the same take-off foot to score (AWG, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is one of the 11 Arctic Sports at the Arctic Winter Games and a WEIO event, with men's and women's records tracked; the short film by Alethea Arnaquq-Baril showed champion Johnny Issaluk at Vancouver 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Within the Arctic and Indigenous sports on this site, its closest relatives are [Blanket Toss](blanket-toss.html), [Snow Snake](snow-snake.html), [Two-Foot High Kick](two-foot-high-kick.html), [Finger Pull](finger-pull.html), [Hand Games](hand-games.html), [World Eskimo Indian Olympics](world-eskimo-indian-olympics.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

A gallows frame suspends the target at adjustable height; the athlete takes off on one foot, strikes the target with the other foot, and lands on the take-off foot with balance. Three attempts per height; the highest successful kick wins (AWG, n.d.).

## History

The high kick signalled a successful whale hunt to the village and tested hunter agility; it has been an Arctic Winter Games event since 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
