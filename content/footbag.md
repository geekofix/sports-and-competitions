## Basic Information

Footbag (hacky sack) is a sport and game in which players keep a small bean-filled bag (footbag) airborne using feet, knees, chest, shoulders and head — never hands or arms — in singles or groups, or perform freestyle tricks in competition. It is governed by the International Footbag Players' Association (IFPA), which runs the World Footbag Championships (freestyle singles, pairs and "shred30" timed events) and recognizes world records.

## Rules

In IFPA freestyle competition, players perform routines (typically 2-3 minutes, sometimes to music) consisting of tricks — stalls (catching the bag on body parts), passes, and acrobatic moves — judged on difficulty, execution, variety and artistry; hands and arms may not touch the bag (a "handball" ends the run or loses points). Events include men's and women's singles, pairs, and the timed "Shred30" (most tricks in 30 seconds). In the casual "circle" game, players stand in a ring and must keep the bag aloft, each touching it once before another player; dropping it scores a letter (spelling out words like "out"). Net footbag (a competitive court game) uses a 5 ft net with tennis-like rules, but the IFPA focuses on freestyle and circle play.

## History

The modern footbag was invented in 1972 by John Stalberger in Portland, Oregon, who sewed a bean-filled bag and kicked it to pass the time; after he patented the design, the International Footbag Players' Association was founded in the early 1980s to organize the sport. The game grew from the 1970s-80s festival scene into organized freestyle competition, with World Championships held since the 1980s. The World Footbag Championships crown world champions annually in men's, women's and pairs freestyle events as well as the timed Shred30.
