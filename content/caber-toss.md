## Basic Information

The caber toss is the iconic event of the Scottish Highland games: an athlete lifts a large wooden pole — the caber — vertically, runs a few steps, and throws it so that it turns end over end and lands with the end that was held in the hands ("the small end") pointing straight away from the thrower, the ideal "12 o'clock" toss. There is no single world federation, but the sport is organized internationally by the International Highland Games Federation (IHGF, founded 1980) and in Scotland by the Royal Scottish Highland Games Association (RSHGA), which sanction the Scottish Heavy Athletics events (Royal Scottish Highland Games Association [RSHGA], n.d.). In 2026, the 160th Scottish Highland Gathering & Games in Pleasanton, California — hosted by the Caledonian Club of San Francisco over Labor Day weekend, September 5–6, 2026 — doubled as the 2026 Men's Scottish Heavy Athletics World Championships and the 50th U.S. Invitational Heavy Athletics Championships, with Meagan McKee of San Jose winning the 2026 Women's Pro Class national championship for the sixth time (RSHGA, n.d.). At the 2026 Grandfather Mountain Highland Games in Linville, North Carolina, July 9–12, 2026, Kyle Lillie won the overall heavy athletics title, including the caber toss and the 56-lb weight toss (Grandfather Mountain, n.d.).

On the women's side, women have competed in the heavy athletics at North American games for decades, and Meagan McKee's sixth U.S. women's professional national title in 2026 makes her the standard-bearer of the women's caber toss today (RSHGA, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the SHGA/RSHGA rules, the caber is a tapered pole of larch or Scots pine, typically 5–6 m long and 40–70 kg — the traditional Braemar caber weighs about 60 kg and is 12 ft 6 in long. The thrower stands behind the trig line (a marked line on the ground), balances the caber vertically on one hand with the bottom end on the ground, takes a short run-up of a few steps, and releases it so it flips over; each athlete gets up to three attempts, and the throw is measured by the angle of the caber's landing position relative to the throwing direction, scored in degrees like a clock face — a perfect toss lands at 12 o'clock (180° from the thrower), and scoring runs up to 90° on either side (RSHGA, n.d.). Because the caber's landing angle is judged rather than measured in distance, there are no official world records for the caber toss; unofficial benchmarks include Douglas Edmunds' 13.24 m with a 40 kg caber in 1985 (distance), Phil Martin's 6.20 m height toss in 1995, and the repetition records of 26 throws in three minutes by Andrew Murphie (2024) and 161 throws in an hour by Jason Baines (2021) (RSHGA, n.d.). Fouls include stepping over the trig line before release, dropping the caber, or a caber that does not turn end over end; the athlete must remain behind the line until the caber lands. The heavy athletics programme around the caber toss includes the stone put (8–16 lb stones), the Scottish hammer (16–28 lb), the weight for distance (28–56 lb), the weight over the bar and the sheaf toss (a 20-lb bale of straw thrown for height over a bar) (RSHGA, n.d.). Men's and women's events use the same rules, with lighter cabers and stones for women and junior (under-18) classes at most games (RSHGA, n.d.).

## History

The caber toss has its roots in the Highland games of Scotland, where soldiers and lumbermen are said to have tossed tree trunks to test strength; the first recorded Highland games with heavy athletics date to the early 19th century, and the Braemar Gathering (founded 1832, patronized by Queen Victoria from 1848) made the caber toss famous. The RSHGA coordinates the Scottish games circuit, and the IHGF (founded 1980) links games in Scotland, the United States, Canada, Australia and elsewhere (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.) (RSHGA, n.d.). The American games boomed in the 20th century — the Grandfather Mountain Highland Games began in 1952 and Pleasanton's Scottish Gathering celebrated its 160th edition in 2026 — and Douglas Edmunds (1944–2020), the Canadian "Godfather of Strongman", won the world caber title in 1976 and 1978, set the Braemar 60-kg 12 o'clock record in 1976, and co-founded the World's Strongest Man competition in 1977 with David Webster (RSHGA, n.d.) (Grandfather Mountain, n.d.).
