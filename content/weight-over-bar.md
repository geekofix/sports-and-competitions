## Basic Information

Weight over bar (also called weight for height throw) is a Highland Games heavy event in which athletes throw a heavy weight — in classic Scottish form a 56-pound (25.4 kg) iron weight with a ring handle — over a horizontal bar using any preferred technique, with the bar raised after each successful round until only one thrower remains. Unlike the weight throw for distance, this is purely a height contest, pitting explosive power against a rising target.

It is a staple of Scottish Highland games gatherings worldwide and is contested at heavyweight and masters classes with implement weights adjusted accordingly, including lighter weights for women and age-group athletes.

## Rules

Each athlete gets three attempts at a given height to toss the weight over the bar, and the bar rises by set increments (often 6 inches or 15 centimetres) after each round. The throw must be delivered from a standing position behind the trig or throwing line; any style is permitted — most throwers use a spinning swing between the legs to build momentum — and the attempt counts only if the weight passes cleanly over the bar and lands on the far side without dislodging the bar. An athlete who fails all three attempts at a height is eliminated, and the last thrower standing wins, with fewest misses used to break ties.

## History

The weight over bar comes from the traditional heavyweight events of the Scottish Highland games, which were formalised in the nineteenth century at gatherings such as the Caledonian clubs' annual games alongside caber toss, stone put and hammer throw. The 56-pound weight thrown for height traces to standard farm and merchant weights of the era. The event crossed the Atlantic with Scottish emigrants and is now standard at Highland games in North America, Australia and New Zealand, governed by bodies such as the Scottish Highland Games Heavy Events Associations in various countries, with world-class athletes regularly clearing heights of around five to six metres.
