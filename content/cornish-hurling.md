## Basic Information

Cornish hurling (Cornish: Hurlian), also called hurling the silver ball, is an ancient outdoor team game played only in Cornwall, England, with a small heavy ball of sterling silver around an applewood core. It is considered by many Cornwall's national game alongside Cornish wrestling; the Cornish saying "hyrl^an yw gen gwar^ nyi" means "hurling is our sport". Today it survives in two communities: St Columb Major (Shrove Tuesday and the second Saturday after, Townsmen vs Countrymen) and St Ives (Feast Monday, played by children), plus the beating of the bounds at Bodmin.

## Rules

There is no formal governing body or written rulebook; local custom governs. Two sides (in St Columb, the Townsmen and the Countrymen of the parish) try to gain possession of the silver ball and carry or throw it to their goal; the winner of the ball may keep it but must have a new ball made at a cost of around 1,000 pounds. The traditional ball weighs 19-21 ounces (about 570 g) and is about 23 cm in circumference, inscribed "Town and Country, do your best". Matches open with the cry "Town and Country do your best, for in this parish I must rest". The game is similar to the Welsh cnapan and other old British mob football games.

## History

Cornish hurling is of Celtic origin and is documented for at least a thousand years; theories link it to Irish hurling brought back after the Norman invasion or to a pagan fertility rite. Up to the 19th century it was played across Cornwall at feasts and fairs. It declined sharply in the 20th century but survives unchanged at St Columb Major and St Ives, where it holds the same place in the Cornish calendar as the 'Obby 'Oss at Padstow or the Furry Dance at Helston. Historic balls are displayed in Cornish museums.
