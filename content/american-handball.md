## Basic Information

American handball (also called wallball) is a wall sport in which players hit a small hard rubber ball against one or more walls of a court; it is governed in the United States by the United States Handball Association (USHA), which has organised the U.S. national championships annually since 1919 — first under the AAU until 1950 — and maintains the Handball Hall of Fame in Tucson, Arizona (United States Handball Association, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The pinnacle event is now the World Wallball Championships: after the first standalone edition in Limerick, Ireland, in 2024 (more than 300 competitors), the 2026 edition brought 391 players in 60 divisions to New York City from 14 to 19 September 2026, hosted by the Inner City Handball Association with the World Handball Council and USHA, with eight Open titles decided at Central Park North Meadow (United States Handball Association, n.d.-a).

The women's game is fully established at the top level: Mikaila Esser is the reigning USHA Women's Open champion (2026 nationals), and the USHA runs a Women's Classic alongside the men's events; women compete in their own Open, A, B and age-group divisions at the nationals and world championships (United States Handball Association, n.d.-b; United States Handball Association, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The classic four-wall court measures 40 by 20 feet (12.2 × 6.1 m) with a 20-foot front wall, side walls and a back wall, and the ball is a small (~1⅜-inch) hard rubber handball; three-wall outdoor courts (as photographed) and one-wall courts are also used, with the one-wall game the urban street form concentrated in New York City (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; United States Handball Association, n.d.-c).

Play is singles, doubles or cutthroat, and scoring is serve-only: a point is won only when the server wins the rally, with games commonly played to 21 points (a match best two of three games, the third to 11) or to 11, 15 or 7 depending on the event; the ball must be hit on the fly or after one bounce against the front wall (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Competition categories cover men's and women's Open and A/B divisions plus age groups from junior to 65+, at both the USHA Nationals (75th edition: Tucson, 3–7 June 2026) and the World Wallball Championships; the Gaelic cousin, 40×20 handball under the GAA, holds its own world championships every three years (2012 Dublin Citywest with 30+ countries and 2,000+ players; 2015 Calgary) (United States Handball Association, n.d.-b; United States Handball Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

American handball grew out of the Irish-American communities of New York and Chicago in the late 19th century; the first U.S. national championships were held in 1919 under the Amateur Athletic Union, and the USHA took over in 1950, with four-wall world championships staged since 1968 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; United States Handball Association, n.d.-c).

One-wall handball became the sport of New York City's public parks, organised by the Inner City Handball Association, and the standalone World Wallball Championships began in Limerick in 2024 before the 2026 edition in New York — “One Wall. One World” — cementing the game's international calendar (United States Handball Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
