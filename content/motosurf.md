## Basic Information

MotoSurf (jet surfing) is a water sport in which the rider stands on a board with a built-in jet engine — no tow rope, no waves needed — carving at up to 60 km/h on flat water or waves (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the board-sports family with jet skiing, surfing and water skiing.

The MotoSurf World Cup (run by the International MotoSurf Federation, IMSF) crowns world champions in slalom and racing formats on buoy courses (IMSF, n.d.). Boards are carbon-fibre with 100–300 cc jet units; riders wear impact vests, helmets and leashes (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Competition formats: slalom (timed through a buoy course), racing (head-to-head laps) and freestyle (judged tricks) (IMSF, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate categories at the world cup.

## History

MotoSurf was developed in the early 2010s by the Czech company JMF (Jet Surf Factory) and exploded in popularity in the mid-2010s, with boards shown at boat shows like Interboot Friedrichshafen (photo above) and world cup rounds in Europe, Asia and the Americas (Wikipedia, n.d.).
