## Basic Information

Sporting clays is the most popular clay-target discipline: shooters move through 15 stations (often called stands) on a course through woods or open ground and take shots at clay pigeons presented from traps in varied trajectories — incoming, outgoing, crossing, rabbits and battues (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the shotgun clay-target family together with trap, skeet, compak, five-stand and helice.

In the United States the National Sporting Clays Association (NSCA), the sister organization of the NSSA, governs the sport and runs the World Skeet/ Sporting championships at the National Shooting Complex in San Antonio, Texas (NSSA-NSCA, n.d.). Internationally the ISSF runs the Olympic trap/skeet program, while FITASC organizes world-level "parcours de chasse" (sporting) competitions (FITASC, n.d.). A standard sporting clays round is 100 targets across 15 stations with pairs and report pairs (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Rules: at each station the shooter calls for the target(s), which are launched on report or following a slight delay; a broken target scores 1, a dead (unbroken) target 0; the course may include rabbits (targets rolled along the ground), battues (fast crossing targets) and towers (high overheads) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Gauge and shot-size limits (typically 12/20/28/.410, max 24–28 g shot depending on the federation) apply at major championships (NSCA, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same events, with Lady and senior/junior classes at championships; sub-gauge events exist for .410 and 28.

## History

Sporting clays evolved from the old "live pigeon" and driven-bird shoots of the 19th century; after live pigeons were outlawed, English shooters developed the "clay pigeon" in the 1880s, and the modern walked-up course with multiple stands appeared in Britain around 1910–1930 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The NSCA was founded in 1973 and the first US national sporting clays championship was held in 1974; the ISSF added "sporting" (compak-style universal trap) to its program in the 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.). Today the NSCA’s registered shoots number in the thousands per year, and world championships alternate between the US and Europe.
