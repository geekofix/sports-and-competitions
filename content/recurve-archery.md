## Basic Information

Recurve archery is the standard Olympic discipline of [archery](archery.html), shot with a recurve bow — limbs that curve away from the archer at the tips — plus a sight, stabilizers and a clicker, with no mechanical release aids. It is governed internationally by World Archery (founded 1931 as FITA, renamed World Archery in 2011), and women have competed in Olympic archery since the Paris 1900 Games, with individual, team and mixed team events for both genders at every major championship (World Archery, n.d.). At the elite level today, the leading women include Lim Si-hyeon of South Korea (born 13 June 2003), the 2025 world champion and triple Olympic gold medalist of Paris 2024 — individual, team and mixed team — who set the world record of 694 in the 720-arrow ranking round, and the leading men include Kim Woo-jin of South Korea (born 20 June 1992), also a triple Olympic champion of Paris 2024 and world No. 1 since 2011; the 2026 Hyundai Archery World Cup (20th edition, 7 April–13 September 2026, five stages) saw India's Dhiraj Bommadevara win the Antalya stage and Kim Woo-jin top the Madrid stage 4 qualification with 695 (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Archery, n.d.).

## Rules

Olympic-format recurve competition at 70 meters uses a 122 cm five-color target with ten scoring zones (inner-10 counts as X): a ranking round of 72 arrows seeds head-to-head match play, in which each archer shoots three arrows per set alternately, the set winner takes 2 points (1 each for a draw), the first to 6 set points wins the match, and a tie goes to a single-arrow shoot-off decided by the arrow closest to the center (World Archery, n.d.). Individual, team (three archers) and mixed team events are contested at world championships and World Cup stages, all on the same faces at 70 m outdoors, while indoor (18 m, 40 cm face) and field rounds run under separate rulebooks (World Archery, n.d.). Equipment rules are strict: sights must be unpowered and fixed to the bow, the string is released with the fingers using a glove or tab, and mechanical release aids are banned; the compound division, shot with cams and magnifying sights, is not on the Olympic programme but is a World Games discipline (World Archery, n.d.). Results feed the official World Archery Rankings, a points system published after major tournaments for recurve men's and women's individual, team and mixed team (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The recurve design descends from Asian horse archery, refined especially in Korea, and the 20th century brought take-down bows of laminated wood, fiberglass and carbon with aluminum-carbon arrows, making the competitive bow a precision instrument (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FITA was founded in 1931, defined the Olympic format, and recurve archery appeared at the Games of 1900, 1904, 1908 and 1920 before a 52-year hiatus and a continuous return since Munich 1972, with team events added in Seoul 1988 (Wikipedia, n.d.). South Korea's women have set the standard: Kim Soo-nyung won four Olympic medals including the 1992 individual gold, Park Sung-hyun took gold in Athens 2004, and An San became the first archer to win three golds at one Games in Tokyo 2020; on the men's side, the American Brady Ellison collected three Olympic team medals and the 2016 individual bronze (Wikipedia, n.d.). World Championships have run since 1931 and the Archery World Cup circuit since 2006 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
