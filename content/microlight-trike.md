## Basic Information

A microlight trike (ultralight trike) is a weight-shift aircraft: a lightweight carriage with three wheels, seat and engine hung under a hang-glider-style wing, steered by a control bar (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is the most affordable powered aviation category, governed internationally by the FAI under CIMA, and belongs to the air-sports family with paramotors and gyrocopters.

*Figure: Oliver Ren. (2012-01-25). Ultralight trike ready at the airfield [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Ultralight_Trike_01.JPG*

FAI microlight classes cover single-seat and two-seat landplanes, amphibians, gyroplanes and paramotors, split by engine type (conventional, electric) and control (weight-shift, aerodynamic) (FAI, n.d.). Competition includes precision tasks, navigation rallies, STOL (short take-off and landing) contests and slalom, with the FAI Microlight World Championships as the pinnacle (FAI, n.d.).

## Rules

Microlights are limited by regulation — typically a maximum take-off weight of 450 kg (472.5 kg with ballistic parachute in some states) for two-seaters, and a stall speed limit — and require a microlight pilot licence, medical and training (FAI, n.d.). National rules vary: in the US they are "ultralight vehicles" under FAA Part 103 flown without a licence, while in the EU they need a national permit (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety equipment includes a ballistic parachute in many modern designs. Men and women compete on equal terms.

## History

The microlight trike was developed in the late 1970s from the hang glider: the French La Mouette company and others added small engines and wheeled carriages to Rogallo wings, and the type exploded in popularity in the 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FAI created its microlight commission in the 1980s and has ratified microlight records ever since (FAI, n.d.). Today tens of thousands of microlights fly worldwide, from recreational "flying motorbikes" to competition machines.
