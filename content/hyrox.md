## Basic Information

HYROX — "the world series of fitness racing" — is a standardized indoor fitness race in which athletes complete eight 1 km runs alternating with eight functional workout stations in a fixed order: SkiErg 1,000 m, sled push 50 m, sled pull 50 m, burpee broad jumps 80 m, rowing 1,000 m, farmer's carry 200 m, sandbag lunges 100 m and 100 wall balls (HYROX, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The format was founded in Hamburg in 2017 by the German Olympic field hockey champion Moritz Förste and the event manager Christian Toetzke, and the first race was held in April 2018 in Hamburg with 650 participants (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Races are held in indoor exhibition halls and arenas and are identical worldwide, so times are comparable; divisions include Singles, Doubles (men's, women's, mixed), Relay (four athletes), Pro (heavier weights), age groups from 16–17 up to 80+, and the invitational Elite 15; by 2023 HYROX ran 65 races with over 175,000 competitors worldwide, and by the end of 2024 more than 5,000 gyms were affiliated (HYROX, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 2026 HYROX World Championships, 18–21 June 2026 at Strawberry Arena in Stockholm, the women's Elite 15 title went to Alyssa McElheny of the USA in her World Championships debut and the men's title to Dylan Scott (USA, 53:47); other leading current athletes are three-time women's world champion Lauren Weeks (2020, 2021, 2023) and the long-time dominant Linda Meier, the defending champion going into Stockholm (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Every HYROX race follows the same fixed sequence: 1 km run, then a station, repeated eight times, so the total race distance is 8 km of running plus the station work (HYROX, n.d.-a). Station weights, reps and distances are standardized in the rulebook (2026/27 edition) and vary by division — for example wall-ball and sandbag weights differ between standard, Pro and age-group divisions — while the SkiErg and RowErg stations are performed on Concept2 machines with the damper setting free; all results are timed with chips and verified (HYROX, n.d.-b). Divisions: Singles (one athlete), Doubles (two athletes of any combination — men's, women's or mixed), Relay (four athletes, each doing two run-station pairs), Pro (heavier implements), Elite 15 (the invited top professionals, ranked by a points system introduced from 1 July 2026), and age groups in ten-year bands from 16–17 to 80+ (HYROX, n.d.-b). Men and women race the identical format and work order, with division-specific weights; penalties (e.g., for uncompleted reps) add time or lead to disqualification under the rulebook (HYROX, n.d.-b).

## History

HYROX was created in 2017 in Hamburg, Germany, by Moritz Förste — Olympic gold medallist with the German field hockey team in 2008 and 2012 — and Christian Toetzke, who wanted a standardized, comparable fitness race for everyone; the first event in April 2018 attracted 650 athletes (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The series expanded rapidly — 65 races and more than 175,000 finishers in 2023, including over 24,000 at the London event, and more than 5,000 affiliated gyms by the end of 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; HYROX, n.d.-b). The World Championships moved from Oberhausen (2018) via Las Vegas (2022), Manchester (2023), Nice (2024) and Chicago (2025) to Stockholm in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Founders Förste and Toetzke remain the sport's best-known figures (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
