## Basic Information

Corgi racing is a novelty event in which Welsh corgis sprint down a short grass course while spectators bet on the winner. The best-known event is the Corgi Derby at Musselburgh Racecourse near Edinburgh, Scotland, first held in 2022 to mark Queen Elizabeth II's Platinum Jubilee and now run every Easter Saturday over about 70 m, with around 15 dogs per race selected by ballot. The starter at early editions was Judy Murray, mother of tennis players Andy and Jamie Murray (Associated Press, 2025).

Women take part as owners, handlers and spectators on the same terms as men.

## Rules

In each race, corgis line up in starting stalls at the top of the 70 m straight and are released by the starter; the first dog across the finish line wins. Owners may encourage their dogs from the railside but may not enter the course. Racing is for entertainment and charity fundraising; there are several races per event with heats and a derby final. Betting on the outcome is a traditional part of the spectacle, and the events draw large crowds to the racecourse.

## History

The Musselburgh Corgi Derby began in 2022 as a one-off for the Queen's Platinum Jubilee and became an annual Easter fixture after its popularity. Winners include Georgie Rumbles (2022), Rodney (2023), Juno (2025) and Islay (2026). Corgi races have since spread to other UK racecourses and agricultural shows. The event celebrates the corgi, the dog breed famously owned by Queen Elizabeth II.
