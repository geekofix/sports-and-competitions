## Basic Information

Systema is a Russian martial art and movement system built on four principles — breathing, relaxation, posture and movement — developed for Soviet special forces and taught worldwide today. The modern system was codified by Colonel Mikhail Ryabko, a Soviet special-operations instructor who died in April 2023, and spread internationally through his student Vladimir Vasiliev, who emigrated to Canada in 1993 and founded Systema Headquarters in Toronto the same year (Russian Martial Art, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Systema has no kata, no belts and no competitive sparring: training consists of partner drills, breathing exercises and natural body mechanics applicable to strikes, grabs, weapons and falls (Wikipedia, n.d.). More than 600 certified instructors teach in over 250 schools across more than 40 countries, and women make up a large share of practitioners, training under the same curriculum as men (Russian Martial Art, n.d.). A related lineage is the Kadochnikov system (Russian Martial Art, n.d.).

## Rules

Systema is not a scored combat sport: there are no weight classes, no belts and no tournament bouts, so "rules" are training and safety rules instead (Wikipedia, n.d.). Classes drill controlled partner work — strikes, grabs, chokes and weapon defenses — at increasing resistance, always governed by the four principles: continuous breathing under stress, releasing tension, maintaining natural posture and moving without breaks in structure (Russian Martial Art, n.d.). Progression is informal: students advance through instructor certification rather than colored belts, and the system is used by military, police and security professionals as well as civilians (Russian Martial Art, n.d.). Men and women train together under identical principles (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Systema's roots are the hand-to-hand combat of the Russian army and Cossack traditions; the modern system was shaped by Colonel Mikhail Ryabko, who taught Soviet special forces (Spetsnaz) and died in April 2023 (Wikipedia, n.d.). His student Vladimir Vasiliev moved to Canada in 1993, founded Systema Headquarters in Toronto and authored the bestselling *Let Every Breath* with Robert Meredith, exporting the art worldwide; a parallel lineage runs through Aleksei Kadochnikov (Russian Martial Art, n.d.). Today the organization counts 600+ certified instructors and 250+ schools in 40+ countries (Russian Martial Art, n.d.).
