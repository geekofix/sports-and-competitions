## Basic Information

Snowboard parallel giant slalom (PGS) is the head-to-head alpine racing discipline of snowboarding: two riders race simultaneously down identical, side-by-side giant slalom courses of red and blue gates, the faster of each duel advancing through knockout rounds. A fixture of the FIS World Cup and world championships, it has been on the Olympic programme since Salt Lake City 2002 \u2014 replacing the single-rider giant slalom of Nagano 1998 \u2014 and with parallel slalom and team events it completes snowboarding's alpine branch.

## Rules

Qualification is run as a single timed leg on one course, after which riders are paired by seed into head-to-head duels. In each duel the two riders race simultaneously on parallel red and blue courses (set symmetrically so neither has an advantage), the winner advancing round by round through 1/16 or 1/8 finals to the final and small final; in two-leg duels the riders switch courses for the second leg to equalise any difference between the red and blue lines. Courses are set with giant slalom-style gates of offset turns over a vertical drop of roughly 200\u2013225 metres; a missed gate or fall loses the duel (or forces re-run in two-leg matches), and finals decide medallists by knockout. Equipment is alpine snowboarding: hard boots, plate bindings, long directional boards and full protective kit \u2014 with helmets mandatory \u2014 quite different from the freestyle disciplines' soft boots. World champions and Olympic champions are crowned annually in PGS at world championships and quadrennially at the Games, alongside a parallel slalom and a parallel mixed team event recently developed.

## History

Alpine snowboard racing began with the giant slalom of the first professional era \u2014 giant slalom and slalom were the racing formats of the 1990s tours \u2014 and the IOC chose giant slalom (alongside halfpipe) for snowboarding's Olympic debut at Nagano 1998, where Ross Rebagliati's gold (and the briefly stripped-then-restored marijuana case) made history. For Salt Lake City 2002 the event was converted to parallel giant slalom head-to-head racing \u2014 the two-rider duel bringing television-friendly direct competition \u2014 and it has stayed on the programme since; champions have included Philipp Schoch (2002, 2006), Jasey Jay Anderson (2010), Vic Wild's double (PGS and parallel slalom) at Sochi 2014, Ester Ledeck\u00e1's remarkable 2018 gold days after her Alpine skiing super-G triumph, and Benjamin Karl (2022). The FIS stages a parallel racing World Cup in which Austria's Benjamin Karl, Italy's Roland Fischnaller and Russia's Ekaterina Tudegesheva dominated the 2010s \u2014 the medal table of the 2013 world championships showing Karl, Fischnaller and Wild on the men's PGS podium \u2014 while from Beijing 2022 a mixed parallel team event joined the Games programme. Alpine snowboarding's heartland has long been Central Europe \u2014 Austria, Italy, Germany, Switzerland \u2014 with South Korea's alpine programme also strong — Lee Sang-ho took Olympic silver in 2018.
