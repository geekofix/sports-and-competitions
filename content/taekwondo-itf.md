## Basic Information

ITF taekwon-do is the traditional style of taekwondo founded by General Choi Hong Hi (1918–2002) and governed internationally by the International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF), which he founded on 22 March 1966 in Seoul with nine founding associations and which is today headquartered in Vienna; it is distinct from the Olympic World Taekwondo (WT) style (International Taekwon-Do Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The ITF competition programme combines patterns (tul), sparring, power breaking and self-defense (hosin sul), and women and men train and compete side by side in the same events, weight classes and age groups (International Taekwon-Do Federation, n.d.). After Choi Hong Hi's death on 15 June 2002 the organization split into three rival ITFs — Vienna, Spain (Chang Hon) and Canada — each continuing the Chang Hon lineage (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

ITF competition follows the federation's Official Rules of Competition: individual and team patterns (tul) from the 24-pattern Chang Hon syllabus — from the foundational Giecho Hyung (created 1947) to the Pyong Ahn series (1982) — judged for correctness, power and grace; sparring is semi-contact with continuous scoring over rounds stopped by the center referee; one-step sparring, free sparring and breaking (special techniques and power tests) complete the syllabus (International Taekwon-Do Federation, n.d.). Bouts are fought on a matted contest area in weight classes with separate men's and women's divisions, and competitors are ranked by the gup (color-belt) and dan (black-belt) system across junior, senior and veteran age classes (International Taekwon-Do Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Choi Hong Hi studied Shotokan karate in Japan during the Second World War and developed the Chang Hon system of taekwon-do, which he named on 11 April 1955; he founded the International Taekwon-Do Federation on 22 March 1966 in Seoul with nine founding nations (South Korea, Vietnam, Malaysia, Singapore, West Germany, the United States, Turkey, Italy and Spain), published his 15-volume Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do beginning in 1983, and after political pressure moved to Toronto in 1972, where he died in exile on 15 June 2002 (Wikipedia, n.d.). His most famous international student was actor and martial artist Chuck Norris, who earned his black belt under ITF grandmaster Jae C. Shin, founder of the World Tang Soo Do Association in 1982 (Wikipedia, n.d.; International Taekwon-Do Federation, n.d.).
