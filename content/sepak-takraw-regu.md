## Basic Information

The regu is the classic three-a-side form of sepak takraw — the Southeast Asian kick-volleyball — in which each team fields a tekong (server), an apit kiri (left feeder) and an apit kanan (right attacker or striker) plus two substitutes, all under the laws of ISTAF, the International Sepaktakraw Federation (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The regu has been the flagship Asian Games event since the sport joined the programme in Beijing 1990, and the King's Cup World Championship in Bangkok decides a team title by playing three regus per nation, the first country to win two of them taking the crown (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Thailand won the men's regu gold 2–0 over Malaysia, and the Thai women won a fifth consecutive women's team regu title by beating South Korea 2–0, while Japan took the men's team regu bronze with Yuki Sato in the squad (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Among the current leading players, the women's regu is headed by Thailand's 2026 Asian Games team regu champions — five straight titles since Guangzhou 2010 — and by India's Khushbu, who won women's regu bronze at the 2022 Asian Games in Hangzhou after losing the semifinal to Thailand 10–21, 13–21; on the men's side the elite is led by Thailand's 2026 regu gold medallists, with Malaysia as runners-up and Japan's Yuki Sato bronze medallist in the team regu (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

## Rules

A regu consists of three players and two substitutes; the tekong stands at the back and serves from the centre service circle (0.60 m diameter, its centre 2.45 m from each baseline and 3.05 m from each sideline), while the apit kiri and apit kanan stand in the quarter circles (0.90 m radius) in their corners of the court at the service (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The court measures 13.40 m × 6.10 m with a net 1.52 m high for men and 1.42 m for women, and the synthetic ball weighs 170–180 g (circumference 42–44 cm) for men and 150–160 g (43–45 cm) for women (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each side may touch the ball a maximum of three times before returning it, no player may touch it twice consecutively, and hands and arms are forbidden in play except for the server's toss (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Faults include net or post contact, more than three touches, carrying or rolling the ball, a foot over the net to block an opponent, serving or standing out of position and playing the ball in the opponents' court (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A set is won at 15 points with a two-point margin — adopted in the 2011 ISTAF rules revision — and a match is best of three sets, with the deciding set played to 15 and extended to 17 at 14–14; in the King's Cup team event, each nation fields three regus and the first to win two regus wins the team title (ISTAF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's regu uses the lower net and the lighter ball, and men's and women's regu are separate events at the Asian Games, the SEA Games and the King's Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The modern regu format crystallised when officials from Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Myanmar standardised the game in Kuala Lumpur in 1960 and the name sepak takraw was adopted from the 1965 Southeast Asian Peninsular Games; the Asian Sepaktakraw Federation (ASTAF) was formed in 1965 and the International Sepaktakraw Federation (ISTAF) in 1988 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's regu has been an Asian Games medal event since Beijing 1990 and women's regu since Bangkok 1998, and the King's Cup World Championship has been held annually in Bangkok since 1985 with Malaysia as inaugural champions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 1990s and 2000s were dominated by Thailand and Malaysia, whose players — among them Pornchai Kaokaew, Suebsak Pansueb and Sittipong Khamchan in Thailand — became national icons, and Myanmar's Kyu Kyu Thin emerged as one of the leading women of her generation with multiple Asian Games medals in regu and doubles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
