## Basic Information

Sepak takraw regu is the standard, three-a-side form of sepak takraw, the Southeast Asian "kick volleyball" in which a woven rattan or synthetic ball is played over a net using only feet, knees, chest and head. A regu is the basic playing unit of the sport: three players with fixed roles, facing an opposing regu across a net on a badminton-sized court. Governed internationally by the International Sepaktakraw Federation (ISTAF, founded 1988), regu is the format contested at the Asian Games, the Southeast Asian Games, the King's Cup World Championship and the ISTAF World Cup, and it is the discipline through which Thailand, Malaysia, Myanmar and Vietnam have built the sport's greatest rivalries.

## Rules

The court measures 13.4 by 6.1 metres — the size of a doubles badminton court — with a net 1.52 m high for men and 1.42 m for women. Each regu fields three players in distinct roles: the tekong, the server who must keep one foot grounded in the serving circle while kicking the ball over the net, and two killers (strikers) positioned near the net who mount the attacks. After the serve, each side may touch the ball up to three times before returning it, and body parts below the shoulder only are legal — hands and arms are forbidden. Sets are played to 21 points with a lead of at least two, capped at 25 if the score reaches 21\u201321, and matches are best of three sets; in the team (regu) event three regus per side play, with the tie decided on regu wins. The sport's signature strikes — the acrobatic roll spike and the mid-air sunback spike — belong chiefly to the regu game, where strikers convert the second touch into spectacular overhead kills.

## History

The regu format grew directly out of the net version of sepak takraw that emerged in Penang in the 1940s (sepak raga jaring) and was standardised in 1960 in Kuala Lumpur, when officials from Malaya, Singapore, Thailand and Myanmar fixed the rules, the court dimensions and the hybrid name. The sport debuted as a medal event at the 1965 Southeast Asian Peninsular Games in Kuala Lumpur, and the three-player regu remained its core competitive unit — alongside later doubles and hoop (circle) formats — when ISTAF was founded in 1988. The King's Cup World Championship, first staged in Thailand in 1985 under royal patronage, is contested in regu and doubles, and a world championship for national regu teams was introduced in the 1990s; Thailand has dominated regu competition throughout, with Malaysia, Myanmar and Vietnam its closest challengers. Regu sepak takraw entered the Asian Games in 1990 at Beijing and remains the sport's premier international team format.
