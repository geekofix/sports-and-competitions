## Basic Information

BASE jumping is an extreme air sport in which participants jump from a fixed object — a building, antenna, span (bridge) or earth (cliff) — using a parachute or, in wingsuit BASE, a wingsuit to glide before deploying the parachute; the acronym BASE was coined in the late 1970s from these four object types. There is no single world federation, but the two premier competitive formats are the World BASE Race, held in the Romsdalen valley in Norway — the 2026 edition returned to Romsdalen from July 9–11, 2026, the same valley where the first race took place in 2008 at Innfjorden — and the BASE Jump Extreme World Cup (EWC), held annually in mid-July at the 186 m Gran Hotel Bali in Benidorm, Spain, since 2008 (World BASE Race, n.d.; BASE Jump EWC, n.d.).

To compete in the World BASE Race, pilots must document at least 150 wingsuit BASE jumps and pass a qualification course; competitors race head-to-head down a marked flight course, and the fastest time down the valley wins. The EWC crowns an individual world champion over a series of rounds. Both events publish official results on their websites (World BASE Race, n.d.).

BASE jumping is strictly an adult sport: participants must be at least 18 years old, hold a skydiving license (typically a USPA C-license or equivalent) and complete specific BASE training; there are no separate women's rule sets — women compete in the same races, and female wingsuit pilots such as the legendary Steph Davis have been among the most influential figures in the sport (World BASE Race, n.d.).

## Rules

In competitive wingsuit BASE racing, the course is marked by ground reference points, and pilots must pass within a defined window; the clock starts at exit and stops at the finish gate. Penalties are assessed for missed gates, and the fastest valid time wins. At the World BASE Race, heats are flown in groups and the fastest advance; the final is head-to-head or timed against the clock depending on conditions (World BASE Race, n.d.).

Equipment rules require a wingsuit appropriate to the pilot's skill level, a single main parachute with a reserve, an automatic activation device (AAD), and an approved helmet; object-specific rules apply — for example, at the Benidorm EWC the exit is from the hotel roof and the landing zone is the beach in front of the hotel, with strict briefing and medical standby requirements (BASE Jump EWC, n.d.). Training requirements are progressive: a minimum number of skydives, then tracking and wingsuit courses, before a first BASE jump is permitted.

Safety is governed by each event's rules and by national aviation authorities; jumps from objects in national parks or urban areas require permits. Because of the fatality risk — the sport's core ethics demand experience, currency and conservative decision-making — there are no youth or junior competitive classes, and all competitions are 18+.

## History

The modern history of BASE jumping begins with Carl Boenish, who in 1978 made the first documented intentional jumps from El Capitan in Yosemite National Park, filming them for the documentary "On the Edge"; the term BASE was coined by filmmaker Phil Abraham in the early 1980s. In 1984, the first legal building jump took place from the unfinished 72-story George Washington Bridge in New York, and the first antenna jump from the 362 m Warsaw radio mast in Poland, performed by a small group of pioneers (World BASE Race, n.d.).

Wingsuit flying — gliding in a fabric suit between ridges and cliffs — emerged in the late 1990s, pioneered by Patrick de Gayardon of France, who died in a test flight in 1998, and was perfected by the BirdMen team; wingsuit BASE racing in Norway began in 2008 with the first World BASE Race at Innfjorden, won by Erik Osterman (World BASE Race, n.d.). Among the most celebrated figures is Steph Davis of the United States, a world-record wingsuit pilot and BASE jumper who, among other feats, free-soloed (climbed without ropes) El Sendero Luminoso in Mexico and wrote influential books on the sport; she was inducted into the BASE jumping hall of fame (BASE Jump EWC, n.d.).

On the women's side, female pioneers such as Ellen Brennan and later wingsuit racers have competed at the highest level of the World BASE Race, and women have held wingsuit distance and speed records (BASE Jump EWC, n.d.). The BASE Jump EWC at Benidorm, Spain, began in 2008 and has been held annually since, becoming the fixed-object world championship.
