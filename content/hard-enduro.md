## Basic Information

Hard enduro is extreme off-road motorcycle racing over natural terrain — rock fields, fallen logs, river beds, steep climbs and man-made obstacles — governed by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM), whose FIM Hard Enduro World Championship ran its 2026 season from April to October across roughly eight to nine rounds on four continents (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme [FIM], n.d.-a). The 2026 rounds included 24MX Alestrem (France, 17–19 April), Extreme XL Lagares (Portugal, 1–3 May), Silver Kings (USA, 18–20 June), the Abestone Rodeo at Miravalle (Italy, 10–12 July and 4–6 September), Forza Orza at Orsa Gröklitt (Sweden, 20–22 August), the Roof of Africa (Lesotho), Sea to Sky (Canada) and Hixpania (Spain) (FIM, n.d.-a). Manuel Lettenbichler of Austria had a clean 2026 sweep — winning the Erzbergrodeo, Romaniacs and the Hard Enduro World Championship — Billy Bolt of Great Britain took his sixth consecutive FIM SuperEnduro world title in 2026, and Tadeusz Kabakchiev won the 2026 Roof of Africa (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A parallel Hard Enduro World Ranking was launched on 1 February 2026, awarding points by event tier — Supreme (2,000 pts, e.g. Erzbergrodeo and Romaniacs), Premium (1,200), Master (500) and Challenger (250), best eight results counting, with US$100,000 prize money for the top ten — and a dedicated Women's class (Women's WC) exists alongside the men's classes (FIM, n.d.-b) (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, n.d.-b) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

## Rules

The FIM Hard Enduro World Championship is run under the FIM Sporting and Technical Regulations for hard enduro (2026 edition), published by the FIM; events combine a prologue, a sprint (or "sprint race") and a main race over extreme terrain, with a maximum of 33 points per event (3 for the prologue, 10 for the sprint, 20 for the main race) and only the first 15 finishers in each class scoring (FIM, n.d.-a). The championship classes are WC (elite), Junior WC, 4-Stroke WC, Senior WC, Women's WC and Youth WC, with cups decided by the best six results of the season (FIM, n.d.-a). Riders are timed through special tests and checkpoints, must pass all controls, and assistance is restricted — outside help in the special sections leads to penalties or exclusion; machines must be road-registered enduro motorcycles meeting the class regulations (FIM, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The parallel Hard Enduro World Ranking (from 1 February 2026) classes events as Supreme, Premium, Master and Challenger, counts the best eight results per rider and pays prize money to the top ten (FIM, n.d.-b). Women compete in their own Women's WC class under identical regulations, and junior/senior/youth age classes exist for both genders (FIM, n.d.-a).

## History

Hard enduro grew out of trials and enduro racing in the 1970s — the Gilles Lalay Classic in France and, from the mid-1970s, the Roof of Africa in Lesotho, which became the archetypal "hard" race over mountain terrain; the Erzbergrodeo in Austria, the brainchild of Martin Freinademetz with Karl Katoch around 1995, turned the idea into a mass spectacle on the Erzberg mountain, now attracting tens of thousands of riders (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-c). The FIM formalized the discipline with the Hard Enduro World Championship, and today's icons include Graham Jarvis of New Zealand, who won the Roof of Africa a record nine times and was still a front-runner at 51, Wade Young of South Africa, and Alfredo Gomez of Spain, a two-time Erzbergrodeo winner (Wikipedia, n.d.-b, n.d.-c).
