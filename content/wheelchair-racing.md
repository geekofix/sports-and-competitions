## Basic Information

Wheelchair racing is the track and road racing discipline of para athletics for athletes with leg and trunk impairments, governed by World Para Athletics; the classes are T51 (limited trunk and arm function), T52, T53 and T54 (T53/54 have full arm and trunk function with leg impairment and are the fastest), raced on the track over 100 m to 5,000 m and on the road over 10 km, half marathon and marathon (World Para Athletics, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-e). Wheelchair racing debuted at the Tokyo 1964 Paralympics and the marathon joined the programme in 1984 (Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

At Paris 2024 the women's T54 marathon at Les Invalides went to Catherine Debrunner of Switzerland in 1:41:50 ahead of Madison de Rozario of Australia (1:46:13) and Susannah Scaroni of the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.). In the 2026 World Marathon Majors season, Debrunner (pictured at London 2024) won in Tokyo, London (1:38:29, her fourth London title, edging Tatyana McFadden 1:38:34) and Berlin (1:35:01), and set the world record of 1:33:16 at the 2026 Chicago Marathon — she holds nine world records, won 15 of 20 Marathon Majors starts, took five golds at Paris 2024 and won the Laureus award in 2023 (World Para Athletics, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b; International Paralympic Committee, 2026, April 27; Olympics.com, 2026, April 26).

Among the men, Marcel Hug of Switzerland — the "Silver Bullet" — took his eighth London Marathon title in 1:24:13 on 26 April 2026, equalling David Weir's record as the most decorated man in the race, and won Berlin 2026 in 1:23:28; Weir himself has six London titles (World Para Athletics, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d). Leading women alongside Debrunner are Tatyana McFadden of the United States, Manuela Schär of Switzerland and Madison de Rozario of Australia (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

World Para Athletics competition rules apply: wheelchair racers use purpose-built racing wheelchairs (two large rear wheels, small front casters, governed by equipment regulations) in marked lanes on the track with staggered starts and crouch or push starts, electronic timing and photo finish; on the road, races follow the marathon/half marathon/10 km courses with drinks stations (World Para Athletics, n.d.-c).

Classification T51–T54 determines the eligible events; T51–T52 athletes use strapped gloves and push rims differently, T53/54 race in cambered chairs at speeds above 30 km/h on the track; men's and women's races are separate, and world records are recorded per class on track and road (World Para Athletics, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-e).

## History

Wheelchair racing began at the first Paralympics in Rome 1960 and made its Games debut as a medal sport at Tokyo 1964; Australia's Louise Sauvage, pictured in the 800 m T54 final at Sydney 2000, won four track gold medals there and became one of the most celebrated racers (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Britain's David Weir won six London Marathon titles and multiple Paralympic golds between 2002 and 2014; other icons include America's Tatyana McFadden, a five-time Paralympic champion, and Switzerland's Marcel Hug and Catherine Debrunner, who have dominated the 2020s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Para Athletics, n.d.-b).
