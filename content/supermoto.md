## Basic Information

Supermoto is hybrid motorcycle racing that sends motocross-derived machines around circuits mixing paved and dirt sections: hard tires and motocross agility combine on tracks built from asphalt with off-road segments, jumps and loose-surface corners. Born in the United States in the late 1970s as the made-for-television Superbikers contest, it is a discipline of slides and back-it-in corner style with one of racing's most spectacular aesthetics.

## Rules

A supermoto bike is a four-stroke single-cylinder motocross or enduro machine converted for the hybrid track: smaller 17-inch wheels, road-race tires, upgraded front brakes and lowered, stiffened suspension. The track is typically laid out on a kart or club circuit with added dirt sections, a jump or two, and a signature loose-surface corner where riders slide the rear wheel; the discipline rewards mix mastery, from road-race lines on the asphalt to body control on the dirt and fast drive out of slow sections. Race formats use short sprints and qualifying heats, with the FIM promoting a Supermoto World Championship in S1 and S2 classes and national series across Europe and North America; gravity-start, back-it-in style remains the sport's signature and can be seen again in the growing discipline of supermoto-style flat-track hybrid events.

## History

Supermoto was invented for American television in 1979, when the ABC Wide World of Sports staged a made-for-TV event called Superbikers at Carlsbad, pitting the best motorcycle racers from motocross, road racing and flat track on a hybrid circuit; the all-rounder concept won a devoted audience and migrated to Europe in the 1980s, where national federations in France and Italy built the first genuine championships. The FIM launched a Supermoto World Championship in the 2000s and the sport flourished especially in France, Italy and the United States, with stars (including riders who crossed over to top road racing careers) and factory-backed teams. The discipline also sold street motorcycles: supermoto-styled street bikes became a lasting market category, and the sport endures through world and continental championships, the X Games-era exposure of its sliding style, and combined racing festivals on hybrid circuits worldwide.
