## Basic Information

Supermoto is motorcycle racing on mixed-surface circuits that combine asphalt sections, dirt sections and jumps: a 450 cc single-cylinder four-stroke dirt bike with 17-inch wheels, slick road tires, lowered suspension and a big front brake. The format was born in 1979 when producer Gavin Trippe created the "Superbikers" event at Carlsbad Raceway in California for ABC's Wide World of Sports, running until 1985; it crossed to Europe in the 1980s, where the Gilera Nordwest of 1991 became the first factory supermoto (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The world championship is the FIM S1GP Supermoto World Championship, held annually since 2002 — its first champion was Thierry van den Bosch — with a single S1 class since 2010 and a mono-tyre rule since 2006 (FIM, n.d.). France's Thomas Chareyre is the most successful rider with eight titles (2010, 2012, 2014–2017, 2019, 2020), and the 2025 champion is Austria's Lukas Höllbacher, crowned in January 2026 ahead of Steve Bonnal (FIM, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Women riders compete in national supermoto series under the same rules (FIM, n.d.).

## Rules

The S1GP machine is a 450 cc single-cylinder four-stroke (about 60 hp) on a motocross chassis with 17-inch wheels, road-racing slicks, long-travel suspension lowered for asphalt and a large front disc brake (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Circuits mix paved and dirt sections with at least one jump; a race weekend runs a timed Superpole qualifying plus two short main races of roughly 15–20 minutes, scored with championship points on a descending scale (FIM, n.d.). Since 2006 all riders use one nominated tire brand (mono-tyre) to control costs, and since 2010 only the single S1 class is contested at world level — the S2 class ran from 2004 to 2009 and no championship was held in 2018 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Protective gear is full motocross kit; men's and women's riders race under identical technical and sporting rules (FIM, n.d.).

## History

Gavin Trippe's Superbikers at Carlsbad (1979–1985) proved the mixed-surface concept on American television; the idea spread to Europe, where French and Italian riders developed the first dedicated supermoto bikes, culminating in the Gilera Nordwest in 1991 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The FIM launched the S1GP World Championship in 2002; early champions Thierry van den Bosch (2002, 2004, 2009), Eddy Seel (2003), Bernd Hiemer (2006, 2008), Adrien Chareyre (2007, 2011) and Mauno Hermunen (2013) built the sport, before Thomas Chareyre's eight titles made him its defining icon (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
