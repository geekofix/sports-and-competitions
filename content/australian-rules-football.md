## Basic Information

Australian rules football is a contact team sport played between two teams of 18 players on a large oval field, with four tall goal posts at each end. A goal kicked between the two central posts is worth 6 points and a "behind" through the outer posts is worth 1 point. The professional men's competition is the Australian Football League (AFL), and the elite women's competition is AFL Women's (AFLW); the AFL is governed at national level under the Australian Football League Commission, with state bodies such as the Victorian Football League governing lower tiers (AFL, n.d.-a). The 2026 AFL season ended with the Brisbane Lions defeating the Fremantle Dockers 14.12 (96) to 12.17 (89) in the Grand Final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on September 26, 2026, before a crowd of 100,023 — Brisbane's third consecutive premiership (2024–2026) and sixth overall, with Logan Morris kicking the late winning goal and the sealer (AFL, n.d.-a; AFL Tables, n.d.). The AFLW Grand Final was played on November 27, 2026. Australian rules football is the 18-a-side code of the football-codes family — [American Football](american-football.html), [Canadian Football](canadian-football.html), [Gaelic Football](gaelic-football.html), [International Rules Football](international-rules-football.html) — and its own variants [Women's Australian Rules Football](women-s-australian-rules-football.html), [AFL 9s](afl-9s.html) and [AFLX](aflx.html); all are listed under Important Links.

The women's game is fully professional and growing fast: AFLW, launched in 2017, now plays a full home-and-away season plus finals, and its 2026 Grand Final again drew record crowds. Current stars of the AFLW include the league's leading goalkickers and midfielders who have made the women's competition a standalone product rather than a curtain-raiser (AFL, n.d.-b). In the men's AFL, the 2026 premiers Brisbane Lions are coached by Chris Fagan, who announced he would step down on October 1, 2026, with Cameron Bruce named as his successor on November 10, 2026 (AFL, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The Laws of Australian Football are set by the AFL Commission. Each team fields 18 players on the ground (plus interchange and emergencies), and a match at AFL level consists of four quarters of approximately 20 minutes each, with time-on added for stoppages. The oval is typically 150–180 m long and 130–150 m wide; each end has four posts: two tall central goal posts and two shorter behind posts. Kicking the ball between the goal posts after the goal line is a goal (6 points); between a goal post and a behind post, or hitting a post, is a behind (1 point); the ball passing between the behind posts is also a behind (AFL, n.d.-a).

Play is continuous: the ball is moved by kicking, handballing (striking the ball with a closed fist) or running. A player may run with the ball for at most 15 m before bouncing it on the ground. Tackling is allowed only between the shoulders and knees and above the ankles, and holding the ball is an offence; a free kick is awarded to the tackled player, who may be held once for a "holding the man" infringement. Marking rules govern catching: a player who catches a kicked ball cleanly from at least 15 m earns a mark and a free kick (AFL, n.d.-a).

Officiating uses three field umpires, two boundary umpires and two goal umpires; the AFL has used a score review system with a video replay umpire since 2014. At AFL level, each club may have up to 46 players on its list, with a salary cap and a player draft; the AFLW plays under the same core laws with modified squad and interchange rules, and community and junior competitions use smaller teams (e.g. nine-a-side) on smaller grounds (AFL, n.d.-b). There are no rule differences between the men's and women's games beyond squad sizes and interchange; the scoring system — goal 6 points, behind 1 point — is identical.

## History

Australian rules football developed in Melbourne in the 1850s, with several competing codes in the colonies. The Melbourne Football Club, founded in 1858, is the world's oldest continuously existing football club, and in 1859 club secretary Tom Wills — a former student of Rugby School in England — helped codify a set of laws for a winter game for cricketers, published in Bell's Life in Victoria. The first recorded matches were played in Richmond Park, Melbourne, in July 1858 (AFL, n.d.-a; AFL Tables, n.d.).

The Victorian Football League (VFL) was founded in 1897 and became the elite competition; it was renamed the Australian Football League in 1990 after expanding beyond Victoria, with clubs from Western Australia (West Coast Eagles, 1987), South Australia (Adelaide, 1991) and Queensland (Brisbane Lions and Gold Coast) joining. The women's competition began with exhibition matches in the 1990s and the first national AFLW season was played in 2017 with eight clubs, expanding to 18 clubs by 2023 (AFL, n.d.-b).

Legendary players include Haydn Bunton Sr. ("the Baron"), three-time Brownlow Medallist; Ron Barassi Sr.; Dick Reynolds, record four premierships as captain; and more recently Gary Ablett Jr., four-time All-Australian. On the women's side, the first AFLW number-one draft pick Daisy Pearce became one of the game's most influential players and administrators. The Grand Final has been played at the MCG since 1902 (with wartime exceptions), and the Norm Smith Medal honors the best player on the ground (AFL Tables, n.d.).
