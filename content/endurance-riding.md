## Basic Information

Endurance riding is an equestrian long-distance sport in which horse and rider cover 80-160 km (50-100 miles) in a day over natural terrain, with mandatory veterinary checks ("vet gates") that the horse must pass to continue. It is governed by the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI), which sanctions the FEI Endurance World Championships (100 miles / 160 km) and European Championships, and national federations run rides from 25 km up to championship distance.

## Rules

At FEI championships, the ride is divided into loops (2-5) with a vet gate after each; the horse's heart rate must recover to 64 bpm or below (56 bpm at the final gate) within a set time (usually 20 minutes for intermediate gates, 30 at some) for the horse to continue. The clock runs only while the horse is moving ("ride time"); time spent at the vet gate is not counted. Riders must complete the course within the maximum time, and minimum weights apply (rider plus equipment, typically 75 kg at FEI level). Horses must be at least 6 years old for 160 km rides (5 years for shorter rides), and compulsory rest periods are enforced. In team events (FEI World Championships), the best three scores count. Anti-doping rules for horse and human apply under the FEI Equine Anti-Doping Rules.

## History

Endurance riding descends from cavalry long-distance rides and the Arabian horse tradition; the modern sport was formalized in the United States in the 1950s, with the first 100-mile Tevis Cup ride in California in 1955 (won by Wendell Robie). The American Endurance Ride Conference (AERC, founded 1972) standardized vet-gate rules in the US, and the FEI (founded 1921) took over international governance, holding the first FEI World Endurance Championship in 1986. The United Arab Emirates, the United States, France, Spain and Australia are leading nations; the UAE dominates elite competition with huge state-supported stables. Champions such as Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (who won the 2012 FEI World Championship on Madji des Rocs) and Australian rider Sam Staggs are among the sport's best-known names.
