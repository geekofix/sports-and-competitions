## Basic Information

Superkart is kart racing at the highest level: 250 cc two-stroke, six-speed gearbox karts with full aerodynamic bodywork that reach about 100 hp, accelerate from 0 to 100 km/h in under three seconds and exceed 250 km/h on long circuits — the fastest karts in the world. The discipline is governed by FIA Karting (CIK-FIA), which runs the CIK-FIA European Superkart Championship in the 250 cc class alongside national series and the Superkart Single-Cylinder Cup; the last world championship was held in 1995 (FIA Karting, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Races take place on full-size car circuits of at least 1500 m — Silverstone, Laguna Seca, Magny-Cours and Castle Combe are classic venues (Wikipedia, n.d.). The class is open to all genders, and women drivers have competed in national superkart series (FIA Karting, n.d.).

## Rules

The premier class, 250 cc International (formerly Formula E), uses a 250 cc two-stroke twin-cylinder engine with a six-speed sequential gearbox in a long-wheelbase kart chassis with full fairing, slick tires and disc brakes; the minimum weight is 218 kg including the driver (Wikipedia, n.d.). Below it sit the 125 cc gearbox (Formula KZ-style shifter) class and the defunct 80 cc class (FIA Karting, n.d.). Races are sprint events of roughly 15–30 km or 20–30 minutes on car circuits of 1500 m or longer, run as heats plus finals with championship points, and scrutineering enforces the homologated engine and chassis rules (FIA Karting, n.d.). Men and women compete under identical technical rules in mixed or separate grids depending on the series (FIA Karting, n.d.).

## History

Superkart was born in 1970s Britain, when enthusiasts fitted 250 cc motorcycle engines and gearboxes into kart chassis; the CIK (now CIK-FIA) created a world championship that ran until 1995, and the European Superkart Championship followed (Wikipedia, n.d.). British and European drivers dominated the early decades, and the class became famous for its extreme speed on long straights — the lap records at circuits like Laguna Seca still stand from the superkart era (Wikipedia, n.d.).
