## Basic Information

Lagori (also known as seven stones, pithoo or dikori in regional forms) is India's traditional chase-and-rebuild game: one team knocks down a pile of seven flat stones with a ball, then tries to stack the stones back up before the fielding team can retrieve the loose ball and tag them out. It is a street and playground classic across India, played from village squares to city schoolyards.

Today the game is played at school sports days and in tournaments that revive India's traditional games, and it remains one of the country's most widely shared childhood games.

## Rules

Two teams of equal size alternate between throwing and defending. The throwers use a soft ball to knock down the pile of seven stones stacked in the centre; once the pile falls, they must rebuild the seven-stone stack while the defenders collect the loose ball and try to hit the throwers with it, each hit putting that thrower out. If the throwers complete the stack, they win the round; if the defenders put out every thrower first, the teams switch roles. House rules vary the number of stones, the throwing distance and the scoring per rebuild across the regions.

## History

Lagori belongs to India's deep tradition of ball-and-target street games, passed down through generations with the simplest equipment of flat stones and a soft ball. Played under names such as pithoo in the north and dikori in the south, the game became shared schoolyard culture across the subcontinent. In recent decades, traditional-game clubs, school programmes and corporate tournaments have revived organised lagori alongside kho kho and gilli danda, keeping the game alive as an accessible cultural sport.
