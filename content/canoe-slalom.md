## Basic Information

Canoe slalom is the Olympic whitewater discipline in which paddlers race down a course of hanging gates on rapids — the fastest clean run wins. The 2026 ICF Canoe Slalom World Championships, the 45th edition, were held from 20 to 25 July 2026 at RIVERSPORT OKC in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma — the venue for canoe slalom at the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics — with more than 250 athletes from about 50 nations and, for the first time, Olympic ranking points for LA28 on the line (ICF, 2026; American Canoe Association [ACA], 2026; Olympics.com, 2026). In the women's K1 final, Australia's Noemie (Jess) Fox took gold, with Great Britain's Kimberley Woods and the USA's Evy Leibfarth — the Paris 2024 K1 bronze medallist — also on the podium; Leibfarth's silver was her best world-championship finish and moved her to second in LA28 Olympic qualification points (Olympics.com, 2026; ACA, 2026). The program at the 2026 worlds covered men's and women's K1 and C1, the three-boat team events, and kayak cross (KX1), the new head-to-head discipline that debuted as an Olympic medal event at Paris 2024 — at the 2026 worlds the kayak cross time trial on 20 July seeded the knockout heats on 25 July (Wikipedia, n.d.). ## Rules

Under the ICF Canoe Slalom Competition Rules, a course is 250–400 m of whitewater with 18–25 gates (pairs of hanging poles); downstream gates are green, upstream gates red, and the sequence is set by the chief course designer. In heats, semifinals and finals each paddler takes one run per round (two runs in heats at world championships); the clock runs from the start signal to the finish line. Penalties: 2 seconds for touching a gate (the pole, not the water), 50 seconds for missing a gate (crossing the gate line in the wrong direction or skipping it); the best time plus penalties wins. Boat classes: K1 (kayak single, seated, double-bladed paddle), C1 (canoe single, kneeling, single-blade) and C2 (now mixed C2 with one woman and one man), each with minimum dimensions and weight (e.g., K1 max 3.5 m, min 0.60 m beam, 8.5–9 kg) (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Team events: three paddlers from the same nation run the course sequentially; all three must finish within 15 seconds of each other or a further 50-second penalty applies. In kayak cross (KX1), three or four paddlers race simultaneously from a starting platform; they may touch gates with the boat and paddle (but not the hand) and must perform a mandatory eskimault (roll) or rotation in a marked zone; the course includes a compulsory portage section, and contact is allowed — boats are plastic, 2.75 m max, min 18 kg — with extra protective gear (helmet, elbow pads, chest protector, mouthguard) (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's and women's events use the same courses and rules; junior and U23 worlds are held separately (e.g., 2026 in Kraków, Poland) (ICF, n.d.).

## History

Slalom canoeing developed in Europe in the 1920s–1930s on Alpine rivers; the first ICF Canoe Slalom World Championships were held in 1949 in Geneva, Switzerland, and the discipline joined the Olympic program at Munich 1972 (returning permanently since 1992), with women's K1 added at Barcelona 1992 and women's C1 at Tokyo 2020 (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Kayak cross (extreme slalom, first at the 2017 Pau worlds) became an Olympic medal event at Paris 2024. The Czech course at Prague-Troja, the Augsburg Eiskanal (site of the 1972 Olympics), Vaires-sur-Marne (Paris 2024) and RIVERSPORT OKC (LA28 venue, 2026 worlds) are among the most famous artificial whitewater venues (ICF, n.d.). Dominant nations include France (Denis Gargaud-Chanut, Olympic champion 2016), Germany (Ricarda Funk, Olympic champion 2020), Czechia (Jiří Prskavec, Jessica Fox of Australia — Olympic champion 2024 and multiple world champion), Slovakia and Slovenia (Peter Kauzer) (Wikipedia, n.d.).
