## Basic Information

Beach volleyball is played by two teams of two players on a 16 m × 8 m sand court divided by a net; it is governed by the Fédération Internationale de Volleyball (FIVB), has been an Olympic sport since Atlanta 1996 and has had its own FIVB World Championships since 1997 (Fédération Internationale de Volleyball [FIVB], n.d.-b). In 2026 the women's elite game was headlined by the Latvian pair Tīna Graudiņa and Anastasija Samoilova, 2025 world champions after beating the USA's Nuss and Brasher 2–1 in the Adelaide final — Latvia's first world title — and by the Brazilian Olympic champions Ana Patrícia Ramos and Duda Lisboa, who won Paris 2024 gold; the 2025 men's world title went to the Swedish pair David Åhman and Jonatan Hellvig, who beat compatriots Hölting Nilsson and Andersson 2–0 in an all-Swedish final in Adelaide, becoming the youngest world champions ever at 23 and 24 (FIVB, n.d.-b). There was no World Championship in 2026 (the next is 2027 in the Netherlands); instead the 2026 Volleyball World Beach Pro Tour ran from February 5 to October 18, 2026 across Future, Challenge and Elite16 tiers, ending with The Finals for the top 10 teams, with Elite16 stops in Rio de Janeiro, João Pessoa, Brasília and Montréal (FIVB, n.d.-a). At the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics at Saly Beach, the Netherlands' Jara Both and Matilda de Groot won the girls' gold 2–1 over Italy and Austria's Hohenauer and Poinstingl won the boys' gold unbeaten, and at the FIVB U18 World Championships 2026 in The Hague the German boys Hüper and Wüst and the Chinese girls Zhuang and Qi took the titles (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.).

## Rules

Under the FIVB Beach Volleyball Rules, a match is best of three sets: sets one and two are played to 21 points and the deciding set to 15, and a team must win by two clear points (FIVB, n.d.-c). The court is 16 m × 8 m and the net is 2.43 m high for men and 2.24 m for women. Each team may touch the ball a maximum of three times before returning it, a block counts as a touch, and the same player may not contact the ball twice in succession (except after a block). An open-hand tip into the opponent's court is not allowed, the serve must be hit from behind the end line, and teams switch ends every seven points in the first two sets and every five points in the deciding set; each team has one timeout per set. Men's and women's matches use identical rules apart from the net height, and youth (U18/U21) events use the same court with adjusted rules at the youngest levels (FIVB, n.d.-c).

## History

Beach volleyball originated in Santa Monica, California, in the 1920s, where six-a-side games were played on the sand for fun; the two-player doubles format was popularized in the 1930s and 1940s, the FIVB began promoting the sport internationally in the 1980s, the first FIVB World Championship was held in Rio de Janeiro in 1997, and beach volleyball entered the Olympic programme at Atlanta 1996 (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.). The most decorated player is American Karch Kiraly, the only athlete to win Olympic gold in both indoor volleyball (1984, 1988) and beach volleyball (1996 with Kent Steffes); on the women's side, the American pair Misty May-Treanor and Kerri Walsh won three consecutive Olympic gold medals (2004, 2008, 2012). At Paris 2024 the Swedish men Åhman and Hellvig and the Brazilians Ana Patrícia and Duda won gold, and it was the first Olympics on which the United States won no beach volleyball medal (FIVB, n.d.-b).
