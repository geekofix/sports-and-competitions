## Basic Information

Toe wrestling is the English novelty sport in which two opponents lock bare feet on a purpose-built Toe Wrestler ring (a small frame with two facing footholds) and attempt to pin each other's foot to the side, using the toes as a wrestler uses hands. The World Toe Wrestling Championship held in Derbyshire each year remains the sport's premier event, crowning champions in men's and women's categories.

## Rules

Competitors sit facing each other, barefoot, and interlock their toes in the ring's center: in the championship's standard format, each bout is contested over three rounds (best of three), with the right foot locked in the first bout, the left in the second, and both locked in a third decider if needed. The goal is to force the opponent's foot to the ground or the pin position while keeping the ankle braced; the ring's small footholds prevent leg use, and toes must be bare and free of contraband grip aids, with competitors' feet inspected before competition, and a bout referee judges pins and fair play.

## History

Toe wrestling was invented in 1974 at the Royal Oak inn in Wetton, Staffordshire, where regulars seeking a game Britain could win at cooked up the sport, staged the first championships and applied (unsuccessfully) for Olympic recognition; the game took its current world championship form in the 1990s at the Bentley Brook Inn near Ashbourne in Derbyshire, where the World Toe Wrestling Championships have been held since, with several long-reigning champions of remarkable toe strength. The sport earned a place in the canon of English pub games and charity-festival novelties, keeps a devoted annual following, and shares with gurning, shin-kicking and the like the robust tradition of eccentric village contests.
