## Basic Information

Toe wrestling is a British novelty combat sport in which two competitors sit opposite each other in a ring, lock the big toes of their right feet together, and try to push or pin the opponent's foot to the opposite wall — "like arm wrestling, but with feet" (BBC, 2024). It is governed by the World Toe Wrestling Championship organization, which stages the annual world championship every August in Ashbourne, Derbyshire — most recently at the Bentley Brook Inn in Fenny Bentley on 30 August 2025 — and crowns separate men's and women's world champions, with the women's division now a full part of the programme (Toe Wrestling Championship, n.d.). Among the current top athletes — women first — are the reigning women's world champion Lisa "Twinkletoes" Shenton of Ashbourne, a multiple-time title holder, and the reigning men's world champion Ben Woodroffe of Fenny Bentley, pictured below (BBC, 2024; Toe Wrestling Championship, n.d.).

## Rules

Bouts take place in the "Toedium", a roughly 8-foot-wide raised ring or podium with a wall on each side; competitors sit facing each other, hook the toes of their right feet together, and on the referee's call "toe!" try to force the opponent's foot or toe against the opposite wall, with the bout decided as the best of three rounds — right foot, then left foot, and a both-feet decider if needed (Toe Wrestling Championship, n.d.). Competitors must keep their seat on the podium and may not unlock their toes, lift their hips excessively or shove with the hands; the referee and judges rule on fouls, and the men's and women's divisions are contested under identical rules, with prize money for both winners — £5,000 each at the 2024 championship (BBC, 2024; Toe Wrestling Championship, n.d.). Tournaments run as knockout brackets over a single day, with the world title going to the last wrestler standing in each division (Toe Wrestling Championship, n.d.).

## History

Toe wrestling was invented in 1974 by drinkers at the Ye Olde Royal Oak Inn in Wetton, Staffordshire — reportedly men frustrated by England's exit from the 1974 FIFA World Cup, with the first winner said to have been a Canadian visitor (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first official championship followed in 1976, and the event grew into an annual tradition that has now reached its 50th edition (2024), moving between venues in Ashbourne — the Market Place and, in recent years, the Bentley Brook Inn at Fenny Bentley, where the 2025 championship was held on 30 August 2025 (BBC, 2024; Toe Wrestling Championship, n.d.). Long-time campaigners such as the record seven-time champion from Stoke-on-Trent and the Fenny Bentley local Ben Woodroffe, who has contested the event 15 times and won multiple men's titles, turned the pub pastime into an organized international competition that now draws competitors from as far as Australia (BBC, 2024).
