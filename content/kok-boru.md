## Basic Information

Kok boru (Kyrgyz for "grey wolf") is a traditional Central Asian team sport played on horseback: riders fight to seize a headless goat carcass (or a dummy) and throw it into the opponents' goal circle, the tai kazan. Related games are known as buzkashi in Afghanistan and Tajikistan, kokpar in Kazakhstan and ulak tartysh in Kyrgyzstan, and in Kyrgyzstan kok-boru is played as a team sport with four riders per side. It is the flagship event of the World Nomad Games, held biennially since 2014, and the games were inscribed by UNESCO in 2021 as a best safeguarding practice of intangible cultural heritage.

## Rules

Under the Kok Boru Federation of the Kyrgyz Republic rules used at the World Nomad Games, two teams of four riders compete on a large field; the goat carcass (about 32-35 kg, head, hooves and entrails removed, or a dummy) is placed in a circle about 25 m from the spectators' line. Riders may grab, carry and throw the carcass but must not hold it against their body or saddle in a way that drags; the aim is to carry or throw it into the opponents' tai kazan goal circle, each goal scoring a point, and the team with the most goals when time expires wins. Matches are divided into halves, referees on horseback and on foot control play, and dangerous play (whipping opponents, deliberate horse collisions) is penalized.

## History

The game originated among the nomadic Turkic peoples of the Eurasian steppe, where shepherds first used a wolf carcass — hence the name "grey wolf" — before switching to goats, and it spread westward toward Afghanistan between the 10th and 15th centuries as buzkashi. For centuries it was both military training for mounted warriors and a highlight of tribal gatherings. In Kyrgyzstan it is the de facto national sport, played at festivals such as Nooruz. The World Nomad Games, created by Kyrgyz sports official Askhat Akibaev and first held in Cholpon-Ata in 2014, gave the game an international stage; editions followed in 2016, 2018 (Cholpon-Ata), 2022 (Iznik, Turkey), 2024 (Astana, Kazakhstan) and 2026 (Kyrgyzstan), and women's teams now compete as well.
