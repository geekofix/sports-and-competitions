## Basic Information

Broomball on ice is the organized indoor form of broomball, played on a prepared ice hockey rink: unlike outdoor snow broomball, the ice is kept smooth and dry so players can run in special rubber-soled broomball shoes. It is governed internationally by the International Federation of Broomball Associations (IFBA) and nationally in the US by the United States Broomball Association (USBA); the biennial IFBA World Broomball Championships are played on ice.

## Rules

The ice version follows IFBA rules: six players per team (goaltender plus five), a 5 x 7 ft goal net, a broomstick that may not be raised above the waist, and offside judged on the center red line only. Because players do not skate, the ice is scraped and dried before play to improve traction, and players wear broomball shoes with rubber soles instead of skates. Indoor balls are smooth; outdoor snow games use a ridged ball like a small soccer ball. Matches at national and world championships run two halves with two referees on the ice, face-offs after goals, and penalty boxes.

## History

Organized broomball moved indoors onto ice hockey rinks in Canada in the 1960s-70s, with Manitoba as the heartland; the IFBA codified international rules in the 1990s-2000s and the World Broomball Championships (first held in the early 2000s, biennial) are contested on ice rinks, e.g. in Austria (2010) and Canada. The US national scene is centered in Minnesota (Bloomington, Chaska, Rosemount), and the USBA now sanctions American play after USA Broomball folded in 2020.
