## Basic Information

Blind tennis (also called sound tennis) is tennis adapted for players who are blind or visually impaired: a louder, rattling ball and modified rules that allow extra bounces. Since August 2026, blind and low-vision tennis is officially part of the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA), which had previously governed showdown (table tennis for the blind) among other sports.

## Rules

In blind tennis, the court is the standard 23.77 x 8.23 m (singles) or 10.97 m wide (doubles), but the ball makes a clear rattle so players can track it by sound. Depending on the classification (B1 totally blind to B3 partially sighted), players are allowed up to three bounces before returning the ball (two for B3), and the net may be lowered for B1 play in some formats. Scoring follows standard tennis. At IBSA events, players compete in classes B1, B2 and B3 with brackets and world championships.

## History

Tennis for visually impaired players developed from the 1980s, with sound-based balls and extra-bounce rules; showdown (a table-tennis game for blind players) has been an IBSA sport for decades. In August 2026 the IBSA officially added blind and low-vision tennis to its portfolio of sports, opening the path to IBSA world championships and future Paralympic inclusion. The sport is growing fastest in Europe and Asia.
