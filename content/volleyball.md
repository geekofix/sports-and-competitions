## Basic Information

Volleyball is the team sport played over a net: two teams of six hit a ball with hands and arms (three touches per side maximum) to return over a net and ground it in the opponent's court, with each rally scoring a point. An Olympic sport since Tokyo 1964 (with beach volleyball and sitting volleyball among its siblings in the programme), it is one of the world's most played team sports with more than 200 member federations of the FIVB.

## Rules

Six-a-side teams rotate positions on a court of 18 by 9 metres divided by a net (set at 2.43 metres for men, 2.24 for women): play begins with a serve from behind the baseline, each side may take three team touches (a block not counting) before returning over the net, and a point ends when the ball lands in, out of bounds, or a fault (catch, double touch, net violation) is called. Rally scoring runs to 25 points winning by two in the best-of-five sets, the fifth set to 15; modern rules include the libero (a back-row defensive specialist with distinct jersey), attack-line rules, rotation and substitution standards, and video challenge systems for line and touch calls, with the FIVB rulebook governing international and Olympic play.

## History

Volleyball was invented in 1895 by William G. Morgan, a YMCA physical director in Holyoke, Massachusetts, as a less contact-intensive indoor game than basketball for his businessmen's classes; the game spread rapidly through the YMCA network worldwide, its rules codified internationally in the early twentieth century and the FIVB founded in 1947. World championships began in 1949, Olympic inclusion arrived at Tokyo 1964, and the sport's modern professional era (with the great leagues of Italy, Brazil, Russia's successors and Asia, plus a strong world grand-champions cycle) has produced dominant national teams from the Soviet-era giants to Brazil's golden generations and today's world-title contenders; its beach volleyball sibling (formalized in California and Brazil) joined the Olympics in 1996.
