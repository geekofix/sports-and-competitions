## Basic Information

Faustball (German for "fist ball") is a team ball sport played on a 50 m x 20 m court (outdoors, on grass) or in a hall, in which two teams of five players hit a ball over a net (2 m high at the center) using only the fist or forearm. It is governed by the International Fistball Association (IFT), which organizes the Faustball World Championship (men's since 1968, women's since 1976) and the Faustball World Cup for club teams.

## Rules

At IFT World Championship level, each team has five players on a 50 x 20 m field with a net 2.00-2.10 m high at the center (lower for youth/women in some categories). The ball (circumference 65-68 cm, weight 340-380 g) is served by one player throwing it up and striking it over the net with a closed fist or forearm; it may bounce once on the opponent's side before being played, and each team may touch the ball up to three times (block at the net counts as a touch). A point is scored when the ball lands in the opponent's court, goes out, or the opponent faults (more than three touches, carrying, net touch, double hit). Matches are played best-of-five sets to 11 points (win by two, capped at 15). Players may be substituted, and line judges and a referee enforce the IFT Laws of the Game. Indoor faustball (on a 40 x 20 m court with a 2 m net) follows similar rules under the IFT.

## History

Faustball developed in Italy in the 16th century (gioco del pallone) and spread to Germany in the 19th century, where it became organized with the founding of the German Gymnastics Federation's fistball section; the first German championship was in 1896. The International Fistball Association (IFT, Internationale Faustball-Vereinigung) was founded in 1960, and the first men's World Championship was held in 1968 in Germany (won by West Germany); the women's followed in 1976. Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Brazil dominate internationally, with Brazil the leading non-European nation (multiple men's world titles since 1990). Faustball was a demonstration sport at the 1936 Berlin Olympics. Stars include German national team players of the 1990s-2000s and Brazilian champions such as the 2018 world champions.
