## Basic Information

The pole vault is the highest of the athletics jumps: the athlete plants a flexible pole in a box at the end of a 40 m runway, swings up and clears a horizontal bar that can be set beyond six metres, landing on a large mat. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic programme since 1896 for men and since 2000 for women (Wikipedia, n.d.). The world record is 6.31 m (20 ft 8 in), set by Armand Duplantis of Sweden at the Mondo Classic in Uppsala on 12 March 2026 — his 15th world record since 2020; his outdoor record is 6.30 m from the Tokyo 2025 World Championships. The women's world record is 5.06 m, set by Yelena Isinbayeva of Russia in Zürich on 28 August 2009 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).

At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by two-time Olympic champion Katie Moon of the USA (pictured at the 2026 Weltklasse Zürich), with Molly Caudery of Great Britain, the 2026 world indoor champion (4.85 m in Toruń), Alysha Newman of Canada and Angelica Moser of Switzerland; the men's event is dominated by Duplantis (pictured at Paris 2024), a two-time Olympic champion (2021, 2024), three-time world outdoor champion (2022, 2023, 2025), four-time world indoor champion (2022, 2024, 2025, 2026) and winner of the inaugural 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship (World Athletics, n.d.-d).

## Rules

The vaulter has one attempt at each height and is eliminated after three consecutive fouls; the bar is raised by at least 5 cm per round (and by at least 1 cm when a world record is attempted), passing is allowed, and ties are decided by count-back as in the high jump (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The runway is at least 40 m long and 1.22 m wide with a box for the pole, and each attempt has a one-minute time limit (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The pole may be of any material and length; the take-off must be from the box, and the result is measured vertically to the nearest centimetre (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete separately with the same bar mechanics; junior and para competitions use adapted equipment and classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

The pole vault has been an Olympic event since 1896, when William Hoyt of the USA won with bamboo poles; the switch to fiberglass and then carbon-fibre poles in the 1960s transformed the event (Wikipedia, n.d.). Sergey Bubka of the USSR, pictured, became the first man over six metres in 1985 and broke the world record 35 times, leaving 6.14 m in 1994 — a mark that stood until Renaud Lavillenie's 6.16 m in 2014 (Olympics, n.d.). On the women's side, Stacy Dragila won the first women's Olympic title in Sydney 2000, and Yelena Isinbayeva, pictured, became the first woman over 5.00 m in 2005 and set the world record of 5.06 m in Zürich 2009, still standing in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e). Duplantis took over from Lavillenie in 2020 and has raised the men's record from 6.17 m to 6.31 m in 2026 (World Athletics, n.d.-e).
