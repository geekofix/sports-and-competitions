## Basic Information

Cycleball (Radball) is a team sport in which two teams of two players each try to score goals with a ball played only by the bicycle or the head — hands are allowed only for the goalkeeper inside his own penalty area (Union Cycliste Internationale [UCI], n.d.-a). It is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) as part of indoor cycling, together with artistic cycling, and has been part of the UCI Indoor Cycling World Championships since 1930 (UCI, n.d.-b). The game was invented in 1883 by the American acrobat Nicholas Edward Kaufmann, whose first match against John Featherly took place on 14 September 1883, and it reached Germany in 1901 through the Lüders brothers (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first UCI world championship was held in Leipzig in 1930, and women's world titles have been awarded since 2023 (UCI, n.d.-b). In 2026, the cycle-ball World Cup series began in Kobe on 22 February 2026 with a women's competition added for the first time, and the 2026 UCI Indoor Cycling World Championships were held in the autumn (UCI, n.d.-b). The current leading players include Austria's Patrick Schnetzer, an eight-time world champion since 2011, pictured below (Wikipedia, n.d.; UCI, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the UCI indoor cycling regulations, cycleball is played on a court 14 × 11 m with goals 2 × 2 m, in two halves of seven minutes (UCI, n.d.-a). The ball — about the size of a handball — may be played with the bicycle (frame, wheels) or with the head and body, but never with hands or arms except by the goalkeeper within his own penalty area (UCI, n.d.-a). If a player touches the floor with a foot or hand, play continues but that player must first return behind his own goal line before touching the ball again; fouls inside the penalty area are punished with a 4-meter penalty shot (UCI, n.d.-a). There is no offside rule; corner kicks, free kicks and goal kicks are taken from the appropriate markings, and the team with the most goals after two halves wins, with extra time in knockout matches (UCI, n.d.-a). Men's and women's teams compete under identical rules — women's world championships were added in 2023 — and youth categories exist at national level (UCI, n.d.-b).

## History

Nicholas Edward Kaufmann staged the first cycleball match on 14 September 1883 and toured the United States demonstrating the game; German immigrants brought it to Germany in 1901, where the Lüders brothers popularized it (Wikipedia, n.d.). The UCI has run world championships since 1930, when Leipzig hosted the first edition — Germany fielded two-a-side teams while France played six-a-side on grass (UCI, n.d.-b). The most dominant players in history are the Czech Pospíšil brothers, who won 20 world titles between 1965 and 1988, and modern icons include Austria's Patrick Schnetzer, who has won eight world titles since 2011 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
