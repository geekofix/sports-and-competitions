## Basic Information

Buhurt (from Old French behourt, "joust/tournament"), also called armored combat or historical medieval battles (HMB), is a modern full-contact fighting sport in which competitors in historically accurate steel armor fight with blunted medieval weapons. The main international governing bodies are the International Medieval Combat Federation (IMCF, founded 2013) and Buhurt International (BI); the old Battle of the Nations series (2009-2022) created the first unified rules.

## Rules

Under IMCF/BI rules, fighters wear historically accurate steel armor over a padded gambeson, with a closed helmet. All weapons are blunt reproductions based on historical originals (swords, axes, maces, polearms); thrusts are forbidden and striking edges must be rounded to 2-8 mm. In buhurt (mass battle) categories — typically 5v5, 12v12, 16v16 up to 150v150 — a fighter is "out" when grounded (a third point of support such as knee, buttocks, arm or shield touches the ground) or forced outside the lists. Duel categories run two 1-minute rounds; a fighter is eliminated after three knockdowns or on submission, otherwise marshals decide on points. The IMCF holds an annual World Championship with national teams.

## History

Modern armored combat began in the 1960s with the Society for Creative Anachronism (SCA) in California using rattan weapons; full-contact steel buhurts emerged in Russia, Belarus and Ukraine in the late 1990s (e.g., the Sword of Russia tournament from 1996, Iron Town at Izborsk). The first Battle of the Nations in 2009 at Khotyn Fortress, Ukraine, united national teams under common rules; after HMB dissolved in 2022 amid the war in Ukraine, the community split into the IMCF and Buhurt International, which now run rival world championships. US promotions such as Armored Combat League were featured on History Channel's "Knight Fight" (2018).
