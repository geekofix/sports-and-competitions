## Basic Information

Flat track racing (flat track motorcycle racing, "dirt track" in the American usage) is motorcycle racing on oval dirt or paved tracks of roughly 1/8 to 1 mile, where riders race counterclockwise at high speed on bikes without brakes, sliding the rear wheel through corners (AMA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). In the United States the pinnacle is the AMA Flat Track Grand Championship, staged annually since 1954 and comprising the Mile, Half-Mile, Short Track and TT disciplines — the 2026 edition, the Roof Systems AMA Flat Track Grand Championship, was held from 5 to 10 July 2026 at the Du Quoin State Fairgrounds in Illinois (AMA, n.d.-a). In 2026 the AMA classes were AFT SuperTwins, Singles, Production Twins plus the new StreetTracker and ProSport 450 classes, and the Triple Challenge (Daytona Short Track II, Lima Half-Mile I, Springfield Mile I) crowned the overall champion (AMA, n.d.-b). In Europe the FIM Flat Track World Championship runs on banked and flat ovals, chiefly in Germany, France and the Czech Republic (FIM, n.d.). See also [Motorcycle Racing](motorcycle-racing.html) and [Motocross](motocross.html) on this site (American Motorcyclist Association, n.d.-a) (American Motorcyclist Association, n.d.-b).

Men and women compete side by side: the Singles and the new ProSport 450 classes include women riders, and women's-only classes have been added at national level in the United States; at the end of 2026 the leading American riders included the defending Grand National champions of the 2026 season at Du Quoin, where the Indian Scout FTR750 and the Harley-Davidson XR-750-style machines dominated the premier classes (AMA, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Flat track bikes have no brakes and a single gear; riders use a steel shoe (slider) on the left boot to slide the rear wheel through left-hand corners, and on TT courses — which include right-hand turns and a jump — riders must also brake and shift body weight differently (AMA, n.d.-b). Races are run over a set number of laps — the Mile main event is typically 25 laps (about 25 miles), the Half-Mile 18–20 laps, the Short Track 10–12 laps and the TT 15–20 laps — with heats, semi-finals and a main event; riders earn places by finishing position in each race (AMA, n.d.-b). The track is a left-turn oval, flat or banked up to about 30 degrees in the corners, with a cushion (air fence) on the inside; TT tracks are ovals with one or more right turns and a jump (AMA, n.d.-b). Scoring in the Grand National Championship is by points accumulated across the disciplines; the rider with the most points over the season becomes Grand National Champion (AMA, n.d.-a). Safety: riders wear a full-face helmet, leather suit, gloves and boots with sliders, and the bikes must pass technical inspection; fuel is methanol in the premier classes (AMA, n.d.-b). Women race in the same classes under identical rules, with separate women's classifications at some events; age classes (amateur, singles) and the youth classes exist below the premier division (AMA, n.d.-b).

## History

Flat track racing grew out of the board-track racing of the 1910s and became organized in the United States after World War II; the AMA launched the Grand National Championship in 1954, and Joe Leonard won the first title (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 1950s–60s were the Harley-Davidson XR-750 era: Carroll Resweber won four consecutive titles from 1958 to 1961, Dick Mann took the 1963 crown on a BSA, Gary Nixon won in 1967 and 1968, and Gene Romero — also a Hollywood actor — won in 1970 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Jared Mees holds the record with nine Grand National Championship titles, dominating the Indian Scout FTR750 era from 2017 onward, and Joe Kopp (2000 champion) was one of the fifteen riders to complete the "Dirt Track Grand Slam" of winning the Mile, Half-Mile, Short Track and TT in one season (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport has deep roots in Europe as well: speedway's cousin, longtrack, and the FIM Flat Track World Championship has been staged in Europe since the late 20th century (FIM, n.d.). Women's flat track has grown since the 2010s with dedicated women's classes in the USA and Europe (AMA, n.d.-b).
