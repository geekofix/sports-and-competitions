## Basic Information

Tent pegging is the cavalry sport in which a mounted rider at the gallop strikes or carries away a small ground target (historically a tent peg) with a sword or lance: one of the ten equestrian disciplines officially recognized by the international federation. Practiced from Australia and Britain to India, Pakistan, Oman and South Africa, it preserves the techniques of the cavalry charge in a judged arena competition.

## Rules

In the standard individual event, the rider gallops down a lane and uses a sword or lance to strike, cut or carry away a series of pegs (flat wooden or cardboard targets) set in the ground: carrying the peg the full length of the run scores highest, striking and dislodging it scores lower, and misses score zero. Competition rules specify target size and composition, the number of consecutive targets on the course, weapon dimensions and weight, minimum time to cover the course and the extent to which each target must be struck or carried. The broader family of equestrian skill-at-arms recognized with the discipline adds ring jousting, lemon sticking and quintain tilting to the programme, and national bodies (plus the international federation since its 2004 recognition) sanction world championships.

## History

Tent pegging is attested from antiquity: mounted riders performed similar skill drills in the ancient Eurasian empires, though the sport's classic name and story are tied to cavalry training and semi-historical accounts of raiding parties collapsing enemy tents by uprooting their pegs. The international federation's historical account also credits medieval Indian battlefields, where cavalry are said to have targeted the sensitive feet of war elephants with spears, and colonial military-mounted competitions in India carried the game into the modern era. It was included as an official sport by the Olympic Council of Asia in 1982 and was recognized as an official equestrian discipline in 2004, with today's world community strongest in Asia, the Middle East, Australia and South Africa and a world championship calendar run by the international and national tent pegging associations.
