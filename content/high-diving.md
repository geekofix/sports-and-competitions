## Basic Information

High diving is the extreme discipline in which athletes dive from platforms 20 m (women) and 27 m (men) high into open water, judged on the difficulty, execution and water entry of each dive; it is governed internationally by World Aquatics, has been part of the World Aquatics World Championships since 2013, and is also contested at the FINA High Diving World Cup (since 2014) and the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series (since 2006) (World Aquatics, n.d.-a; Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 2026 World Aquatics Championships in Singapore, high diving was again on the program from the 27 m and 20 m platforms, with descent speeds of up to roughly 96 km/h and scuba rescue teams standing by at every event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among today's leading athletes are Australia's Rhiannan Iffland, the dominant women's cliff diver of the Red Bull series, and the reigning World Aquatics high diving world champions crowned in Singapore 2026; the first women's world champion was the USA's Cesilie Carlton in 2013 (Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

At World Aquatics Championships, men dive from a 27 m platform and women from a 20 m platform; at other international high diving events the platforms range from 22 to 27 m for men and 18 to 23 m for women, and the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series uses 27 m (men) and 20 m (women), whether from purpose-built platforms or natural cliffs (World Aquatics, n.d.-a; Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.). Each competitor performs a set number of dives — typically three to four per competition as fixed in the event regulations — and five judges award execution points from 0 to 10 in half-point steps; the execution score is multiplied by the dive's degree of difficulty (DD, up to about 5.0 for the hardest twisting and somersaulting dives), and the highest total wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Divers enter the water feet-first or head-first depending on the dive, and the entry is judged on verticality and splash; the water below the platform must be several meters deep (at least about 5–6 m) with a certified scuba rescue team on site, and competitors must meet the minimum age and medical requirements of the World Aquatics High Diving Rules (World Aquatics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate events but under identical judging and safety rules, each on their own platform height; junior and age-group events use lower platforms (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## History

Cliff diving has roots in the Hawaiian tradition of *lele kawa* — diving into water from high cliffs — which King Kahekili II of Maui is said to have demonstrated around 1770 to prove his courage, and in the famous La Quebrada cliff divers of Acapulco, Mexico, who have leapt from 27–35 m cliffs into a narrow sea inlet since the 1930s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series was launched in 2006 and became the premier 27 m/20 m competition, and in 2013 FINA (now World Aquatics) added high diving to the World Championships in Barcelona — the first world champions were Colombia's Orlando Duque (590.20 points from 27 m) and the USA's Cesilie Carlton (211.60 points from 20 m), with the first FINA High Diving World Cup following in Kazan in 2014 (Duque again; the USA's Rachelle Simpson won the women's title) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). The sport's icons include Orlando Duque (born 1974 in Cali), five-time world champion and the first man to win the Barcelona title, and Britain's Gary Hunt (born 1984), a seven-time Red Bull Cliff Diving world champion (Red Bull Cliff Diving, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
