## Basic Information

High diving is the extreme diving discipline in which athletes dive from platforms 20 m (women) and 27 m (men) high into water, judged on difficulty, execution and entry, and run as the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series (27 m/20 m) and as a World Aquatics World Championships event since 2013. See [Diving](diving.html) for pool diving (springboard/platform up to 10 m).

## Rules

At World Aquatics high diving events, divers perform 3-4 dives from the 27 m (men) / 20 m (women) platform; each dive has a degree of difficulty (DD up to about 5.0 for the hardest twists/flips), and judges award execution points; the total decides the ranking. Divers enter the water feet-first or head-first depending on the dive; entry is judged on splash. At Red Bull Cliff Diving, men dive from 27 m and women from 20 m, with 3-4 dives scored by five judges on execution and difficulty of the dives, as in platform diving. Safety rules: minimum water depth (about 5-6 m), medical staff on site, and divers must be certified; the entry speed reaches about 85 km/h from 27 m.

## History

Cliff diving has roots in centuries-old Hawaiian cliff diving traditions, and the modern sport began with competitions in Acapulco, Mexico, in the 1930s-40s (La Quebrada divers). Red Bull launched the Cliff Diving World Series in 2006, and World Aquatics (FINA) added high diving to the World Championships in 2013 in Barcelona (27 m men, 20 m women). Stars include Orlando Duque (Colombia, first men's world champion 2013), Gary Hunt (Great Britain, multiple world champion), and Australia's Rhiannan Iffland, a multiple-time women's world champion.
