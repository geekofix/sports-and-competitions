## Basic Information

Golf is the individual or team sport of striking a small ball with a club from a tee into a hole on a course of 18 holes, aiming for the lowest number of strokes; the modern game was codified in 15th-century Scotland, and the rules are maintained worldwide by The R&A (St Andrews) and the USGA, whose *Rules of Golf* were first published in 1744 by the Honourable Company of Edinburgh Golfers and are revised every four years — the next edition takes effect on 1 January 2027 (The R&A, n.d.-a; USGA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The four men's major championships in 2026 were won by Rory McIlroy (Masters, Augusta National, April 9–12 — his second consecutive Green Jacket), Aaron Rai (PGA Championship, Aronimink Golf Club — the first English winner since 1919), Wyndham Clark (U.S. Open, Shinnecock Hills, June 18–21, wire-to-wire at 4 under, 276) and Ryan Fox (The 154th Open Championship, Royal Birkdale, July 16–19 — 270, −10, by one stroke over Cameron Young, birdie on the 72nd hole; the third New Zealander to win a major after Bob Charles and Michael Campbell) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; The R&A, n.d.-b). In the women's game, the 2026 majors went to Haeran Ryu (Amundi Evian Championship, July 9–12) and Shiho Kuwaki (AIG Women's Open, Royal Lytham & St Annes, July 30 – August 2) (Sky Sports, n.d.). The world rankings are the Official World Golf Ranking (OWGR) for men and the Women's World Golf Rankings (Rolex Rankings) for women (OWGR, n.d.) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are the top of the Rolex Women's World Golf Rankings — Nelly Korda (USA) and Lydia Ko (New Zealand) among the leaders, with 2026 major winners Haeran Ryu and Shiho Kuwaki — while on the men's side Scottie Scheffler (USA) leads the OWGR ahead of Rory McIlroy and the 2026 major champions (OWGR, n.d.; Rolex Rankings, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the Rules of Golf (R&A/USGA), a round consists of 18 holes (or 9), each beginning at a teeing ground and ending when the ball is holed; the player farthest from the hole plays first, and a ball must be played as it lies, with relief available under the rules — penalty strokes for lost balls or out-of-bounds (stroke-and-distance or local rules), free relief for abnormal course conditions, unplayable balls (stroke-and-distance, back-on-the-line, or two-club-length lateral relief for an unplayable ball) (The R&A, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The maximum score per hole in stroke play may be set by the committee (commonly double par plus penalty strokes or a fixed maximum such as 10 under the 2019 revision); a player may carry up to 14 clubs; caddies may not test the condition of a green or move a ball on the green before 2019 — since 2019 flagsticks may be left in, and since 2019 grounding the club in a bunker is allowed away from the ball and raking is unrestricted (The R&A, n.d.-a). Match play (hole-by-hole, won by the player leading after 18) and stableford (points per hole against par) are alternative formats (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play from different tee markers but the same rules; the handicap systems (World Handicap System, effective 2020/2024) apply equally to both sexes, and amateur status rules were replaced by maximum-handicap and eligibility rules in 2024 (The R&A, n.d.-a; USGA, n.d.-a).

## History

Golf's origins are debated — the Dutch game *kolf* and the Scottish game of *gowf* both claim ancestry — but the modern rules were codified in Scotland: the first written rules were published in 1744 by the Honourable Company of Edinburgh Golfers at Leith, the first golf club, the Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews, was founded in 1754 (receiving its royal charter in 1897 and becoming The R&A), and the first Open Championship was played at Prestwick in 1860, won by Willie Park Sr. (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; The R&A, n.d.-b). The game spread worldwide in the 19th century — the USGA was founded in 1894 and the first U.S. Open was played in 1895 at Newport Country Club, won by Horace Rawlins — and the 20th century produced icons such as Bobby Jones (the 1930 Grand Slam), Ben Hogan, Sam Snead (record 82 PGA Tour wins), Arnold Palmer, Jack Nicklaus (record 18 majors) and Tiger Woods (15 majors, 2000–2019) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, the LPGA was founded in 1950 by 13 players, and icons include Babe Zaharias, Mickey Wright, Annika Sörenstam and today's Nelly Korda and Lydia Ko (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Golf returned to the Olympics at Rio 2016 after 112 years (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
