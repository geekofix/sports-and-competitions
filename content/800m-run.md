## Basic Information

The 800 metres — two laps of the track from a staggered start, breaking to the inside lane after the first bend — is the classic middle-distance race, combining sprint speed with endurance. It is governed by World Athletics and has been on the Olympic programme since 1896 (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 1:40.91 for men, set by David Rudisha of Kenya at the London 2012 Olympics, and 1:53.28 for women, set by Jarmila Kratochvílová of Czechoslovakia in 1983 — the oldest standing world record in track athletics (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by Olympic champion Keely Hodgkinson of Great Britain (pictured), while the men's race was headed by Olympic champion Emmanuel Wanyonyi of Kenya (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

## Rules

The 800 m starts from staggered lane positions with a crouch start in blocks, but after the first bend — marked by a break line — athletes may move to the inside lane; the race is then run as a pack. Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

The 800 m has been contested at every modern Olympics since 1896 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Sebastian Coe of Great Britain won back-to-back Olympic golds in 1980 and 1984 with a then world record of 1:41.73, before David Rudisha (pictured) produced the legendary front-running 1:40.91 at London 2012, still the world record (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). On the women's side, Jarmila Kratochvílová's 1:53.28 from Munich 1983 is the oldest world record in the sport, and Pamela Jelimo of Kenya ran 1:54.01 in 2008 as a teenager (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
