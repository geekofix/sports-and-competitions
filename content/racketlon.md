## Basic Information

Racketlon is a combination sport in which the same two players (or doubles pairs) compete against each other in all four major racket sports — [table tennis](table-tennis.html), [badminton](badminton.html), [squash](squash.html) and [tennis](tennis.html) — one set in each sport, with every rally counting toward the total score. It is the racket-sport equivalent of triathlon and decathlon and is governed internationally by the FIR — Federation of International Racketlon (formerly the International Racketlon Federation), which counts more than 30 national member federations.

## Rules

Under the official FIR Rules of Racketlon, a match consists of four sets played in the order table tennis, badminton, squash, tennis — from the smallest to the biggest racket. Each set is played with running score to 21 points with a two-point margin needed, and the winner of the match is the player with the most points in total across all four sports, which makes it possible to win a match while losing three of the four sets. Serves alternate two at a time, one from the right and one from the left, with a single coin toss at the start fixing the serve-and-receive order across all four sets: the player who serves in table tennis receives in badminton, serves in squash and receives in tennis. If the total score is tied after all four sets, a single "gummiarm" (rubber arm) point is played in tennis, with only one serve, and the winner of that rally wins the entire match. The individual rules of the four sports otherwise apply, with feather shuttles, double-yellow-dot squash balls and ITF-approved tennis balls required at FIR events.

## History

Racketlon grew out of experiments in Finland and Sweden, where "four-racket" tournaments — under names such as mailapelit and, from 1989 in Sweden, Peter Landberg's national tournament that coined the word racketlon — settled the format by 1994 into the current four sets of 21 points. The first international tournament was held in Gothenburg in 2001 as an unofficial world championship between Finland and Sweden. The international federation was founded in 2002, renamed FIR, and since 2003 has run a worldwide circuit with world championships for singles (including elite, seniors and junior classes), doubles and national teams. Austria, Sweden, Finland and the Czech Republic have dominated the medal tables, led by Austria's Christine Seehofer, the most decorated player in the sport's history — a multiple world champion in singles, doubles and mixed doubles — and Denmark's Jesper Ratzer, unbeaten in well over a hundred elite singles matches through the 2010s.
