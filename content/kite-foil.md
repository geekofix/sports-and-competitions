## Basic Information

Kite foiling (competitively the Formula Kite class) is a hydrofoil kiteboarding discipline in which a foil kite pulls a rider on a board mounted on a hydrofoil that lifts the hull above the water (Formula Kite, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The class is managed by the International Kiteboarding Association (IKA) under World Sailing and uses registered production equipment rather than one-design gear (Formula Kite, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Formula Kite was the equipment for the kiteboarding events at the Paris 2024 Olympics in Marseille, where Ellie Aldridge of Great Britain won women's gold on 8 August 2024 and Valentin Bontus of Austria took men's gold, with Toni Vodišek of Slovenia silver and Max Maeder of Singapore bronze (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the top of the women's class today stands Lauriane Nolot of France — world champion 2023 and again at the 2026 Formula Kite Worlds (9–16 May 2026 at Praia do Cabedelo, Viana do Castelo, Portugal) and 2026 European champion — ahead of rivals such as Alina Kornelli, while Max Maeder of Singapore won the men's 2026 world title after his 2023 and 2024 crowns (Formula Kite, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The men's and women's kite events are confirmed for Los Angeles 2028 at Belmont Shore, Long Beach, with the first medals on 19 July 2028 (World Sailing, n.d.).

## Rules

The Formula Kite class rules are set by World Sailing and administered by the IKA: kites and boards must be registered production equipment from approved builders, and the class is open to men and women in separate events (Formula Kite, n.d.; World Sailing, n.d.). Olympic and world championship racing uses a course-racing format — a series of short upwind/downwind races of about 10–15 minutes each, with the top riders after the qualifying series advancing to a medal series, and low-points scoring with discards deciding the final rankings (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Riders launch from the beach, water-start the kite, and foil around marks at speeds well above 30 knots; capsizing or breaching the course rules costs positions (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Safety equipment includes a helmet and impact vest, and riders use a quick-release harness (World Sailing, n.d.). Age classes exist for youth events, and men's and women's fleets race under identical equipment and course rules, separately at the Olympics since Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Rule source: World Sailing / IKA Formula Kite, https://www.formulakite.org/ (Formula Kite, n.d.).

## History

Hydrofoil boards entered kiteboarding around 2009, and the IKA developed the Formula Kite class — foil kite plus hydrofoil board — as a high-performance course-racing class, with the first world championships in the early 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In 2019 the World Sailing Council selected Formula Kite as the equipment for the kiteboarding events at Paris 2024 (World Sailing, n.d.). At the Paris Olympics in Marseille in August 2024, Valentin Bontus won the men's event and Ellie Aldridge the women's (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The world title roll of honor includes Maxime Nocher, Nicolas Parlier, Max Maeder (2023 The Hague, 2024 Hyères) and Riccardo Pianosi (2025 Cagliari) on the men's side, and Lauriane Nolot (2023 The Hague) on the women's side (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Formula Kite remains the Olympic class for Los Angeles 2028 (World Sailing, n.d.). Icons of the class are Max Maeder, Valentin Bontus, Toni Vodišek, Lauriane Nolot and Ellie Aldridge (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
