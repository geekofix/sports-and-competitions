## Basic Information

Double trap is the ISSF's pairs shotgun event: from five stations in a straight line, a trench in front of the shooter holds three trap machines, and on the call two clay targets are thrown simultaneously at random angles — a “double” — and the shooter may fire up to two shots, one at each target (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed by the International Shooting Sport Federation, is part of the World Championship and Shotgun World Cup programmes, but was removed from the Olympic programme before Tokyo 2020 — the men's event was last Olympic at Rio 2016, won by Kuwait's Fehaid Al-Deehani competing as an IOC athlete (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

The 2026 ISSF season includes Shotgun World Cups in Tangier (25 March – 3 April), Almaty (2–111 May) and Lonato (3–13 July), and the World Championship in Doha (1–14 November 2026), where shotgun titles are decided at the Lusail Shooting Complex (ISSF, n.d.-a, n.d.-d). At the 2018 World Championships in Changwon, India's Ankur Mittal won the men's double trap gold — the last world title before Doha 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-d). Among current leading shooters, the women lead in the World Championship field; the discipline's elite is shared with the Olympic shotgun stars of trap and skeet, with Kuwait and India traditionally strong in the men's event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the ISSF shotgun rules, double trap qualification is 150 targets — three rounds of 50 targets (25 doubles per round) — shot in squads of six rotating through the five stations, with each shooter firing one shot at each of the two targets of every double (ISSF, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The top six advance to the semifinal (30 targets / 15 doubles), and the medal matches are shot on stations 2–4 for 15 targets; ties are broken by shoot-offs alternating regular and reverse doubles (ISSF, n.d.-c).

Equipment is a 12-gauge over-under shotgun meeting the ISSF shotgun equipment rules, with ISSF cartridge limits (lead shot size and payload); eye and ear protection are mandatory, and a VAR system supports the referees under the 2026 rulebook (ISSF, n.d.-c; ISSF, 2026). Men and women compete in separate classifications at World Championships and World Cups; the women's event was Olympic from Atlanta 1996 to Athens 2004 — America's Kim Rhode won the first Olympic women's double trap title in 1996 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-c).

## History

Trap shooting with live pigeons dates to the 18th century, and clay targets were introduced in the late 1800s; double trap was introduced at the 1989 World Shotgun Championships in Montecatini Terme, Italy, and joined the full World Championship programme in 1994 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). It was Olympic for men from Atlanta 1996 to Rio 2016 and for women from 1996 to 2004.

America's Walton Eller, pictured in the 2008 Olympic final, won double trap gold at Beijing 2008; other icons include America's Kim Rhode — Olympic double trap champion 1996 and six-time Olympic medallist in the shotgun events — Kuwait's Fehaid Al-Deehani (Rio 2016 gold) and India's Ankur Mittal, 2018 world champion and former world No. 1 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c, n.d.-d).
