## Basic Information

Three-cushion billiards is the premier carom (carambole) discipline: played on a pocketless table with three balls, the cue ball must contact at least three cushions before hitting the second object ball to score a point, which makes it a game of precision, position play and long series (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the Union Mondiale de Billard (UMB), which runs the World Three-cushion Championship (since 1928), the Women's World Championship (since 1999), the team world championship and — together with the continental confederations — the Three-Cushion World Cup (Union Mondiale de Billard, n.d.). Among the leading women players today are multiple women's world champion Therese Klompenhouwer of the Netherlands and the current title holders of the women's world championship, contested separately since 1999, while on the men's side Dick Jaspers of the Netherlands, a many-time world champion, heads the field (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

The match table has a playing surface of 2.84 × 1.42 m (10 ft × 5 ft) with 3.5 cm-high cushions and no pockets, and the three balls (white cue ball, yellow, red object ball) are 61.5 mm in diameter (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A point is scored when the cue ball, after being struck, hits both object balls and has touched at least three cushions before contacting the second object ball; a shot that fails this scores nothing and play passes to the opponent, and miscue, pushing the cue ball or touching a ball with hand or clothing are fouls (Union Mondiale de Billard, n.d.). World Championship matches are played to 40 points in a set format — historically single frames to 50 points (1928–1958 and 1985–1987) or 60 points (1960–1984) — while World Cup legs use shorter distances, and players alternate breaks after each point; averages (points per inning) decide close finishes (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The break is decided by lagging, and timeouts are not permitted at the table (Union Mondiale de Billard, n.d.). Men and women play under identical rules in separate world championships — the women's event has been held since 1999 — and junior and team (national pairs, long held in Viersen, Germany) titles exist alongside the individual events (Union Mondiale de Billard, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Carom billiards developed from 18th- and 19th-century French and English table games; straight rail gave way to balkline and finally to three-cushion, the most demanding discipline, in the late 19th century (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The international federation lineage began with the UIFAB, founded in 1923, and the Union Mondiale de Billard (UMB) was formally founded on 1 June 1959 in Madrid; the first world three-cushion championship was held in Reims, France, in 1928 and won by the Egyptian Edmond Soussa (1898–1989), who collected eleven world titles across carom disciplines (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Record icons include Raymond Ceulemans of Belgium (b. 1937), with a record 61 world championship titles in carom disciplines, and Sweden's Torbjörn Blomdahl (b. 1962), an eight-time three-cushion world champion who retired from the elite game (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Three-Cushion World Cup was founded in 1986 by German patron Werner Bayer and became the sport's showcase series; after a dispute the 1992 and 1993 world titles were later declared unofficial, and the UMB today co-founded the World Confederation of Billiards Sports (WCBS) together with the pool and snooker bodies (Union Mondiale de Billard, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
