## Basic Information

Cricket is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams of eleven players on a large oval field; the batting side tries to score runs off the bowling side, which tries to dismiss the batters ("get them out"), and the team with the most runs wins (International Cricket Council [ICC], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Cricket Council (ICC), founded in 1909 as the Imperial Cricket Conference and today uniting more than 100 member nations; the playing rules are the Laws of Cricket, owned and maintained by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) in London, the game's spiritual home since Lord's was established as its headquarters in 1787 (MCC, n.d.; ICC, n.d.-a). Cricket is played internationally in three formats — Tests (up to five days), One Day Internationals (50 overs per side) and Twenty20 Internationals (20 overs per side), covered in detail on [Cricket ODI](cricket-odi.html) and [Cricket T20](cricket-t20.html) on this site (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 season is defined by the ICC Men's T20 World Cup, co-hosted by India and Sri Lanka from 7 February to 8 March 2026 — 20 teams, 55 matches, eight venues — whose final on 8 March at the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad ended with India beating New Zealand by 96 runs (255/5 to 159), India's third T20 world title and the first successful defense of the trophy by a host nation, with Sanju Samson top-scoring 89 and Jasprit Bumrah taking 4/15 (ICC, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). In women's cricket, Australia won their record seventh Women's T20 World Cup at Lord's on 5 July 2026 under captain Sophie Molineux, chasing down England, while India claimed their maiden Women's ODI World Cup on 2 November 2025 in Navi Mumbai, beating South Africa by 52 runs with Shafali Verma's 87 player-of-the-match and Deepti Sharma player of the tournament (ICC, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The World Test Championship 2025–27 runs to a final at The Oval by 12 June 2027 (ICC, n.d.-b). Cricket is played at the top level in separate men's and women's internationals under identical Laws; women's cricket has grown into a fully professional sport with central contracts in India, England, Australia and New Zealand, and para-cricket (blind and visually impaired, deaf, intellectual disability, amputee and wheelchair categories) is governed by the ICC alongside the mainstream game (ICC, n.d.-a). At the end of 2026, the leading current players are — in women's cricket — the 2026 and 2025 world champion names Sophie Molineux (Australia, T20), Shafali Verma, Deepti Sharma and Smriti Mandhana (India, ODI), and in men's cricket the 2026 T20 world champions Sanju Samson and Jasprit Bumrah of India, alongside stars such as Pat Cummins and Travis Head of Australia (ICC, n.d.-b) (International Cricket Council, n.d.-a) (International Cricket Council, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Cricket is played under the Laws of Cricket, the code maintained by the MCC and applied worldwide by the ICC; the Laws define the equipment, the field, the conduct of play and the manners of dismissal (MCC, n.d.). The field is an oval with a rectangular 22-yard (20.12 m) pitch in the center; at each end stand three wooden stumps ("wickets") topped by two bails (MCC, n.d.). The ball is a hard leather sphere, 22.4–22.9 cm in circumference and 155.9–163 g in weight in men's cricket, slightly lighter and smaller in women's cricket; the bat may be at most 96.5 cm (38 in) long and 10.8 cm (4.25 in) wide (MCC, n.d.). Each team fields eleven players — a bowler, a wicketkeeper and nine fielders — with no more than two fielders (other than the wicketkeeper) behind the popping crease on the leg side, a restriction that in limited-overs cricket becomes a powerplay fielding limit (ICC, n.d.-a). An innings consists of overs: a bowler delivers six legal balls (a "legal" delivery must not be a no-ball or a wide), then another bowler bowls the next over from the other end; in Tests an innings ends when ten batters are dismissed or the captain declares, in ODIs after 50 overs per side and in T20s after 20 (ICC, n.d.-a). Batters score runs by hitting the ball and running between the wickets, or 4 runs if the ball reaches the boundary and 6 if it clears it on the full (MCC, n.d.). A batter is out in eleven ways defined by the Laws — bowled, caught, leg before wicket (LBW), run out, stumped, hit wicket, handled the ball, obstructing the field, hit the ball twice and timed out (MCC, n.d.). The Laws also govern the spirit of cricket, umpiring (two on-field umpires, a third umpire, the Decision Review System with ball-tracking), and playing conditions for each format; gender and age classifications are organizational, not legal: men's and women's matches use the same Laws with slightly different ball specifications, and U19, senior and para-cricket categories run their own competitions (ICC, n.d.-a; MCC, n.d.). The ICC publishes official team and player rankings for all three formats (ICC, n.d.-b).

## History

Cricket's origins lie in 16th-century England, with the earliest certain reference to a game called "creckett" in Guildford, Surrey, in 1597; the game spread through the 18th century, centered on the Hambledon Club in Hampshire, and the Marylebone Cricket Club — founded in 1787 and based at Lord's from 1814 — codified the first Laws of Cricket in 1744 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first Test match was played in 1877 at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, won by Australia over England by 45 runs; the famous Ashes series began in 1882 after England's defeat at The Oval, when a satirical obituary declared English cricket dead and "the body will be cremated and the ashes taken to Australia". The first ODI was played in 1971 at Melbourne — a rain-shortened eight-over-a-side match — and the first men's T20 World Cup in 2007 in South Africa, won by India over Pakistan in the final at Johannesburg (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The Cricket World Cup, first held in 1975 in England, has been won by the West Indies (1975, 1979), Pakistan (1992), Sri Lanka co-hosts (1996), Australia a record six times (1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2015, 2023), India (1983, 2011) and England (2019); the 2023 final at the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad — the world's largest cricket ground, pictured below — saw Australia beat India by six wickets with Travis Head's 137 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The icons of the game include W. G. Grace (1848–1915), the Victorian master who played 44 Tests; Sir Donald Bradman (1908–2001), whose Test batting average of 99.94 remains the most famous statistic in sport; Sachin Tendulkar (b. 1973), who scored 100 international centuries; and the great bowlers Shane Warne (1969–2022) and Muttiah Muralitharan (b. 1972), with 800 Test wickets (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's cricket has its own pantheon — Belinda Clark of Australia, the first woman to score a double century in ODIs; England's Charlotte Edwards; India's Mithali Raj; and Australia's Ellyse Perry, the only player to reach 1,000 runs and 500 wickets in ODIs (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
