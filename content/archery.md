## Basic Information

Archery is the sport of shooting arrows with a bow at a target. It is governed worldwide by World Archery, founded in 1931, which runs the World Archery Championships and the Hyundai Archery World Cup (World Archery [WA], n.d.-b). Archery has been on the Olympic program since 1972 — after appearances in 1900, 1904, 1908 and 1920 — and a mixed team compound event will debut at Los Angeles 2028. At Paris 2024, South Korea dominated: Lim Si-hyeon won three gold medals — women's individual, women's team and mixed team — making her only the second archer after compatriot An San (Tokyo 2020) to win three golds at a single Games, while Kim Woo-jin took the men's individual title and South Korea swept both team events; Mexico's Andrea Becerra won the women's compound individual title (WA, n.d.-c; Olympics.com, n.d.) (World Archery, n.d.-a) (World Archery, n.d.-b) (World Archery, n.d.-c) (World Archery, n.d.-d).

Among today's leading women, South Korea's Lim Si-hyeon — a three-time Olympic champion — tops the list, and India's Deepika Kumari became the first Indian archer to reach world No. 1 in 2012 and regained the top ranking in 2021 (WA, n.d.-d). The 2026 Hyundai Archery World Cup (its 20th edition) ran from April 7 to September 13, 2026, with stages in Puebla, Shanghai and Antalya and the Final in Saltillo; the 2026 World Archery Field Championships took place in Yankton, USA, September 22–27, 2026, and the 2027 World Championships will be held in Medellín, Colombia, August 16–22, 2027 (WA, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In Olympic recurve archery, individuals shoot from 70 m at a 122 cm target face with ten scoring rings plus the inner-10 "X". The ranking round consists of 72 arrows, after which archers advance to head-to-head match play: best of five sets, three arrows per set, shot alternately with 20 seconds per arrow; a tied match goes to a single-arrow shoot-off. Compound archery is shot from 50 m at an 80 cm face with a six-ring 10, using the same set system. Team events consist of three archers shooting 24 arrows under a time limit, and mixed teams pair one woman and one man. Field archery shoots at 3D targets over unmarked distances. World Archery's Book of Rules governs equipment — bow poundage, arrow length, release aids — and conduct (WA, n.d.-a).

## History

Bow and arrow hunting and warfare date back tens of thousands of years; organized target archery revived in 19th-century Britain with the Grand National Archery Society (1844), and the international federation FITA — now World Archery — was founded in 1931 in Lwów, Poland, holding its first World Championships the same year. Archery appeared at the Olympics in 1900, 1904, 1908 and 1920, then returned in 1972 and has remained permanent since. Icons of the modern era include the USA's Brady Ellison, a three-time Olympian and field world champion, Belgium's Hubert Van Innis, who won 59 medals at the 1900 and 1920 Games, and New Zealand's Neroli Fairhall, who competed at the 1972 Paralympics with a jaw brace. On the women's side, South Korea's dominance — An San's three golds at Tokyo 2020 and Lim Si-hyeon's three at Paris 2024 — defines the modern era (WA, n.d.-b; Olympics.com, n.d.).
