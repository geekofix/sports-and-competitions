## Basic Information

Archery is the precision sport of shooting arrows with a bow at a target. The international governing body is World Archery; recurve archery has been an Olympic sport since 1972 (reintroduced; first 1900-1920), with compound added at the Olympic program discussions and featured in the Youth Olympics. World Archery runs the World Championships and World Cup.

## Rules

At the Olympics, recurve archers shoot 72 arrows in a ranking round at 70 m on a 122 cm target with 10 scoring rings (the inner 10 is the X). The top archers then compete in head-to-head knockout matches: best of five sets, three arrows per set (20 seconds per arrow, shooting alternately); the archer with more set points wins, with a single-arrow shoot-off to break ties. Team and mixed team events are also contested. Variants: compound bows (with sights and mechanical releases) use the same target format at World Archery events; indoor archery is shot at 18 m on a 40 cm face; field and 3D archery use outdoor courses.

## History

Archery is one of the oldest sports, used for hunting and warfare since prehistoric times; it was an event at the first modern Olympics in 1900. It was dropped after 1920 and returned in 1972 in Munich. Legendary archers include Hubert Van Innis (BEL), who won six golds around 1900-1920, and modern stars like Brady Ellison (USA) and An San (KOR).
