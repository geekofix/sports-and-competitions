## Basic Information

Walking rugby is the low-impact, non-contact form of rugby union created at Reading Rugby Club in England in late October 2015, in response to local enquiries for a game like walking football: players walk instead of run, and the tackle is replaced by a two-handed touch on the ball carrier's shorts, so mixed teams of women and men of any age and experience can play a competitive passing game (Reading RFC, n.d.; Rugby Football Union, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The format follows RFU guidance published from January 2017 and is now played at more than 200 clubs across the United Kingdom, with county unions running standardised sessions (Reading RFC, n.d.).

## Rules

The core RFU rules: no running or jogging at any time — walking is judged by one foot remaining in contact with the ground when in motion — and no contact beyond a simultaneous two-handed touch on the ball carrier, from shoulders down to shorts including arms, which counts as the tackle; after a touch the defender steps back and the attacker must pass within one pace (Rugby Football Union, n.d.). Passes must be backwards or lateral, offside rules apply, and a try is scored by walking the ball over the try line — no bending or diving; there are no mauls, rucks or lineouts, and scrums are limited to a maximum of three players per side, standing upright and non-contested (or two-man scrums in smaller games) (Rugby Football Union, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Teams of up to seven a side play on a reduced pitch, ball out of touch restarts with a free pass from the touchline, and knock-ons or forward passes restart with a tap-and-pass or small scrum; women and men play together in mixed teams as standard (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Walking rugby was started at Reading RFC in late October 2015 — the club's fixtures secretary Mike Wickson is credited with the programme after local players asked whether a walking version of the game existed — and it grew from weekly sessions at the club into a nationwide format with more than 200 clubs taking part (Wikipedia, n.d.; Reading RFC, n.d.). The RFU and county unions such as Berkshire and Surrey published standard guidance from January 2017, cementing the two-handed-touch rule, the walking definition and the small-scrum format, and the game is now offered from cradle to grave alongside full-contact rugby at English clubs, aimed at ex-players, people returning from injury and complete beginners of retirement age (Rugby Football Union, n.d.).
