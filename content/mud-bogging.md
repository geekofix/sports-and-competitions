## Basic Information

Mud bogging is an American off-road motorsport in which modified trucks, jeeps and buggies race through a deep mud pit or a muddy course, straightest and fastest line wins — or last vehicle running in circle events (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the off-road family with rock crawling and monster trucks.

![Truck mud bogging at full spray](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Mud_bogging.jpg?width=420)

*Figure: Heightsalum at English Wikipedia. (30 March 2008 (original upload date)). Truck mud bogging at full spray [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Mud_bogging.jpg*

Events run as straight-line drags through a water-filled trench (typically 50–100 m of deep mud) or as oval mud circuits with jumps and whoops; classes range from stock 4x4s to unlimited tube-frame "boggers" with huge paddle tyres (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Safety rules: roll cage, harness, helmet, and fire-resistant clothing; vehicles must have a tow point and a kill switch; no passengers (Wikipedia, n.d.). Most events are sanctioned by local clubs and insurers under rules similar to monster-truck shows. Men and women compete in separate and mixed classes.

## History

Mud bogging began in the 1960s–70s at US fairgrounds and farm fields after logging roads turned to mud; it became a staple of county fairs and truck shows, with big events like the "Bog" at ATPA-sanctioned shows (Wikipedia, n.d.). TV coverage in the 2000s spread it nationally.
