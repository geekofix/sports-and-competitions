## Basic Information

Lacrosse sixes (World Lacrosse Sixes) is a compact, fast-paced version of lacrosse played outdoors with six players per side including the goalkeeper, created by World Lacrosse to lower the barriers to entry — cost, field size, roster size — and to fit a potential Olympic framework; the first official playing rules were approved on 15 November 2020, the format was formally announced on 18 May 2021, and the IOC approved lacrosse sixes for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics on 16 October 2023 — the first Olympic medal event for lacrosse since 1908 (World Lacrosse, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.; Olympics, n.d.). Sixes will make its Olympic debut at LA28 with six men's and six women's teams, and the first World Lacrosse Sixes Championships are scheduled for 4–10 October 2027 in Colorado Springs, with the top four per gender plus the host qualifying for LA28 (LA28, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

Women and men compete in parallel tournaments under identical rules: the 2026 continental qualifiers — European Championships in Salou, Pan-American in Oshawa, Asia-Pacific on the Sunshine Coast and African in Johannesburg — were held for both genders, and the 2025 World Games in Chengdu featured a women's sixes tournament, where China finished eighth (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Lacrosse, n.d.).

## Rules

The field is a rectangle of 70 × 36 m, and each team has six players on the field — five runners and one goalkeeper; games last four 8-minute quarters with a 2-minute break after the first and third quarters and a 5-minute half-time, and there is a 30-second shot clock once a team gains possession (World Lacrosse, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Play restarts with a centre draw at the start of each quarter, and after every goal the goalkeeper puts the ball back in play — no face-offs after goals; everyone plays on both halves of the field, long defensive poles are not permitted, and body checking in any form is prohibited for women and men alike (World Lacrosse, n.d.). Scoring is one point per goal, with sudden-victory overtime in 3-minute periods if tied; three major fouls and a player is out; men wear helmet, facemask, chinstrap and gloves, women play under the women's stick and equipment rules, but the field, team size and game structure are identical for both genders (World Lacrosse, n.d.).


## History

World Lacrosse's General Assembly began developing sixes in 2018 through the Blue Skies Working Group chaired by vice-president Steve Stenersen; the rules were approved on 15 November 2020 and the format announced on 18 May 2021 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Its first major international showcase was the 2022 World Games in Birmingham, Alabama, where the United States won men's gold and Japan took bronze; the 2025 World Games in Chengdu added a women's tournament (China eighth), and the IOC's approval on 16 October 2023 put sixes on the LA28 programme with six teams per gender (Wikipedia, n.d.; Olympics, n.d.). Continental qualification for 2027 and LA28 ran through 2026 — Europe in Salou (Israel and Great Britain won the women's and men's titles), Pan-America in Oshawa (United States won both), Asia-Pacific on the Sunshine Coast and Africa in Johannesburg — and the 2027 World Lacrosse Sixes Championships in Colorado Springs, 4–10 October 2027, will decide the Olympic berths (Wikipedia, n.d.; LA28, n.d.).
