## Basic Information

Lacrosse sixes (World Lacrosse Sixes) is the six-a-side, short-format version of lacrosse created by World Lacrosse in 2021 to fit within the Olympic Games framework. Described as a hybrid of field and box lacrosse, it is played outdoors on a smaller field with a fast game clock, fewer specialists and more parity between nations; it will make its Olympic debut at the Los Angeles 2028 Games in both men's and women's competitions.

## Rules

Sixes is played six-a-side (a goalkeeper and five runners) on a smaller cross-field (70 by 36 metres) with goals closer together than in the ten-a-side field game, using a 30-second shot clock so possessions are short and fast. Games consist of four eight-minute quarters, with draws restarting play after each goal and a short clock at the end of quarters; rosters are small (about ten to twelve players) and there are no long-pole defence sticks or other specialist equipment \u2014 all field players use short sticks, and the face-off/draw after goals is a sprint contest. Body contact is moderated compared with field lacrosse \u2014 the rules were designed to reduce checking and hence injury risk, and indeed to make the game low-barrier (low cost, small squads, small fields) \u2014 and penalties are served in a box with the offending team playing a man down.

## History

The sixes format was developed from 2018 by a World Lacrosse working group led by vice-president Steve Stenersen, tested informally in 2018\u20132020, given its first official playing rules by the World Lacrosse General Assembly on 15 November 2020, and formally launched as "World Lacrosse Sixes" on 18 May 2021. Its purpose was explicitly Olympic: to reduce barriers to entry (cost, field size, player numbers), remove specialist positions, and give more nations a realistic chance to compete against the traditional North American powers. The format debuted at a major multi-sport event at the 2022 World Games in Birmingham, Alabama, and in October 2023 the International Olympic Committee approved lacrosse for the Los Angeles 2028 programme in the sixes format \u2014 the first Olympic lacrosse events since the full-field game appeared at the 1904 and 1908 Games and was demonstrated in 1928, 1932 and 1948. World Lacrosse now stages sixes world championships for men and women, and the format is spreading quickly through national federations as the standard international version of lacrosse.
