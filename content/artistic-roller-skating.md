## Basic Information

Artistic roller skating is figure skating on quad or inline roller skates: athletes perform figures, free-skating programs, dance and solo dance to music in front of judges, at the World Skate Artistic Roller Skating World Championships (World Skate, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). At the 2018 Artistic World Roller Skating Championships, Russia's Anastasia Nosova won the senior women's title; the sport has separate men's and women's events in every discipline, plus pairs and groups.

## Rules

World Skate artistic rules: free skating programs 2–4 minutes by age group, judged on technical elements and program components (artistry, choreography) with deductions for falls; dance compulsory patterns plus free dance; figures on a 23 m circle pattern; equipment: quad skates (or inline for some events) with toe stops, mandatory helmets in lower categories (World Skate, n.d.).

## History

Artistic roller skating grew from 19th-century roller rink culture; the first international championships date to the 1930s–50s, and the international federation (today World Skate, which took over roller sports in 2017 from FIRS) runs world championships annually in artistic, speed and other disciplines; Germany, Italy, Spain, the USA and Argentina are the strongest nations (Wikipedia, n.d.).
