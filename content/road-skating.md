## Basic Information

Road skating is inline skating on roads: disciplines range from the 100 m and 200 m track-style sprints to 1,000 m, half marathon and marathon (42.195 km) road races, governed for the elite by World Skate (road world championships) and mass-participation series such as the World Inline Cup and city marathons (e.g., Linz, Berlin, Paris) (World Skate, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). World-class marathon times are under 1:03; both men and women compete, with separate fields.

## Rules

World Skate road rules: mass-start road races with staggered starts by category; drafting is allowed on road courses; helmet mandatory in most events; inline skates with wheels up to 110–125 mm for marathon; distances from 100 m to the marathon at world championships; medals per distance; men's and women's races, junior and senior categories (World Skate, n.d.).

## History

Inline road racing began in the 1980s with the first inline marathons; the Berlin Marathon has officially timed inline skaters since the 1970s, and World Skate has run road world championships since the 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.).
