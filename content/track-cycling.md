## Basic Information

Track cycling is bicycle racing on a velodrome: a steeply banked oval wooden track (250 to 400 metres in circumference) designed so riders can hold speed through the turns at angles impossible on the road. One of the oldest forms of cycle sport, it combines sprint disciplines (match sprint, keirin, team sprint), endurance bunch races (scratch, points, elimination, madison, omnium) and time-trial formats (individual and team pursuit) into the programme of the Olympic Games and world championships.

## Rules

Velodromes bank their turns at up to roughly 45 degrees, and racing is counterclockwise on fixed-gear brakeless track bikes, riding the blue band, black measurement line and red sprinter's line on the track's marked surface. UCI rules govern each discipline: sprints are contested over three laps with a tactical standstill cat-and-mouse phase, endurance races score intermediate sprints, laps or eliminations (defined in the individual discipline pages of this site), pursuits and team sprints are timed to the thousandth of a second, and bunch races enforce strict conduct rules (track craft, no movement down in the final 200 metres of a sprint). Olympic and world championship programmes run both sprint (sprint, keirin, team sprint) and endurance (team pursuit, madison, omnium) event families for men and women.

## History

Track racing predates the road sport: high-wheel racers were setting records on banked tracks in the late nineteenth century, and the status of six-day racing in America made track stars the first cycling celebrities; the sport was part of the first modern Olympic Games (1896) and has remained on the programme ever since. The twentieth century saw the velodrome move indoors and the disciplines specialize, with European keirin (born in Japan as a betting sport), the madison named after New York's six-day races at Madison Square Garden, and pursuit racing defining the endurance events. The modern UCI world championships and Olympic programme keep breaking records on the super-fast indoor boards, and track cycling remains the sport's purest test: no wind, no hills, no drafting luck, just wattage, tactics and nerve.
