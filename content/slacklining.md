## Basic Information

Slacklining is the sport and art of balancing and walking on a narrow, flat webbing strap tensioned between two anchors; at height over ground or water it is called highlining, and competitive disciplines include tricklining, longlining, highlining, waterlining, rodeolining and speedlining (ISA, n.d.-b). The International Slackline Association (ISA), founded in August 2015 in Bern by Swiss, Austrian, German and US associations, governs the sport, maintains world rankings via its SportCom platform, and now counts around 19 national federations; it became a UIAA observer in 2018–19, an ICAR member, a SAIR member since 2024, and was recognised by Swiss Olympic in 2025 (ISA, n.d.-a). The third Highline World Championships were held 21–26 July 2026 in Flims/Laax, Switzerland, covering freestyle and speed disciplines for men and women; the 2026 World Cup calendar included Kempes (25–26 April), Embrun (3–6 May), Munich (5 July) and Chuntiva (11–13 December) (ISA, n.d.-a). Other major events are the World Slackline Masters in Stuttgart (2019) and the Campeonato Mundial in Viña del Mar (2017). The longline world record stands at 610 m, set by Alexander Schulz in Mongolia in June 2014, and the polyester highline record at 477 m, set by Samuel Volery on 26 September 2015 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women compete in every discipline with their own world rankings and titles, and the current leading women include the top-ranked female freestyle and speed highliners on the ISA SportCom ranking (ISA, n.d.-a).

## Rules

In tricklining, performed on a 5 cm wide webbing 10–25 m long and a few metres above the ground over padded landing areas, athletes perform acrobatic tricks — jumps, spins, bounces and static balances — and a panel of judges scores difficulty, execution, composition and style (ISA, n.d.-b). Longlining is the endurance discipline of crossing very long lines (over 30–40 m) without falling, with world records ratified for polyester and dyneema webbing. Highlining is slacklining at great height, where every athlete wears a climbing harness clipped to the line with a leash and the line itself has a backup (redundant) system; freestyle highlining and speed highlining (timed crossings) are the competitive formats at the world championships (ISA, n.d.-a). Waterlining is performed low over water, rodeolining on a rodeo-style line, and speedlining is a timed race across a line. The ISA SportCom world ranking covers freestyle trickline and highline, speedline and highline, plus jib and rigging disciplines (ISA, n.d.-a). Competitions are divided into men's and women's categories in every discipline, with junior age classes at championships (ISA, n.d.-b).

## History

Slacklining originated in the early 1980s in the Yosemite Valley climbing scene in California, where climbers such as those around the Camp 4 area began walking the chains and webbing between granite boulders for fun and balance training; the first dedicated webbing lines were rigged there in the mid-1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The highlining pioneer Dean Potter, who walked famous lines such as the 900 m gap between the Cathedral Spires in Yosemite before his death in 2015, turned the activity into a recognised discipline, and the sport spread to Europe in the 2000s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Slackline Association was founded in August 2015 in Bern, Switzerland, by the Swiss, Austrian, German and US slackline associations, formalising competitions, safety standards and rankings (ISA, n.d.-a). Record-setters such as Alexander Schulz (610 m longline, 2014) and Samuel Volery (477 m highline, 2015) pushed the physical limits of the sport in the 2010s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
