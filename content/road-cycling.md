## Basic Information

Road cycling is the discipline of bicycle racing on paved roads, contested in mass-start road races and individual time trials, and it is governed worldwide by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), founded on 14 April 1900 in Paris by the national federations of the era (UCI, n.d.). The professional calendar spans one-day classics, stage races — the three Grand Tours (Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, Vuelta a España) and the five Monuments — and the annual UCI Road World Championships with the rainbow jersey, with top squads organized as WorldTeams, ProTeams and Continental teams in the UCI WorldTour of roughly 35–40 races per year (UCI, n.d.). At the 99th UCI Road World Championships, held 20–27 September 2026 in Montreal, Canada — the third time the event went to Canada after 1974 and 2003 — the women's elite road race over 180.1 km on 26 September was won by the Netherlands' Demi Vollering ahead of Kasia Niewiadoma-Phinney of Poland and Elisa Longo Borghini of Italy, while the men's elite race over 273.4 km on 27 September went to Brandon McNulty of the USA, the first American man in the rainbow jersey in 33 years; Remco Evenepoel of Belgium won a record fourth consecutive elite men's time trial, and Marlen Reusser of Switzerland defended the women's time trial title (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

The two fundamental formats are the mass-start road race, where all riders start together and the first across the finish line wins, and the individual time trial, where riders start at intervals against the clock and the fastest time wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In WorldTour road races teams of seven to eight riders support their leaders, with team cars, feeding zones and radio earpieces regulated by the UCI; the bike itself must weigh at least 6.8 kg and fit UCI dimension limits, helmets are mandatory, and the UCI Biological Passport has monitored riders since 2008 (UCI, n.d.). Stage races are ranked by general classification (cumulative time), with points, mountains, youth and team classifications; the mixed team relay has been part of the World Championships since 2019 (UCI, n.d.). World Championship categories exist for women and men in elite, under-23 and junior classes, plus the mixed relay, and the women's professional calendar has run as its own UCI Women's WorldTour since 2016 with equal prize money at the Worlds (UCI, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Road racing began with the velocipede era: the first recorded race was the 1868 Saint-Cloud–Paris ride and the 1,200-km Paris–Brest–Paris of 1891 set the template for long-distance events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Tour de France was first held in 1903 and won by Maurice Garin, the Giro d'Italia followed in 1909 and the Vuelta a España in 1935; the UCI was founded in 1900 and has staged the Road World Championships since 1927 at the Nürburgring, awarding the rainbow jersey to the winners (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the sport include Italy's Alfredo Binda (three world titles 1927–1933 and five Giro wins), Fausto Coppi and Gino Bartali of the 1940s–1950s, Belgium's Eddy Merckx (born 1945), five-time Tour winner and three-time world champion, France's Bernard Hinault, and on the women's side France's Jeannie Longo (born 1958), five consecutive world titles from 1985 to 1989 and Olympic champion at Atlanta 1996, plus the Netherlands' Marianne Vos, a five-time world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
