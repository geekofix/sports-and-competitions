## Basic Information

Snowboarding is the umbrella winter sport in which riders descend snow-covered slopes on a single board attached to both feet, encompassing the alpine disciplines (parallel giant slalom, parallel slalom) and the freestyle disciplines (halfpipe, slopestyle, big air, snowboard cross); it is governed internationally by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), which recognised the sport in 1994, has staged a Snowboard World Cup since 1994 — the 2025–26 season is the 32nd — and biennial World Championships since 1996 (FIS, n.d.-b). Snowboarding debuted at the Nagano 1998 Winter Olympics, and at Milano Cortina 2026 eleven snowboard events were held at Livigno Snow Park from 5 to 18 February 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the current leading women are Choi Ga-on of South Korea (halfpipe gold 2026), Kokomo Murase of Japan (big air gold 2026), Mari Fukada of Japan (slopestyle gold 2026), Zuzana Maděrová of Czechia (parallel giant slalom gold 2026), Josie Baff of Australia (snowboard cross gold 2026) and Zoi Sadowski-Synnott of New Zealand; among the men are Yuto Totsuka (halfpipe), Kira Kimura (big air), Su Yiming (slopestyle), Benjamin Karl (parallel giant slalom) and Alessandro Hämmerle (snowboard cross) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under FIS regulations, snowboard competition splits into alpine disciplines raced on hard-boot equipment with directional boards — parallel giant slalom and parallel slalom, decided head-to-head on parallel courses — and freestyle disciplines on soft boots: halfpipe, slopestyle, big air and snowboard cross (FIS, n.d.-a). Each discipline has its own course, format and judging or timing system — timed knockout heats for alpine, judged runs for halfpipe, slopestyle and big air, and mass-start heats for snowboard cross (FIS, n.d.-a). Equipment rules cover board dimensions, boot and binding systems, helmets and protective gear, and only FIS-sanctioned events are contested at the Olympics (FIS, n.d.-a). Women and men compete in separate classifications of every discipline on the same courses, and junior and youth categories exist at the FIS Junior World Championships and the Winter Youth Olympic Games (FIS, n.d.-b).

## History

The sport began in 1965 when Sherman Poppen of Muskegon, Michigan, bolted two skis together for his daughter and called the result the Snurfer; the first snurfing competition was held in Michigan in February 1968 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In the 1970s and 1980s pioneers commercialised the sport: Tom Sims founded Sims Snowboards in 1976 and won the 1983 world championship, Jake Burton Carpenter founded Burton Snowboards in 1977 in Londonderry, Vermont, and Dimitrije Milovich started Winterstick (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Snowboard Federation was founded in 1990 and ran world championships from 1993; the FIS recognised snowboarding in 1994, launched the first Snowboard World Cup the same year and the first FIS World Championships in 1996 at Lienz, Austria (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Snowboarding debuted at the Nagano 1998 Winter Olympics with giant slalom and halfpipe — Canada's Ross Rebagliati won the men's giant slalom gold — while Norway's Terje Håkonsen boycotted the Games; parallel giant slalom replaced giant slalom in 2002, snowboard cross arrived in 2006, slopestyle in 2014, big air in 2018 and the mixed team cross in 2022 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Retired icons include Håkonsen, America's Shaun White (three Olympic halfpipe golds, 2006–2018), Australia's Torah Bright (2010), the Schöch brothers of Switzerland and Ross Rebagliati (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
