## Basic Information

Jet ski racing — officially Aquabike — is powerboat racing on personal watercraft (jet skis) purpose-built or modified for competition: riders race on closed circuits, offshore courses, endurance courses, jet raid stages, a parallel slalom and freestyle routines, in classes such as Runabout GP1/GP2/GP3/Stock (sit-down) and Ski GP1/GP2/GP3 plus Ski Ladies (stand-up) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Union Internationale Motonautique, n.d.-b). The UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship, the premier title, has been run since 1992 and is organized and promoted by H2O Racing on behalf of the Union Internationale Motonautique, the IOC-recognized world governing body of powerboating; the 2026 UIM Aquabike Rulebook was published on 22 December 2025 (Union Internationale Motonautique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Jet ski racing has also featured at the Asian Games since 2014 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The 2026 UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship concluded in Olbia, Italy, on 18 October 2026 with Sweden's Samuel Johansson taking the Runabout GP1 title after a dramatic finale, Belgium's Quinten Bossche completing a perfect unbeaten season in Ski GP1, France's Jessica Chavanne winning her third Ski Ladies GP1 world title (2020, 2023, 2026), and Rashid Al Mulla of the UAE the Freestyle title; the offshore world titles went to François Medori of France (Runabout GP1), Davide Di Maio of Italy (GP2) and Ian Traba Arce of Spain (GP3), with the 2026 circuit opening in Shanghai in spring 2026 (UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship, 2026, October 18; UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the most successful riders in the championship's history is Yousef Al Abdulrazzaq of Kuwait, a multiple Runabout GP1 world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Closed-circuit races are held near coastlines on a marked course of no more than 1300 m, buoyed with yellow buoys for right turns and red buoys for left turns; riders complete a set number of laps and the chequered flag decides the moto, with the championship decided over several rounds (typically three motos per event) by points (Union Internationale Motonautique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Runabout bikes (sit-down, saddle) are used in closed circuit, offshore, endurance, jet raid and parallel slalom; Ski bikes (stand-up) in closed circuit, parallel slalom and freestyle (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Freestyle is a judged routine: each rider has three minutes to perform a rehearsed sequence of tricks — backflips, barrel rolls, 360s, superman and submarine — evaluated by five expert judges on quality, quantity and variety (Union Internationale Motonautique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Safety requirements are strict: rescue teams must hold personal-watercraft rescue certificates, firefighters and patrol boats must be on site, a trauma-specialized medical team and — offshore — a helicopter rescue service must be available (Union Internationale Motonautique, n.d.-a). Classes are split by modification level (GP1 most modified, GP3/Stock for younger riders aged 11–14 and juniors) plus Ladies and Veterans divisions, and men's and women's categories compete separately (Union Internationale Motonautique, n.d.-a).

## History

The personal watercraft itself dates to the early 1970s, when Clayton Jacobson II developed the sit-down Jet Ski for Kawasaki — launched commercially in 1972–73 — after experimenting with a stand-up watercraft in the late 1960s; racing on these machines began in the late 1980s and early 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship was founded in 1992 and grew into a global series with rounds from Shanghai to Olbia; the 2026 edition ran from spring 2026 in Shanghai through the European Championship in Vichy to the finale in Olbia in October 2026 (UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship, 2026, October 18; UIM-ABP Aquabike World Championship, n.d.-b).
