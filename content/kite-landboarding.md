## Basic Information

Kite landboarding (KLB) combines a mountainboard with a steerable power kite: the kite pulls the rider across beaches, fields and dry lake beds, and competitions include racing on a course, freestyle and long-jump/distance events at the Kite Landboarding World Championship (KLB, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Top speeds on hard sand exceed 80 km/h; men and women compete in separate classes.

## Rules

KLB competition rules: race courses are marked with buoys and ridden upwind/downwind in heats; freestyle is judged on tricks; kites must pass safety checks (quick-release systems mandatory); helmets and pads mandatory; categories by kite size class, age and gender (KLB, n.d.).

## History

Kite landboarding grew out of kite buggying and landboarding in the 2000s, with the first world championships in the 2010s; strong scenes exist in France, the UK, the USA and Australia, mostly on beaches at low tide (Wikipedia, n.d.).
