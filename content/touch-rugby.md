## Basic Information

Touch rugby (touch football) is the minimal-contact form of rugby football: attacking players run and pass, and defenders stop them with a touch (a legal, one-hand contact on the ball carrier) instead of a tackle. Played socially and competitively by mixed teams of all ages, it is one of the world's most played rugby formats, with standardized rules, national associations and international cups in men's, women's and mixed categories.

## Rules

Games are played six-a-side (commonly) on a rectangular pitch: the touched ball carrier performs a rollball at the mark, the defence must retire a set distance (typically seven metres) until the rollball is taken, and the attacking team has six touches to score a touchdown in the in-goal zone before possession turns over. Forward passes, offside and touch-and-pass violations are penalized; no kicking is allowed in some formats and the touchdown is scored by grounding the ball like a rugby try. Standardized rules govern the touch count, tap penalties, substitutions and mixed-game gender requirements, and the international sanctioning body stages world cups in which more than twenty nations compete.

## History

Touch began in Australia in the 1960s as a training and schoolyard derivative of rugby league, codified as a safe, fast sport that spread through school programs and park competitions; the Australian governing body formed in the 1970s and international play followed quickly. New Zealand, England, South Africa, Japan and other rugby nations built large touch communities, and the Federation of International Touch stages world cups in the sport's men's, women's, mixed and senior categories. Its simplicity and speed have made touch one of the world's fastest-growing recreational team sports, and it fits athletes of every shape and size into the handling rhythms of the rugby family.
