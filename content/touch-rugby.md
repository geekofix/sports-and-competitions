## Basic Information

Touch rugby (also called touch or touch football) is a non-contact variant of rugby league in which defenders stop the ball carrier by touching them with the hand instead of tackling; the most codified form is played six-a-side under the Federation of International Touch (FIT) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). FIT was formed in Melbourne in 1985 and sanctions the Touch World Cup every four years — the 2024 edition at the University of Nottingham, 15–21 July 2024, drew 4,000 players from 39 nations in 187 teams across 13 categories, with the 2028 World Cup going to New Zealand (Federation of International Touch, n.d.). The women's open and mixed open divisions are core World Cup categories, and Australia's women — captained by Kim Sue See, player of the 2024 women's open final — won the 2024 women's open title 6–2 over New Zealand alongside the men's open (9–6) and mixed open titles, Australia's 12th gold of 13 divisions (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Touch is played on a rectangular grass pitch 70 m long and 50 m wide with a try zone at each end, six players per side with up to eight substitutes rolling on and off, and matches last 40 minutes in two 20-minute halves with three referees (Federation of International Touch, n.d.). A "touch" is hand contact with any part of the ball carrier's body, ball, clothing or hair at minimum force; the attacking team has six touches, after which possession changes with a rollball at the mark, and a try — grounding the ball in the opponent's try zone, worth one point — may not be scored by the acting half (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After a touch the defenders must retreat 5 m; at a rollball the offside line is the 7 m line, and at a penalty tap the defense must be behind the 10 m line before advancing (Federation of International Touch, n.d.). The ball may be passed sideways or backwards but not forwards or kicked, a player may not pass after being touched, and infringements (offside, obstruction, forward pass, touch-and-pass) are penalized with a tap to the non-offending team (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Divisions at major events include men's, women's and mixed open plus age-group categories (under-27s, 30s, 35s, 40s and masters), and pool matches may end in draws (Federation of International Touch, n.d.). The official rulebook is the FIT Playing Rules, 5th edition (Federation of International Touch, n.d.).

## History

Touch developed in Australia in the late 1960s when rugby league players wanted a fast, non-contact version of their game, and it spread through Queensland, New South Wales and the ACT, with more than 700,000 registered players in Australia today (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Federation of International Touch was formed at a meeting held alongside the Australian national championships and the first Australia–New Zealand fixtures in Melbourne in 1985, and today recognizes 49 national governing bodies (Federation of International Touch, n.d.). The first Touch World Cup was held on the Gold Coast in 1988, with editions since in Auckland 1991, Hawaii 1995, Sydney 1999, Kumagaya 2003, Stellenbosch 2007, Edinburgh 2011, Coffs Harbour 2015, Putrajaya 2019 and Nottingham 2024; almost all finals have been contested between Australia and New Zealand, and Australia has won the most — and has never lost a World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Variants such as one-touch in South Africa and beach touch extend the family, and the RFU-partnered England Touch Association has hosted European championships since 1995 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
