## Basic Information

The madison is a relay race for pairs in track cycling: only one rider per team is active at any time, and the resting partner is hurled back into the race by a hand-sling from the departing teammate (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). The name comes from Madison Square Garden in New York, where the two-rider format was devised; it has been a UCI World Championship event for men since 1995 and for women since 2017, and an Olympic event for men from 2000 to 2008 and for both genders again since Tokyo 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the current leading riders — women first — are the British pair Katie Archibald and Maddie Leech, the 2025 world champions (Archibald also an Olympic team-pursuit champion), and the Belgian men's pair Lindsay De Vylder and Fabio van den Bossche, the 2025 world champions; at Paris 2024 the Italian women Ciara Consonni and Vittoria Guazzini took Olympic gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Teams of two (occasionally three) riders take turns, with exchanges made by touching, pushing or hand-slinging the partner forward — often at over 50 km/h (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The team covering the most laps wins; teams tied on laps are ranked by points from intermediate sprints (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.). UCI-sanctioned elite madisons run 50 km for men (200 laps on a 250 m track) and 30 km for women (120 laps), with a sprint every 10 laps awarding 10, 6, 4 and 2 points (doubled in the final sprint); gaining a lap on the field is worth 20 points and being lapped deducts 20 (Union Cycliste Internationale, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women race the same format at different distances — the women's world title has been awarded since 2017 and the women's Olympic madison since Tokyo 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Six-day racing began at Madison Square Garden in 1889 as solo rides around the clock; after New York and Illinois restricted competitors to 12 hours a day in 1898 — formalized by a New York State law approved on 14 April 1899 — the Garden's promoter paired riders so the race could run 24 hours a day while no individual exceeded the limit, and the two-man tag format took the venue's name (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Solo riders like Charlie Miller rode 3,633 km in six days, but the Australian Alf Goullet and a partner covered 2,790 miles (4,490 km) together, and speeds and crowds soared (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The format became the highlight of six-day races in New York, Berlin, Zurich and Paris in the 1920s and 1930s; after the war only Rotterdam and Gent still run annual six-days (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The madison entered the UCI Track World Championships in 1995 (men) and 2017 (women), was an Olympic event in 2000, 2004 and 2008, was dropped for 2012 and 2016, and returned for both genders at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The fastest known average speed is 59.921 km/h, set by Australians Sam Welsford and Leigh Howard at a World Cup race in Glasgow on 9 November 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
