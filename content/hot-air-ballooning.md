## Basic Information

Competitive hot air ballooning is the air sport in which pilots fly balloons of different size classes (FAI AX classes) through a series of set tasks — distance, speed and navigation challenges with targets and markers — and are ranked by the points scored, governed worldwide by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) through its Ballooning Commission (CIA) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale [FAI], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 26th FAI World Hot Air Balloon Championship was held 19–25 September 2026 in Krosno, Poland, with 101 pilots, and was won by Britain's Dominic Bareford with 15,649 points — his second world title after 2018 (FAI, n.d.-b). On the women's side, Australia's Nicola Scaife won the 5th FAI World Women's Hot Air Balloon Championship in 2023 in Newcastle, New South Wales; women compete in the same open classes as men at the world championships, in addition to the dedicated women's championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The oldest and most famous distance race is the Gordon Bennett Cup, created in 1906 by James Gordon Bennett Jr. of the New York Herald and revived in 1983, in which the balloon flying the greatest distance from the launch point wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-b) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Balloons are classified by the FAI into AX classes according to envelope volume (AX-1 up to AX-15); each competition consists of a series of tasks set by the competition director — for example maximum or minimum distance in a set time, a declared goal with a marker drop, a target, or a timed navigation task — and the pilot scores points based on the measured distance of the dropped marker (a weighted ribbon) or the achieved result, logged with GPS recorders (FAI, n.d.-a). The pilot with the highest total points after all tasks wins; scoring uses a descending points scale with 1,000 points for the best result per task (FAI, n.d.-a). Safety and airspace rules apply throughout: no flying over assembled crowds, minimum altitudes over built-up areas, and coordination with air traffic control; burners, envelopes and instruments must be airworthy and registered with the national aeronautical authority (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women compete together in the same classes at the World Championships, with separate women's championships additionally; junior categories exist at national events (FAI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## History

Hot air ballooning began with the Montgolfier brothers, Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier of Annonay, France, whose hot-air balloon made the first public manned flight on 21 November 1783 in Paris, carrying Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes for about 25 minutes over the city (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitive ballooning started with the Gordon Bennett Cup, created in 1906 by the newspaper publisher James Gordon Bennett Jr. — the first race was won by the American Frank Lahm — and revived in 1983 after a long hiatus; the FAI's Ballooning Commission (CIA) has organized the World Hot Air Balloon Championships since 1973 and the World Gas Balloon Championship (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Icons of the sport include the Montgolfier brothers, Pilâtre de Rozier and d'Arlandes, and the Swiss Bertrand Piccard, who with Brian Jones completed the first nonstop balloon circumnavigation of the globe in March 1999 in the Breitling Orbiter 3 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
