## Basic Information

Australian rules football (AFL, "Aussie rules") is a high-contact ball game played on a large oval field (up to 185 m x 155 m) by two teams of 18, in which players mark, kick and handball an oval ball toward goals (four posts at each end: two big goal posts and two behind posts). It is governed in Australia by the Australian Football League (AFL), which runs the AFL premiership, and internationally by AFL Europe and the international governing body AFL (league) / the IOC-recognized International Australian Football Council.

## Rules

Under the Laws of Australian Football, each team has 18 players on the field and four interchange players; the match is four 20-minute quarters. Players score by kicking the ball between the two central goal posts (a goal, 6 points) or between a goal post and a behind post (a behind, 1 point). The ball may be advanced by kicking or handballing (striking the ball with a closed fist of the other hand); a player who catches a kicked ball cleanly (a "mark") gets a free kick. Tackling is allowed above the knees and below the shoulders (no high or low tackles); holding the ball results in a free kick to the tackler. Ruck contests restart play after goals and behinds. The AFL Grand Final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG) in September is one of the biggest club sporting events in the world (100,000+ crowd).

## History

Australian rules football evolved in Melbourne in the 1850s, with the first codified rules written in 1859 by Tom Wills, J.B. Thompson and others (the Melbourne Football Club, founded 1858, is the world's oldest football club). The Victorian Football League (VFL) was founded in 1897 and became the AFL in 1990 (after absorbing interstate clubs). The sport is dominant in Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory. Legends include Haydn Bunton Sr. (three Brownlow Medals), Leigh Matthews, Gary Ablett Sr./Jr., Ted Whitten and Lance "Buddy" Franklin. Internationally, the sport is played in 30+ countries; Australian football was a demonstration sport at the 1956 Melbourne Olympics and will be a demonstration sport at LA 2028.
