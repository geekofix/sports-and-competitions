## Basic Information

Four-cross mountain biking (4X) is a downhill eliminator race in which four riders start simultaneously on a prepared course of jumps, berms and rollers and race head-to-head to the finish — first across the line wins (UCI, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Riders use full-suspension mountain bikes with about 140–160 mm of travel. The discipline was part of the UCI Mountain Bike World Cup and World Championships from 2002 (replacing dual slalom) but was removed from the World Cup after 2011 and from the World Championships after the 2021 Val di Sole edition; since 2012 it has been run mainly as the independent 4X Pro Tour, while the UCI Mountain Bike Regulations (Part 4) still contain a four-cross chapter and the UCI publishes a 4X ranking (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 UCI Mountain Bike World Championships in Val di Sole (August 26–30, 2026) therefore featured cross-country Olympic (XCO), cross-country short track (XCC), cross-country eliminator (XCE), downhill (DH) and e-MTB — but no four-cross (UCI, n.d.-a).

At the end of 2026 the leading women in four-cross include the multiple world champions Anneke Beerten (NED) and Jill Kintner (USA) as the discipline's benchmarks, with the current 4X Pro Tour leaders topping the UCI 4X ranking, while on the men's side the record is held by riders such as Brian Lopes (USA, three world titles), Cédric Gracia (FRA) and Romain Mayet (FRA, 2019 world champion) (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the UCI MTB Regulations Part 4 (four-cross chapter), four riders per race start from a gate on a course of roughly 500–800 m with a vertical drop, mixing jumps, bermed corners, rollers and technical sections (UCI, n.d.-b). The format is eliminator-style: heats of four riders race down the course, and the fastest two (or the configured number of qualifiers) advance to the next round until a final of four decides the winner; the last rider across the line in each heat is eliminated (UCI, n.d.-b). Riders must wear full-face helmets, body protection and a full-finger glove set; a rider leaving the course is relegated to last place of the heat, and dangerous riding is sanctioned by the commissaires (UCI, n.d.-b). The UCI publishes an official four-cross ranking based on race results, and the discipline is open to men and women in separate elite categories, with junior (U19) classes at major events (UCI, n.d.-b). Because 4X is no longer part of the World Cup, the 4X Pro Tour and national series (e.g., in France, the UK and Czechia) now carry the competitive calendar (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Four-cross evolved from dual slalom, the head-to-head downhill format popular in the 1990s, and was added to the UCI Mountain Bike World Championships in 2002 in Les Gets, France, and to the World Cup the same year (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The American Brian Lopes became the era's dominant rider with world titles in 2002, 2005 and 2007, followed by Michal Prokop of Czechia (2003, 2006, 2011), Eric Carter (USA), Roger Rinderknecht (SUI), Jared Graves (AUS), Joost Wichman (NED), Cédric Gracia (FRA), Tomáš Slavík (CZE), Quentin Derbier (FRA) and Romain Mayet (FRA, 2019) on the men's side (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the women, Anneke Beerten of the Netherlands (four world titles), Jill Kintner and Melissa Buhl of the USA defined the early era (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). After the UCI removed 4X from the World Cup (2012) and from the World Championships (after Val di Sole 2021, announced in September 2022), the 4X Pro Tour took over the elite calendar, and the 2026 memo from the UCI confirmed that a UCI 4X ranking continues to be published (UCI, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
