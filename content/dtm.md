## Basic Information

The DTM (Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters) is Europe's premier touring car championship, featuring heavily modified production-based silhouette cars (BMW, Mercedes-AMG, Audi, Porsche, Ferrari, Lamborghini, Aston Martin) racing on a mix of permanent circuits and street courses. It is organized by ITR e.V. and sanctioned under German motorsport regulations with FIA-homologated safety standards.

## Rules

A DTM race weekend consists of two races (Saturday and Sunday) of about 55 minutes plus one lap over a minimum distance (roughly 80-100 km at most circuits), with a mandatory pit stop including a tire change in each race. Points are awarded to the top 10 finishers in each race (25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-3-1), plus points for qualifying pole position and fastest lap. Cars run on standardized control fuel and spec tires (Hankook), with a Balance of Performance (BoP) adjusting weight and aerodynamics to equalize the different manufacturers. Qualifying uses a single-lap format; the starting grid for race 2 is the reverse of race 1's finishing order in some seasons, or based on qualifying. DTM uses a standing start, full-course yellow flags, and the FIA Appendix K historic-style sporting regulations adapted for touring cars; drivers must hold an FIA super license or equivalent.

## History

The DTM was founded in 1984 by the German ADAC and manufacturers, replacing the Deutsche Rennsport-Meisterschaft (DRM), and became the benchmark for touring car racing worldwide. The 1990s "Class 1" era with Mercedes, Alfa Romeo and Opel produced legendary rivalries (Bernd Schneider, Klaus Ludwig, Manuel Reuter, 1996 champion). After a troubled 2000s merger attempt with the ITC, the series was revived in 2000 with cheaper silhouette cars based on saloon bodies. Champions include Mattias Ekström (two titles), Timo Scheider (three), Paul di Resta, Mike Rockenfeller and René Rast (three titles). Since 2019 the DTM has also included the electric-ish "DTM Trophy" feeder series and, from 2023, uses sustainable fuel; races are broadcast internationally and the series regularly fills Hockenheim and the Norisring to capacity.
