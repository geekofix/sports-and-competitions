## Basic Information

The DTM — Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters, originally the Deutsche Tourenwagen-Meisterschaft founded in 1984 — is Germany's premier touring car racing series, run under the ADAC and contested with GT3-based machinery on Pirelli tires (DTM, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 season was the 40th in the series' history, the 27th under the DTM name and the sixth with GT3 cars, comprising eight rounds and sixteen races; the finale was held at the Hockenheimring from 9 to 11 October 2026 (DTM, n.d.-a). Thomas Preining of Manthey Porsche won the 2026 drivers' championship — his second title — with 225 points, twelve ahead of Maro Engel, with BMW's Marco Wittmann third (DTM, n.d.-a). The grid runs Porsche 911 GT3 R, BMW M4 GT3 Evo, Ferrari 296 GT3, Audi R8 LMS, Mercedes-AMG GT3, Aston Martin Vantage, Ford Mustang, McLaren 720S and Lamborghini Huracán cars (DTM, n.d.-b). Women and men compete together in the same championship, with female drivers regularly in the 2026 field (DTM, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The 2026 DTM technical regulations are based on the FIA GT3 platform: cars are production-derived coupés with Balance of Performance adjustments, running Pirelli slick tires and a standardized ECU (DTM, n.d.-b). Each weekend consists of two races of about 60 minutes plus one full lap, with a qualifying session setting the grid; points follow a 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 scale, and championships are awarded for drivers, teams and manufacturers (DTM, n.d.-b). DRS and a mandatory pit stop window are regulated, parc fermé applies after qualifying, and the stewards can impose time penalties or disqualification for dangerous driving, ignoring flags or technical infringements (DTM, n.d.-b). A Rookie classification runs alongside the main title, and men and women compete in the same races under identical regulations — there are no separate women's classes (DTM, n.d.-a).

## History

The Deutsche Tourenwagen-Meisterschaft was founded in 1984 with BMW and Mercedes-Benz, grew through the 1990s with the famous "German tin-top" duels, and ran as the International Touring Car Championship in 1995–96 before being reborn as the DTM in 2000 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Since the ADAC took over the organization, the series has moved to GT3 machinery in 2021 (DTM, n.d.-a). Icons of the series include Bernd Schneider with four titles, Mattias Ekström and Timo Scheider with two each, Marco Wittmann with two, and René Rast with three; on the women's side, drivers have competed regularly since the 1990s (Wikipedia, n.d.).
