## Basic Information

The 100 metres is the shortest outdoor sprint in athletics: one straight dash down the home straight of a 400 m track from a crouch start, often called the blue-riband event of the sport. It is governed worldwide by World Athletics, which ratifies the world records and runs the World Championships and the Olympic tournament (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 9.58 s for men, set by Usain Bolt of Jamaica at the 2009 World Championships in Berlin, and 10.49 s for women, set by Florence Griffith-Joyner of the USA in 1988 — both still standing in 2026 (Olympics, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the end of the 2026 season the women's sprint was headlined by Olympic champion Julien Alfred of Saint Lucia (pictured) and world champion Melissa Jefferson-Wooden of the USA, while the men's event was shared by 2025 world champion Oblique Seville, Noah Lyles and Kishane Thompson of Jamaica, with Letsile Tebogo of Botswana the reigning Olympic 200 m champion (World Athletics, n.d.-c).

## Rules

The 100 m is run in lanes on the home straight of the track, each athlete in a separate lane, from a crouch start in starting blocks; the winner is the first whose torso crosses the finish line, decided by photo finish (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Under the World Athletics Competition and Technical Rules (2026 edition), outdoor track races are run on a standard 400 m track with at least eight lanes at international competitions; every lane is 1.22 m ± 0.01 m wide and each athlete has a separate lane in all races up to and including 400 m, with staggered starts in the 200 m, 400 m, 800 m and the relays (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Races up to and including 400 m — including the first leg of the 4x200 m, the medley relay and the 4x400 m — use a compulsory crouch start in starting blocks, while longer races use a standing start (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Since 2010 a single false start disqualifies the athlete; the finish is judged when the torso crosses the vertical plane of the finish line, with a photo-finish camera reading to 1/1000 s and times officially recorded to 1/100 s at major championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). A wind gauge is read in the running direction and a reading above +2.0 m/s invalidates a record in events up to 200 m; since January 2025 shoe soles are limited to 25 mm in track events and spike pins to 9 mm (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women compete in separate races under identical rules; U18 and U20 age-group competitions use the same distances, and para athletics offers the same events in classification classes (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

Sprinting is the oldest Olympic event: the stadion, a race of about 192 m, was the only event of the first ancient Olympic Games in 776 BC, and the 100 m has been on the modern Olympic programme since Athens 1896 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Jesse Owens of the USA won four gold medals at the 1936 Berlin Games, including the 100 m, in a historic rebuke to Nazi ideology; Carl Lewis repeated the four-gold feat at Los Angeles 1984 and won nine Olympic gold medals in his career (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Usain Bolt of Jamaica dominated the event from 2008 to 2016, winning three consecutive Olympic sprint trebles (100 m, 200 m, 4x100 m) and setting the 9.58 world record in Berlin 2009 (Olympics, n.d.-a). On the women's side, Wilma Rudolph won three golds at Rome 1960, and Florence Griffith-Joyner's 10.49 from Seoul 1988 remains the world record; Elaine Thompson-Herah of Jamaica broke the Olympic record with 10.61 at Tokyo 2020 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
