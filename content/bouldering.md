## Basic Information

Bouldering is the rope-free discipline of sport climbing in which athletes climb short, difficult routes ("problems") on low walls over crash pads, using only hands and feet. It is governed by the International Federation of Sport Climbing (International Federation of Sport Climbing [IFSC], n.d.-a) — renamed World Climbing in December 2025 — which runs the World Climbing Series (the 2026 season, its first under the new name, comprised 13 locations and 19 events per gender: 6 boulder, 6 lead, 7 speed, from Keqiao, China, on May 1 to Santiago, Chile, on October 25, 2026, the first-ever Chilean event) (IFSC, n.d.-b) (International Federation of Sport Climbing, n.d.-a) (International Federation of Sport Climbing, n.d.-b).

At the Paris 2024 Olympics at the Le Bourget Sport Climbing Venue (August 5–10, 2024), Janja Garnbret of Slovenia defended her Tokyo 2021 title in the women's boulder-and-lead combined (168.8 points), ahead of Brooke Raboutou (USA, silver) and Jessica Pilz (Austria, bronze); the men's combined gold went to Toby Roberts (Great Britain, 155.2), silver to Sorato Anraku (Japan) and bronze to Jakob Schubert (Austria) (IFSC, n.d.-b). Garnbret, the most successful competition climber in history with a record 10 senior world titles after the 2025 Seoul worlds and 52 World Climbing Series wins by October 2026, became in June 2026 the first woman to climb Bibliographie, graded 9b+ (IFSC, n.d.-a). At LA 2028 bouldering will be a separate medal event from lead for the first time.

## Rules

In competition bouldering, each problem is attempted within a four- or five-minute time limit; a "zone" is a controlled intermediate hold and a "top" completes the problem. Scoring rewards tops first, then zones, with fewer attempts ("fewer tries") breaking ties — the countback goes top, zone, then number of attempts, and a flash (first-try top) beats any non-flash (IFSC, n.d.-a). Athletes get a set number of problems (four in Olympic semifinals/finals, up to five or more in World Climbing Series rounds), with rest between attempts; falls and time-outs are recorded. The wall is standardized: holds on standardized panels, problems set by an international route-setting team. Men's and women's events use identical formats, though the route setters may set slightly different difficulty profiles; youth categories (U16/U18/U20) use fewer problems and lower grades, and paraclimbing has its own classes (IFSC, n.d.-b).

## History

Bouldering grew out of Fontainebleau's boulder fields near Paris in the late 19th century, where climbers trained on low rocks; it became a distinct competition discipline in the 1990s, with the first world cups in the late 1990s and Olympic debut at Tokyo 2020/2021 (combined with lead and speed). The IFSC, founded in 2007, has governed the World Climbing Series and world championships since; no senior world championships were held in 2026 (the next is 2027), but the youth worlds took place in Arco, Italy, July 18–25, 2026, and climbing was on the 2026 Asian Games programme in Nagoya, where Seo Chae-hyun became the first Korean woman to win an Asian Games climbing title (IFSC, n.d.-b). Iconic figures include Lynn Hill (first free ascent of The Nose on El Capitan, 1993), Adam Ondra (first 9c), and the competition generation of Janja Garnbret, Jakob Schubert and Jakob's rival Sorato Anraku, who won the 2026 Innsbruck boulder world cup (IFSC, n.d.-a).
