## Basic Information

Pinball is an arcade game in which a player scores points by manipulating a steel ball on an inclined playfield using flippers, and modern competitive pinball is an organized sport with world rankings and championships (Wikipedia, n.d.). The sport's leading organizations are the International Flipper Pinball Association (IFPA), which maintains the IFPA World Pinball Player Rankings, and the Professional and Amateur Pinball Association (PAPA), whose brand, owned by the Replay Foundation, runs the World Pinball Championships (International Flipper Pinball Association, n.d.). Competitive pinball is open to all genders; the IFPA World Pinball Championship includes a women's division alongside the open division, and top female players such as the multiple-time women's world champion are ranked on the same world ranking list as men (International Flipper Pinball Association, n.d.). ## Rules

Competitive pinball is governed by the unified PAPA/IFPA ruleset, the baseline for sanctioned competition (International Flipper Pinball Association, 2020). Tournament machines are put into tournament mode, disabling random awards and extra balls so that scores reflect skill, and physical settings are adjusted to increase difficulty: outlane posts are widened, the playfield angle increased and tilt mechanisms made strict to limit nudging. Match-play formats, such as Pinburgh, group players into direct head-to-head rounds, while qualifying formats like the World Pinball Championships let players enter repeatedly, scoring points per machine relative to all other entries (100 points for the highest score down to zero), with the best entry ranking the player before bracketed finals. Malfunctions are ruled on by tournament directors under documented procedures, and betting on any sanctioned event is penalized by suppression from the world rankings (International Flipper Pinball Association, n.d.). Divisions include open, women's, seniors and juniors, so the same rules apply across genders and age groups (Professional and Amateur Pinball Association, n.d.).

## History

Pinball descends from the 18th-century French bagatelle and, in its modern coin-operated form, from the 1930s; early machines were pure games of chance until flippers were introduced in 1947, making skill decisive (Wikipedia, n.d.). In 1976 pinball historian Roger Sharpe demonstrated before the New York City Council that the game was one of skill rather than gambling, and the city's ban was lifted (Wikipedia, n.d.). PAPA was founded by Roger Sharpe and Steve Epstein, owner of the Broadway Arcade in Manhattan, and ran its first tournaments in New York in the 1980s; the organization passed to Kevin Martin in 2004 and its tournaments moved to the Pittsburgh area, where the Replay Foundation housed a collection of hundreds of machines (Professional and Amateur Pinball Association, n.d.). The World Pinball Championships, known as PAPA 13 in 2010 and numbered in this way since the New York years, continues today under IFPA license, alongside the Pinburgh Match-Play Championship; majors now include the IFPA World Pinball Championship, the PAPA Worlds, the European Pinball Championships, Pinburgh and The Open. The Replay Foundation discontinued events in 2020 during the pandemic before relaunching Pinburgh in 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
