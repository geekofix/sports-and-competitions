## Basic Information

A pumpkin regatta is a water race in which competitors pilot giant hollowed-out pumpkins — often weighing 1,000 pounds or more — as boats, paddling or motoring them over a short course as the highlight of autumn festivals. The best-known events are the Windsor Pumpkin Regatta in Nova Scotia, Canada, and the Damariscotta Pumpkinfest & Regatta in Maine, USA, which draws thousands of spectators to a small harbor town every Columbus Day weekend.

## Rules

Racers use Atlantic giant pumpkins large enough to hold a seated adult; the fruit is gutted, shaped for "hull lines", fitted with a seat and launched with the captain aboard — often in costume. Regattas commonly run two divisions, paddle-powered and motor-powered, with individual heats and relay heats between teams; in Damariscotta the paddleboats race in two or three individual heats and relay heats from the town dock to an offshore buoy and back. Any of the usual paddle craft rules apply informally: the pumpkin must float with the captain aboard, suction-driven water ingress is the racer's own problem, and the first hull across the finish line wins the plastic trophy. Wearing zany costumes is a tradition in its own right.

## History

The first pumpkin regatta was organized by Danny Dill and held in 1999 at Lake Pesaquid in Windsor, Nova Scotia, where it ran annually over the years, at times entered by the Canadian Olympic rower Silken Laumann. The idea spread quickly across North America: a regatta was held at Goffstown, New Hampshire by 2001, the West Coast Giant Pumpkin Regatta began at Tualatin, Oregon in 2004, and in 2005 regattas were held both in Damariscotta, Maine and on Lake Mendota in Wisconsin, the latter organized by horticulture faculty of the University of Wisconsin-Madison. The Damariscotta event was started in 2005 by Buzz Pinkham, the first person to captain a pumpkin in the town's harbor, and Billy Clark; it became an official town festival, now a weekend-long Pumpkinfest with giant-pumpkin weigh-offs, a parade and a derby, drawing thousands of visitors. Pumpkin regattas have since been held elsewhere in the United States, in the United Kingdom and in Germany, wherever growers produce fruit big enough — and crews brave enough — to float.
