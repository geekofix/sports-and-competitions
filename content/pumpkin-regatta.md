## Basic Information

A pumpkin regatta is a water race in which competitors pilot giant hollowed-out pumpkins — often weighing 450 kg or more — as boats, paddling or motoring them over a short course as the highlight of autumn harvest festivals (Wikipedia, n.d.). The best-known events are the Windsor Pumpkin Regatta in Nova Scotia, Canada, and the Damariscotta Pumpkinfest & Regatta in Maine, USA, which draws thousands of spectators every Columbus Day weekend (Damariscotta Pumpkinfest & Regatta, n.d.). Regattas are open to women and men alike, in mixed or single-gender crews, and the 2026 Windsor regatta again fielded dozens of pumpkin hulls on Lake Pesaquid (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Racers use Atlantic giant pumpkins large enough to hold a seated adult; the fruit is gutted, shaped for "hull lines", fitted with a seat and launched with the captain aboard — often in costume (Wikipedia, n.d.). Regattas commonly run two divisions, paddle-powered and motor-powered, with individual heats and relay heats between teams; at Damariscotta the paddleboats race in two or three individual heats plus relay heats from the town dock to an offshore buoy and back (Damariscotta Pumpkinfest & Regatta, n.d.). The pumpkin must float with the captain aboard, water ingress is the racer's own problem, and the first hull across the finish line wins; there are no age or gender classes, only crew categories (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The first pumpkin regatta was organized by Danny Dill and held in 1999 at Lake Pesaquid in Windsor, Nova Scotia, at times entered by Canadian Olympic rower Silken Laumann; the idea spread quickly across North America — Goffstown, New Hampshire by 2001, the West Coast Giant Pumpkin Regatta at Tualatin, Oregon from 2004, and events on Lake Mendota in Wisconsin from 2005 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Damariscotta event was started in 2005 by Buzz Pinkham, the first person to captain a pumpkin in the town's harbour, and Billy Clark, and became an official town festival with weigh-offs, a parade and a derby (Bangor Daily News, 2025).
