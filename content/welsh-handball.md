## Basic Information

Welsh handball — *Pâl-law* in Welsh, once so beloved it was simply called *Chwarae Pâl* (playing ball) and has been described as Wales's first national sport — is an ancient native game in which players strike a small, hard, leather-cased ball (a Spaldeen is often used today) with the palm of the hand against a wall, trying to place shots the opponent cannot return (Wikipedia, n.d.). Two forms survive: the open-air one-wall game (wallball) played against a single flat wall, and the three- or four-wall court game. It is closely related to Gaelic handball, Eton fives, Basque pelota and American handball — of which it is a possible antecedent — and today the surviving purpose-built court at Nelson in Glamorgan hosts both versions, with international one-wall play codified since the 1990s (Atlas Obscura, n.d.). Games are usually singles, but doubles are also played, and men and women compete under the same rules in club and one-wall competition.

## Rules

In the one-wall game, the server strikes the ball against the flat front wall from behind the serving line so that it clears a marked nail or line on the wall, and the receiver may let it bounce once before returning it; a rally ends when a player fails to return, hits the ball out of bounds or below the line, and points are traditionally marked with the box scoring system directly on the front wall, overseen by a score marker and a referee (Wikipedia, n.d.). Games are played to a set total — commonly 11 or 15 points — and kill shots, hit so low and hard that no return is possible, decide many rallies. In the multi-wall court game the ball must strike the front wall before or after one floor bounce and remain within the court markings, with the side walls in play. Historically the rules varied from village to village, but the scoring method stayed constant; the modern international one-wall game, codified after 1987, uses courts with standardised markings and hosts world and European championships (Atlas Obscura, n.d.).

## History

Pâl-law has been attested in Welsh literature since the Middle Ages — the ninth-century writer Nennius mentioned it, and courts existed at Tintern Abbey and inside Raglan Castle's Great Hall — with the game offering working people prize money, bookkeeping and even professional careers in the industrial valleys of south Wales (Wikipedia, n.d.). Its heartland was Nelson in Glamorgan, where it was first played against the flat wall of the Nelson Inn (now the Village Inn), probably brought by Irish labourers on the Great Western and Taff Vale railway lines; the rival Royal Oak's landlord then had a purpose-built three-sided court constructed from the 1860s — the brewery owner Evan Evans completed his court in 1864, complete with a stone heart bearing the motto "Gwrol Galon Hyd Angau" (a brave heart till death) (People's Collection Wales, n.d.). The Nelson court was famous from the 1880s until the Second World War, with an annual tournament running May to August accompanied by heavy betting, and wire netting added in 1913; after the war the number of courts dwindled as team sports grew (Atlas Obscura, n.d.). Revival came with the Welsh Handball Association, founded in 1987, which devised international rules and re-marked the Nelson court; the first One Wall World Championships were held in Dublin in October 1994 and the first European One Wall tournament in Wales in 1995, with Wales's Lee Davies winning the 1997 world title in a 1990s high point for the game (Wikipedia, n.d.).
