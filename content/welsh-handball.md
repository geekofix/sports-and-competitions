## Basic Information

Welsh handball (pêl-law in Welsh) is a traditional ball game played against a wall, in which players or pairs strike a hard leather ball with the palm of the hand so that it rebounds over a marked line to an opponent on the other side of a rectangular yard — surviving in both one-wall and four-wall forms. Closely related to the fives and pelota family of wall games, it was once one of the most popular sports in Wales and remains a cherished traditional sport in a handful of strongholds.

The one-wall game in particular is associated with dedicated open-air courts in south Wales villages such as Nelson, where the game has been played continuously for generations.

## Rules

In the one-wall game a square yard is bounded by side and back walls, and the front wall carries a horizontal line (often the "nail line") that every shot must strike above. The server hits the ball against the front wall so it rebounds across the yard to the receiver, and play alternates until a player fails to return legally — failing to reach the wall, striking below the line, or putting the ball out of bounds. A game is traditionally scored to eleven or fifteen points, with players or doubles pairs rotating service and the ball allowed one bounce on the ground before striking. Four-wall formats add side and back walls that stay in play, giving faster and more complex rallies.

## History

Handball against a wall has been recorded in Wales since at least the eighteenth century, and through the nineteenth century it rivalled and often exceeded football in popularity, played in yards attached to public houses and purpose-built courts across the mining valleys. The game faded with urban change and the rise of association football and rugby, but persisted in Nelson and a few hotbeds, where dedicated courts stayed in use and local championships kept the tradition alive; Welsh national championships continue to be contested, alongside revived interest through the Nelson club, which traces an unbroken lineage of championship play.
