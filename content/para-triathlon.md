## Basic Information

Para triathlon is swim–bike–run for athletes with physical or visual impairments, governed by World Triathlon (World Triathlon, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Classes are PTWC (wheelchair users, who race a handcycle and a racing wheelchair), PTS2–PTS5 (standing athletes with ambulant impairments, 2 = most severe) and PTVI (visually impaired, racing with a sighted guide who also receives a medal) (World Triathlon, n.d.-c).

Para triathlon debuted at the Rio 2016 Paralympics and at Paris 2024 raced the sprint distance (750 m swim, 20 km bike, 5 km run) at the Pont Alexandre III; the 2026 World Triathlon Para Series runs through Yokohama, Montreal (27 June) and Hamburg (11–12 July) before the World Para Championships in Pontevedra, Spain, on 25 September 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Olympics, n.d.; World Triathlon, n.d.-a).

Leading the women today: Grace Norman of the United States (PTS5) won gold at Paris 2024 and defended her world title in Pontevedra 2026 in 1:08:19, her seventh career world title as the most decorated PTS5 woman in history; Lauren Parker of Australia (pictured) took PTWC gold at Paris 2024 and again in Pontevedra 2026, 65 seconds ahead of America's Kendall Gretsch (2026, September 25; World Triathlon, n.d.-b). Among the men, Chris Hammer of the United States (PTS5) swept the Paris 2024 and 2025–2026 world titles, and Thomas Frühwirth of Austria (PTWC) won in Pontevedra 2026 by 55 seconds (World Triathlon, n.d.-b; 2026, September 25).

## Rules

World Triathlon competition rules apply with para adaptations: at the Paralympics and World Para Championships the sprint distance is 750 m swim, 20 km bike and 5 km run; PTWC athletes use a handcycle on the bike leg and a racing wheelchair on the run, PTS classes use standard or adapted bikes, and PTVI athletes race tethered to a guide in all three legs (World Triathlon, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Transitions are timed and equipped per class (wheelchairs/handcycles parked in assigned boxes); men and women race separately per class; European Para Championships (Besançon 2025) and the Para Series feed the world ranking and LA28 qualification (World Triathlon, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Para triathlon entered the Paralympic programme at Rio 2016 after years of development by the IPC and the former ITU; the picture shows ParalympicGB's paratriathlon team in training in 2015 ahead of that debut (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Early icons include the Netherlands' Jetze Plat, PTWC gold medallist at Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2020, and Great Britain's Dave Ellis, PTVI champion with guide Kevin McDowell at Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2020; at the 2025 European Para Championships in Besançon, Thomas Frühwirth won the PTWC men's race and Eva María Moral Pedrero the PTWC women's (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
