## Basic Information

Mountaineering — also called alpinism — is the sport of climbing mountains on foot over rock, snow, ice and glaciers, using ropes, harnesses, crampons and ice axes; it includes rock climbing, ice climbing and [ski mountaineering](ski-mountaineering.html), which made its Olympic debut at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics at the Stelvio Ski Centre in Bormio, where 36 athletes (18 women and 18 men) contested the sprint on 19 February and the mixed relay on 21 February 2026 (International Ski Mountaineering Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The international federation of alpinism is the UIAA (Union Internationale des Associations d'Alpinisme), founded in 1932 in Chamonix, and the Piolet d'Or — whose first lifetime-achievement award went to Walter Bonatti in 2009 — honors the greatest careers in the sport (UIAA, n.d.). At Milano Cortina 2026 the men's sprint was won by Spain's Oriol Cardona in 2:34.03, the women's sprint by Switzerland's Marianne Fatton in 2:59.77 ahead of France's Emily Harrop and Spain's Ana Alonso, and the mixed relay by France (Harrop/Anselmet) in 26:57.44 ahead of Switzerland and Spain (International Ski Mountaineering Federation, n.d.). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women in ski mountaineering are Olympic champions Marianne Fatton of Switzerland, Emily Harrop of France and Ana Alonso of Spain (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Mountaineering itself has no single rulebook — routes are graded on the UIAA rock scale, the French adjectival scale for ice and mixed climbing, and similar systems, and climbers choose between alpine style (carrying all gear, moving fast and light) and expedition style (fixed camps and large teams) (UIAA, n.d.). Safety equipment — rope, harness, helmet, crampons, ice axe — is mandatory on glaciers and technical terrain, and in competition disciplines such as competitive ice climbing ([ice climbing](ice-climbing.html)) and [sport climbing](sport-climbing.html), the UIAA and IFSC rulebooks define the formats, scoring and safety requirements. Ski mountaineering races, governed by the ISMF, combine uphill climbing on skins with downhill skiing in transition areas with mandatory equipment — skis, boots, poles, helmet and an avalanche transceiver in some events — and the fastest time wins in individual, sprint, mass-start and relay formats; women and men compete in separate categories in all disciplines (International Ski Mountaineering Federation, n.d.).

## History

The UIAA was founded in 1932 in Chamonix, and organized alpinism has grown from the first guided ascents of the 19th century into a global sport with the Piolet d'Or awards since 2009 (UIAA, n.d.). The defining moment came on 29 May 1953, when New Zealander Edmund Hillary and Nepali Sherpa Tenzing Norgay became the first climbers to stand on the summit of Mount Everest (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Italy's Reinhold Messner made the first solo ascent of Everest without supplementary oxygen in 1978 and by 1986 had climbed all fourteen eight-thousanders, while Japan's Junko Tabei became the first woman on Everest on 16 May 1975 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitive ski mountaineering traces back to the first documented civilian race in the Pyrenees in 1927, the Trofeo Mezzalama in 1933 and the Patrouille des Glaciers in 1943; the ISMF was constituted on 27 February 2008 in Champéry, gained IOC recognition in 2016, and the IOC approved ski mountaineering for the Olympic programme on 20 July 2021 — realized at Milano Cortina 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
