## Basic Information

Slalom skateboarding is downhill skateboard racing through courses of plastic cones placed at set spacings, in which riders weave and pump through the gates for the fastest time with the fewest penalties. It is the oldest organised form of skateboarding competition \u2014 the sport's first contest formats of the 1960s and 1970s \u2014 and after decades of dormancy it has enjoyed a strong revival since the 2000s, with world championships and a world ranking contested in tight, hybrid, giant and special slalom formats.

## Rules

A slalom course is set on a downhill slope with timing gates and plastic cones; the racer must pass through or around each cone, and a hit or displaced cone adds a fixed time penalty to the run. The classic formats differ in cone spacing and speed: tight slalom sets cones close together for rapid-fire turns, special slalom spaces them a little wider, hybrid blends the two, and giant slalom sets the widest gate spacings for the highest speeds. Each racer gets multiple runs with the best time plus penalties counting, races are timed to hundredths of a second, and divisions run by age and gender under slalom-community rules and at World Skate's slalom events of the World Skate Games.

## History

Slalom was born with skateboarding itself: the first contests of the 1960s on California hills had slalom as a natural downhill format, and in the 1970s slalom \u2014 alongside freestyle and vertical skateboarding \u2014 led contest culture, with the World Skateboard Championships slalom events of that era crowning icons like Henry Hester. After slalom all but vanished during the 1980s\u20131990s street-and-vert era, an internet-organised revival began around 2000, led by racers in California and Europe, formalised through dedicated websites, timing systems and rankings under the International SlalomSkateboarding Association (ISSA), with world championship events resuming in tight, special, hybrid and giant disciplines. Today's elite scene \u2014 racers such as Latvia's Janis Kuzmins and the USA's Lynn Kramer \u2014 gathers at world title and World Skate Games slalom events (which staged slalom, giant, hybrid and special slalom championships at the 2022 Buenos Aires World Skate Games), while hard-carving slalom boards with front trucks and grippy wheels remain the nearest thing to a downhill slalom race on asphalt.
