## Basic Information

Senet is the most famous ancient Egyptian board game (c. 3100 BC, played until the Roman era): a 3x10 grid of 30 squares; players move pieces according to dice/throw-sticks, with special squares (e.g. the 'House of Rebirth'); the goal is to move all your pieces off the board first. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Mehen](mehen.html), [Latrunculi](latrunculi.html), [Nine Men's Morris](nine-men-s-morris.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Walters Art Museum, n.d.).

## Rules

3x10 board; 5–10 pieces per player (later sets); moves by four throw-sticks or knucklebones; landing on certain squares has effects (e.g. must retreat); blocking rules apply; first to clear all pieces wins (Walters Art Museum, n.d.).

## History

Senet appears in tomb paintings from c. 3100 BC (e.g. Nefertari's tomb, photographed); the Walters Art Museum and British Museum hold original boards and pieces; the Royal Ontario Museum displays a senet board; Berlin's Egyptian Museum has a playing set (2013) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
