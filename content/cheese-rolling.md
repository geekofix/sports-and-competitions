## Basic Information

The Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling and Wake is an annual folk event held on the Spring Bank Holiday (the last Monday of May) at Cooper's Hill in Brockworth, Gloucestershire, England: competitors chase a wheel of Double Gloucester cheese down a 200-yard (180 m) hill with a gradient of about 50 percent (1:2), and the first person over the finish line at the bottom wins the cheese (BBC News, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). The cheese, a round of 7–9 lb (3–4 kg) supplied by a local cheesemaker, is released from the top with about a one-second head start and can reach speeds around 70 mph — fast enough to knock over and injure spectators, and broken bones among racers are common (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 edition on 25 May 2026 — officially the hottest May day on record in the UK — featured seven races: Tom Kopke, a 24-year-old German YouTuber known as "Tooleko," won the first men's downhill race for his third consecutive title, narrowly beating Chris Anderson, the 38-year-old local and 23-time champion (2002–2022) who had come out of retirement after being challenged by Kopke; Alix Heugas of France won the women's downhill race; and the children's uphill races were won by William Anderson (11) and Ariadne (BBC News, 2026; Stroud News and Journal, 2026). Today's leading names are therefore, on the women's side, reigning 2026 women's winner Alix Heugas and the legendary Dionne Carter of New Zealand, who won the ladies' race in 2006 on the third year of a winning streak, pictured below; on the men's side, the 2026 champion Tom Kopke (BBC News, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

There is no world federation for cheese rolling — the event is run by the Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling and Wake committee of volunteers, and Guinness World Records keeps the official record book (most races won), currently held by Chris Anderson with 23 victories between 2002 and 2022 (Guinness World Records, n.d.). The format is simple and brutal: from the top of the hill the cheese is sent rolling after a countdown, and the racers start after it; the first competitor across the finish line at the bottom wins, and the winner keeps the cheese — the cheese itself is never actually caught (Wikipedia, n.d.). The day comprises multiple races with distinct classifications: usually three men's downhill races and one women's downhill race — downhill entrants must be at least 18 years old — plus less hazardous uphill races open to minors, in recent editions split into children's categories (under 11 and 11 and over in 2025/2026) and a mixed adult uphill race (BBC News, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's and women's downhill races are separate classifications, while the adult uphill race is mixed; there are no weight classes, no protective-gear requirements (racers wear whatever they like), and no timed heats — every race is a single mass start (Wikipedia, n.d.). Because the hill is privately owned and the event is unlicensed, the organizers cancelled the official 2010 edition on health-and-safety grounds, but unofficial races have continued, and the event remains one of England's strangest sporting traditions (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

The earliest known written attestation of the Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling and Wake dates to 1836, when the Gloucester town crier recorded the event, though the tradition was already noted in 1826 and may be older still — some trace it to a pagan spring fertility rite in which a burning wheel of cheese was rolled down the hill to bless the fields (Wikipedia, n.d.). The "Wake" part of the name refers to the wake celebration that followed the race. For most of its history the event was a purely local affair, but from the 1970s onward international winners appeared, and by 2013 The Guardian called it a world-famous event with winners from Australia, Belgium, Canada, Egypt, Germany, Japan, New Zealand and the United States (Wikipedia, n.d.). The modern era of record-keeping began with Chris Anderson of Brockworth, who won 23 races between 2002 and 2022 and was named a Guinness World Record holder before the 2026 races, and on the women's side with Dionne Carter of New Zealand, the 2004–2006 ladies' champion pictured above (BBC News, 2026; Guinness World Records, n.d.). In 2013 a foam replica replaced the real cheese for safety reasons; a real wheel of Double Gloucester was restored the following year and has been used since, including the 2026 edition (Wikipedia, n.d.).
