## Basic Information

Sungka (also sungkaan) is the traditional mancala of the Philippines, played especially in Mindanao and the Visayas: a wooden board with two rows of seven pits plus two larger end pits (rumah, “house”), and 98 cowrie shells (7 per pit). It is played by men and women of all ages — often as a pastime in cafés — and sungka tournaments are held across the archipelago. The name is a corruption of Sanskrit sankhya (“number”). Within the mancala and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Congkak](congkak.html), [Kalah](kalah.html), [Mancala](mancala.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Each player owns one row of seven pits and the store at their right. A move scoops all shells from one of the player's pits and sows them one by one counterclockwise around all 14 pits and the player's own store (not the opponent's). If the last shell lands in the player's own store, the turn is repeated. If it lands in an occupied pit on the player's side, relay sowing continues; captures follow the standard sungka rules (last shell in an empty pit on your side captures the opposite pit's shells; chains possible). The player with more shells in the store at the end wins; a player unable to move loses their remaining shells to the opponent.

## History

Sungka is one of the Philippine games of Southeast Asian mancala lineage — related to congkak in Malaysia and Indonesia and, further back, to South Indian pallanguzhi. It has been played in the archipelago for centuries with cowrie shells on carved wooden boards (some antique boards are museum pieces, e.g., the Mindanao Bangsamoro Islamic game set). Sungka remains a popular parlor game in the Philippines and among Filipino communities abroad.
