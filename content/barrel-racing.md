## Basic Information

Barrel racing is a rodeo timed event in which a horse and rider race a cloverleaf pattern around three barrels in an arena. The fastest clean time wins; knocking over a barrel adds a 5-second penalty. It is the premier women's event in professional rodeo, governed in the United States by the Women's Professional Rodeo Association (WPRA) and the PRCA, with the WPRA World Championship deciding the season title.

## Rules

Under WPRA and PRCA rules, the standard pattern is three barrels set in a triangle (first barrel 20-21 ft from the starting line, 30-35 ft between barrels). The rider may start from either side but must complete the full cloverleaf — left-right-left or right-left-right — without breaking the pattern. Each barrel knocked over adds 5 seconds. The clock starts and stops as the horse crosses the start/finish line. The fastest times, usually in the 15-17 second range at the elite level, win.

## History

Barrel racing developed from women's exhibition riding at rodeos in the 1930s-40s, when female contestants were barred from roughstock events. The Women's Rodeo Association (founded 1948, now the WPRA) made barrel racing its championship event. Legends like Charmgillett and contemporary stars such as Hailey Kinsel have turned it into one of rodeo's most watched events, with the WPRA finals drawing national television coverage.
