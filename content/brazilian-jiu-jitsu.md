## Basic Information

Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) is a grappling martial art and combat sport that emphasizes ground fighting, submissions (joint locks and chokes) and positional control, using the principle that a smaller person can defeat a larger opponent through leverage. The main international federation is the International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation (IBJJF), which organizes the World Jiu-Jitsu Championship (gi and no-gi); United World Wrestling (UWW) also runs grappling world championships including gi and no-gi BJJ-style rules.

## Rules

At IBJJF World Championships, competitors are divided by belt rank, age, weight and gender, and fight in the gi (kimono) or no-gi events. A match lasts 5-10 minutes depending on division; points are awarded for takedowns (2), guard passes (3), sweeps (2), mount (4) and back control (4), and a submission (joint lock or choke) or opponent disqualification wins immediately. At the ADCC Submission Wrestling World Championship (the other major no-gi event), matches are 10 minutes with points only in the first half. UWW grappling events use similar point systems with pins/submissions as victory conditions.

## History

BJJ developed in Brazil in the 1910s-1920s when Mitsuyo Maeda, a student of judo master Jigoro Kano, taught the Gracie family, who refined the art into "Gracie jiu-jitsu". The Gracie family popularized it through challenge matches and the first UFC in 1993, where Royce Gracie won. The IBJJF was founded in 1994 and has run the World Jiu-Jitsu Championship since 1996; the ADCC was founded in 1998 by Sheik Tahnoon Bin Saeed Al Nahyan. BJJ is now practiced worldwide and is the base art of modern MMA.
