## Basic Information

Bezique (bésigue) is a French trick-taking game for two (or more) with a 64-card double deck: players declare 'bezigue' combinations (four queens etc.) and score in tricks; it is the ancestor of pinochle. Within the mind, board and card games on this site, its closest relatives are [Piquet](piquet.html), [Pinochle](pinochle.html), [Écarté](ecarte.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Caillebotte, G, 1880).

## Rules

64-card deck (two standard decks two 32-card decks (7–A twice)); declarations: four queens (bezigue, 500), four kings (400), four jacks (200), four aces (150), royal marriage— ; trick-taking to— first to 2,000— game to 1,000+ points; Caillebotte's 1880 painting shows a game (Caillebotte, G, 1880).

## History

Bezique was invented in France in the 19th century (c. 1850s) and directly inspired pinochle (which uses a double deck); Caillebotte painted 'Partita a bezique' in 1880 (Louvre Abu Dhabi) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
