## Basic Information

Synchronized skating is team precision skating on ice: a team of up to 20 skaters performs a choreographed program of formations, blocks, circles, lines, intersections, lifts, spins and footwork to music, judged like figure skating; it is governed by the International Skating Union (ISU) and is not an Olympic sport, with the annual ISU World Synchronized Skating Championships as its pinnacle event since 2000 (International Skating Union, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The 2026 ISU World Synchronized Skating Championships were held in the Salzburgarena, Austria, on 10–11 April 2026, with 22 teams and 489 skaters from 17 ISU Members; Canada's Les Suprêmes of Saint-Léonard, Québec (pictured celebrating), won their fourth world title with 233.00 points, ahead of the United States' Team Haydenettes (229.28) and the defending champions, Finland's Helsinki Rockettes (228.67, with the best free skate of 156.23); the new nine-skater Synchro9 format was demonstrated for the first time, and the 2027 edition will be held in Nottingham, 2–3 April (International Skating Union, n.d.-b; Skate Canada, 2026, April 11; International Skating Union, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under ISU regulations, a senior team consists of 16–20 skaters and performs a short program and a free skate of about three and four minutes; a technical panel scores the required elements — blocks, circles, creative elements, intersections, lifts, lines, moving formations, spins and wheels — and a program components panel scores composition, performance and skating skills; the combined short-program and free-skate totals decide the rankings (2026 winning total: 233.00) (International Skating Union, n.d.-a).

Levels range from basic novice to senior at national championships (e.g., the U.S. Synchronized Skating Championships), with junior teams at the Junior World Challenge Cup and senior teams at the World Championships; men and women may compete together on senior teams, and teams represent clubs or countries; the sport is judged under the same ISU judging system as singles, pairs and ice dance (International Skating Union, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Synchronized skating grew out of the precision skating teams of the 1950s–1970s; the first ISU World Synchronized Skating Championships were held in Minneapolis, Minnesota, in 2000, won by Sweden's Team Surprise, and the event has been held annually except in 2020 and 2021 (COVID-19) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Until the 2020s the podium was dominated by Finland and Sweden — Marigold IceUnity with five titles, Helsinki Rockettes with four, Team Surprise with six — before Canada's Les Suprêmes won four consecutive titles from 2023 to 2026; teams pictured at the 2009 European Championships show the international spread of the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).
