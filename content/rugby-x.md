## Basic Information

Rugby X (styled RugbyX) is a fast, continuous variant of rugby sevens devised by Ben Ryan — the coach of Fiji’s gold-medal team at the Rio 2016 Olympics — together with World Rugby and the Rugby Football Union (RFU) (Wikipedia, n.d.). Teams consist of five players, typically three forwards and two backs, and matches last only ten minutes with no halftime, so play is near-continuous with quick tap-and-go restarts instead of set pieces (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Rugby, n.d.). The concept was launched with the first tournament at the O2 Arena in London in October 2019, and Rugby X has since been staged as a festival-style event combining elite men’s and women’s tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.).

Rugby X is designed as entertainment as much as sport: short matches, music and a party atmosphere at arena venues, aimed at bringing rugby to new audiences (World Rugby, n.d.). Scoring follows sevens conventions — five points for a try and two for a conversion — and tournaments use pool stages followed by knockout finals (World Rugby, n.d.). Both a men’s and a women’s competition are contested, with mixed-gender festival events increasingly common (Wikipedia, n.d.). The game is played on a full-size rugby pitch, which is what distinguishes it from the reduced-pitch formats such as rugby tens (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Rugby X is played under its own Laws administered by World Rugby: five players per side, ten-minute matches without a halftime, and continuous play with rapid restarts — the ball is put back in play with a quick tap rather than a scrum or line-out (World Rugby, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Tries are worth five points and conversions two, as in sevens, and the standard rugby offside, knock-on and foul rules apply in adapted form (World Rugby, n.d.). Tournaments typically run pool groups followed by cup-plate knockout stages over a single day at one venue (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women play the same format in separate competitions (World Rugby, n.d.).

## History

After coaching Fiji to Olympic sevens gold at Rio 2016, New Zealander Ben Ryan developed Rugby X with World Rugby and the RFU as a shorter, faster spectacle; the inaugural RugbyX tournament was held at the O2 Arena in London on 29 October 2019 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The format has since been showcased at further arena events, with national and invitational teams competing in the men’s and women’s tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.; World Rugby, n.d.). Ryan’s idea was to keep the continuous, open play of sevens while removing the stop-start set pieces that slow the game down (Wikipedia, n.d.).
