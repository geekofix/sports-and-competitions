## Basic Information

Helice (French for "propeller", also called "ZZ" shooting) is a spectacular FITASC clay discipline: two armoured-glass targets shaped like propeller blades are launched from a bunker trap in front of the shooter, who fires from a central cage as they fly away — the double target and the speed make it the hardest of the FITASC disciplines (FITASC, n.d.). It belongs to the clay-target family and is unique to FITASC.

![Shooter at the Helice European Championship (Helice EB), target launch](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Urbin_Gy%C3%B6rgy_52._Helice_EB.jpg?width=560)

*Figure: Csermanek. (2020-08-14). Shooter at the Helice European Championship (Helice EB), target launch [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Urbin_Gy%C3%B6rgy_52._Helice_EB.jpg*

The ZZ target is a 5-inch armoured glass disc with two arms; a "double" (both targets at once) is worth extra and the round is 30 targets (FITASC, n.d.). The shooter stands in a cage at the centre of the layout while targets are thrown from traps angled to the left and right at speeds up to 90 km/h (Wikipedia, n.d.). Scoring is 1 point per broken target, with the double counting as two (FITASC, n.d.).

## Rules

Rules: 12 gauge, max 24 g shot (28 g in some events), two shells maximum loaded, gun mounted before the call (FITASC, n.d.). World and European championships are on the FITASC calendar — the 57th European Championship Finale European Beretta Cup was held at Le Touquet, France, in June 2025 (FITASC, 2025). Men and women compete in separate categories at championships; the discipline is also shot as "helice nationale" in national variants (FITASC, n.d.).

## History

Helice shooting originated in the Mediterranean in the early 20th century with live pigeons released from boxes; the armoured-glass "helice" target was introduced in France in the 1950s and became the standard FITASC discipline (Wikipedia, n.d.). FITASC has governed it internationally since its founding in 1921, and today helice world championships draw entries from Europe, the Americas, Africa and Asia (FITASC, n.d.).
