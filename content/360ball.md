## Basic Information

360ball (styled 360BALL) is a young racquet sport played on a circular court with no fixed sides. Players use a racquet to hit a rubber ball onto a centrally placed concave disc, which rebounds the ball in an unpredictable direction; in doubles, teammates may pass the ball to each other between strikes.

## Rules

360ball is played as singles (1v1) or doubles (2v2) on a circular court — a permanent court with walls, a mobile inflatable court, or just a boundary rope with a starter set. Players rotate 360° around the central disc, and a point is won when the ball bounces on the disc and the opponent cannot return it, or when the ball is hit out of court. Matches are played in sets, similar to tennis. Because there is no world governing federation yet, exact scoring, court size and disc dimensions vary between the leagues in South Africa, Spain, France and Germany — a typical example of a young sport whose official rules are still being standardized.

## History

360ball was invented in South Africa by the brothers Mark and John Collins — an early version in 1990, recreated in 2010 in Knysna on the Garden Route. In 2011 it won the ISPO Brand New award in Munich as the first new ball sport since 1950. The Collins brothers' company Magnetic South promotes the sport internationally.
