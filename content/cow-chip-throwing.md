## Basic Information

Cow chip throwing is an American novelty contest in which competitors throw dried cow pats — "cow chips" — for distance. The World Championship Cow Chip Throwing Contest has been held in Beaver, Oklahoma, since 1969, on the third Saturday of April at the Beaver County Fairgrounds as part of the Cimarron Territory Celebration (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.). Each contestant throws two chips, and only chips more than 6 inches (15 cm) in diameter are allowed; divisions exist for women, men, VIPs and teams (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.). The chips are collected from pastures as dried manure patties — no animal is present at or harmed in the contest, which is a harvest by-product competition, not an animal event (Modern Farmer, 2014). The 2026 edition again drew competitors to the fairgrounds, with the team competition won by a Farm Bureau team whose combined throws totaled 373.8 inches (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.). The standing world record is 185 feet (Modern Farmer, 2014). The contest is a fully mixed event: women and men compete in their own divisions under identical rules, and family teams are a tradition (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.).

## Rules

Contestants throw their chips from behind a foul line, standing still with both feet behind the line, as in other throwing contests at county fairs; the farthest point reached by the chip counts, and if a chip breaks in mid-air the farthest piece is measured (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.). Each competitor gets two chips and the better of the two distances is recorded; only chips exceeding 6 inches (15 cm) in diameter are measured — smaller fragments do not count (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.). Divisions are run for women, men, VIPs and teams, and the winners of each division receive trophies; the team event adds the best combined distances of the team's throwers (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.). The contest is judged by officials on the fairgrounds, and throwing order is drawn by lot before each division (Modern Farmer, 2014). Because the chips are dried and inert, the only safety concern is the throwing area itself, which is fenced off from spectators (Modern Farmer, 2014).


## History

The Beaver contest began in 1969 and became the recognized world championship of cow chip throwing, drawing competitors and spectators to the small Oklahoma town every April as part of the wider Cimarron Territory Celebration, which also features a parade, chili cook-off and other festivities (Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.; The Oklahoman, 2022). Similar chip- or pat-throwing contests exist at other US fairs, but Beaver remains the best-known, covered regularly by regional newspapers and magazines, and its winners are treated as world champions (Modern Farmer, 2014). After a hiatus during the pandemic years, the contest returned to full scale and continues annually; the 2026 event on the third Saturday of April again crowned individual and team champions at the Beaver County Fairgrounds (The Oklahoman, 2022; Beaver County Chamber of Commerce, n.d.).
