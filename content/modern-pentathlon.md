## Basic Information

Modern pentathlon is an Olympic multisport that combines five disciplines: épée fencing, 200 m freestyle swimming, equestrian show jumping on an unfamiliar horse, and the combined laser run of laser pistol shooting and cross-country running. It was created by Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympic Games, and has been on the Olympic program continuously since Stockholm 1912. The sport is governed worldwide by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM), which has member federations in more than 130 countries. From the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics, obstacle course racing will replace show jumping as the fifth discipline.

## Rules

In the Olympic format, the fencing ranking round consists of one-minute, one-touch épée bouts against every other competitor, followed by a bonus round of direct elimination. The 200 m freestyle swim is scored against a target time, and the show jumping round starts at 300 points with penalties for knockdowns, refusals and overtime, and elimination after a fall. Points from these disciplines determine the staggered start of the final laser run: athletes alternate short cross-country legs (about 600-800 m) with five laser shots on a target, which must be completed within 50 seconds, and the first athlete to cross the finish line wins. Since Paris 2024 the semifinal and final have been condensed into a roughly 90-minute session at a single venue, with riding held first.

## History

Coubertin designed the modern pentathlon to model the skills of a 19th-century cavalry officer who had to ride an unfamiliar horse, fight with a sword, shoot a pistol, swim and run to deliver a message behind enemy lines. It debuted at the Stockholm 1912 Olympics, a team event was added at Helsinki 1952, and the women's event joined the program at Sydney 2000. The one-day format was introduced at Atlanta 1996, and at London 2012 the separate pistol shooting and running events were merged into the laser run using laser guns. Paris 2024 brought the new 90-minute single-venue format at the Palace of Versailles, where Egypt's Ahmed El-Gendy won the first Olympic gold by an African pentathlete. Hungary is the most successful nation, with Michelle Gulyás winning women's gold in Paris 2024.
