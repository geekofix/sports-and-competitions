## Basic Information

Competition ice climbing is governed by the International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation (UIAA), which has regulated the sport since the first UIAA Ice Climbing World Cup in 2000; it has two disciplines — lead, climbed with dry-tooling techniques on largely artificial bolted structures with some natural ice features (routes at least 15 m on structures at least 12 m high), and speed, raced head-to-head on a standardized 12–18 m wall of solid ice, top-roped (International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation [UIAA], n.d.-a, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The annual World Cup (the "Ice Climbing World Tour") runs from December to March with three to six events, and biennial World Championships award lead, speed and combined titles, with youth categories U16, U19 and U22; the 2026 competition regulations were approved on 9 October 2025 (UIAA, n.d.-a). Women compete in the identical disciplines with separate rankings — among the leading current women is Russia's Maria Tolokonina, dominant in both lead and speed, while Iranian climber Zeinabkobra Moosavi, shown in the photograph, represented Iran at the 2019 World Championship in Kirov (UIAA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the 2026 UIAA Ice Climbing Competition Regulations, every athlete must wear a UIAA-approved harness and helmet, one pair of crampons and two ice axes that fit the mandatory "Ice Box," non-fingerless gloves and clothing covering the whole body; tool leashes are prohibited (so tools cannot be used as aid), and the use of heel spurs for resting is controlled (UIAA, n.d.-a). In lead, athletes climb within a set time limit, clipping quickdraws sequentially; the height reached (or distance along traverses/roofs) determines the ranking, and top-rope attempts are permitted in some rounds (UIAA, n.d.-a). In speed, two athletes race simultaneously up the standardized ice wall on parallel lanes, top-roped, and advance through qualification, semifinal and final brackets by time (UIAA, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). World Championships combine lead, speed and a combined title; the World Cup season crowns overall lead, speed and combined champions; men and women compete under identical rules in separate categories, and youth athletes compete in U16, U19 and U22 brackets (UIAA, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## History

Competition ice climbing grew out of alpine ice and waterfall climbing in the 1970s and 1980s, when climbers first began timing themselves on steep frozen waterfalls in France, Italy and North America; the UIAA formalized the sport with the first Ice Climbing World Cup in 2000, won in men's lead by Canada's Will Gadd (Wikipedia, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). The speed discipline on standardized ice walls was added in the early 2000s and became a separate world title; the 2000s and 2010s were dominated by athletes such as Austria's Harald Berger and the Russian brothers Maxim and Alexey Tomilov in lead, South Korea's Park Hee-yong, and Nikolai Kuzovlev, who took multiple world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Women's competition has run alongside the men's since the early World Cups, with identical disciplines and walls (UIAA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
