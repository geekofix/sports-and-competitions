## Basic Information

Dressage is the equestrian discipline often described as "horse ballet": horse and rider perform a memorized series of movements (a "test") in a rectangular arena, judged on precision, impulsion, obedience and harmony. It is governed by the Fédération Equestre Internationale (FEI) and is one of the three Olympic equestrian disciplines, alongside eventing and show jumping.

## Rules

At FEI competitions, including the Olympic Games and FEI World Championships, horse-rider pairs perform a prescribed test (set by the FEI Dressage Rules) in a 20 m x 60 m (or 20 m x 40 m for lower levels) arena marked with letters (A, K, V, E, S, H, C, M, B, F). Judges award marks from 0 to 10 for each movement and for collective marks (gaits, impulsion, submission, rider's position and aids), with the percentage score determining the ranking. Olympic dressage uses the Grand Prix test for individuals and the Grand Prix Special for the team competition. Riders must wear approved helmets and dress (top hat or approved helmet at the highest levels), and horses must meet minimum age requirements (6 years at the Olympics, 7 at World Championships for Grand Prix level). The FEI Dressage Rules define illegal equipment, doping control and the judging system.

## History

Dressage derives from classical cavalry training of the Renaissance, systematized in works such as those of Xenophon and later Federico Grisone and Antoine de Pluvinel. The modern sport was formalized in the late 19th century, and dressage entered the Olympic program in Stockholm 1912, won by Swedish officer Axel von Fersen. The FEI (founded 1921) governs the sport internationally and has run World Championships since 1966. The German, Dutch and Scandinavian schools dominated the 20th century, with legendary riders such as Hans Günter Winkler, Reiner Klimke and Isabell Werth (most decorated Olympic equestrian of all time with multiple team and individual gold medals). Charlotte Dujardin of Great Britain revolutionized the sport in the 2010s with the horse Valegro.
