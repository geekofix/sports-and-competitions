## Basic Information

Nordic combined is a winter sport that merges the two disciplines of [ski jumping](ski-jumping.html) and [cross-country skiing](cross-country-skiing.html) into a single competition: athletes first take a jump on a large hill, and the jumping result is converted into a time handicap for the subsequent cross-country race (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a). The sport is governed worldwide by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS) and has been on the Olympic Winter Games program since the first Winter Olympics at Chamonix 1924 (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate events — the women's individual Gundersen debuted at the World Championships in 2021 and at the Olympics at Beijing 2022 — and today's leading athletes include Norway's Jarl Magnus Riiber on the men's side and Norway's Gyda Westvold Hansen, the 2022 Olympic champion, on the women's side; Austria's Tobias Kammerlander, shown below, is another front-runner of the current generation.

## Rules

In the classic Gundersen method, each athlete takes one jump (two jumps in some team events), and the jumping points are converted into seconds on the start list for the cross-country race, so the leading jumper starts first and every following athlete starts as many seconds behind as the point deficit; the athlete who crosses the finish line first wins (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a). The main individual formats at World Cup and World Championship level are the Individual Gundersen (one jump plus 10 km), the Compact (one jump plus 7.5 km) and the Mass Start (10 km cross-country first, with the results converted into a time handicap for a final jumping round) (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b). Team events comprise four athletes per nation, each jumping and skiing a 5 km leg, and there is a mixed team event with two women and two men per nation (International Olympic Committee, n.d.). Races are timed electronically to hundredths of a second, and the athlete with the best combined result over the two disciplines wins; men's and women's competitions use the same formats and regulations, with women racing 5 km (individual Gundersen) instead of 10 km (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-b).

## History

Nordic combined originated in Norway in the late 19th century, where soldiers and civilians combined ski jumping and cross-country skiing over the winter terrain; the first recorded competition is the Holmenkollen event of 1892, and the sport debuted at the first Winter Olympics in Chamonix 1924 (International Olympic Committee, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Norway dominated the early decades, with Thorleif Haug one of the most successful athletes of the era — three Olympic golds in 1924 — and later East Germany and Germany became the leading nations (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FIS Nordic Combined World Cup has been held since the 1983-84 season, and women's events at senior World Championship level were introduced in 2021, with the Olympic debut at Beijing 2022 (International Ski and Snowboard Federation, n.d.-a). German athletes such as Eric Frenzel, a three-time Olympic champion (2014-2018), defined the modern era before the current generation (Wikipedia, n.d.).
