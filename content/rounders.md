## Basic Information

Rounders is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams that alternate between batting and fielding: the batter tries to hit an underarm-pitched ball and run around four bases (posts) in a square, scoring a "rounder" for a full circuit, while the fielding team tries to get three batters out per half-innings (Wikipedia, n.d.). The earliest nationally formalized rules were drawn up by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) in Ireland in 1884, where the game is played on a 70 × 70 m square pitch with bases 25 m apart and up to nine players per side; in England and Wales the National Rounders Association (formed 1943) plays a smaller version with bases 12 m apart (GAA, n.d.; Rounders England, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). GAA rounders runs All-Ireland championships at junior, intermediate and senior levels, in single-sex and mixed teams — women's (ladies') rounders is a full championship code alongside the mixed and men's competitions (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 All-Ireland championships were contested across all codes in 2026 (GAA, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the GAA rules, the pitch is a 70 m square with four bases 25 m apart; teams field up to nine players and may make five substitutions (GAA, n.d.). A game consists of five innings (seven in All-Ireland semi-finals and finals); each innings has two halves, and a half ends after three outs (GAA, n.d.). The pitcher delivers the sliotar (ball circumference 22.7–25.5 cm) underarm from the pitching mat, and a "good ball" passes over home base between the batter's knee and shoulder; the batter gets three good balls and must run on the third whether or not they hit it (GAA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Bats are 70–110 cm long and up to 7 cm in diameter (GAA, n.d.). A batter is out if the hit ball is caught before bouncing, or if a fielder holding the ball touches a base before the runner arrives; runners score one rounder for completing all four posts on one hit, and a half rounder for reaching the second post (Rounders England, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The team with the most rounders after the innings wins; knockout ties are decided by extra innings and replays (GAA, n.d.). The English code (Rounders England) uses a smaller pitch with bases 12 m apart and similar scoring, with two innings typical in schools and clubs (Rounders England, n.d.). Women's, men's and mixed teams all compete under the GAA structure (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Rounders originates in England, most likely from the older game of stool ball, and the first printed rules appeared in William Clarke's The Boy's Own Book in 1828, which also described an early form of baseball (Wikipedia, n.d.). Associations were set up in Liverpool and Scotland in 1889, and the first nationally formalized rules were drawn up by the GAA in Ireland in 1884, where rounders (cluiche corr) is one of the four official GAA sports alongside Gaelic football, hurling and handball (Wikipedia, n.d.). Play in Ireland faded for decades until 1958, when the Antrim club Erin's Own staged the first formal match under the GAA Official Guide, led by promoter Peadar Ó Tuatáin; the Tailteann Games had kept the game alive earlier in the 20th century (Wikipedia, n.d.). In Britain the National Rounders Association was formed in 1943 and now governs the English game (Rounders England, n.d.).
