## Basic Information

The deadlift is the third and final lift in competitive [powerlifting](powerlifting.html): the barbell is pulled from the floor to a standing position with hips and shoulders locked, using one continuous motion and no assistance from momentum. It is the simplest of the three lifts — no referee start command, just the bar, the floor and the clock — and it is also contested as a standalone single-lift event and as a popular test of strength beyond organized sport, including in strongman competition.

## Rules

Under IPF technical rules, the bar is loaded horizontally on the platform and the lifter grips it with either a conventional or alternate grip (hooks grip allowed); supportive equipment in classic lifting is limited to a belt, wrist wraps and shin-length socks, which are mandatory to protect the shins, while equipped lifters may wear a supportive deadlift suit. The lifter pulls the bar upward in one continuous motion with no downward movement, and the lift is complete only when knees are locked, shoulders are back and the whole body is motionless and upright in a finished position; the referee then gives the "down" command and the bar is returned to the platform. Faults include supporting the bar on the thighs during the pull (hitching), any downward motion of the bar, stepping or feet movement, releasing the bar before the command, or dropping the bar at the finish rather than keeping hands on it. Each lifter receives three attempts, and single-lift deadlift championships are sanctioned on the same technical basis.

## History

The loaded barbell pull from the floor is as old as weightlifting itself, and the term "dead lift" — lifting "dead" weight without momentum — was established in 19th-century strongman performances, making it a staple of the "odd lift" contests from which powerlifting was standardized. It became powerlifting's closing discipline, its drama matched by its simplicity, and the lift has kept a life of its own far beyond the IPF: strongman's silver-dollar and car-deadlift variants, CrossFit workouts and gym culture standardized the deadlift as the classic measure of full-body strength, while powerlifting's equipped era produced enormous suited totals and the classic era renewed a raw record race. IPF single-lift deadlift world records continue to be reset in all categories; outside the IPF, the heaviest confirmed pulls (with straps and in strongman formats) exceed 500 kg, with the strongest powerlifters and strongmen of recent decades, such as the British lifter Eddie Hall and Iceland's Hafthor Bjornsson in strongman, and the IPF's own world champions, defining the discipline.
