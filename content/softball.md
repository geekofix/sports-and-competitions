## Basic Information

Softball is the bat-and-ball game derived from baseball in which a pitcher underhand-delivers a larger ball to batters on a smaller diamond; it is governed worldwide by the World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC), formed in 2013 from the International Softball Federation founded in 1952 (WBSC, n.d.-b). Fastpitch softball — the Olympic variant — is played on a 60-foot diamond with a 43-foot pitching distance in women's play and games of seven innings (WBSC, n.d.-a). Softball was an Olympic sport from Atlanta 1996 to Beijing 2008 — the United States won gold in 1996, 2000 and 2004 and Japan at Tokyo 2020 — was absent from Paris 2024 and returns at Los Angeles 2028 in Oklahoma City (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). At the 2025 World Games in Chengdu, the United States women beat Japan to gold behind Tiare Jennings' walk-off home run, and the fastest recorded pitch is 79.4 mph, thrown by Karlyn Pickens in May 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the current leading women are Tiare Jennings and Karlyn Pickens of the United States national team, the 2025 World Games champions; the US men's national team competes at WBSC men's World Cups.

## Rules

In fastpitch softball, the pitcher must deliver the 12-inch ball underhand with a windmill motion from 43 feet in women's play (46 feet in men's fastpitch) toward home plate, where the batter tries to hit it; the defence fields nine players on a diamond with 60-foot base paths (WBSC, n.d.-a). A game lasts seven innings and the team with the most runs wins; a run scores when a runner legally touches all bases including home plate (WBSC, n.d.-a). The batter is out on three strikes, a caught fly ball, or a force or tag out; four balls produce a walk, and base runners may steal but leave early on a pitched ball at their own risk (WBSC, n.d.-a). Fouls include illegal pitches (a delivery without the required windmill motion is a ball), batting out of turn and interference, all signalled by the umpires (WBSC, n.d.-a). Tournament formats at the Olympics, World Games and WBSC World Cups are round-robin followed by medal games; age-group World Cups exist from under-12 upward, such as the 2025 U-15 Women's World Cup in Caronno Pertusella, Italy, won by Japan over Puerto Rico 4–0 (WBSC, n.d.-b). Women's fastpitch is the Olympic discipline with the 43-foot mound and 60-foot bases; men's fastpitch uses 46 feet; slowpitch, played with a 16-inch ball and an arc pitch, is a separate discipline (see our slowpitch page) (WBSC, n.d.-a).

## History

Softball was invented on Thanksgiving Day 1887 in Chicago, when George Hancock, a reporter for the Chicago Board of Trade, improvised a game at the Farragut Boat Club using a boxing glove as a ball and a broom handle as a bat; the first rulebook followed in 1889 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The game spread under names such as kitten ball and diamond ball, and at the 1933 Chicago World's Fair Leo Fischer and Michael Pauley organised a tournament that drew about 70,000 spectators, prompting the founding of the Amateur Softball Association in 1933 — now USA Softball — which standardised the rules by 1936 through a Joint Rules Committee (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ASA moved its headquarters to Oklahoma City in 1966 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Retired icons of women's fastpitch include Jennie Finch (Olympic gold 2004, retired 2010), Dot Richardson (Olympic gold 1996 and 2000) and Cat Osterman, who retired from international play in 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
