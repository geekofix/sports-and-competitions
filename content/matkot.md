## Basic Information

Matkot is a beach paddleball game played mainly in Israel, where it is often called the national beach sport. Two or more players stand on the sand and hit a small rubber ball back and forth with wooden paddles, trying to keep the ball in the air for as many shots as possible. The name matkot comes from matka, the Hebrew word for the paddle. Similar games are played elsewhere, for example frescobol in Brazil and kadima in the United States.

## Rules

Matkot has no formal rules, points or winners in its classic form: the goal is cooperative, to keep the ball rallying as long as possible. The paddles are traditionally made of wood, with short handles and a round head about 30 cm in diameter; modern paddles are also made of carbon fiber. The standard ball is the same small rubber ball used in squash, though beginners sometimes use a lighter, bouncier ball. Players must keep a safe distance from lifeguard stations, and the first organized Israeli matkot competition was held in 2000, capped by a national championship in Tel Aviv that has been held each summer since.

## History

Matkot has been played on the beaches of Tel Aviv since the 1920s, during the British Mandate period. A 1932 painting by the Israeli artist Nahum Gutman shows two young men with rounded paddles playing on a Tel Aviv beach, and some players trace the game to immigrants from Libya, Turkey or Poland in the early 20th century. The game became a fixture of Israeli beach culture, with a dedicated paddle museum run by veteran player Amnon Nissim in Tel Aviv, and national championships have been held each summer since 2000.
