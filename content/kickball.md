## Basic Information

Kickball is a team game derived from baseball in which an inflated rubber ball is rolled by the pitcher and kicked by the batter, who then runs around four bases; the first documented game was played in the United States around 1917, and the game spread from the 1920s mainly through school physical-education classes before becoming a popular playground game in the 1970s (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The world governing body for adult play is the World Kickball Association (WAKA, now operating as CLUBWAKA), founded in 1998 in Washington, D.C., with sanctioned leagues in more than 35 U.S. states and abroad, and the season culminates in the Founder's Cup in Las Vegas each October — a three-day, 72-team event held as part of Wakapalooza (World Kickball Association, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

Kickball is played by women and men together in most social leagues — co-ed divisions are the standard — and at championship level there are separate men's, women's and co-ed divisions, so all genders compete on equal terms (World Kickball Association, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

WAKA's official rules are played on a 60-foot softball diamond with the 10-inch red WAKA ball inflated to 1.5 psi; the pitcher rolls the ball on the ground toward home plate (bouncies — kicks that bounce before reaching the plate — are allowed), and the kicker kicks from the kicking box and runs to first base (World Kickball Association, n.d.-b; World Kickball Association, n.d.-a).

A team fields 10 players (minimum 8 to start), with pitcher and catcher among them; an inning ends after three outs — a strikeout (three strikes or fouls), a caught fly ball, or a tag/force out — and games last seven innings or 55 minutes, whichever comes first, with any inning begun before the 50-minute mark allowed to finish; leading off and stealing bases are forbidden, and head shots are not allowed; a tie is broken by an accuracy kick toward second base (World Kickball Association, n.d.-b).

## History

Kickball was likely invented around 1910–1917 in the United States and spread through public-school PE classes from the 1920s; related games include soccer baseball in Canada, football rounders in the United Kingdom and foot baseball in South Korea (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The adult league era began when WAKA was founded in Washington, D.C., in 1998 — it was named one of America's fastest-growing private companies by Inc. magazine in 2010 — and after a 2005–2008 legal dispute with a rival league over the official rules, WAKA's rulebook became the de facto world standard, now maintained as CLUBWAKA (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; World Kickball Association, n.d.-b; World Kickball Association, n.d.-a).
