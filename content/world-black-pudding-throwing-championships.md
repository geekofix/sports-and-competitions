## Basic Information

The World Black Pudding Throwing Championships is an annual contest held in Ramsbottom, Greater Manchester in England, in which competitors hurl black puddings at a stack of Yorkshire puddings balanced on a platform some 20 feet high, aiming to knock them down — turning the inter-county jest about Lancashire and Yorkshire rivalry into a celebrated village competition.

The event is part of Ramsbottom's annual summer festival weekend each September and draws competitors, spectators and national media coverage to the town.

## Rules

Each competitor gets a set number of black puddings (traditionally three), thrown underarm from behind a marked line at the target: a stack of Yorkshire puddings perched on a platform around 20 feet above the ground. A throw scores by dislodging at least one Yorkshire pudding from the stack, and the thrower who dislodges the most wins, with sudden-death throw-offs settling ties. Only regulation black puddings may be thrown, and the comic framing of Lancashire black pudding against Yorkshire pudding is part of the event's constitution.

## History

The event was revived in its modern form in 1984 by the landlord of the Oaks pub at the time in Ramsbottom to boost trade, but its mythic origin evokes a legendary War of the Roses incident said to have been fought with thrown food between the rival counties of Yorkshire (Yorkshire puddings) and Lancashire (black puddings). Now tied to the Ramsbottom festival weekend, the contest has drawn national press and television coverage, moving from the pub yard to larger festival venues while keeping the inter-county comic tradition alive.
