## Basic Information

The World Black Pudding Throwing Championships are held annually in Ramsbottom, Greater Manchester, England, outside The Oaks (formerly the Royal Oak) pub on Bridge Street on the second Sunday of September: competitors hurl black puddings at a stack of twelve Yorkshire puddings balanced on a 20-foot (6.1 m) plinth, and whoever knocks down the most wins (Wikipedia, n.d.). The contest turns the centuries-old Lancashire–Yorkshire rivalry into comic combat and draws competitors from as far afield as Australia, the USA, Kenya and Peru, plus national media coverage; entry is open to men and women alike, with separate seniors and juniors leaderboards (Atlas Obscura, n.d.; Manchester Evening News, n.d.).

Each thrower stands on a small painted block called the "golden grid" — brought to Ramsbottom station by steam train and escorted to the pub by bagpipers — and may hurl three black puddings, underarm only, overarm throwing being strictly forbidden; entry fees go to charity (Wikipedia, n.d.). The all-time record stands at seven Yorkshire puddings toppled in three throws, set by John Burns of Edenfield in 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Contestants pay an entry fee (historically £1–£3 for three throws, with extra throws purchasable) and take up to three underarm throws from the golden grid at the target: twelve large Yorkshire puddings stacked on the 20-foot plinth (Wikipedia, n.d.; Atlas Obscura, n.d.). The winner is whoever dislodges the most Yorkshire puddings from the plinth in their three shots; ties are settled by throw-offs, and seniors and juniors are ranked on separate leaderboards with cash prizes for the adult and junior winners (Manchester Evening News, n.d.). Only regulation black puddings supplied for the event may be thrown — traditionally wrapped to keep them intact — and all entry money goes to local charities such as the Stubbins Community Trust, Bury Hospice and Ramsbottom Pantry (Atlas Obscura, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). There is no international federation; the Stubbins Community Trust organises the annual championship (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Local legend traces the contest to a 1455 battle at Stubbins, near Ramsbottom, during the Wars of the Roses, when the warring houses of Lancaster and York supposedly ran out of ammunition and resorted to throwing food at each other — Lancashire black pudding against Yorkshire puddings (Wikipedia, n.d.). The competition is said to have been revived by a pub landlord in 1839 and again in its modern form in 1984 by the Stubbins Community Trust as a charity fundraiser — born, as one organiser recalled, out of a conversation in the pub about what to do for charity: "let's throw black puddings at Yorkshire puddings" (Wikipedia, n.d.). Held outside the Royal Oak and later moved along Bridge Street when the pub closed, the event now forms the centrepiece of Ramsbottom's annual September festival weekend, with the golden grid arriving by steam train amid bagpipers and crowds, and it has been covered by national newspapers and television since the 2010s (Manchester Evening News, n.d.).
