## Basic Information

Wallyball (sometimes called rebound volleyball) is a fast-paced team sport played on a racquetball court with a net strung across the middle — essentially volleyball adapted to a four-walled court, where hitting the ball off the side and back walls is a legal and central part of play. Teams of two to six players per side contest extremely quick rallies in which wall rebounds replace long rallies of pure volleyball.

The name is a portmanteau of wall and volleyball, and the sport is popular in recreation centres, health clubs and college intramural leagues wherever racquetball courts are available.

## Rules

Play takes place on a standard racquetball court measuring 40 feet long, 20 feet wide and 20 feet high, with a net up to 8 feet high for men and 7 feet 6 inches for women. Teams get up to three hits to return the ball over the net, and during those hits the ball may contact any number of walls on their own side — but a ball crossing the net may legally touch only one side wall, never the ceiling or the opponent's back wall. The serve is made from behind the service area, may strike one side wall, and jump serves are permitted. Scoring uses rally scoring with a freeze point three points short of the game target, or traditional side-out scoring; two 30-second timeouts per team are allowed.

## History

Wallyball was created by Joe Garcia at the Calabasas Racquetball Club in California in 1979, building on earlier wall-volley experiments such as Deckerball. The American Wallyball Association was formed the same year and organises United States championships for men's, women's and coed teams. Rally scoring was officially adopted in 1989, and in 2000 rules director Steve Fuhrman worked with USA Volleyball to codify ball-handling guidelines that remain in force for wallyball today.
