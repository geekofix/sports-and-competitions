## Basic Information

Chindro (also stapoo, kidi kada) is a hopscotch-like stone game of the Indian subcontinent: a player tosses or slides a stone across a drawn court of squares, hops through the court on one foot, picks the stone up and continues, trying to cover the whole court. It is played by children — especially girls — across India, Pakistan and Nepal, and related hopscotch games include ekka-dokka in Bengal and dhyakki in Nepal. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [Ekka Dokka](ekka-dokka.html), [Tipcat](tipcat.html), [Gutte](gutte.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

A court of squares (typically 6–8 in a row, sometimes with side branches) is drawn in the ground. The player stands at point X, throws a small stone so it lands in the first square without touching the lines, then hops on one foot through the court, skipping the square with the stone, picks the stone up on the return, and throws it into the next square — repeating until all squares are covered. Touching a line, landing off balance, or moving the stone with the wrong foot loses the turn or the game, depending on local rules.

## History

Hopscotch-type games are ancient in the subcontinent and reflect concepts of land division and ownership; the Bengali ekka-dokka, the Nepali dhyakki and the Rajasthani variants are closely related. Chindro/stapoo remains a standard schoolyard game across South Asia, played with a flat stone or a broken pottery shard (guti).
