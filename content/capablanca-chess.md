## Basic Information

Capablanca chess is the 10x8 variant proposed by world champion José Raúl Capablanca in 1921: two new pieces — the chancellor (rook+knight) and the archbishop (bishop+knight) — placed between the knights and bishops. Capablanca played it in 1929 (photograph preserved). Within the chess variants and mind games on this site, its closest relatives are [Grand Chess](grand-chess.html), [Glinski Hexagonal Chess](glinski-hexagonal-chess.html), [Chess](chess.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

10 files x 8 ranks; the chancellor moves as rook+knight, the archbishop as bishop+knight; starting array: R N B A Q K C B N R (a1 rook, b1 knight, c1 bishop, d1 archbishop, e1 queen, f1 king, g1 chancellor, h1 bishop, i1 knight, j1 rook) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Capablanca proposed the variant in 1921 after losing the 1921 world title to Lasker, to add variety and reduce draws; he played it publicly in 1929. Modern organizations (e.g. the Capablanca Chess Association) play 10x8 and 10x10 variants (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
