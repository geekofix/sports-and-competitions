## Basic Information

Tuna throwing is the Australian festival event made famous by the Tunarama festival in Port Lincoln, South Australia: contestants hurl a frozen tuna (a genuine whole fish, frozen solid, in the order of 8 to 10 kilograms) for maximum distance, the way another athlete throws the hammer. Tunarama's tuna toss is the festival's signature contest, and its long-running record stands as one of Australian festival sport's most famous marks.

## Rules

Contestants grip the frozen tuna by its tail and, spinning like a hammer or discus thrower, release it down a marked throwing lane for distance measured from the throwing line to the fish's landing point; each entrant typically receives a set number of throws (commonly three), and the longest legal throw wins. Classes include men's and women's divisions and novelty variations (with juniors throwing lighter fish); the technique of spinning the tuna around the body before launch has become the event's iconic spectacle, and festival rules govern the fish (a frozen whole tuna), measuring procedure, throwing order, and (with animal-welfare and practical-era adjustments) occasional use of replica fish in some years. Judging is by distance to the nearest measurable point of landing.

## History

Port Lincoln, on South Australia's Eyre Peninsula, is one of the world's largest tuna-fishing ports, and its Tunarama festival began in 1962 as a community celebration of the fishing industry; the now-legendary tuna toss (with the frozen tuna hurled by spinnaker-sea veterans) became the festival's headline event and a beloved Australian novelty-sport story across the event's more than 60-year run. Its record distance of roughly 37 metres has stood as the benchmark for decades, repeated attempts have challenged it during subsequent Tunaramas, and the event has drawn international media attention as one of the world's best-known festival throwing contests.
