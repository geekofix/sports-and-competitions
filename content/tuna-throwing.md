## Basic Information

Tuna throwing is the signature novelty contest of the Tunarama festival in Port Lincoln, South Australia: competitors grip a frozen whole southern bluefin tuna — typically 9–10 kg, frozen solid, with a rope handle through the gills — spin like a hammer thrower and hurl it for maximum distance down a marked lane (ABC News, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The festival itself was founded in January 1962 on the Australia Day weekend to celebrate the town's tuna-fishing industry, and the tuna toss has been its headline event since 1979–1980 (Tunarama, n.d.). The event record stands at 37.23 m, set in 1998 by Sean Carlin, the Australian Olympic and Commonwealth Games hammer thrower, and women compete in their own division — Jackie Hockaday won the women's title with 11.12 m in 2014 — while the 2023 edition was won by Levi Proude before the festival was revived by the Port Lincoln City Council for January 2026 with a 10 kg rubber replica (ABC News, n.d.). Since 2008 the initial heats have used a weighted plastic replica, with real frozen fish reserved for later rounds; the fish are dead by-products of the commercial tuna industry, so no live animal is harmed (ABC News, n.d.; Port Lincoln City Council, n.d.).

## Rules

Contestants throw from a marked throwing line inside a lane, gripping the frozen tuna by the tail or rope handle and spinning one or more full turns before release, hammer-style; the distance is measured from the throwing line to the first point where the fish lands, and each entrant receives a set number of throws — commonly three — with the longest legal throw counting (Wikipedia, n.d.). Separate men's and women's divisions are contested, plus junior novelty events such as the under-10 "prawn toss" with lighter objects, and house rules govern the fish (a frozen whole southern bluefin tuna of roughly 9–10 kg in finals; a weighted plastic replica in heats since 2008), the measuring procedure, the throwing order and safety (Wikipedia, n.d.; ABC News, n.d.). Throwing over the lane boundaries, touching the ground before release or a fish that breaks apart can void a throw under festival rules, and officials judge the landing point (ABC News, n.d.). There is no international federation: the rules are set by the Tunarama organizers, and women and men follow the same measuring and throwing rules in their own classes (Port Lincoln City Council, n.d.).

## History

Port Lincoln, on South Australia's Eyre Peninsula, is one of the world's great tuna ports, and its Tunarama festival began in January 1962 as a community celebration of the fishing industry on the Australia Day weekend (Tunarama, n.d.). The tuna toss was first held in 1979–1980 — accounts credit the inaugural throws to South Australian agriculture minister Ted Chapman and local fishermen, who had long tossed fish from boat decks onto wharf trucks (ABC News, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The contest quickly became the festival's signature event: Sean Carlin, the hammer thrower who represented Australia at the Olympics and Commonwealth Games, set the enduring record of 37.23 m in 1998, and women's titles have been contested for decades, with Jackie Hockaday's 11.12 m winning the women's division in 2014 (ABC News, n.d.). After the 2023 festival — won by Levi Proude — the event was canned amid funding and community fatigue, but the Port Lincoln City Council revived it for January 2026, switching the competition to a 10 kg rubber replica (ABC News, n.d.; Port Lincoln City Council, n.d.).
