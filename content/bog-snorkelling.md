## Basic Information

Bog snorkelling is a quirky British sporting event in which competitors wearing a snorkel, diving mask and flippers race through two consecutive lengths of a 60-yard (55 m) water-filled trench cut through a peat bog — 120 yards (110 m) in total — without using conventional swimming strokes, relying on flipper power alone. The World Bog Snorkelling Championships are organized by Green Events and take place every August bank holiday at the Waen Rhydd bog near Llanwrtyd Wells, Powys, Wales (Green Events, n.d.-a). The 40th edition was held on Sunday, August 30, 2026; the 2026 women's champion was Emily Hall with 1:27.97 (BBC, 2026).

The standing world records are 1:18.81 for men, set by Neil Rutter at Llanwrtyd Wells in 2018, and 1:22.56 for women, set by Kirsty Johnson in 2014; the event also features a fancy-dress category and raises money for local charities (Green Events, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The organizers cut a water-filled trench 60 yards (55 m) long through the peat bog; competitors must wear a snorkel, mask and flippers (a wetsuit is optional but common) and must complete two consecutive lengths — 120 yards (110 m) in total — in the shortest time possible (Green Events, n.d.-a). Conventional swimming strokes are not allowed; propulsion comes from flippers only, and competitors must keep their face in the water with the snorkel above the surface. Penalty time is added for losing a flipper or breaking the surface incorrectly; heats run in the afternoon with a fancy-dress category alongside the serious races. Men's and women's titles are awarded separately with identical rules and course (BBC, 2026).

## History

The World Bog Snorkelling Championships were first held in 1985 at Llanwrtyd Wells, conceived in the Neuadd Arms pub by local enthusiasts who wanted a challenge for the village; the event has been run every year since except for cancellations, and it has grown from a handful of competitors to hundreds, drawing athletes from Australia, Belgium, Canada, China, Ireland, New Zealand, Poland, South Africa, South Korea, the US and across the UK (Green Events, n.d.-a). World records have fallen steadily from 1:44 in 1997 to Neil Rutter's 1:18.81 in 2018 (men) and Kirsty Johnson's 1:22.56 in 2014 (women). A related Bog Snorkelling Triathlon — 120 yards of snorkelling, a 19-mile bike ride and a 7.5-mile run — is also held in Wales, and the 2026 championship in Powys was again covered by the BBC, with the first professional winner taking a sixth title (BBC, 2026).
