## Basic Information

Long track speedway (longtrack, Langbahn) is ice speedway on long ice ovals — 1,000 m and more — instead of the classic 400 m ice track, with speeds well over 100 km/h on the straights (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the speedway family of motorsport.

![Jawa 500 longtrack ice speedway machine, 1980](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Jawa_1980_Ice_track_racer_7.jpg?width=420)

*Figure: Piero at Dutch Wikipedia. (5 March 2005 (original upload date)). Jawa 500 longtrack ice speedway machine, 1980 [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Jawa_1980_Ice_track_racer_7.jpg*

Bikes are 500 cc single-cylinder Jawa/ GM engines with spiked tyres (up to 28 spikes per tyre), no brakes, running on methanol (Wikipedia, n.d.). The FIM Ice Speedway World Championship includes a longtrack round at venues like Inzell, Germany, and Assen, Netherlands (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Races run heats and finals over 4–6 laps on the long oval with a starting gate; the standing start on ice with full spikes is a spectacle in itself (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety: leathers, helmet, spiked-tyre rules and medical cover (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete — women’s classes exist at national level in Sweden and Germany.

## History

Longtrack ice speedway developed in the Netherlands and Germany in the 1960s–70s as riders looked for bigger, faster ice ovals; the FIM added longtrack rounds to the world championship program in the 1980s–90s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The 1980s Jawa 500 shown in the photo is the classic longtrack machine.
