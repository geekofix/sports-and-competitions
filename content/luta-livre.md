## Basic Information

Luta livre (literally "free fight") is Brazilian catch wrestling: a grappling-only combat sport fought without a gi, in which the aim is to take the opponent down and submit him with chokes, joint locks or pins. It developed in Brazil in the early 20th century from European and American catch-as-catch-can wrestling and became the foundation of vale tudo (no-rules) fighting and, later, mixed martial arts. In Brazil it is governed within the wrestling confederation system (Confederação Brasileira de Lutas Associadas, CBLA), and internationally it falls under United World Wrestling's grappling disciplines.

## Rules

A luta livre match starts standing; the wrestler tries to take the opponent to the mat and win by submission (choke or joint lock forcing a tap-out), by pinning both shoulders to the mat, or by referee stoppage when a fighter is unable to defend. Strikes are not allowed. Matches are held on a mat in weight classes, with rounds of a few minutes and overtime/points formats depending on the event. Competitions range from submission-only events to vale tudo-style contests, and luta livre is also practiced as self-defense and as the grappling base of MMA fighters.

## History

Catch wrestling reached Brazil with European immigrants and American circus wrestlers in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and merged with local fighting culture; it was practiced in Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo from the 1920s. From the 1920s to the 1990s, luta livre contests (and vale tudo events derived from them) were central to Brazilian fighting culture and produced legendary fighters of the early MMA era, when the Gracie family's challenge matches brought Brazilian jiu-jitsu and luta livre to world attention. Today luta livre is practiced in academies worldwide and under United World Wrestling as a grappling discipline.
