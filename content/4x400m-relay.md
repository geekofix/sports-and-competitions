## Basic Information

The 4x400 metres relay is the one-lap-per-runner relay — four athletes each covering 400 m, totalling 1600 m — traditionally the closing event of the track programme, governed by World Athletics (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The world records are 2:54.20 for men, set by the USA (Andrew Valmon, Quincy Watts, Butch Reynolds, Michael Johnson) at the 1993 Stuttgart World Championships, and 3:15.17 for women, set by the Soviet Union at the Seoul 1988 Olympics; the mixed 4x400 m world record is 3:09.34 by the USA from Doha 2019 (World Athletics, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.). At the end of the 2026 season the women's event was led by the USA and the Bahamas — with Shaunae Miller-Uibo (pictured) the 2016 Olympic champion — while the men's race featured Botswana, whose team with Letsile Tebogo (pictured) on the second leg ran a competition record 2:54.47 at the 2026 World Athletics Relays in Gaborone (World Athletics, n.d.-b; World Athletics, n.d.-c).

## Rules

In the 4x400 m each runner covers one full lap; the first leg uses the staggered 400 m start with a crouch start in blocks, and the first two changeovers are made in lanes, after which runners may break to the inside (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The changeover zone is 20 m, centred on the scratch line, and the baton must be passed within it; dropping the baton is allowed if the dropper retrieves it, while false starts, out-of-zone passes or impeding disqualify the team (World Athletics, n.d.-a). The finish is judged by the anchor runner's torso with photo finish; wind readings do not affect record ratification over 400 m (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men, women and mixed teams compete at the Olympics, World Championships and World Athletics Relays, with U20 categories at age-group events (World Athletics, n.d.-a; World Athletics, n.d.-b).

## History

The men's 4x400 m has been an Olympic event since 1912 and the women's since 1964; the mixed relay debuted at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, won by Poland in 3:09.87 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 1993 US men's team — Valmon, Watts, Reynolds and Michael Johnson, who anchored in 42.94 — set the 2:54.20 world record that still stands in 2026; the Soviet women's 3:15.17 from Seoul 1988 has also stood for decades (World Athletics, n.d.-d). At the 2026 World Athletics Relays in Gaborone, Botswana's team with Letsile Tebogo (pictured) on the second leg won in a competition record of 2:54.47, the third fastest performance ever (World Athletics, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.).
