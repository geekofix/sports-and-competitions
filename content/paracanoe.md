## Basic Information

Paracanoe is the Paralympic discipline of paddle sport: athletes with impairments race kayaks and va'a outrigger canoes over 200 metre sprint courses on flat water, using trunk, shoulder and arm power from fixed seats. Governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF), it has been a Paralympic sport since Rio 2016 and world championships are held on the ICF world championship programme.

Classification categories KL1 to KL3 (kayak) and VL1 to VL3 (va'a) group athletes by functional trunk and leg ability, so racing is fair between similar levels of impairment.

## Rules

Races are straight 200 metre sprints on a regatta course, in single kayaks (KL) and single va'a outriggers (VL), each class from 1 (least trunk function) to 3 (most) racing separately. Boats are standardized with fixed seats and backrests suited to each class, paddlers use double kayak blades or single va'a blades, and races are run under ICF sprint racing rules: heats, semifinals and finals, lane discipline, and false-start and equipment regulations, with classification panels examining athletes before competition to place them in the correct class.

## History

Paracanoe was developed within the ICF's paddle-ability programmes in the 2000s, as adaptive paddlers in trunk-support seats began racing kayaks and outriggers under unified classification. The ICF staged its first paracanoe world championships in 2010 in conjunction with its sprint worlds, and the discipline joined the Paralympic Games at Rio 2016 with KL classes; va'a VL classes were added to the world programme and joined the Tokyo 2020 Paralympic events. The sport is strongest in paddling nations from Europe to Brazil and continues to grow through national federation camps and classification development.
