## Basic Information

Paracanoe is the Paralympic discipline of paddle sport: athletes with impairments race kayaks and va'a outrigger canoes over 200 metre sprint courses on flat water, using trunk, shoulder and arm power from fixed seats (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-a). Governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF), it has been a Paralympic sport since Rio 2016, and world championships are held on the ICF world championship programme (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). Classification categories KL1 to KL3 (kayak) and VL1 to VL3 (va'a) group athletes by functional trunk and leg ability, and men's and women's events are held in every class under identical rules (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-b). Among today's leading athletes is Australia's Dylan Littlehales, pictured below, the KL3 men's 200 m Paralympic champion from Tokyo 2020.

## Rules

Races are straight 200 metre sprints on a regatta course, in single kayaks (KL) and single va'a outriggers (VL), each class from 1 (least trunk function) to 3 (most) racing separately (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-b). Boats are standardized with fixed seats and backrests suited to each class, paddlers use double kayak blades or single va'a blades, and races are run under ICF sprint racing rules: heats, semifinals and finals, lane discipline, and false-start and equipment regulations (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-b). Classification panels examine athletes before competition to place them in the correct class (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-b). At the Paralympics, men's and women's KL1, KL2, KL3, VL2 and VL3 events are contested — VL1 was added for Paris 2024 — all over the same 200 m distance with the same boat rules per class (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.).

## History

Paracanoe was developed in the 2000s by the ICF as the para discipline of canoe sprint, with the first ICF Paracanoe World Championships held in 2009 in Dartmouth, Canada, and the sport made its Paralympic debut at Rio 2016 with nine medal events (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). The classification system KL1-KL3 and VL1-VL3 was formalized in the early 2010s to group athletes by trunk and leg function (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-b). Champions such as Australia's Curtis McGrath — KL2 gold at Rio 2016, Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 — and Dylan Littlehales — KL3 gold at Tokyo 2020 — have raised the sport's profile (Wikipedia, n.d.). The ICF Paracanoe World Championships now run alongside the able-bodied sprint worlds (International Canoe Federation, n.d.-a).
