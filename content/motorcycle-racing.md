## Basic Information

Motorcycle racing is the umbrella sport of all competitive motorcycle sport, governed internationally by the FIM (Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme, founded 1904) and split into road racing (the Grand Prix world championship with its [MotoGP](motogp.html), [Moto2](moto2.html) and [Moto3](moto3.html) classes), production-based circuit racing (WorldSBK, WorldSSP and the women-only WorldWCR), off-road disciplines ([motocross](motocross.html), enduro, trials) and road racing on closed public roads such as the Isle of Man TT (FIM, n.d.-a). In 2026 Italy's Nicolò Bulega won the WorldSBK title with Ducati, sealing it at Misano with a single-season record of 32 race wins — 25 of them consecutively — the first time a manufacturer has won every race of a season since Ducati in 2003; Turkey's Toprak Razgatlıoğlu, three-time WorldSBK champion (2021 with Yamaha, 2024 and 2025 with BMW), moved to MotoGP with Prima Pramac Yamaha for 2026, and Spain's Albert Arenas won the 2026 WorldSSP crown (WorldSBK, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The FIM's women-only WorldWCR, launched in 2024 on Yamaha R7 machines, was won by Ana Carrasco in 2024 and Maria Herrera in 2025 — Carrasco having become the first woman to win a solo FIM world championship race, in SSP300 in 2018 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2026 Isle of Man TT, held 25 May–6 June, saw Dean Harrison win the Superbike TT and be declared the 2026 Senior TT winner after the final day was abandoned; a new Sportbike TT class was added and the sidecar class was suspended after qualifying crashes (Isle of Man TT, n.d.). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women in the sport are WorldWCR riders Ana Carrasco and Maria Herrera of Spain, while the 2026 Senior TT winner is Dean Harrison of England (Isle of Man TT, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Each discipline has its own FIM rulebook. Grand Prix classes race prototype machines on permanent circuits ([MotoGP](motogp.html), [Moto2](moto2.html), [Moto3](moto3.html)); WorldSBK races production-derived 1000 cc machines over two races per weekend plus a Superpole qualifying race, WorldSSP uses 600 cc production machines, and the women-only WorldWCR races identical Yamaha R7 machines (Wikipedia, n.d.-c). [Motocross](motocross.html) and enduro run on natural terrain, and road races such as the Isle of Man TT run on the 60.72 km (37.73 mi) Mountain Course, a loop of closed public roads where over 100 riders start together and average speeds exceed 130 mph (Isle of Man TT, n.d.). Safety, machine and conduct rules — scrutineering, protective gear, penalties and disqualification — are set by the FIM Sporting and Technical Regulations in every class; all classes except the women-only WorldWCR are open to women and men under identical rules (FIM, n.d.-a).

## History

Motorcycle racing began in the late 19th century, the FIM was founded in 1904, and the first Tourist Trophy race on the Isle of Man was held in 1907, making the TT the oldest race on the international calendar (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Isle of Man TT produced its most celebrated icons in Mike Hailwood, with 14 TT wins between 1957 and 1967, and Joey Dunlop of Northern Ireland, with 26 wins between 1977 and 2000 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The Grand Prix world championship started in 1949, and the premier class became MotoGP in 2002 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
