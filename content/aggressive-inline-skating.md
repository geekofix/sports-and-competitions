## Basic Information

Aggressive Inline Skating (aggressive skating / rollerblading) is a roller sports discipline in which skaters perform tricks on rails, ledges, stairs and ramps in skateparks or street spots, using inline skates with hard-soled boots and grind plates. It is governed internationally under World Skate and was featured at the X Games from 1995 to 2005.

## Rules

In contests, each skater gets runs (typically two) in which they link tricks — grinds, slides, aerials and flip tricks — and are judged on difficulty, execution, variety, style and use of the course. Categories include street, park and vert. The highest-scoring run wins. Inline speed skating and artistic skating are separate disciplines under the same federation.

## History

Aggressive inline skating emerged in the late 1980s and 1990s from skateboarding culture as inline skates became more technical. It was included in the X Games from 1995 until 2005, when it was dropped in favor of skateboarding and BMX. World-level contests continue under World Skate.
