## Basic Information

The mud pit belly flop is a novelty competition in which contestants leap or dive belly-first into a pit of watery mud, and are judged on the size of the splash, the style of the flop and showmanship rather than on distance or speed (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is best known as one of the signature events of the Summer Redneck Games, an annual festival of deliberately silly rural competitions held in East Dublin, Georgia, and of similar events such as the Canadian Redneck Games in Minto, Ontario (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Both women and men compete in the same heats, and the event has become one of the most photographed novelty contests in the United States.

## Rules

Contestants take turns jumping from a platform or the pit edge into a shallow pit filled with watery mud, aiming to land flat on their stomach and chest to create the biggest possible splash (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Judges score each attempt on splash size, style and overall entertainment value, and the winner is awarded a trophy — traditionally a half-crushed beer can at the Redneck Games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There are no age or fitness requirements beyond signing a liability waiver, fancy dress is common, and both men's and women's divisions are simply mixed heats, so gender and age classes do not differ. Related mud-pit novelty events at the same festivals include mud pit tug-of-war, mud pit volleyball, bobbing for pig's feet and a mud pit slip and slide (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).


## History

The Redneck Games were started in 1996 by Mac Davis, general manager of radio station WQZY-FM in East Dublin, Georgia, after commentators said the 1996 Atlanta Olympics would be run by "rednecks" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first Redneck Summer Games drew about 5,000 people — more than double the town's population — and the mud pit belly flop quickly became one of its most photographed events (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The games ran annually until 2021 and have been revived since, spawning copycat events such as the Canadian Redneck Games; the belly flop contest has been won by both women and men, including winners such as Ron Johnson (2002) and Andrew Fremming (2006) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
