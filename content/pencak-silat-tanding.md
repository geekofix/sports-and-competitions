## Basic Information

Pencak silat tanding is the full-contact sparring discipline of [pencak silat](pencak-silat.html), the traditional martial art of Indonesia and the Malay world. Two opponents contest a bout on a circular arena wearing a full-body elastic uniform (baju silat) and protective gear, with points scored for clean hand and foot strikes, sweeps and takedowns (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Tanding is a standard medal sport at the Southeast Asian Games since 1987 and at the world championships of the International Pencak Silat Federation (PERSILAT), alongside the artistic discipline [seni](pencak-silat-seni.html), and it debuted at the Asian Games in 2018 in Jakarta and Palembang (Wikipedia, n.d.). Tanding is contested by men and women in parallel weight classes — the men's class E (65–70 kg) final at the 2011 SEA Games between Al Jufferi of Malaysia (gold) and Nguyen Duy Chien of Vietnam (silver) is pictured below — and women's tanding has been a full medal event at the SEA Games since the sport's inclusion (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Tanding competition follows the International Pencak Silat Competition Rules and Regulations issued by PERSILAT (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Bouts are contested by weight class in roughly 5 kg increments, with one athlete per category per federation. The approved protective equipment comprises a padded helmet, chest protector, groin protector, hand gloves, shin guards and, for women, a breast protector. Points are awarded for valid strikes and for sweep-based takedowns and throws, which are the most common scoring techniques; attacks to the spine, throat and groin are prohibited, as are unsporting actions such as attacking a downed opponent. Each federation may enter only a limited number of weight categories at major games, with elimination through preliminary rounds to a final. Bouts are overseen by a referee on the floor and a jury that scores each exchange (International Pencak Silat Federation, 2026). Men's and women's bouts use identical arenas, durations, weight-class spacing and scoring; the only equipment difference is the additional breast protector for women (National Olympic Committee of Indonesia, 2025).

## History

Tanding developed from the combative side of Indonesian silat as the art was standardized under the Indonesian Pencak Silat Association (IPSI, founded 1948) and internationally under PERSILAT from 1980, when the first international rulebook was drafted jointly by the founding federations of Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Brunei (International Pencak Silat Federation, n.d.). The discipline entered the Southeast Asian Games when pencak silat debuted at the 1987 SEA Games in Jakarta — the 2011 finals at the Padepokan arena in Jakarta, pictured above, show the classic tanding contest — and it joined the Asian Games in 2018 when Jakarta and Palembang hosted the event, with Indonesia topping the medal table (Wikipedia, n.d.). Sweep-based takedowns remain the hallmark of the sport, and Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore have been the leading nations at recent SEA Games and world championships (National Olympic Committee of Indonesia, 2025).
