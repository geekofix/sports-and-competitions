## Basic Information

Ski orienteering is the winter discipline of orienteering: athletes navigate on skis through snow-covered terrain using a detailed map and compass, racing to visit a series of control points in the fastest possible time (IOF, n.d.-a). It is governed worldwide by the International Orienteering Federation (IOF), which was founded on 21 May 1961 in Copenhagen and also runs the foot-orienteering World Championships (IOF, n.d.-a). The 2026 World Ski Orienteering Championships and the World Cup Final were held together from 2–6 March 2026 under the IOF calendar, and the 2024 World Ski Orienteering Championships in Ramsau, Austria, saw Jørgen Baklid of Norway take the men's sprint title, with Sweden's women — among them Magdalena Olsson and Anna Ulvensøen — leading the female field (IOF, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the current leading women are Sweden's Anna Ulvensøen and Magdalena Olsson; among the men, Norway's Jørgen Baklid remains the standard-bearer of the sport (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Ski orienteering follows the IOF Competition Rules for ski orienteering: competitors receive a map on which the course — a sequence of numbered control circles — is printed, and must visit every control in order by the fastest route of their own choosing, punching an electronic timing card at each control (IOF, n.d.-a). Events are offered over sprint, middle and long distances with individual interval starts, plus relays in which team members hand over the map; in sprint events, qualification heats feed a final (IOF, n.d.-b). Both classic technique (in prepared tracks) and free technique are allowed, and athletes may leave the tracks at any time, which makes route choice the decisive skill at racing speeds of up to 80 km/h downhill (IOF, n.d.-a). Missed or unpunched controls bring disqualification, and outside assistance is forbidden. Men and women compete in separate elite classes (W/ME) on identical courses, with junior classes (W20/M20, W18/M18) and masters classes at major championships (IOF, n.d.-b). The IOF calendar comprises the World Cup, the biennial World Ski Orienteering Championships, the Junior World Championships and continental championships (IOF, n.d.-b).


## History

Ski orienteering grew out of Scandinavian military orienteering in the 1930s, with the first dedicated ski-orienteering competitions held in Sweden and Norway, and national championships became regular from the 1950s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The International Orienteering Federation, founded on 21 May 1961 in Copenhagen by ten national associations, brought the sport under one umbrella, and the first foot-orienteering World Championships followed in 1966 in Fiskars, Finland, where Norway's Åge Hadler won the men's title and Sweden's Ulla Lindkvist the first women's title (IOF, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Hadler went on to win five consecutive individual world titles from 1966 to 1974, and Lindkvist added the 1968 women's crown, making them the defining icons of the sport's first decade (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first World Ski Orienteering Championships were held in 1975, and since then the Nordic countries, Russia and Estonia have dominated the medal tables, a tradition that continued at Ramsau 2024 and at the 2026 championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
