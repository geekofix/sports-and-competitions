## Basic Information

Ski orienteering (SkiO) is the winter racing hybrid of cross-country skiing and orienteering: competitors ski at high speed along a dense network of snow tracks \u2014 navigating with a highly detailed map fixed in a chest-mounted map holder \u2014 visiting control points in the shortest possible time. It is one of the four orienteering disciplines governed by the International Orienteering Federation (IOF), alongside foot, mountain bike and trail orienteering, with a world championship history reaching back to 1975.

## Rules

A ski orienteering course is a dense, groomed track network \u2014 with no marked route in the terrain \u2014 printed on a special map that shows the quality and navigable width of every track, and whether roads are snow-covered or clear (a separate ski-orienteering mapping standard, ISSkiOM, has applied since 2019). Athletes race skate-skiing style past a sequence of control points that must be visited in the right order, verified by an electronic punch card, with hundreds of route-choice decisions taken at racing speed; compared with pure cross-country skiing, upper body strength matters more because of the double-poling along narrow trails. The championship programme includes Sprint, Middle and Long distances plus relays for men and women; the clock is the only judge, and the fastest total time wins. Ski orienteering's heartland is the Nordic countries, Russia and Central Europe, and it is on the programme of the Asian Winter Games and the CISM World Military Winter Games, with the IOF having campaigned for its inclusion in the Olympic Winter Games.

## History

The first known public ski orienteering competition was held as early as 1887 in Sundsvall, Sweden, before the sport was formally organised; the IOF was founded in 1961 and recognised ski orienteering in 1975, staging the first World Ski Orienteering Championships in Finland that same year \u2014 with the "classic distance" run 1975\u20131986 before the modern Long distance name, a short (later middle) distance from 1988, and sprint added in 2002. A World Cup in ski orienteering has run since 1989, with junior world championships since 1994 and world masters events annually, and the world championships are now organised every odd year with the World Cup in even years. The sport has been dominated historically by the Nordic nations and Russia \u2014 with stars such as Eduard Khrennikov and Tove Dahlen-era successors in the modern field \u2014 and today around twenty nations contest the world titles, with the 2024 championships held at Ramsau in Austria.
