## Basic Information

Czech handball (Czech: národní házená, "national handball") is an outdoor 11-a-side ball game created in Prague in 1905, closely related to team handball but with its own rules and traditions. Teams of eleven (ten outfield players plus a goalkeeper) play on a 45 x 30 m pitch divided into three zones, with a 6 m semicircular goal area. It was once hugely popular in Czechoslovakia — over 26,000 registered players in 447 clubs in 1954 — and today survives as a Czech tradition governed by the Svaz národní házené, while the Czech Handball Federation (ČSH) governs Olympic handball.

## Rules

Under the rules of the Svaz národní házené, the game is played 11 versus 11 on a pitch of about 45 x 30 m divided into three equal zones; only the goalkeeper may enter the 6 m goal area, and goals count when the ball fully crosses the goal line. Players may run with the ball for a limited number of steps and dribble, and body contact is allowed in defense. Matches at championship level last two halves of 30 minutes. The game differs from modern handball in pitch size, number of players and the three-zone rule.

## History

Czech handball was first described in 1905 by Prague teacher Václav Karas in a Brno sports journal; teacher Antonín Krištof developed the rules further and published them in 1908, and the first association was founded in Prague. In 1921 the Czechoslovak association joined the International Women's Sports Federation, which made the rules official internationally, and the first women's world cup was held in 1930. During the German occupation in World War II the game became a symbol of patriotism, with 25,884 players in the early 1940s. After 1947 the IHF promoted seven-a-side handball instead, and národní házená became a national specialty.
