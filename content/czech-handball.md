## Basic Information

Czech handball — národní házená, also called házená — is the Czech variant of handball, played outdoors on a grass pitch by teams of seven (six court players plus a goalkeeper) with a smaller, lighter ball than team handball (Czech Handball Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was created in 1905 in Prague by the physical-education teacher Václav Karas, who published the first rules in 1908, and it remains the traditional ball game of Czechia, governed today by the Czech Handball Association (Svaz národní házené) (Czech Handball Association, n.d.). The international governing body for handball as a whole is the International Handball Federation (IHF), of which the Czechoslovak handball association became a member in 1921 (International Handball Federation [IHF], n.d.). The game is played by both women and men, with women's and men's leagues and a long tradition of women's international competition — Czechoslovakia won the first women's world championship in 1930 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the Czech Handball Association rules, národní házená is played on a grass field with two goals 3 m wide and 2 m high, by seven players per side in two halves of 30 minutes (Czech Handball Association, n.d.). The characteristic rules are the three-step and three-second limits — a player may hold the ball for at most three seconds and take at most three steps — plus a smaller ball than in eleven-a-side team handball, which makes one-handed passing and long throws central to the game (Czech Handball Association, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Fouls are punished with free throws and 7-meter penalty throws, and the goalkeeper may use his whole body within the goal area (Czech Handball Association, n.d.). Men's and women's matches use the same rules and pitch dimensions, with women's competitions organized at the same level as the men's (Czech Handball Association, n.d.). The eleven-a-side field handball variant, covered on [Field handball](field-handball.html) on this site, shares the ancestry but differs in team size and pitch (IHF, n.d.).

## History

Václav Karas devised házená in 1905 in Prague as a winter alternative to football, first described in a Brno sports journal that year, and published the rules in 1908 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Czechoslovak association joined the International Amateur Handball Federation in 1921, and Czechoslovakia's women won the first women's world championship in 1930, with Yugoslavia second and Poland third; at the second women's world championship in London in 1934, Yugoslavia beat Czechoslovakia 6–4 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 1935 a holiday camp in Scarborough, England, introduced the game to British players, and the Civil Service Club in London became the first British club (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). During the Second World War the game reached its peak in the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia with 25,884 registered players; after 1946 the IHF favored eleven-a-side team handball internationally, while házená remained Czechia's national game under the Czech Handball Association (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; IHF, n.d.).
