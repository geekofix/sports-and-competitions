## Basic Information

Boxing is a combat sport in which two athletes, wearing padded gloves, exchange punches in a roped ring under the supervision of a referee; bouts are won by knockout, technical knockout, referee stoppage, decision of the judges, or withdrawal. Since 2025 the Olympic and world-level amateur game is governed by World Boxing, the IOC-recognized international federation that runs the World Boxing Championships, the continental championships and the World Boxing Cup (World Boxing [WB], n.d.). Its 2026 calendar included the Futures Cup in Bangkok (March 8–15), the Asian Championships in Ulaanbaatar (March 28–April 11), the European Elite Championships in Sofia, Bulgaria (September 17–25, more than 350 bouts over 14 weight classes for men and women from 50 kg to over 90 kg, run under European Boxing), the Pan American Championships in Pueblo, Mexico (October 10–17), the U19 World Championships in Budva (October 16–27) and the World Boxing Cup Finals in Tashkent (November 25–December 2) (WB, n.d.). At Paris 2024, Imane Khelif of Algeria won the Olympic gold medal in the women's 66 kg division and Oleksandr Khyzhniak of Ukraine the men's 80 kg gold (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.).

On the women's side, 2026 was another landmark year: at the European Elite Championships in Sofia, Lisa O'Rourke of Ireland won gold at 80 kg on September 24, beating Anastasiia Demurchian 4–1, and Busenaz Sürmeneli of Türkiye took gold at 70 kg on September 25, beating Chantelle Reid, while Michaela Walsh won bronze at 57 kg and Nurselen Yalgettekin bronze at 48 kg (WB, n.d.). Women's boxing debuted at the Olympic Games at London 2012, and all-time great Mary Kom of India — six-time world champion and 2012 Olympic bronze medalist — together with Tokyo 2020 bronze medalist Lovlina Borgohain remain the standard-bearers of the women's game internationally (IOC, n.d.). The rival International Boxing Association (IBA) continues to run its own biennial world championships, but without IOC recognition (International Boxing Association [IBA], n.d.).

## Rules

Under the World Boxing competition rules, elite men's and women's bouts at world championships and the Olympic Games consist of three rounds of three minutes with one-minute breaks — the same format for both sexes since Paris 2024; professional title bouts are scheduled for 12 rounds, other professional bouts for 4 to 10 rounds of three minutes (WB, n.d.). The ring is a square of about 6.10 m (20 ft) per side enclosed by four ropes on raised posts, with a 25 mm canvas. Boxers wear boxing shorts and vest, approved boxing shoes, a mouthguard and a protective cup (men); gloves weigh 10 oz (283 g) at Olympic and world championship level for men and women, while professionals use 8 to 12 oz gloves depending on the weight class. Headguards were abolished in elite international boxing — for men since 2013 and for all elite categories by Paris 2024 — but are still used in youth categories (WB, n.d.) (IOC, n.d.).

Scoring at amateur events uses the 10-point-must system: a panel of judges scores each round, awarding 10 points to the better boxer of the round and fewer to the opponent; the boxer with the higher total wins on decision, and bouts end early by knockout (KO), technical knockout (TKO) when the referee stops the contest, or referee stoppage contest (RSC, including RSC-H for repeated head blows); three knockdowns in one round also end the bout. Fouls include blows below the belt, holding, hitting with the back of the hand or open glove, hitting the back of the head or kidneys, rabbit punches and intentional head clashes; offenders receive warnings, point deductions or disqualification. Weight classes at World Boxing elite events run in 14 categories from 50 kg to over 90 kg, identical for men and women, with weigh-ins on competition days; youth (U19) and U22 categories have their own championships and adjusted formats (WB, n.d.).

## History

Boxing is one of the oldest Olympic sports: it entered the ancient Olympic Games in 688 BC, with Onomastus of Smyrna as the first recorded victor, and was fought with leather hand straps (himantes) until the Romans introduced heavier cestus gloves. The modern Queensberry rules, named after the Marquess of Queensberry and drafted by John Graham Chambers, were published in 1867 and introduced padded gloves, three-minute rounds with one-minute breaks and a 24 ft ring. Boxing debuted at the modern Olympic Games at St. Louis 1904 and has been on every programme since; the International Amateur Boxing Association (AIBA) was founded in Paris in 1946 and later evolved into World Boxing, which the IOC recognized in 2025. Women's boxing debuted at the Olympic Games at London 2012 (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.) (IOC, n.d.).

Among the icons of the sport are Joe Louis (1914–1981), whose 25 consecutive heavyweight title defenses from 1937 to 1948 remain a record and whose 1938 rematch victory over Germany's Max Schmeling became a global symbol; Muhammad Ali (1942–2016), three-time world heavyweight champion, 1960 Rome Olympic gold medalist and winner of the 1974 "Rumble in the Jungle"; Sugar Ray Robinson, widely regarded as the greatest pound-for-pound boxer of all time; and Mike Tyson, the youngest heavyweight champion in history at age 20 in 1986 (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.).
