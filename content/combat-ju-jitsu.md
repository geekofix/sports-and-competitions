## Basic Information

Combat ju-jitsu is the full-contact form of ju-jitsu in which striking, throwing, takedowns and submissions are all permitted — the modern competitive form of the "combat" branch of ju-jitsu, distinct from the throwing-only judo and the ground-only Brazilian jiu-jitsu (JJIF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Two international bodies organize world-level competition: the JJIF (Ju-Jitsu International Federation), whose *Contact Ju-Jitsu* (Fighting system) has been full-contact since 2017 — it debuted at the 2017 Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games in Ashgabat — and which published its Rule Book Contact Ju-Jitsu 2026, version 4.4, in March 2026, including a head-impact-free (HIF) variant; and the CJJI (Combat Jiu-Jitsu International Federation), which held its World Championship in Prague in 2026 (JJIF, 2026; CJJI, n.d.). In JJIF Fighting, bouts last three minutes and are contested in 14 categories by weight and sex — men's classes from −56 kg to +94 kg and women's from −45 kg upward — so men's and women's divisions each have their own weight classes (JJIF, 2026). The parallel American format, Eddie Bravo's *Combat Jiu-Jitsu* (CJJ), is a single 10-minute round with palm strikes allowed only when grounded, submission or TKO as the only ways to win, and EBI-style overtime (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the 2026 JJIF World Championships the reigning world champions in the men's and women's weight classes are the current top names of the sport, with women's world champions competing first among equals in every weight class (JJIF, 2026).

 Within the combat sports and martial arts family on this site, its closest relatives are [Judo](judo.html), [Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu](brazilian-jiu-jitsu.html), [Wrestling](wrestling.html), [Sambo](sambo.html), [Boxing](boxing.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Under the JJIF Rule Book Contact Ju-Jitsu 2026 (v4.4, March 2026), a Fighting bout runs for three minutes of continuous time; competitors score points for strikes (punches, kicks), throws, takedowns and controlled ground work, and the bout can end early by submission, by referee stoppage (KO/TKO) or by a decisive point margin at the bell (JJIF, 2026). The full-contact rules permit punches and kicks to the body and legs and — in the standard variant — to the head with protective headgear; the HIF (head-impact-free) variant bans all head contact and is increasingly used at youth and development events (JJIF, 2026). Competitors wear a gi or rash guard plus protective gear (gloves, shin guards, mouthguard, groin protector, headgear where applicable) and are classified into 14 weight-and-sex categories: men −56, −62, −69, −77, −85, −94 and +94 kg; women −45, −48 and upward — men's and women's divisions are separate, and junior and youth categories mirror the senior classes (JJIF, 2026). Eddie Bravo's Combat Jiu-Jitsu rules differ: one 10-minute round, no strikes standing, palm strikes allowed on the ground, win only by submission or TKO, with an overtime system (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The official rule sources are the JJIF (ju-jitsu.sport) and the CJJI (cjjif.org), the two world bodies of full-combat ju-jitsu (JJIF, 2026; CJJI, n.d.).

## History

Ju-jitsu's combat roots go back to the samurai battlefield arts (*jūjutsu*), and the modern sport spread from Japan in the late 19th and early 20th centuries — U.S. Army soldiers were already drilling jiu-jitsu at Fort Myer, Virginia, in July 1918 under Captain Allen Smith, as pictured below (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Jigoro Kano's Kodokan judo (founded 1882) split off the throwing art, while the JJIF — founded in 1997 as the successor of the Ju-Jitsu international federations — kept the full combat system and made striking official in 2017 with the Contact Ju-Jitsu Fighting rules, debuting at the Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games in Ashgabat (JJIF, 2026; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The European and World Championships have since been joined by the JJIF World Games, and the 2026 rulebook (v4.4) introduced the HIF variant to protect younger athletes (JJIF, 2026). In parallel, the CJJI was founded in the United States to run its own full-combat world championships — the 2026 edition in Prague — and Eddie Bravo's Combat Jiu-Jitsu, created for the Eddie Bravo Invitational (EBI) submission-only scene, became a televised full-contact hybrid in the 2020s (CJJI, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The women's side has grown steadily since the 1990s, when women first entered international ju-jitsu fighting, and today women contest full world championship weight classes at both JJIF and CJJI events (JJIF, 2026).
