## Basic Information

Powered paragliding, also known as paramotoring or PPG, is a form of ultralight aviation in which the pilot wears a backpack-mounted motor that provides enough thrust to take off with a paraglider wing from level ground, alone and in still air; flown low and slow at roughly 25 to 80 km/h, it has two competitive identities under the FAI — classic all-round endurance and navigation flying, and slalom racing around inflatable pylons — governed worldwide through the FAI Microlight and Paramotor Commission (CIMA) (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). At the 7th FAI World Paramotor Slalom Championship in Bornos, Spain, in October 2025, France's Jérémy Penone won the PF1 foot-launch title ahead of Aleš Tryml of Czechia and Eissa Al-Hajjaji of Qatar, while the 12th FAI World Paramotor Championship at Chambley, France, in 2025 crowned Italy's 21-year-old Andrea Cecchetto as classic PF1 world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The women's class (PF1F) is flown under the same rules, and French pilots such as Coralie Mateos have been women's slalom world champions, with women and men competing side by side in the same championships (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In FAI competition, pilots race in solo foot-launch (PF1) and trike (PL1) classes, a women's class (PF1F), a tandem class (PL2) and an electric class (PF1E), with team events completing the programme (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.). A classic championship tests all-round flying: precise navigation over courses of tens of kilometres, fuel economy, and precision landing and take-off tasks, with FAI world records for distance and altitude ratified under its sporting code. Slalom championships send pilots around a tight course of inflatable pylons in heats — touching the ground or a pylon, or missing a gate, adds time penalties, and each run is timed to the second; the new Serial class (PF1S/PL1S, engines up to 205 cc) was introduced at Bornos 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Classes are open to women and men alike, with the PF1F women's class flown on the same course; recreational PPG is minimally regulated in many countries and training is run by national federations (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.).

## History

The paramotor was born when British pilot Mike Byrne, inspired by the emerging sport of paragliding, flew a motorized paraglider for the first time on 8 June 1980 in Essex, in a flight of about four and a half minutes; the concept spread through France, Poland and the US during the 1980s, and reliable two-stroke units from French and Polish manufacturers enabled long cross-country flights and altitude records (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first FAI-approved World Slalom Championship was held at Aspres-sur-Buëch, France, in 2013 with 80 pilots from 18 countries, followed by Legnica 2015, Faiyum 2018, Strachotín 2022, Bornos 2023 and Lusail 2024, with France, Poland and Qatar becoming the leading nations; the first FAI World Paramotor Endurance Championship followed in 2024 at Manston, England (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
