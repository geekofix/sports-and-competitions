## Basic Information

Rugby league is a full-contact team sport played by two teams of 13 players on a rectangular field 112–122 m long and up to 68 m wide, with H-shaped posts at each end; it is one of the two major codes of rugby football and is governed worldwide by the International Rugby League (IRL), which publishes the International Laws of the Game (International Rugby League, n.d.). The pinnacle event is the Rugby League World Cup, played every four years; the 2026 edition ran from 15 October to 19 November 2026 in Australia, New Zealand and Papua New Guinea with men's (10 teams), women's (8) and wheelchair (8) tournaments plus the new physical disability World Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). At the finals on 15 November 2026 in Brisbane, Australia won both the men's title — the Kangaroos beat New Zealand 32–12 for a record 13th World Cup, with Jack Bostock named player of the match — and the women's title, the Jillaroos beating the Kiwi Ferns 34–6 for their fourth straight; England defended the wheelchair title (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Sky Sports, 2026). The top professional leagues are Australia's National Rugby League (NRL) and England's Super League; women's rugby league has been fully professional in England since 2018 and its World Cup has been held since 2000 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Rugby league is the 13-a-side code alongside [Rugby Union](rugby-union.html), with its own reduced formats [Rugby League Sevens](rugby-league-sevens.html) and [Rugby League Nines](rugby-league-nines.html); the full rugby-codes family is listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Rugby league is played under the IRL International Laws of the Game (2026 edition): 13 players per side, matches of 80 minutes in two 40-minute halves, on a field up to 100 m plus 22 m in-goal areas and 68 m wide (International Rugby League, n.d.). The attacking team has six tackles to score: after each tackle the ball is restarted with a play-the-ball, and on the sixth tackle possession is handed over unless the ball is kicked into touch, a penalty is awarded or the ball is grounded in-goal (International Rugby League, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scoring: try 4 points, conversion 2, penalty goal 2, drop goal 1 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Scrums of six and the 40/20 rule remain part of the game, and the sin bin (10 minutes) and video referee are standard at elite level (International Rugby League, n.d.). Men's and women's matches are played under identical laws, with separate World Cups and professional competitions; youth (under-19) and wheelchair and physical-disability classifications have their own adapted rulebooks (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Rugby league was born in 1895 when 21 clubs in northern England broke away from the Rugby Football Union over broken-time payments and formed the Northern Rugby Football Union, renamed the Rugby Football League in 1922 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The code spread to Australia, New Zealand and France, and the first Rugby League World Cup was played in 1954 in France (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of the game now retired include Australia's Wally Lewis, "The King of Rugby League," who captained Queensland and Australia in the 1980s; Cameron Smith, the most-capped Australian international; England's Jamie Peacock; and in the women's game Ali Brigginshaw, player of the match in the 2022 Women's World Cup final and one of the most decorated Jillaroos of all time (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
