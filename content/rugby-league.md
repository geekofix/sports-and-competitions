## Basic Information

Rugby league is a full-contact teamsport played thirteen-a-side with an oval ball, in which a try is worth four points and teams have six tackles to score before possession changes hands. It originated in 1895 in Huddersfield, England, when northern clubs broke from the Rugby Football Union over payments to players, and it is governed internationally by International Rugby League; its premier club competitions are Australia's National Rugby League (NRL) and the European Super League, and it is the national sport of Papua New Guinea.

## Rules

Rugby league is played on a rectangular field up to 120 m long by 68 m wide with H-shaped goals, in two 40-minute halves. The defining rules are the play-the-ball and the tackle count: when a player in possession is held, play restarts with the tackled player heeling the ball back to a teammate while defenders retire ten metres, and the attacking team has six tackles to advance before the ball is handed over. Points come from tries (grounding the ball in-goal, worth four points), conversions (two points, taken as a place kick after a try), penalty goals (two points) and drop goals (one point). Scrums are uncontested six-man set pieces, there is no line-out, and kicking on the last (sixth) tackle is standard tactics. Positions divide into six forwards and seven backs, and rule variations are the responsibility of the Rugby League International Federation through the International Laws of the Game.

## History

The game began on 29 August 1895, when twenty-two northern English clubs, frustrated by the RFU's ban on "broken-time" payments to working-class players, met at the George Hotel in Huddersfield and formed the Northern Rugby Football Union. The NRFU progressively reshaped rugby: professionalism was introduced in 1898, the line-out was abolished, player numbers were cut from 15 to 13 and rucks replaced by the play-the-ball in 1906, and the body adopted the name Rugby League in 1922 — making it the first fully professional football code. The tackle rule evolved from three play-the-balls (1966) to four (1967) and the modern six tackles in 1972, with a handover rather than a scrum from 1983. The first Rugby League World Cup followed in 1954 — 33 years before union's — and international strongholds developed in England, Australia (where the NRL grew out of the Sydney premiership), New Zealand and the Pacific Islands; Papua New Guinea, where the sport also paired with the islands' identity, and the Cook Islands consider it their national game. The two great club competitions today are the NRL (formed by the 1990s Super League war settlement between the Australian Rugby League and News Limited) and Super League in Europe, whose champion meets the NRL champion in the World Club Challenge.
