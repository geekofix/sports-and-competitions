## Basic Information

Cross country running is racing over natural terrain — grass, mud, hills, even snow — on a marked loop course, governed by World Athletics, which runs the annual World Athletics Cross Country Championships (World Athletics, n.d.-d). The 2026 edition was held on 10 January 2026 at Apalachee Regional Park in Tallahassee, Florida, where Jakob Kiplimo of Uganda won the senior men's race (10 km) and Agnes Ngetich of Kenya (pictured) won the senior women's race (8 km); Ethiopia won both team titles (World Athletics, n.d.-b). The senior distances are 10 km for men and 8 km for women, with U20 races over 8 km and 6 km, and team standings are decided by the placing of the first three counters (World Athletics, n.d.-a; World Athletics, n.d.-c).

## Rules

The course is a loop of 1.5-2 km on natural terrain with start and finish in the same area, slopes and obstacles allowed; athletes must stay on the marked course, spikes are permitted, and races are timed electronically (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Individual races are decided by order of finish; team competitions score the first three runners per nation, with the fourth as a tie-breaker, and mixed relay formats have been added at the World Championships (World Athletics, n.d.-a). Men and women race separately in senior and U20 categories, and para cross country exists within para athletics classifications (World Athletics, n.d.-a).

## History

Cross country running has roots in the 19th-century English fell races and was first contested as an international championship in 1903 at the International Cross Country Championships in England, the forerunner of today's World Athletics Cross Country Championships, held since 1973 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The great champions include Kenenisa Bekele of Ethiopia, who won the long and short course titles from 2002 to 2008, Mo Farah of Great Britain, and on the women's side Tirunesh Dibaba and Hellen Obiri of Kenya (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
