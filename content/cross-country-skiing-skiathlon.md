## Basic Information

Skiathlon is a cross-country skiing race that combines both techniques in one event: the first half is skied in the classic style, then after a pit stop where athletes change skis and poles, the second half is skied freestyle (skate). Olympic and World Championship distances are 50 km for men (25 km classic + 25 km freestyle) and 30 km for women (15 km + 15 km); sprint skiathlons of 10 km + 10 km are also contested. It is governed by the FIS under the same rules as cross-country skiing.

## Rules

Under the FIS International Competition Rules, skiathlon starts as a mass start in classic technique on prepared tracks; at the halfway point athletes enter a pit lane to change to freestyle equipment, after which they continue on wide groomed lanes in skating technique. The first athlete across the finish line wins. Pacing is crucial because the technique change costs time and energy. Skiathlon has been on the Olympic program since Cortina d'Ampezzo 1956 and is a staple of the FIS World Championships and World Cup.

## History

The skiathlon was invented in Norway in the mid-20th century to combine the two techniques in a single test of endurance; it debuted at the 1956 Winter Olympics in Cortina d'Ampezzo. The format was refined over the decades, with the mass start and mandatory equipment change introduced later to make it spectator-friendly. Norwegian and Swedish skiers historically dominated, with stars such as Vegard Ulvang, Bjørn Dæhlie and, more recently, Johannes Høsflot Klæbo and Therese Johaug winning Olympic and world titles.
