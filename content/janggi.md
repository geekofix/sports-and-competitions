## Basic Information

Janggi (Korean chess) is the Korean board game of the xiangqi family played on a 9x10 board: two armies of carriages, horses, elephants, guards and soldiers defend or attack the palace of the rival general, and unlike Chinese xiangqi the Janggi general moves along the lines of his palace and the pieces differ in detail.

It is Korea's professional board sport: janggi associations operate leagues with salaried masters, and the game is included in national mind-sport contests and amateur world championships.

## Rules

Each side commands pieces akin to xiangqi with distinctive Korean moves: the chariot and horse move similarly to chess rook and knight, the elephant leaps diagonally along extended paths, the guards protect the general inside the palace, and soldiers move one point forward or (after crossing the river-free board, since Janggi has no river) sideways. The general moves within his 3x3 palace along its lines. Play alternates with captures by displacement; checks must be answered and the game ends with checkmate or stalemate scoring (in Janggi, a stalemated player loses as with xiangqi tradition), with point-counting rules deciding games with insufficient material in tournament play.

## History

Janggi descends from xiangqi, arriving in Korea centuries ago and evolving distinct rules through Joseon dynasty play. The twentieth century brought professional organization: the Korean Janggi Association, founded in 1956, stages title matches and master leagues, and the game remains a popular pastime in Korean parks, with live broadcasts of major matches. International amateur participation grows through world janggi championships and cultural association tournaments beyond Korea.
