## Basic Information

Control line speed (FAI class F2A) is model aeroplane racing on two steel lines: the pilot stands in the centre of a circle and the model — a single-bladed-propeller, 2.5 cc "pan" model — flies tethered at the end of 15.92 m lines, clocked over a measured course (FAI, 2025). It belongs to the FAI aeromodelling family and is one of the fastest model aircraft classes.

![Model aircraft under construction with tools on the workbench](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/72nd_36-12.JPG?width=640)

*Figure: Operator III. (1899-11-30). Model aircraft under construction with tools on the workbench [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/72nd_36-12.JPG*

The F2A model has one inner wing panel, an outside stabilizer, a 2.5 cc (.15 ci) engine with tuned pipe and a one-bladed propeller; speeds above 300 km/h have been recorded, and the FAI ratifies world records in the class (FAI, n.d.). At the 2025 US National Aeromodeling Championships, Bill Hughes won F2A with 293.3 km/h (182.25 mph) (AMA, 2025).

## Rules

Competition rules (FAI Sporting Code, Volume F2) define the 15.92 m line length, the pylon/circle dimensions, fuel (standard FAI fuel for F2A), and the number of attempts per round; timing is electronic over a set number of laps (FAI, n.d.). The World Championships for control line are held every two years with F2A, F2B (aerobatics), F2C (team racing) and F2D (combat) (FAI, n.d.). Pilots of all ages compete; juniors fly the same class with the same rules.

## History

Control line aeromodelling began in the 1930s — the first model control-line contests were flown in Britain and the US in the late 1930s — and F2A speed became a world championship class with the first FAI World Championships in 1951 at Knokke, Belgium (FAI, 2025). Jim Cleveland and other American pioneers pushed speeds past 200 km/h in the 1960s–70s; carbon-fibre, asymmetric one-wing designs of the 21st century now exceed 300 km/h (FAI, 2025).
