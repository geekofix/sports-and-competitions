## Basic Information

Cross-country skiing (Nordic skiing) is skiing over snow-covered terrain using self-propulsion, in two techniques: classic (in set tracks, diagonal stride) and freestyle/skate (on groomed wide lanes). It is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), which runs the FIS Cross-Country World Cup, the World Championships and the Olympic cross-country events. Races include individual start, mass start, sprint, skiathlon and relays, over distances from 1.5 km sprints to 50 km.

## Rules

Under the FIS International Competition Rules, classic races are run in parallel tracks with a maximum of two skiers abreast, while freestyle races use wide groomed lanes. In individual start races, skiers start at intervals and the fastest time wins; in mass start and sprint, heats/quarterfinals/semifinals lead to a final. Skiathlon combines both techniques with a pit stop to change equipment mid-race. Courses must meet FIS homologation standards, and waxing rules and anti-doping rules apply. At the Olympics, cross-country has been on the program since the first Winter Games in Chamonix 1924.

## History

Cross-country skiing is one of the oldest forms of transport and recreation in Scandinavia, with military ski races in Norway from the 18th century. The first Olympic cross-country events were at Chamonix 1924, and the FIS (founded 1924 in Oslo, originally as the International Ski Commission) has governed the sport since. The Holmenkollen ski festival in Oslo (since 1892) is the sport's most famous venue. Norwegian, Swedish and Russian skiers dominated the early decades; today stars include Johannes Høsflot Klæbo (Norway) and Therese Johaug.
