## Basic Information

Tag rugby (also called flag rugby or rippa rugby) is a non-contact team game in which each player wears a belt with two velcro tags and defenders stop the ball carrier by pulling one tag; its mode of play is based on rugby league with similarities to touch football (Wikipedia, n.d.). The international governing body, the International Tag Federation (ITF), was formed in the summer of 2008 in Auckland at a meeting between Oztag Australia and New Zealand Tag Football Incorporated, and publishes the ITF Rule Book (2022 edition, revised 2023) (International Tag Federation, n.d.). In Australia alone some 150,000 players take part in Oztag competitions across six divisions — women's open, mixed, men's open and men's over-30s, over-35s and over-40s — so women compete both in all-female open teams and in mixed sides (Wikipedia, n.d.). The flagship event is the ITF Tag World Cup: after editions at the Sunshine Coast (2015), Coffs Harbour (2018) and the University of Limerick (2023, the first in the northern hemisphere), the 2026 Tag World Cup in Coffs Harbour, Australia, will bring together 30 nations in the largest international tag event ever (International Tag Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the ITF Rule Book, tag rugby is played six-a-side: the ball carrier may run, kick or pass, and the attacking team is allowed six successive tags before a changeover (International Tag Federation, n.d.). Contact is not permitted — a ball carrier caught with only one tag left, or none, must stop and play a roll ball (a rule also used in the British Try Tag Rugby leagues) (Wikipedia, n.d.). A try is worth 1 point, a try scored by a female player in a mixed game is worth 2 points, and at ITF-sanctioned events a try grounded in the bonus box earns an additional bonus point (International Tag Federation, n.d.). Games last 40 minutes (two halves of 20), and in mixed teams at most four of the six players on the field may be male — a rule designed to keep women's participation central (International Tag Federation, n.d.). The official rule source is the International Tag Federation Rule Book 2022 (International Tag Federation, n.d.).

## History

Tag rugby grew out of Oztag — tag versions of rugby league played in Australia from the early 1990s — and spread through Touch Football Australia's networks before the ITF was created in Auckland in 2008 to standardize the laws worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.; International Tag Federation, n.d.). The Pig 'n' Porter Festival in Limerick, Ireland, became the world's largest tag tournament, and the Tag Rugby World Cup has been held at the Sunshine Coast (2015), Coffs Harbour (2018), Limerick (2023) and Coffs Harbour again in 2026 with 30 nations (Wikipedia, n.d.). Great Britain recorded its best ever medal haul at the 2023 World Cup, including the Women's 30s title (Wikipedia, n.d.).
