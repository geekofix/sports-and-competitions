## Basic Information

The still rings are the men's artistic gymnastics apparatus of pure strength: two 18 cm rings suspended by straps from a frame about 2.8 m above the floor, on which the gymnast performs slow strength holds (cross, inverted cross, L-sit, planche), swings up to a handstand and finishes with a dismount (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). It is the third men's apparatus; at Paris 2024 the Olympic rings title was decided among the strength specialists of the 2026 world champion team of Japan (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.).

Among the leading ring workers of the 2026 season: a Swiss gymnast pictured competing on the rings at the 2026 Swiss Artistic Gymnastics Championships in Winterthur, alongside the Japanese world team champions and Armenia's Artur Davtyan, the Tokyo 2020 Olympic rings medallist and multiple world champion. This is a men's apparatus — the women's counterpart is the uneven bars (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

## Rules

The rings frame stands about 5.75 m high so the rings hang about 2.8 m above the floor; each ring has an inner diameter of about 18 cm and the straps are adjustable; a landing mat with an additional 10 cm soft mat on top of the 20 cm base mat is mandatory (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). The routine has no time limit and must include at least two strength holds and a dismount; eight elements count toward the D-score, and holds held less than 2 s earn deductions (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a).

A fall costs 0.5 plus execution deductions; the E-panel starts at 10.0 and deducts for swing, form and landing; the top eight from qualification advance to the final, where the highest D + E wins (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). Men compete on the rings; women have no elite equivalent (the uneven bars are the closest counterpart) (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

## History

The rings evolved from the swinging rings of 19th-century *Turnen* and have been an Olympic apparatus since 1896 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Koji Gushiken of Japan won the rings gold at Los Angeles 1984 with his powerful holds, Greece's Eleftherios Petrounias became Olympic rings champion at Rio 2016 after winning four straight European titles, and the USSR's Albert Azaryan and Nikolai Andrianov set the standard for strength in the 1970s–80s (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
