## Basic Information

Haidong Gumdo (also spelled Hae Dong Gum Do, "the way of the sword of the sea sun") is a Korean sword martial art founded in 1984 by Grandmaster Jeong Ho Seol, combining Korean sword techniques (kumdo/kendo-influenced cutting) with qigong breathing and meditation. It is practiced worldwide under the World Haidong Gumdo Federation, which holds international championships with cutting (beom) and forms (poomsae) divisions.

## Rules

In Haidong Gumdo competition, events include individual and team cutting (beom) on targets (rolled straw mats / tatami targets), forms (poomsae) judged on technique, and sparring in protective gear. Cutting events are scored on clean, powerful cuts through the target at prescribed angles; forms are judged on accuracy, power, balance and spirit. The World Championships, organized by the World Haidong Gumdo Federation, include individual, team and demonstration categories, with dan (black belt) and gup (color belt) divisions. Traditional etiquette (saluting, dojang conduct) is part of the art.

## History

Haidong Gumdo was founded in 1984 in Korea by Grandmaster Jeong Ho Seol, who synthesized the sword methods of Korean history (including the 16th-century "Muyedobotongji" military manual) with breathing and meditative practices; the art spread internationally from the 1990s, with the World Haidong Gumdo Federation organizing international tournaments. It is one of the Korean sword arts alongside kumdo (Korean kendo) and kumdo federations; demonstrations are common at martial arts festivals and Korean cultural events worldwide.
