## Basic Information

Haidong Gumdo is the Korean art of the sword — literally "the way of the sword of the East Sea" — a modern martial art reconstructed from the classical Korean sword methods and organized today by the World Haidong Gumdo Federation, founded on 25 November 1996 (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Grandmaster Kim Jeong-ho (10th dan), regarded as the founder of the modern art, opened his first dojang on 24 July 1982 in Anyang and founded the Korean Haidong Gumdo Association on 10 April 1984 in Seocho, Seoul, after being taught from 1961 by the monk Jang Baek San (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Today the federation reports around 1.5 million practitioners at more than 1,600 training centers in some 50 countries; the first World Championship was held on 23 July 2002 in Yongpyong, Kangwon Province, with more than 10,000 competitors from 14 countries (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.). Competitions are open to women and men in the same categories — forms, cutting and sparring — and women's world champions are crowned alongside the men's at the annual world championships (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.).

## Rules

Haidong Gumdo training and competition rest on the concept of shimgum — the unity of sword and spirit — and comprise forms (bon), cutting practice (against a target stand), sparring (kyorugi) and breaking; practitioners progress through the dan/kyu grading system under the World Haidong Gumdo Federation's regulations (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Sparring follows a graded progression of weapons — from the bamboo sword (chukdo) to the wooden sword (mokgum) and finally the steel sword — and competitors wear protective gear (head guard, chest protector, gloves, arm and leg guards) while scoring points for controlled cuts and thrusts to valid targets; illegal techniques such as strikes to the back of the head or joint locks in sparring are penalized and repeated fouls disqualify (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In addition to the sword (jingum) the curriculum includes the short sword, dagger, spear and other classical weapons, as well as empty-hand techniques; competition categories cover forms, cutting, sparring and team demonstration, with separate age and gender divisions at national and world championships — women and men compete under identical rules (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.). The official rules and event calendar are published by the World Haidong Gumdo Federation as the world governing body (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.).

## History

Haidong Gumdo traces its roots to the sword methods of the ancient Korean kingdom of Goguryeo and the warrior class (Samurang), preserved through temple and court traditions; the modern art was revived by Grandmaster Kim Jeong-ho, who learned from the monk Jang Baek San from 1961, opened his dojang in Anyang on 24 July 1982, and founded the Korean Haidong Gumdo Association on 10 April 1984 before establishing the World Haidong Gumdo Federation on 25 November 1996 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.). The first World Championship in Yongpyong on 23 July 2002 drew more than 10,000 competitors from 14 countries, and by 2004 the event had grown to 31 nations, establishing the annual world championship tradition (World Haidong Gumdo Federation, n.d.). The federation also organizes the Mulimpia demonstration games — first held in Korea in 2013 and in Europe at Cologne in October 2016 — and a related offshoot, Haedong Kumdo under Na Han-il, gained wider attention through a 1989 television drama (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
