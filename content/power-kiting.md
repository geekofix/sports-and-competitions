## Basic Information

Power kiting is flying large steerable foil or leading-edge kites to generate traction — for kite buggying, landboarding, snowkiting and kitesurfing — and as a sport of its own it contests kite size/pull records and precision flying: the world's largest kite and the strongest pull are documented record categories (Wikipedia, n.d.). Power kites range from 1 m² training kites to 20+ m² traction kites producing hundreds of kilograms of pull; competitions also include kite-fighting variants where the goal is to cut the opponent's line.

## Rules

Power-kiting rules and records: pull records are measured on a calibrated load cell with a harnessed pilot; size records by certified flat area; safety rules require quick-release systems, helmets and a minimum distance from spectators (often 2× line length); competitions class kites by size; men's and women's record categories exist (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Power kiting developed from Rogallo's flexible-wing research (1940s–60s, NASA parawing program) and Francis Jalbert's cell kite (1960s); traction kiting for boats and boards matured in the 1990s–2000s, and record flying (largest kite flown by a team) is documented by Guinness and kite associations (Wikipedia, n.d.).
