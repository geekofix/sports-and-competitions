## Basic Information

Indoor skydiving — also called bodyflying — is the sport of flying freely in a vertical wind tunnel, where a powerful upward airflow of roughly 190–290 km/h (120–180 mph) keeps the flyer aloft in a controlled, safe environment (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tunnels have flight chambers of about 12–32 ft (3.7–9.8 m) in diameter and are used both for skydiving training and as a competitive sport in their own right. The sport is governed internationally by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI), which sanctions the FAI Indoor Skydiving World Cup and the World Championships (WISC), first held in 2014 and 2015 respectively (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The main competition disciplines are Formation Skydiving 4-way (FS4), Vertical Formation Skydiving 4-way (VFS4), Dynamic 2-way and 4-way, and Solo Freestyle, with para-athlete classes making their championship debut at the 2026 FAI World Cup events in Marseille and Lille (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Among the leading current athletes, women are at the very top of the sport: Singapore's Kyra Poh, nicknamed the world's fastest flyer with speeds around 230 km/h, won solo freestyle gold at The Wind Games 2017, and Poland's Maja Kuczyńska is a multiple world champion in formation and VFS flying (The Wind Games, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the men's side, the reigning world and World Games champions in FS4, VFS4 and Dynamic head the 2026 World Cup standings (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Competitive indoor skydiving follows the FAI competition regulations and the WISC rulebook (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In Formation Skydiving 4-way, teams of four plus a videoman fly a set number of compulsory formations and then their own selection in a limited time window; judges award points for correctly completed points, with penalties for collisions, infringements and omitted points. VFS4 works the same way with four flyers in a vertical stack. In Dynamic (2-way or 4-way), pairs or teams take turns performing acrobatic moves — turns, transitions and carves — judged on difficulty, execution and presentation. Solo Freestyle is a choreographed routine performed to music, judged on difficulty, execution, artistic impression and presentation (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tunnel safety rules require a minimum age and weight, a briefing and helmet use for beginners, and a tunnel operator on duty at all times; flyers wear a jumpsuit and may use a helmet (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in the same disciplines under identical rules; championships offer open and women's podiums, and para classes were added to the World Cup program in 2026 (Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, n.d.-a). Recreational flyers progress through AFF-style levels with an instructor, from belly flying to turns, levels and head-up flying (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The vertical wind tunnel was invented for military parachute training: the first full-scale vertical wind tunnel was built in 1964 at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio, USA, and in 1979 the French engineer Jean St-Germain built the first Aerodium in Saint-Simon-de-Bagot, Quebec, Canada, opening it to the public (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The first commercial recreational tunnel, SkyVenture in Orlando, Florida, opened in 1999 and turned bodyflying into a leisure attraction worldwide (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Competitive bodyflying organized itself quickly: the World Indoor Skydiving Championships (WISC) held the first World Cup at iFLY Austin, Texas, in November 2014 and the first World Championships in Prague in 2015, and the FAI took the sport under its wing in the following years (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Among the icons of the sport are Lise Hernandez Girouard of Canada, who in the 1980s became the first flyer to perform choreographed routines to music, Amy Watson of the USA, who set the world record of 44 rotations in 60 seconds in 2017, and Kyra Poh of Singapore, the dominant solo freestyle flyer of the late 2010s with her gold medal at The Wind Games 2017 in Empuriabrava, Spain (The Wind Games, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
