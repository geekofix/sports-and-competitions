## Basic Information

Indoor skydiving is the sport of flying the human body in a vertical wind tunnel: a powerful upward airflow of up to roughly 250-300 km/h in a transparent flight chamber lets flyers float in a stable belly-to-earth freefall position, exactly as in freefall from an aircraft, but without a parachute. It is used both as recreation at commercial wind tunnel centers and as a competitive sport with judged disciplines, governed internationally by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI), the world air sports federation.

## Rules

Competitive indoor skydiving under FAI rules is divided into artistic events — Formation Skydiving (teams of four or eight build formations), Vertical Formation Skydiving (VFS), Solo Freestyle and Dynamic (head-to-head racing through a course of maneuvers against the clock) — plus Solo Speed. Competitors wear a jumpsuit, helmet and goggles, enter through the tunnel door and must complete compulsory and free routines within the allotted time; judges score difficulty, execution and presentation, and penalties apply for infringing the flight chamber or tunnel safety rules. In formation events, teams must link grips in the correct sequence as fast as possible.

## History

The first recorded instance of indoor skydiving dates to 1964, when a NASA scientist working on the Apollo program experimented with human flight in a vertical wind tunnel built for research at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio. Vertical wind tunnels had been used for parachute research since the mid-20th century, and the first commercial entertainment tunnels opened in North America in the early 1980s. The number of tunnels worldwide grew from fewer than 50 in 2012 to more than 200 in recent years, and the FAI embraced indoor skydiving as an official discipline, staging the first FAI World Indoor Skydiving Championships in 2015, with further World Championships and World Cups held since, including the 5th edition in Charleroi, Belgium, in 2025.
