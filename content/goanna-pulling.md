## Basic Information

Goanna pulling is a uniquely Australian novelty strength sport in which two competitors, on all fours and linked by a heavy leather strap around their heads/necks, pull against each other in a tug-of-war — the first to pull the opponent over a center line wins. Despite the name, no goannas (lizards) are involved; the competitors simply look like goannas while pulling. The Australian National Goanna Pulling Championships have been held annually in Wooli, New South Wales, since 1984/85.

## Rules

Competitors face each other on all fours on a pulling pad, each with a leather strap (the "goanna strap") fitted around the head/neck, connected over a center line. On the starter's signal both pull backward using neck, back and leg strength; the first to drag the opponent across the center line wins the bout. Events are organized in weight divisions (men's lightweight, middleweight, heavyweight; women's under and over 70 kg) plus juniors, in a knockout or round-robin format. The championship day in Wooli also includes wood chopping, tug-of-war and novelty races.

## History

The origins are traced to 19th-century Australian bush and mining culture, where men tested neck and back strength in informal contests; the first organized Australian National Goanna Pulling Championships were held in 1984 by the Woolie Sports Club in Wooli, NSW, and have run every October long weekend since 1985, attracting thousands of visitors. The event is covered by Australian media and the NSW government events calendar, and it remains the flagship of Wooli's annual festival alongside other novelty competitions.
