## Basic Information

Hornussen is an indigenous Swiss team sport — one of Switzerland's three national sports alongside wrestling (*Schwingen*) and stone throwing (*Steinstössen*) — in which a batter hits a 78 g plastic disc called the *Nouss* (certified "Hornuss 98") off a ramp called the *Bock* with a flexible stick up to 3 m long, sending it buzzing at up to 300 km/h into the field called the *Ries*, where the opposing team tries to stop it in mid-air with a wooden paddle called the *Schindel* (Eidgenössischer Hornusserverband [EHV], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Teams consist of 16–20 players; a *Durchgang* is a pair of turns in which each player of the batting team hits twice, and a championship match is played over two Durchgänge (three to four at festival events); points are scored for distance from 100 m onward (+1 point per further 10 m), and a Nouss that lands in the Ries unstopped costs the defending team one *Numero* (penalty point) — the team with the fewest Numeros wins, with the higher point total as tie-breaker (EHV, n.d.-a). The EHV was founded in 1902 in Burgdorf with 24 clubs (over 170 clubs by 2011) and organizes the championship in several league tiers (*Ligen*) plus the triennial Eidgenössisches Hornusserfest — the 40th edition was held in August 2024 in Höchstetten with 226 teams on 32 fields. Women play in the same mixed teams as men (there is no separate category; a 2016 survey counted 9 % women among active members) and contest an annual dedicated Frauen- und Meitlihornussen (the 17th in 2026) (EHV, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The batter places the Nouss on the Bock, takes a measured run-up (*Anmessen*) to signal readiness, and strikes it with the flexible stick (*Stecken*/*Träf*); a shot guard (*Schussblende*) in front of the Bock prevents dangerously flat trajectories (EHV, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Ries begins 100 m from the Bock, is up to 180 m deep and widens from about 8 m to 14 m; number boards along the Ries show the distance achieved. The defending team positions its players (*Abtuer*) in the Ries and stops the flying Nouss with the Schindel before it touches the ground; every Nouss that lands in the Ries unstopped is one Numero, and the batting team scores one point for the first 100 m plus one point per additional 10 m (EHV, n.d.-a). Each match consists of a set number of Durchgänge (two in the championship game, three to four at fests); within a Durchgang each batter hits twice, and the teams swap roles after each turn; the team with the fewest Numeros wins, ties are broken by the higher point total, and the *Schlägerkönig* (hitting king) is crowned at every Eidgenössisches Hornusserfest (EHV, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two referees (one per team) control play under the official *Spielreglement* (current edition valid from 1 March 2026); players born in 1984 or later must wear a helmet, and women and men play under identical rules in the same teams (EHV, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

## History

The first referee of a hornussen game is recorded in 1625 in Lauperswil (canton of Lucerne), and the first documented match took place in 1655 in Trub (canton of Bern); the Bernese writer Jeremias Gotthelf brought the game wider literary attention in his 1842 novella *Die schwarze Spinne* (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Organized play began in the early 20th century: the Eidgenössischer Hornusserverband was founded in 1902 in Burgdorf with 24 societies, and it has organized the national league championship (today in five *Ligen*) and the Eidgenössische Hornusserfeste ever since — the 40th fest in Höchstetten in August 2024 drew 226 teams on 32 Ries of about 350 m each (EHV, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). An international hornussen association has existed since 2012, and hornussen was part of the demonstration program at the Eidgenössische Schwing- und Älplerfest until 2019 (EHV, n.d.-b).
