## Basic Information

Hornussen is a traditional Swiss team sport in which a heavy rubber puck (the "nouss", up to about 780 g) is struck with a flexible whip-like stick (the hornuss whip, up to 3 m long) from a wooden ramp, and the opposing team tries to catch it with wooden shovels (schaufeln). Points are scored for long flights and catches. It is organized in Switzerland by the Swiss Hornussen Federation (hornussen.ch) and played mainly in the cantons of Bern, Solothurn and Aargau.

## Rules

Each team fields up to 16 players (batters, catchers and scorers). The batter places the nouss on the wooden ramp (the bock) and strikes it with the whip toward the opposing field; the defending team positions catchers with wooden shovels across the field to stop or catch the puck. Points are awarded for long, uncaught flights, and the defending team scores for catches. After each round the teams switch roles; the team with more points after both games (Durchgänge) wins.

## History

Hornussen is documented in Switzerland from the 17th century, possibly with earlier German roots in similar bat-and-puck games. It became organized in the 19th century with local clubs, and the Swiss Hornussen Federation coordinates the national championship and the Eidgenössisches Hornusserfest, held every three years since the 1980s. The sport remains a beloved folk tradition in central Switzerland, with thousands of players in over a hundred clubs.
