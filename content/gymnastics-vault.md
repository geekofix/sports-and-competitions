## Basic Information

Vault is the explosive artistic gymnastics event in which the gymnast sprints down a 25 m runway, springs off a board onto a vaulting table set at 1.25 m for women and 1.35 m for men (1.60 m long, 0.95 m wide, front face inclined), and performs a single acrobatic vault — measured by its difficulty value and execution (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It is the first apparatus in the women's rotation and the fourth for men; at Paris 2024 the men's title went to Carlos Yulo of the Philippines and the women's to Simone Biles of the USA (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.).

Among the leading vaulters of the 2026 season, the women come first: Skye Blakely of the United States (pictured), the 2026 world all-around champion with a powerful vault final, Simone Biles, the 2024 Olympic vault champion with her eponymous Yurchenko double pike, and Rebeca Andrade of Brazil (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). On the men's side Carlos Yulo, double Olympic vault/floor champion, and the 2024 Olympic silver medallist Mehdi Olfati of Iran lead the lists; Uzbekistan's Oksana Chusovitina remains the longevity icon, having competed at eight Olympic Games from 1992 to 2024 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The runway is 25 m long, ending in a springboard in front of the vaulting table (1.25 m high for women, 1.35 m for men; 1.60 m long and 0.95 m wide); the gymnast may touch the table with hands only and must land feet-first in the designated area on a landing mat with an additional 10 cm soft mat on top of the 20 cm base mat at FIG events (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-c; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). Each vault belongs to a difficulty group (Yurchenko, Tsukahara, handspring, etc.) with a fixed value in the Code of Points table; in qualification and the all-around each gymnast performs one vault, and in the apparatus final two vaults from different groups whose scores are averaged (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a).

A fall or landing on the head/neck is prohibited and scored zero; the E-panel deducts from 10.0 for flight, execution, landing and deductions, and the final score is D + E; the top eight from qualification advance to the final (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a; Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e). Men and women use the same apparatus and rules with different table heights, and the event is part of the team and all-around competitions; junior categories use a lower table (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-e).

## History

Vaulting evolved from the 19th-century vaulting horse — used at the first Olympics in 1896 — to the modern vaulting table, introduced internationally in the early 2000s; the springboard and table made vaulting faster and more acrobatic (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Oksana Chusovitina of Uzbekistan, pictured, is the sport's great longevity icon: she competed at eight Olympic Games from Barcelona 1992 to Paris 2024 and won vault silver at Beijing 2008; Romania's Marian Drăgulescu won the 2004 Athens vault title. In the modern era the Yurchenko family of vaults, perfected by Svetlana Boginskaya and later Simone Biles (whose double-pike is named after her), defines the event's difficulty frontier (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
