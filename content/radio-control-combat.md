## Basic Information

Control line combat (FAI class F2D) is model aeroplane dogfighting: two pilots fly semi-scale combat models on 15.92 m lines at over 150 km/h, each towing a crepe-paper streamer, and try to cut the opponent’s streamer while avoiding mid-air collisions (FAI, n.d.). It belongs to the FAI aeromodelling family.

![F2D combat model on the grass](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/F2d-modell.jpg?width=420)

*Figure: Benutzer:MinCe. (4 July 2006 (original upload date)). F2D combat model on the grass [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 3.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/F2d-modell.jpg*

A bout lasts up to 4 minutes (with a maximum engine run of 7 seconds plus 2 minutes of landing time in competition formats); each cut of the opponent’s streamer scores a point, and a mid-air collision costs the colliding pilot points (FAI, n.d.). Models are 2.5 cc diesel-powered, with a 3 m wingspan limit and a minimum weight; the streamer is 3 m of crepe paper on a 2 m line (FAI, n.d.).

## Rules

Competition is run under the FAI F2D rules at world and continental championships, held every two years together with the other control-line classes (FAI, n.d.). Safety rules require helmets for pilots and helpers, a fenced flight circle and a kill switch (FAI, n.d.). Radio-control (RC) combat variants (e.g. RC combat with electric models) exist under national associations. Men and women both compete at national level.

## History

F2D combat was developed in the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe in the 1950s–60s and became an FAI world championship class in the 1960s; the 2013 world championships in Russia drew entries from more than 20 countries (Wikipedia, n.d.). The modern diesel combat model with its one-bladed look and screaming engine is one of the most spectacular classes in aeromodelling (FAI, n.d.).
