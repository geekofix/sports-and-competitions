## Basic Information

Teqball is a ball sport invented in 2014 in Hungary by former professional footballer Gábor Borsányi together with György Gattyán and Viktor Huszár, combining football, table tennis and freestyle skills on a curved table; it was first showcased publicly in Budapest with Brazilian football icon Ronaldinho (Wikipedia, n.d.). The International Teqball Federation (FITEQ), founded on 28 March 2017 in Budapest, governs the sport with more than 124 member federations; it was recognized by the Olympic Council of Asia in 2018, by ANOCA in 2019 and became a full GAISF member in 2020 (FITEQ, n.d.). Teqball was a medal sport at the European Games 2023 in Kraków and won its first Asian Games medals at the 20th Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya 2026, and the 9th Teqball World Championships were held in Budapest from 1 to 6 December 2026 (Asian Games Aichi-Nagoya 2026, n.d.; FITEQ, n.d.). Among the current top female players — named first — is Brazil's Natalia Guitler, the 2021 women's singles world champion, pictured below, alongside the leaders of the FITEQ world rankings; India's Quimcy Dsouza competed in women's singles at the 2026 Asian Games (FITEQ, n.d.).

## Rules

Teqball is played on a curved table 3 m long and 1.7 m wide, standing 0.76 m high at the center and 0.565 m at the edges, with a 0.14 m high plexiglass net across the middle, using a size-5 football (FITEQ, n.d.). Players may use any body part except hands and arms; the ball must cross to the opponent's side and bounce there once (FITEQ, n.d.). A team may touch the ball at most three times, no body part may be used twice in a row, and the ball must be returned over the net (FITEQ, n.d.). A set is played to 12 points, win by 2, and the match is best of three sets; under the rules in force from 16 May 2026 the service alternates every two points and a player may smash at most twice per set (FITEQ, n.d.). Events are men's and women's singles, men's and women's doubles and mixed doubles, with youth categories — women and men compete under identical rules (FITEQ, n.d.). The official rule source is the FITEQ competition rules (FITEQ, n.d.).

## History

Gábor Borsányi conceived teqball in 2014 in Budapest, and the first table was built the same year; the sport was unveiled to the public in Budapest in 2016 with Ronaldinho presenting it internationally (Wikipedia, n.d.). FITEQ was founded on 28 March 2017, the first Teqball World Championships were held in Budapest in 2019 with 160 athletes from 58 countries, and the sport received OCA recognition in 2018, ANOCA recognition in 2019 and full GAISF membership in 2020 (FITEQ, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). It was a medal sport at the 2023 European Games in Kraków, won its first Asian Games medals at Aichi-Nagoya 2026, and returned to Budapest for the 9th World Championships on 1–6 December 2026 (Asian Games Aichi-Nagoya 2026, n.d.; FITEQ, n.d.).
