## Basic Information

Artistic swimming — formerly known as synchronized swimming, renamed by the international federation in 2017 — is an aquatic discipline in which swimmers perform choreographed routines to music, judged on technical merit, artistic impression, execution, difficulty and synchronization. It is governed by World Aquatics and has been an Olympic event since Los Angeles 1984 (World Aquatics [WA], n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.). Since Paris 2024 only the duet and team events are on the Olympic program (96 athletes); men are allowed in the team event for the first time — though none competed in Paris — and at Los Angeles 2028 up to two men per eight-member team will make their Olympic debut. At the 2025 World Aquatics Championships in Singapore, China swept all three team titles (technical, free and acrobatic routines) and won the first women's solo gold (Zhang), while Spain's Iris Tió and Dennis González took mixed duet free gold with 323.8563 points (WA, n.d.-b) (World Aquatics, n.d.-a) (World Aquatics, n.d.-b).

Among the athletes shaping the sport today, the leading women come first: Great Britain's Ranjuo Tomblin won the solo free, solo technical and mixed duet (with Isabelle Thorpe) at the 2026 European Championships in August 2026, and Spain's Blanca Toledano — Olympic bronze with the Spanish team at Paris 2024 — remains one of Europe's leading swimmers (WA, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Routines are performed in the pool to music and include figures, lifts, acrobatics and highlights out of the water. At World Championships and the Olympics, the team competition consists of a technical routine, a free routine and — since 2023 — an acrobatic routine, each within set time limits and with required elements; solo, duet, mixed duet and team of eight are contested. Panels of judges award technical merit, artistic impression and execution scores. Men may compete in team events since Paris 2024, with a maximum of two men per team at Los Angeles 2028; men and women otherwise compete in separate events (WA, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).

## History

The sport began as "water ballet" in the 1890s — Australian Annette Kellerman popularized it around 1907 — and entered the Olympic program at Los Angeles 1984. From Sydney 2000 to Tokyo 2020, Russia and the Russian Olympic Committee won all twelve Olympic gold medals in artistic swimming; since 2022 China has dominated, sweeping the team events at Singapore 2025. The international federation FINA renamed the discipline "artistic swimming" in July 2017 (WA, n.d.-a; Olympics.com, n.d.).
