## Basic Information

Pond hockey is a form of ice hockey played on a natural frozen body of water — a pond, lake or river — rather than in an indoor rink, using simplified rules with no boards, low nets and usually no goaltender, so the game emphasizes skating, puckhandling and teamwork over shooting and checking (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It survives both as informal pickup play — Canadians call it shinny — and as an organized tournament sport, whose flagship event is the World Pond Hockey Championship held every February on Roulston Lake in Plaster Rock, New Brunswick, Canada (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2026 edition, played 19–22 February on Roulston Lake, fielded 90 teams — 80 men's and 10 women's — from Canada, the United States, Slovakia, Czechia, the United Kingdom and the United Arab Emirates, with more than 250 games over four days; in the women's division The Bras of Grand Falls, New Brunswick defended their title, and in the men's final the team from Kolín, Czechia beat the Aces of Fredericton (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Pond hockey is open to women and men, who compete in separate divisions at the world championship alongside mixed recreational play.

## Rules

Tournament pond hockey is played four-on-four with substitutes on the bench on rinks about 50 to 80 percent the size of an NHL rink, with no boards — a barrier of snow keeps the puck in play (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). There are no goalies: a player who guards the net may concede an automatic goal, and the goals are low — about 6 feet wide but only roughly 10 inches high — so pucks stay on the ice and out of neighboring rinks and spectators. Goals may only be scored from past the center line, there is no formal penalty box, and penalties are punished by an awarded goal or ejection; helmets and skates are mandatory in organized events and games commonly run two 15-minute halves (US Pond Hockey Championship, n.d.). Formats at the world championship run round-robin groups into a final-day bracket of the top 32 men's and top 8 women's teams (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

Hockey itself was born outdoors on frozen ponds and rivers, and informal shinny remains the game's most accessible form across northern climates. The organized version grew up in the 2000s: the World Pond Hockey Championship was first held in 2002 in Plaster Rock, New Brunswick, created by Tom Chamberlain and Danny Braun as a fundraiser for the local recreation centre, growing from 40 teams in its first year to about 120 international teams playing on 20 rinks built on Roulston Lake; in 2007 the profits helped open the Tobique-Plex and Canadian Prime Minister Stephen Harper dropped the puck (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Other events followed, including the Canadian National Pond Hockey Championships in Huntsville, Ontario (first held 2006) and the USA Hockey-sanctioned US Pond Hockey Championship in Minneapolis, which started in 2006 on Lake Nokomis (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
