## Basic Information

Pond hockey is a form of ice hockey played on a natural frozen body of water—a pond, lake or river—rather than an indoor rink. It uses simplified rules, no body checking and typically no goalies, with low nets that force shots along the ice, and it emphasizes skating, puckhandling and teamwork over shooting and checking. The game survives both as informal shinny and as an organized tournament sport, whose flagship events include the World Pond Hockey Championship in Plaster Rock, New Brunswick, Canada, and national championships in Canada and the United States.

## Rules

Tournament pond hockey is typically played four-on-four (with substitutes on the bench) on rinks about 50 to 80 percent the size of an NHL rink and with no boards; a barrier of snow usually keeps the puck in play. There are no goalies and no goaltending: a player who guards the net may concede an automatic goal, and goals are low—nets about 6 feet wide but only about 10 inches high—so pucks stay on the ice and out of neighboring rinks and spectators. Players must generally be past the center line to score, and there is no traditional penalty box: penalties are punished by awarding a goal to the opposing team or by ejecting the offender. Helmets and skates are mandatory in organized events, goalie equipment is prohibited, and games commonly run two 15-minute halves.

## History

Hockey itself was born outdoors on frozen ponds and rivers, and informal pond play—which Canadians call shinny—remains the game's most accessible form across northern climates: pick-up games with boots for goalposts and no referees. The organized version grew up in the 2000s: the World Pond Hockey Championship was first held in 2002 in Plaster Rock, New Brunswick, created by Tom Chamberlain and Danny Braun as a fundraiser for the local recreation centre, and grew from 40 teams in its first year to about 120 teams from many countries, played on 20 rinks built on Roulston Lake. In 2007 the profits helped open the Tobique-Plex recreation centre, and that year the Canadian Prime Minister Stephen Harper dropped the puck. Other events followed, such as the Canadian National Pond Hockey Championships in Huntsville, Ontario, first held in 2006, and the USA Hockey-sanctioned pond hockey events across the northern states, including the US Pond Hockey Championship in Minneapolis, which started in 2006 and stages its rinks on Lake Nokomis.
