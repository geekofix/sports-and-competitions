## Basic Information

Irish road bowling (ból an bhóthair, also called "bullets" or "long bullets") is an ancient Irish sport in which competitors throw a heavy iron ball — the "bullet", weighing about 794 g (28 oz) with a circumference of about 18 cm — along a set course of country roads, aiming to reach the finish in the fewest possible throws. It is played mainly in counties Cork and Armagh, where it remains a living tradition with weekly fixtures, and is governed in Ireland by Ból Chumann na hÉireann, the Irish Road Bowling Association, founded in 1954.

## Rules

A match or "score" is played by two individuals or teams over a fixed course of typically several kilometres. The thrower runs up to a throwing mark and releases the bullet underhand — in the Northern (Armagh) style the arm is swung behind the back, in the Southern (Cork) style the arm is whirled overhead into the throw — and must not step over the mark. Wherever the ball comes to rest, a mark is made and the next throw is taken from behind it; over tight corners the ball may be lofted through the air, and a failed loft counts as a shot. The competitor with the fewest throws to the finish wins; if both reach the line with equal throws, the one whose final throw travels farthest beyond the line wins. A "road shower" advises the thrower on the best line, like a golf caddy.

## History

Road bowling is documented in Ireland from at least the 17th century; one theory holds that it was brought to Ireland by Dutch troops during the Williamite War in 1689, and the similar Dutch game klootschieten survives in the Netherlands and northern Germany. Until the 19th century the game was also played in Scotland, northern England and North America, and in the past the winner was the player who threw farthest in a set number of shots. Ból Chumann na hÉireann was formed in 1954 at Enniskeane, County Cork, and the first national Cork-Armagh championship followed in 1963; the first World Championship was staged as part of the Cork 800 celebrations in 1985. Today the All-Ireland Senior Championship is the highlight of the season, and Irish road bowling clubs also thrive in Boston, New York and West Virginia in the United States.
