## Basic Information

Skyrunning is mountain running at high altitude, governed internationally by the International Skyrunning Federation (ISF), founded in 2008, and organised in three main categories — VERTICAL (uphill-only races), SKY (races up to about 50 km with significant elevation) and SKYULTRA (ultra distances of 50 km and beyond) — plus downhill and combined formats (ISF, n.d.-a). The Skyrunner World Series has run since 2004, and the 2026 Skyrunning World Championships, the ninth edition, were held 18–20 September 2026 on La Gomera in the Canary Islands: Paola Stampanoni of Switzerland and Lluís Puigvert of Spain won the VERTICAL titles; Cristian Minoggio of Italy and Shangave Balendran of Norway won the 63 km SKYULTRA; and Luca Del Pero of Italy and Patrycja Stanek of Poland won the SKY titles (ISF, n.d.-b). The 2026 Masters World Championships were staged at the Pierra Menta in Arêches, France, 3–4 July, and at Pisão Extreme in Portugal, 3–4 October, and the VK OPEN World Cup comprised eight races in six countries on four continents (ISF, n.d.-b). Among the current leading women are Paola Stampanoni, Patrycja Stanek and Shangave Balendran, winners at the 2026 World Championships (ISF, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under ISF regulations, races are point-to-point or circuit events on marked mountain trails with mandatory checkpoints and cut-off times; runners must carry the mandatory kit listed for each race (water, jacket, whistle, headlamp for ultras), and failure to carry it or to pass a checkpoint brings disqualification (ISF, n.d.-a). The three categories are defined by distance and elevation: VERTICAL races climb at least 1,000 metres with no significant downhill; SKY races are up to roughly 50 km at altitude below about 2,200 metres with major climbs; SKYULTRA races are 50 km and longer with extreme elevation, such as the 63 km and +4,100 m course at the 2026 world championships on La Gomera (ISF, n.d.-b). Rankings are decided by points from World Series, World Cup and championship races, published in the ISF world ranking; medals are awarded per category and an overall combined title as well (ISF, n.d.-b). Men and women race in separate classifications on the same courses, with junior (under-20 and under-23) and masters age groups at championships (ISF, n.d.-a).

## History

Skyrunning as a named discipline dates to 1992, when a series of extreme high-altitude mountain races in the Italian Alps — organised under the banner coined by the Italian mountaineer Marino Giambattista — gave birth to the term "skyrunning" (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first Skyrunning World Championship followed in 1998, the Skyrunner World Series was launched in 2004, and the International Skyrunning Federation (ISF) was founded in 2008 to govern the sport, with the World Championships held biennially since (ISF, n.d.-a). Iconic races of the early era — the Tromsø SkyRace in Norway, Zegama-Aizkorri in the Spanish Basque Country and the Pikes Peak Marathon in Colorado — built the sport's following, and Giambattista remains its founding figure (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The ninth edition of the world championships on La Gomera in September 2026 confirmed Spain, Italy and Japan as the leading nations in the country ranking (ISF, n.d.-b).
