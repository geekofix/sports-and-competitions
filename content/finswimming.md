## Basic Information

Finswimming is the competitive swimming of short distances with fins — either one large monofin or a pair of bi-fins — on the surface of a 50 m pool, in apnoea (breath-hold) disciplines, in immersion disciplines with scuba equipment, and over long distances in open water; races are timed electronically to hundredths of a second (CMAS, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.). It is governed worldwide by the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS), founded in Monaco in 1959, which organizes the biennial Finswimming World Championships and maintains the official world records; finswimming has been on the World Games program since 2017 and was contested at the Chengdu 2025 World Games (CMAS, n.d.-a; The World Games Association, n.d.). The 2026 CMAS World Championship Finswimming Indoor was held in Incheon, South Korea, from 24 to 28 June 2026 at the Munhak Park Tae-Hwan Aquatics Center, with 36 events (CMAS, n.d.-a). See also [Swimming](swimming.html), [Artistic Swimming](artistic-swimming.html) and [Diving](diving.html) on this site.

At the 2026 World Championships in Incheon, the host nation South Korea dominated the sprint events: Hwang Gyu-jin won the men's 50 m surface in an Asian-record 15.03 s, ahead of his compatriot Park Tae-ho; Nándor Kiss of Hungary swept the men's middle distances from 200 m to 800 m (CMAS, n.d.-a). Women contest the identical programme of events — surface, bi-fins, apnoea and immersion, individual and mixed relays — and the senior (18 and over) and junior (12–17) classifications are scored separately for both genders, so the top women are ranked alongside the men in the same CMAS world-record tables (CMAS, n.d.-b).

## Rules

All pool races are swum in a 50 m pool with electronic touch-pad timing, and CMAS recognizes world records only in long-course pools for seniors (18+) and juniors (12–17) in both genders (CMAS, n.d.-b). The surface (SF) programme comprises individual races over 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 and 1500 m plus relays of 4 × 100 m, 4 × 200 m and a mixed 4 × 50 m; bi-fins (BF) are raced over 50, 100, 200 and 400 m plus a mixed 4 × 100 m relay; apnoea (AP) is contested over 50 m (juniors under 14 swim 25 m), holding the breath after a surface start; and immersion (IM) over 100 m and 400 m with a scuba tank and mask, the swimmer breathing from the tank while swimming under the surface (CMAS, n.d.-b). Surface swimmers wear fins and goggles and may use a snorkel in some events, start from the starting block, and must break the surface within 15 m of the start and turns; apnoea and immersion starts are push-offs from the wall (CMAS, n.d.-b). In open-water long-distance finswimming, individuals race 6 km and teams a mixed 4 × 2 km relay, using surface or bi-fins techniques (CMAS, n.d.-b). Equipment is checked before races: monofins and bi-fins must meet size rules, masks and snorkels must be approved, and in apnoea no breathing apparatus is allowed (CMAS, n.d.-b). Fouls such as false starts, incorrect turns or interfering with another lane lead to disqualification (CMAS, n.d.-b). Men and women swim the same distances and classifications; masters categories from 30 to 75+ years exist at dedicated masters championships (CMAS, n.d.-a). Para finswimming for athletes with impairments is also contested under CMAS at world level (CMAS, n.d.-a).

## History

Finswimming as a sport became possible with the invention of the aqualung by Jacques-Yves Cousteau and Émile Gagnan in 1943, which made extended underwater swimming safe, and with the rubber fins developed in the 1930s–40s; underwater swimming clubs appeared in France and Italy in the 1940s–50s (Wikipedia, n.d.). The Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS) was founded in Monaco in 1959 by delegates of ten national federations and has governed finswimming, apnoea diving and underwater hockey ever since; the first senior Finswimming World Championship was held in Hanover in 1976, the junior championships began in 1989, and the long-distance open-water events were added later (CMAS, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.). Icons of the sport include the Italian freediving pioneer Enzo Maiorca (1931–2014), who set dozens of world records in the 1950s–70s, and his French rival Jacques Mayol (1927–2001), the first human to reach 100 m on a single breath in 1976 (Wikipedia, n.d.). On the women's side, the Russian Natalia Molchanova (1962–2015) won more than 40 world records and 23 world titles in freediving and finswimming before her death in 2015, remaining the most decorated female underwater athlete in history (Wikipedia, n.d.). Finswimming entered the World Games program in 2017 and was part of the Chengdu 2025 World Games, where the men's 50 m apnoea world record of 13.70 s by Pavel Kabanov of Russia stood as the benchmark of the sport (CMAS, n.d.-a; The World Games Association, n.d.).
