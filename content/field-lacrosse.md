## Basic Information

Field lacrosse is a fast, full-contact team sport played with a small rubber ball and a long-handled stick (the crosse) with a netted head, in which players cradle, pass and shoot the ball into the opponent's goal while checking opponents with their bodies and sticks (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men's field lacrosse is played 10 against 10 on a large outdoor field; women's field lacrosse uses modified rules without stick or body checking. The sport is governed worldwide by World Lacrosse (62 member nations), which runs the quadrennial men's World Championship — the next edition is Tokyo, Japan, 18–27 June 2027 — and the Women's World Championship; lacrosse returns to the Olympic program at Los Angeles 2028 in the compact six-a-side "sixes" format, its first Olympic appearance since 1908 (World Lacrosse, n.d.-a; World Lacrosse, n.d.-b). See also [Box Lacrosse](box-lacrosse.html) on this site.

At the 2026 World Lacrosse Women's Championship in Tokyo (24 July–2 August 2026), the United States, led by stars such as Charlotte North, defended the 2022 title with Canada taking silver; on the men's side Canada won the inaugural 2026 Pan-American Championship in Kingston, Jamaica, 17–23 August 2026, defeating the United States 9–8, with the Haudenosaunee taking bronze (World Lacrosse, n.d.-a).

 Within the lacrosse and stick-games family on this site, its closest relatives are [Box Lacrosse](box-lacrosse.html), [Lacrosse](lacrosse.html), [Sixes Lacrosse](sixes-lacrosse.html), [Hurling](hurling.html), [Camogie](camogie.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Men's field lacrosse is played 10 v 10 — three attackmen, three midfielders, three defendemen and one goalkeeper — on a field 110 yards (100 m) long and 60 yards (55 m) wide, with goals of 6 × 6 ft (1.8 m) set 80 yards apart; matches at World Lacrosse events consist of four 15-minute quarters (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c). After each goal and at the start of each quarter a face-off ("draw") is held at the center; play is continuous, with offside rules of four players over the midfield line for the attacking team and three for the defending team (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c). The goalkeeper defends a crease (a circle of 9 ft / 2.7 m radius — the goal crease is 18 ft in diameter, i.e. 5.5 m) and may use hands; a goal counts only if the ball fully crosses the goal line (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c). Players may carry ("cradle"), pass and shoot the ball with the stick, and body checks and stick checks are legal within the rules, while slashing, cross-checking, hits to the head and dangerous play are fouls punished with time-serving penalties of 30 seconds to 3 minutes in a penalty box (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c). Women's field lacrosse is played 12 v 12 on the same field dimensions with two 30-minute halves, but stick checking and body contact are prohibited, players must wear goggles and mouthguards (optional field players' gloves), and there is no offside in the same sense; draws after goals are taken inside a marked draw circle around each goal (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c). Sixes lacrosse, the Olympic format for LA 2028, is played 6 v 6 on a smaller field with short sticks only, four 8-minute quarters and face-offs after every goal (World Lacrosse, n.d.-b). Men and women therefore compete under clearly different contact rules, while age classifications (under-19, under-21) and para/box variants exist alongside (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c).

## History

Lacrosse grew out of the Indigenous ball games of North America — the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) game of tewaarathon, documented from the 12th century — which French settlers in Canada observed and named "la crosse" (the bishop's crozier) because of the stick's resemblance to it (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The modern men's game was codified in 1867 by William George Beers of the Montreal Lacrosse Club, who wrote the first official rules — field dimensions, positions, face-offs and penalties — and founded the National Lacrosse Association of Canada (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Lacrosse was an Olympic demonstration sport in 1904 and 1908, when Canada won both tournaments (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The International Lacrosse Federation was founded in 1974; the men's and women's world bodies merged in 2008 into the Federation of International Lacrosse, renamed World Lacrosse in 2019 (World Lacrosse, n.d.-c). The first men's World Championship was held in 1967, won by Canada, and the first women's World Championship in 1982 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Iconic players include Jim Brown (b. 1936, Syracuse All-American who went on to NFL fame with the Cleveland Browns), and Gary Gait (b. 1967), the four-time NCAA player of the year from Syracuse who starred for Canada and in the professional leagues and is widely regarded as the greatest offensive player of his era (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). On the women's side, the United States has dominated the world championships since the 1980s under coaches such as Sue Enquist, and the game returned to the Olympics at LA 2028 in sixes format (World Lacrosse, n.d.-b).
