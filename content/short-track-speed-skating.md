## Basic Information

Short track speed skating is the pack-racing discipline of ice speed skating, in which up to eight skaters race counterclockwise around a compact 111.12-metre oval marked with blocks inside a standard 60 by 30-metre ice rink; it is governed worldwide by the International Skating Union (ISU), which stages the ISU World Short Track Speed Skating Championships and the Short Track World Tour (ISU, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The sport became a full Olympic medal discipline at Albertville 1992 after a demonstration at Calgary 1988, and at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics at the Mediolanum Forum the Dutchwoman Xandra Velzeboer won the women's 500 m — setting a world record of 41.399 in the semifinal — and the 1000 m, while South Korea's Kim Gil-li won the women's 1500 m, Korea's first individual Olympic short track gold, ahead of Choi Min-jeong (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Among today's leading skaters, the women's elite is headed by Xandra Velzeboer of the Netherlands — 500 m and 1000 m Olympic champion and 500 m world record holder at Milano Cortina 2026 — together with South Korea's Kim Gil-li (1500 m gold) and Choi Min-jeong, whose seven Winter Olympic medals make her South Korea's most decorated winter Olympian, and the United States' Kristen Santos-Griswold, bronze over 1500 m in 2026 as the first American woman on an Olympic short track podium since 2010; on the men's side the Netherlands' Jens van 't Wout took three golds at Milano Cortina 2026 (1000 m, 1500 m and the relay) plus 500 m bronze, equalling Viktor Ahn's record, and Canada's Steven Dubois won the men's 500 m, Canada's first individual Olympic short track gold (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

The competition oval measures 111.12 m and is laid out on a 60 × 30 m rink; the Olympic individual distances are 500 m, 1000 m and 1500 m for both women and men, plus the 3000 m women's relay, the 5000 m men's relay and — introduced for Beijing 2022 — the 2000 m mixed team relay (ISU, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Races run as heats, quarter-finals, semi-finals and A/B finals decided by finishing order rather than the clock; skaters advance on position, and a skater who is impeded may be advanced to the next round by the referee, while fouls such as impeding, blocking, shortening the course or dangerous relay exchanges can relegate the offender to the back of the next round or to the B final (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Mandatory equipment includes a hard-shell helmet, a cut-resistant suit, gloves, knee pads and a neck guard, and the blades are mounted slightly left of centre — the klapskate principle — for cornering at speed with the left hand often brushing the ice (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In relays, teams of four (five in the mixed relay) may change skaters at any point with a push from behind. Men and women race identical distances on the same track; the only gender difference is the separate men's and women's events, with no separate classifications (ISU, n.d.).

## History

Mass-start pack racing on small indoor rinks originated in North America in the early 20th century — crowds packed venues such as Madison Square Garden, and a rough mass-start format was even used at the 1932 Lake Placid Olympics, to the distress of European officials; the ISU formally adopted short track as a distinct discipline in 1967, staged unofficial world championships from 1976 and the first official ISU World Short Track Speed Skating Championships in 1981, and the sport joined the Olympic programme at Albertville 1992 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Olympics, n.d.). South Korea is the most successful nation in Olympic history with 53 medals, including 26 golds. Among the retired icons, Viktor Ahn — born Ahn Hyun-soo in 1985, who switched nationality to Russia in 2011 — won three golds at Turin 2006 and three at Sochi 2014, becoming the first skater ever to win all four individual Olympic titles at a single Games; Apolo Anton Ohno (born 1982) collected eight Olympic medals, including gold in 2002 and 2006, between 2002 and 2010; China's Wang Meng (born 1985) won four golds between 2006 and 2010; and Australia's Steven Bradbury (born 1973) won the 1000 m at Salt Lake City 2002 after the leading pack crashed on the last corner and again at Vancouver 2010 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
