## Basic Information

Short track speed skating is the pack-racing branch of ice speed skating, in which four to six skaters race counterclockwise around a compact 111.12-metre oval laid out inside an ice-hockey-sized rink. Unlike long track speed skating, where athletes race in pairs against the clock, short track is a tactical, contact-avoiding contest of overtaking, positioning and nerve, governed internationally by the International Skating Union (ISU). It is one of the Winter Olympic Games' most dramatic sports, dominated historically by South Korea, China, Canada and Hungary.

## Rules

The track is a 111.12 m oval marked on a 60 by 30 metre rink, with individual Olympic distances of 500 m, 1000 m and 1500 m for both men and women, plus a relay (3000 m women, 5000 m men) and, since Beijing 2022, a mixed team relay. Races begin with a mass start of typically four to eight skaters in heats, with the top two finishers of each heat advancing through quarter-finals and semi-finals to the final. Because the tight corners are taken at speed with the left hand often brushing the ice, passing must be clean \u2014 impeding, charging or blocking an opponent draws a penalty, and an impeded skater may be advanced to the next round; a skater also falls if off-track on the short interior. Skaters wear hard-shell helmets, cut-resistant suits, gloves, knee pads and neck guards, and use long-grounded klapskate-style blades fixed left-of-centre for cornering. Relay teams may change skater at any moment with a push from behind, and races are decided on finishing order rather than the clock.

## History

Mass-start racing on small indoor rinks originated in North America in the early 1900s, when crowds packed venues such as Madison Square Garden for pack racing's thrills and spills; a rough-and-tumble mass-start format was even used at the 1932 Lake Placid Olympics to the distress of European officials. The ISU formally adopted short track as a distinct branch in 1967, began arranging international competitions from 1976, and staged annual world championships from 1978\u20131980, with the first official ISU World Short Track Speed Skating Championships in 1981; world team championships followed in 1990\u20131991. After serving as a demonstration sport at Calgary 1988, short track became a full Olympic discipline at Albertville 1992 with individual and relay events, expanded to eight events by Turin 2006, and added the mixed team relay at Beijing 2022. South Korea is the sport's all-time Olympic power, with China, Canada, Italy and Hungary also collecting multiple titles, and the ISU recently reframed its circuit as the Short Track World Tour.
