## Basic Information

Gel blasting is a recreational target and skirmish sport in which replica blasters fire 6–8 mm water-absorbing gel balls (biodegradable cornstarch-based spheres) at low muzzle energy; it is played on dedicated fields and in arenas, often as a lower-impact alternative to paintball and airsoft (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the tactical simulation family with airsoft and laser tag.

Blasters (spring, electric AEG or gas) shoot gel balls at 200–400 fps depending on the model and local rules; fields enforce eye protection, chronograph limits and minimum engagement distances (Wikipedia, n.d.). Game modes mirror airsoft: team deathmatch, capture the flag, hostage rescue and target practice (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Gel balls are made of superabsorbent polymer and burst on impact, leaving no paint residue and dissolving over time — the main reason the sport is popular in Asia, Australia and increasingly in Europe, where it is legal without firearms licensing in most countries (Wikipedia, n.d.). Safety rules still require full-seal goggles on the field and blasters carried in bags off the field (Wikipedia, n.d.). There is no single world federation; national communities and arena leagues organize tournaments. Men and women play together.

## History

Gel blasting grew out of the toy "gel ball blaster" market in China in the 2010s and became a organized sport in Australia and Southeast Asia around 2018–2020, with commercial arenas opening in major cities (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitive gel blasting now includes speedsoft-style leagues and IPSC-style practical courses (Wikipedia, n.d.).
