## Basic Information

An ultramarathon is any foot race longer than the marathon — commonly 50 km, 50 miles, 100 km, 100 miles or 24-hour timed races — run on roads, trails or loops, with distances and records governed by the International Association of Ultrarunners (IAU) and recognized by World Athletics as world bests (World Athletics, n.d.-b). At the end of the 2026 season the women's scene was dominated by Courtney Dauwalter of the USA (pictured), winner of the Western States 100, Hardrock 100 and UTMB, while the men's field was led by Jim Walmsley, Kilian Jornet and the 2026 leaders of the UTMB World Series (World Athletics, n.d.-b; UTMB World Series, n.d.). Major championships include the IAU 24-hour and 100 km World Championships and the World Mountain and Trail Running Championships long-trail race (International Association of Ultrarunners, n.d.; World Athletics, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Ultra races are point-to-point or loop courses with aid stations every 5-20 km, self-sufficiency allowed, and chip or GPS timing; cut-off times apply, and pacers and crew are permitted in most races under the organizer's rules (World Athletics, n.d.-a). World Athletics recognizes world bests over 50 km, 100 km and 24 hours on the road, while trail ultras are ranked through ITRA points; the IAU runs the 24-hour and 100 km world championships (World Athletics, n.d.-b; International Trail Running Association, n.d.). Men and women compete in separate categories with equal prize structures at the biggest races, and age-group and relay formats exist at mass events (UTMB World Series, n.d.-a).

## History

The modern ultramarathon movement grew from the 1970s-80s road ultras in the USA and UK — the Sri Chinmoy 24-hour races and the London-to-Brighton 52-miler (1981) — and from the Comrades Marathon in South Africa (90 km, since 1921) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Kilian Jornet of Spain (pictured) revolutionized mountain ultrarunning with victories at Hardrock 100, UTMB and a double ascent of Everest; Courtney Dauwalter won the 2023 Western States 100, Hardrock 100 and UTMB in a historic triple year (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The classic road ultras include the 100 km World Championships, the Marathon des Sables in the Sahara and the Spartan Race series (International Association of Ultrarunners, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).
