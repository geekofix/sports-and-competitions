## Basic Information

Para swimming is competitive swimming for athletes with physical, visual, intellectual or hearing impairments, governed by World Para Swimming within the IPC and raced under World Aquatics rules per class (World Para Swimming, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). Classes run S1–S10 for physical impairments (1 = most severe), S11–S13 for visual impairment (S11 swimmers wear blackened goggles and use tappers at the walls), S14 for intellectual impairment and S15 for hearing impairment, with SB for breaststroke and SM for individual medley; freestyle, backstroke and butterfly use the S prefix (World Para Swimming, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Swimming has been on every Paralympic programme since Rome 1960; Paris 2024 at the Paris La Défense Arena staged 141 events (71 men's, 64 women's, 6 mixed open relays), and the World Para Swimming Championships are biennial — Manchester 2023 (12th) and Singapore 2025 (13th, OCBC Aquatic Centre, 21–27 September 2025, the first edition in Asia, with 23 world records set), with the World Series running since 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a; 2025, September 27; World Para Swimming, n.d.-a).

Leading the women today: Yip Pin Xiu of Singapore (S2) is a seven-time Paralympic champion who three-peated the 50 m and 100 m backstroke S2 at Rio 2016, Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 and holds two world records, and won gold and silver at Singapore 2025; Emeline Pierre of France (pictured scene) won the 100 m freestyle at Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; 2025, September 27; Olympics, n.d.). Among the men, Italy's Francesco Bocciardo and Australia's Callum Simpson and Rowan Crothers were 2025 world champions (2025, September 27; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

World Aquatics competition rules apply per class: dives starts from the blocks for freestyle, backstroke (started in the water), butterfly and medley, and breaststroke; S11–S13 swimmers use tapper sticks signalled by an official at turns and finishes; relays combine classes within a points limit (e.g. a 34-point freestyle relay may be two S8 and two S9 swimmers) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Para Swimming, n.d.-c).

Distances range from 50 m to 1,500 m depending on class; men's and women's events are separate, plus mixed open relays; world and Paralympic records are kept per class, and classification (medical and technical) determines eligibility (World Para Swimming, n.d.-c; World Para Swimming, n.d.-b).

## History

Swimming for athletes with disabilities has been part of the Paralympics since Rome 1960; among the icons are Trischa Zorn of the United States, pictured, who won 55 Paralympic medals (41 gold) between 1980 and 2004 and is the most decorated US Paralympian, Britain's Ellie Simmonds (four golds at London 2012 at age 15), Brazil's Daniel Dias (24 medals, the most decorated male), and Australians Ellie Cole and Americans Jessica Long and Brad Snyder (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
