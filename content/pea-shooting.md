## Basic Information

Pea shooting is a traditional English village contest in which competitors blow dried peas through a blowpipe (a peashooter) at a target, a pastime now organized as a competitive sport. Its flagship event is the World Pea Shooting Championship, held annually since 1971 on the second Saturday of July in the village of Witcham near Ely in Cambridgeshire, as part of the village fete (Witcham Village Hall, n.d.). Anyone may enter on the day — men, women and children in separate categories — and the championship has drawn competitors from the United States, Canada, Scandinavia, France, Spain, New Zealand and the Netherlands (Atlas Obscura, n.d.). The sport belongs to the wider family of blowpipe and target-shooting disciplines that includes [shooting](shooting.html) and [archery](archery.html) on this site, but with the lung-powered peashooter as the only permitted weapon.

## Rules

The rules of the World Pea Shooting Championship are set by the Witcham Events Committee and published on the organizer's site (Witcham Village Hall, n.d.). Peas are shot at a 12-inch target smeared with glazing putty. The peashooter may not exceed 12 inches in length and may not be shared between competitors; only the peas provided on the day may be used, five per contestant in the early rounds and ten in the semi-finals and finals. Peas must be propelled only by blowing air by mouth — no mechanical aids are allowed. The target distance is 12 feet for adults, 10 feet for children aged 8 and over and 8 feet for those aged 7 and under, so the event is genuinely open across age groups and both genders. Target scores are five points for the center ring, three for the middle ring and one for the outer ring. Ties are decided by sudden-death "pea-off" shots until there is a clear winner. Laser sights are permitted in the open category but not in the children's event (Witcham Village Hall, n.d.). Fouls such as using an oversized pipe or a borrowed peashooter lead to disqualification.


## History

The championship was created in 1971 by John Tyson, the village schoolmaster of Witcham, who is said to have caught pupils shooting peas at each other with peashooters and decided to put their skills to good use by holding a contest alongside the village fete to raise funds for the village hall (Atlas Obscura, n.d.). The event has been the central attraction of the fete ever since, and after Tyson's death in 2003 the Parish Council funded the John Tyson Shield, on which each champion's name is recorded. Notable champions include Rob Bresler of Haddenham, who won the men's title three years in a row — the BBC covered his 2014 victory — and three-times champion Ian Ashmeade, whose image was mounted on the sign of the local pub in 2018 (British Broadcasting Corporation, 2014). The 2020, 2021 and 2022 editions were cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and the championship returned on 8 July 2023 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The underlying pastime of pea shooting in England dates back centuries, when peashooters were common toys (Wikipedia, n.d.).
