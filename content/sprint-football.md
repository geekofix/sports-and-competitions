## Basic Information

Sprint football is a lightweight variant of American football played at US colleges: 11-on-11 under standard NCAA football rules, except that every player must weigh less than 183 pounds (178 in alumni games) and have at least 5 percent body fat, making speed and technique decisive (Collegiate Sprint Football League, n.d.). The Collegiate Sprint Football League (CSFL), originally founded in 1934 as the Eastern 150-Pound Football League and later the Eastern Lightweight Football League, governs nine active member teams for 2026 — including Penn, Cornell, Army and Navy — with three more (NYIT, Bellarmine and Quincy) joining in 2027, and its championship game has been played annually since 2017; the 2025 championship saw the Navy Midshipmen defeat the Army Black Knights in overtime (Wikipedia, n.d.). The related Midwest Sprint Football League, founded in 2022, has wound down with no teams left, while flag football — a 5-on-5 non-contact variant on a 50-yard field — will make its Olympic debut at Los Angeles 2028 with men's and women's events (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Sprint football is played on a standard 100-yard football field with 11 players per side, goalposts, four 12-minute quarters and NCAA football rules for downs, scoring and fouls, with kickoffs and punts retained (Collegiate Sprint Football League, n.d.). The defining rule is the weight-and-composition cap: every rostered player must stay under 183 lb and at 5 percent body fat or more, checked at weigh-ins, and players over the limit are ineligible. A seven-game regular season leads to the CSFL championship game, and teams play with full contact but at high pace because of the size limit (Wikipedia, n.d.). The league fields men's teams; women play the related flag-football variant, which at LA 2028 will be 5v5 on a 50-yard field with both a women's and a men's tournament under LA28 and NFL flag rules (Wikipedia, n.d.). Flag football was also played at the 2022 and 2025 World Games (Collegiate Sprint Football League, n.d.).


## History

Organized lightweight football began in 1934 with the Eastern 150-Pound Football League in the northeastern United States, renamed the Eastern Lightweight Football League and later the Collegiate Sprint Football League, which formalized the 183-pound limit and the seven-game season (Wikipedia, n.d.). Army and Navy have dominated the modern era, and the CSFL championship game has been staged since 2017, with Navy beating Army in overtime in the 2025 final; the league expanded to nine teams for 2026 and plans thirteen by 2027 with the additions of NYIT, Bellarmine and Quincy (Collegiate Sprint Football League, n.d.). The Midwest Sprint Football League was founded in 2022 but has since lost all its teams (Wikipedia, n.d.).
