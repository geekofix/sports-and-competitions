## Basic Information

Women's Australian rules football is the female-only elite form of Australian football, played on the same oval grounds with the same core actions — kicking and handballing the ball toward goals between four tall posts, scoring six points for a goal and one for a behind — under the governance of the Australian Football League (AFL), whose AFL Women's (AFLW) competition is the national professional league (AFL, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The inaugural AFLW season ran from February to March 2017 with eight clubs — the first match was Collingwood v Carlton at Ikon Park on 3 February 2017 — and Adelaide won the first premiership, defeating Brisbane 35–29 in the grand final with Erin Phillips best on ground (National Museum of Australia, n.d.). As the elite women's code it belongs to the Australian rules family — [Australian Rules Football](australian-rules-football.html), [AFL 9s](afl-9s.html), [AFLX](aflx.html) — within the wider football codes listed under Important Links.

The league expanded to 10 clubs in 2019, 14 in 2020 and all 18 AFL clubs in 2022, with the 2023 season the first contested by every club; the record grand final crowd of 53,034 was set at the 2019 decider at Adelaide Oval (Wikipedia, n.d.). At the top of the women's game today, North Melbourne won back-to-back premierships in 2024 — an unbeaten season — and again in 2025 with a perfect season, the club's 27th overall AFL/AFLW premiership, while stars such as Ashleigh Woodland of Adelaide, shown below attempting a spectacular mark, lead the league's highlight reels (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

The playing laws follow the men's game with defined modifications: AFLW teams field 16 players at a time instead of 18, quarters last 17 minutes (15 before 2023) with time-on only in the final two minutes, rotations are capped at 60 per team (unlimited before 2023), the ball is slightly smaller than the men's, throw-ins are taken from inside the boundary line, and a "last touch" rule applies out of bounds (Wikipedia, n.d.). Tackling follows the AFL laws — contact between the knees and shoulders, holding-the-ball provisions — and scoring is identical: six points for a goal between the two middle posts, one point for a behind through the outer posts (AFL, n.d.). League-specific provisions cover list sizes, drafts and salary structures rather than the laws of play, which the AFL keeps continuous between the men's and women's games (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Women have played Australian rules football since the earliest years of the code — matches were recorded in Western Australia as early as 1915 — and state leagues formed through the late twentieth century, beginning with the Victorian Women's Football League in 1981, followed by the West Australian Women's Football League in 1988 and the South Australian Women's Football League in 1991; the AFL Women's National Championships were inaugurated in 1992 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The AFL assumed control of the women's game in 2010 with the aim of professionalising it, exhibition matches followed in 2013, and the national AFLW competition launched in 2017 (National Museum of Australia, n.d.). The league has since expanded to all 18 clubs, seasons have lengthened, prize money was raised to equal the men's competition from 2023, and the player cohort is now fully professional; among its icons is Erin Phillips — three-time premiership player, two-time league best and fairest and two-time grand final best on ground — who retired in 2023 after 66 matches for Adelaide and Port Adelaide (Wikipedia, n.d.).
