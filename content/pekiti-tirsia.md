## Basic Information

Pekiti-Tirsia (Pekiti-Tirsia Kali) is a strictly combat-oriented Filipino fighting system of the Tortal family of Negros, said to date back to 1897 and developed further by Conrado Tortal (1930–36); it covers all ranges — solo and double stick, spear, sword-and-dagger, knife-to-knife, mano y mano and dumog grappling — with counter-offensive principles and the 12 Doce Methodos. Leo Gaje (1938–2026), sole heir, introduced it to the US in 1972 and heads the global organization; it is a standard combatives program for elite military and law-enforcement units (e.g. Philippine Marine Corps). Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Larnis](larnis.html), [Dumog](dumog.html), [Arnis de Mano](arnis-de-mano.html), [Eskrima](eskrima.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Pekiti-Tirsia is a combat system, not a ring sport; its competitive expression is through FMA point sparring under WEKAF/PEKAF rules and through military combatives testing. The system's own training rules: 64 Attacks condensed curriculum, seguindas/contradas/recontras/recontradas counters, Tri-V formula; knife and live-blade work only with protective gear and full control. Tournaments use padded sticks, three two-minute rounds, judges' points (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

The Tortal family system dates to 1897 (centennial celebrated at Subic Bay, 25 October 1997 — the only FMA officially recognized with a centennial). Conrado Tortal refined it 1930–36; his grandson Leo Gaje took it to the United States in 1972 and founded international organizations (Pekiti-Tirsia International 1995, PTKGO 2003). Gaje trained Dan Inosanto, the Dog Brothers and numerous special-operations units; he died 25 September 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
