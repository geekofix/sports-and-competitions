## Basic Information

World Chase Tag (WCT) is a competitive team format of tag played on an obstacle course known as the quadrangle — a square arena of walls, ramps and obstacles — in which teams take turns chasing and evading in a series of one-on-one timed duels. Combining the schoolyard game of tag with parkour movement, matches ask evaders to survive and chasers to catch.

Created in Britain in the 2010s, the sport presents itself as the world's first competitive tag league, with international championships, national teams and a strong following through online video coverage.

## Rules

Matches consist of a series of one-on-one chase segments. In each segment one team fields a chaser and the other an evader: the chaser has 20 seconds to tag the evader inside the quadrangle. A tag scores a point for the chasing team, while surviving the full 20 seconds scores a point for the evader's team, and pairs alternate through the roster until every athlete has chased and evaded a set number of times. Evaders may not leave the quadrangle, hold the chaser or hide motionless behind obstacles, and chasers may not use excessive contact or dangerous play; officials penalise fouls, with cumulative points deciding the match.

## History

The sport was devised by the brothers Christian and Damien Devaux, who staged the first World Chase Tag event in London in the 2010s, building the format from their childhood games and the parkour community. The competitive scene expanded across Europe and the United States, and video coverage of the evasive parkour skill on display brought global visibility in the late 2010s, with sell-out championships and youth and women's categories. It now features national teams and clubs competing in international championship series under the WCT brand.
