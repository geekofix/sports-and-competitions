## Basic Information

CrossFit is a fitness and competition program built on constantly varied, functional movements performed at high intensity — Olympic-weightlifting, gymnastics and monostructural cardio such as running, rowing and cycling — with a published "workout of the day" (WOD) as its core (CrossFit, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). It was founded in 2000 in Santa Cruz, California, by Greg Glassman (b. 1956) and Lauren Jenai, and grew from a single gym into a global network of more than 10,000 affiliated gyms ("boxes") in over 150 countries (CrossFit, n.d.). The competitive pinnacle is the CrossFit Games, first held in 2007 on a ranch in Aromas, California, which crown the "Fittest on Earth"; the 2026 Games were held in Fort Worth, Texas, from 6 to 9 August 2026, after the 2025 edition at the Fort Worth Convention Center, and the CrossFit Open — the mass-participation first stage open to everyone — precedes the Games each year (CrossFit Games, n.d.). The program is split into divisions: men's and women's individual (RX, "as prescribed"), scaled (adjusted weights and movements), Foundations, age-group divisions for teens (14–15, 16–17) and masters (35–39 up to the 80+ bands), and an adaptive division for athletes with physical impairments; team competition fields teams of four. The movements and scoring are essentially identical across genders — the same WODs, the same standards — with only load and rep-scheme adjustments in scaled divisions (CrossFit Games, n.d.). At the end of 2026, the leading current names are the reigning Games champions from Fort Worth alongside the long-dominant Laura Horvath of the USA (b. 1986), who won five consecutive women's titles from 2021 to 2025, and on the men's side the likes of five-time champion Jeffrey Adler of the USA, who won in 2021 and 2022 (CrossFit Games, n.d.). ## Rules

Competitive CrossFit is governed by the CrossFit Games Rulebook, published by CrossFit, Inc.; athletes are scored on workouts that combine repetitions, loads, distances and time caps, with points awarded for completed rounds/repetitions and, in timed workouts, a tiebreak at a specified checkpoint (CrossFit Games, n.d.). A workout may be scored "for time" (fastest wins), "for max reps/load" (most repetitions or heaviest lift wins), or "AMRAP" (as many rounds as possible within a time cap); if an athlete cannot complete the prescribed standard, they are relegated to the scaled division or score the as-prescribed result only if the movement standard is met — every repetition must pass a video-review standard (CrossFit Games, n.d.). The Games run over several days with a series of WODs — typically a dozen or more — and the athlete with the most points across all events wins the division; ties are broken by the athlete who placed higher in more events (CrossFit Games, n.d.). The movement library includes the nine foundational movements — air squat, front squat, overhead squat, overhead press, push press, push jerk, deadlift, sumo deadlift high pull, medicine-ball clean — plus Olympic lifts (snatch, clean and jerk), gymnastics (pull-ups, muscle-ups, handstand push-ups) and cardio machines (rower, ski erg, assault bike, running, double-unders) (CrossFit, n.d.). Divisions and classifications: individual men/women (RX and scaled), teams of four (mixed-gender team division), teens 14–15 and 16–17, masters in five-year bands from 35–39 to 80+, and adaptive athletes; there are no gender-specific rules — men and women in the same division lift and move the same standards at the elite level, and age groups scale loads and movements (CrossFit Games, n.d.). Anti-doping follows the CrossFit Games testing program aligned with the World Anti-Doping Code (CrossFit Games, n.d.).

## History

CrossFit was created in 2000 by Greg Glassman, a former gymnast who had been training police officers in Santa Cruz, and his then-wife Lauren Jenai; the first affiliate gyms opened in the early 2000s and the methodology — functional movements, constantly varied, at high intensity — spread through the CrossFit.com WOD page (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first CrossFit Games in 2007 at the Aromas ranch had about 70 athletes and no prize money; by 2010 the event moved to a stadium setting in Carson, California, and Reebok became title sponsor in 2011, turning the Games into a televised spectacle with a $500,000 top prize by 2017 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The competition's most iconic champions are Rich Froning Jr. (b. 1986), who won the first four men's titles from 2011 to 2014 and became the face of the sport, and his successor Jason Khalipa (b. 1985), four-time champion 2015–2018; on the women's side, Annie Thorisdottir (b. 1989) of Iceland won in 2011 and 2012 and remains one of the most recognizable athletes in the sport, followed by Sam Briggs (b. 1990) of Australia, five-time champion 2012–2016, and Tia-Clair Toomey (b. 1993) of Australia, five-time champion 2017–2021, before Laura Horvath (b. 1986) won 2021–2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). In 2022 the Games moved to Madison, Wisconsin, and since 2024 they have been held in Fort Worth, Texas; the 2026 edition ran there from 6 to 9 August (CrossFit Games, n.d.). The brand also grew beyond the Games: the CrossFit Open became the world's largest participatory sporting event, and CrossFit, Inc. continues to run the affiliate network (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
