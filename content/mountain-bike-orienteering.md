## Basic Information

Mountain bike orienteering (MTBO) is [orienteering](orienteering.html) on a mountain bike: riders navigate from control to control across varied terrain using a map and compass, punching each control electronically, and the fastest time wins (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-a). It is governed by the International Orienteering Federation (IOF), and the World MTB Orienteering Championships (WMTBOC) were first held in 2002 and have run annually since 2004 (except 2020) (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-a). The disciplines are sprint, middle distance, long distance, mass start (official since 2017) and relay in men's, women's and mixed teams (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-b). At the 2026 WMTBOC and CX80 World Cup, held 26–30 August 2026, Finland took both sprint titles, Sweden's Gabriella Gustafsson and the Czech Republic's Samuel Pokala repeated as long-distance winners, and Denmark's Nikoline Splittorff finished fifth in the overall World Cup; the 2026 World Masters Championships followed in Moray/Roseisle and Sanquhar, Scotland, on 9–12 September 2026 (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-b). At the end of the 2026 season the leading women in the sport are Nikoline Splittorff of Denmark and Gabriella Gustafsson of Sweden (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-b).

## Rules

In the individual disciplines (sprint, middle, long distance) riders start at intervals and must visit the controls in the given order; the rider with the lowest total time wins, and riding outside allowed paths or receiving outside assistance brings disqualification (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-a). In the mass start all competitors start together and the first across the finish line wins; in the relay each team's riders run or ride their legs in sequence and the combined time decides (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-b). Maps follow the ISMTBOM specification, usually at 1:10,000 or 1:15,000 scale (1:7,500 for masters aged 70 and over), with the sprint run on a smaller-scale, more detailed map; riders must carry map and compass and may use no GPS or other electronic navigation aids. Women and men compete in separate individual classes (W21/W35/W40 … and M21 …) and together in the mixed relay class (International Orienteering Federation, n.d.-b).

## History

MTBO emerged in the Nordic countries in the 1990s, when orienteers began using mountain bikes on forest roads and trails; the IOF held the first World MTB Orienteering Championships in 2002 and made them annual from 2004 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The all-time leading woman in the sport is Emily Benham, with ten individual world titles — the most of any MTBO athlete, male or female — while Switzerland's Christine Schaffner holds the record of four long-distance world titles (Wikipedia, n.d.).
