## Basic Information

Air racing is the motorsport of the skies: aircraft race around pylons or along circuits at low altitude and high speed, with classes from small single-engine sport planes to purpose-built racers exceeding 400 km/h. Races are decided over laps of a marked course, passing within metres of the ground in front of spectators.

The discipline is governed internationally by the FAI (World Air Sports Federation), which sanctions classes and record categories, while famous standalone meetings such as the Reno Air Races in Nevada and the Red Bull Air Race series (2003-2019) brought the sport to television audiences worldwide.

## Rules

Race formats divide into pylon racing — laps around inflatable air gates, with time penalties for cutting pylons or incorrect passes — and cross-country or circuit time trials against the clock. Classes specify aircraft type and engine limits so that races stay close: from Formula One class racers (small engines, high agility) to unlimited-class warbirds at Reno. Pilots must hold racing licences, fly the prescribed course profile, and start and finish under FAI and event rules; collisions with gates or dangerous flying bring disqualification.

## History

Air racing predates circuit motorsport: the Gordon Bennett races from 1909 and the interwar air tours of Europe and America made aviators national heroes. Pylon racing took root after World War Two with surplus aircraft, and the Reno National Championship Air Races began in 1964, growing into the world's fastest motorsport week. The Red Bull Air Race series from 2003 created a standardized pylon-slalom world championship with masterclass pilots touring globally until its final 2019 season. FAI classes and national championships keep classic and sport racing alive today.
