## Basic Information

The clean and jerk is one of the two lifts contested in Olympic [weightlifting](weightlifting.html), alongside the snatch: the barbell is lifted from the ground to the shoulders in one continuous movement (the clean) and then driven overhead with a split or squat jerk, all in a single attempt. Together with the snatch it forms the two-lift total that decides Olympic and world titles. The lift is governed by the International Weightlifting Federation (IWF), and weightlifting has been on the Olympic program for men since Athens 1896 and for women since Sydney 2000.

## Rules

In the clean and jerk the lifter must first pull the barbell from the platform to the shoulders in one motion, without pausing, catching it in a front rack position with the elbows forward; any downward movement of the bar between the pull and the catch, or a touch of the bar on the chest or thighs during the pull, invalidates the lift. The lifter then recovers to a standing position and, in the jerk, drives the bar from the shoulders to full arm extension overhead, either splitting one leg forward or squatting under the bar, and must hold it motionless with arms and legs extended until the referees' down signal. Each lifter gets three attempts at the clean and jerk in competition, performed after the snatch attempts, and the heaviest successful lift counts toward the total; ties are broken in favor of the lighter bodyweight lifter.

## History

The clean and jerk has been part of weightlifting competitions since the late 19th century and appeared at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens 1896, when the two-hand lift was contested in a similar style. The press, clean and jerk and snatch were the three Olympic lifts until the press was abolished after the 1972 Munich Games, leaving the two-lift format from the 1973 World Championships and the 1976 Montreal Olympics onward. The women's two-lift program was introduced at the Sydney 2000 Olympics. Legends of the lift include the American Norbert Schemansky, the Soviet superheavyweight Vasily Alekseyev, and China's Lü Xiaojun, who set world records in the 81 kg class. At the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics weightlifting will be contested in a new set of twelve bodyweight categories, equally split between men and women.
