## Basic Information

Ampe is a Ghanaian clapping and jumping game played by two people or two groups: players take turns making a move (a clap pattern plus a leg kick) and must mirror the opponent's move; a player who fails to mirror loses (Wikipedia, n.d.). It is a staple of Ghanaian schoolyards and playgrounds, played by girls and boys alike. Within the folk and festival games on this site, its closest relatives are [Asafo Atwele](asafo-atwele.html), [Gidigbo](gidigbo.html), [Abula](abula.html), [Langa](langa.html), [Rock Paper Scissors](rock-paper-scissors.html), [Hopscotch](dodgeball.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Players alternate turns; each turn consists of clapping a rhythm and kicking one leg forward in a chosen style. The opponent must repeat the exact move; failure to do so loses the round. Games are best-of series; no equipment is needed. Both children and adults play (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Ampe is one of the most widespread West African children's games, known by different names across the region; it develops rhythm, memory and coordination (Wikipedia, n.d.).
