## Basic Information

Wrestling is the Olympic combat sport of grappling, in which two competitors seek to pin each other's shoulders to the mat or win on points for takedowns, reversals and controlling holds, without striking — contested internationally in two Olympic styles, Greco-Roman and freestyle. Greco-Roman forbids holds below the waist, while freestyle allows attacks on the legs, and both are wrestling's showcase disciplines governed by United World Wrestling.

Wrestling is one of the oldest sports in human history: forms of wrestling appeared at the ancient Olympic Games, and the modern Olympic programme has included it since 1896, today with men's and women's freestyle events alongside Greco-Roman.

## Rules

Matches run two three-minute periods, scored on takedowns, exposures and escapes with points values set by the rules, and won by technical superiority (an 8-point margin in Greco-Roman or 10 in freestyle), a fall (pinning both shoulder blades), or, failing those, the highest point total; criteria decide ties. Illegal holds, fleeing the mat and passivity violations bring warnings and penalty points. Bouts are contested on a circular mat within weight categories, one hour weigh-ins set exact limits for each class, and women's freestyle uses the same takedown-based framework with adapted rules on headgear and holds.

## History

Wrestling appears in the earliest records of sport, from ancient Egyptian wall paintings to Greek palaestra wrestling at the ancient Olympics from 708 BC, and folk wrestling styles flourished in every culture from the Caucasus to West Africa. Wrestling was on the Athens 1896 Olympic programme and continuous since 1904, with Greco-Roman on the programme from the beginning and freestyle joining in 1904, and the world governing body United World Wrestling (founded in 1912 and renamed in 2014) running world championships since the early twentieth century. Women's wrestling debuted at the Athens 2004 Olympics.
