## Basic Information

Snow rugby sevens is the seven-a-side snow form of rugby union: fast, festival-style rugby sevens adapted to snow-covered pitches, with short halves, softer contact and winter clothing. It is the most common tournament format of snow rugby, staged at winter festivals and dedicated snow-rugby events from the Russian winter championships to Kashmir's Gulmarg tournaments and ski-resort sevens in Europe and the Americas.

## Rules

The format follows rugby sevens with winter adaptations: seven players a side, short halves (typically seven minutes, and often shortened further in extreme cold), scrums and restarts per sevens, and try-based scoring identical to the sevens game. Differences come from the surface and season — a standard ball but treacherously slippery, footing that demands adjusted running and evasive technique, tackles into soft snowbanks, and dress of thermals, gloves and grip footwear rather than studs — with contact rules commonly softened by organisers to keep the festival game safe (variations set by the hosting event, as in rugby sevens itself). Tournaments typically run cup-and-plate knockouts over a single festival day, with men's, women's and mixed brackets.

## History

Rugby has been played in snow since the game's earliest cold-climate decades — a 1939 Ranfurly Shield match in New Zealand was famously contested in snow, and a 1978 Russian club match went ahead at \u221223 \u00b0C — but the organised sevens-on-snow format is a modern festival development. The snow sevens idea spread through the 2000s and 2010s: snow rugby was a demonstration event at India's 2008 Winter Games and the first dedicated snow-rugby tournament was held at Gulmarg, Kashmir, in 2009; New Zealand hosted "20 Below Rugby in the Snow" seven-a-side at Mount Dobson during the 2011 Rugby World Cup; and European and Russian winter circuits adopted the format as their own cold-weather version of the ever-growing world sevens festival circuit. In Russia, snow sevens became a national-championship discipline of the rugby federation's winter programme, and in the Kashmir resorts it doubles as a tourism showcase; the format remains closest in spirit to the rugby festival movement — "more of a relaxed atmosphere than fifteen-a-side games", as sevens rugby is traditionally described — with the snow adding spectacle, comedy falls and a striking white arena to the world's most adaptable team sport.
