## Basic Information

Platform tennis (often called paddle tennis in the United States) is a winter racquet sport derived from tennis, played on a raised aluminum court surrounded by taut wire screens. It was devised in 1928 in Scarsdale, New York, by James Cogswell and Fessenden Blanchard as an outdoor game that could be played in cold weather, and the sport is led in the United States by the American Platform Tennis Association (APTA). It is one of the few outdoor winter racquet sports, thanks to heating elements under the platform decks that melt snow and ice.

## Rules

The court is one-quarter the size of a tennis court: the platform deck measures 60 by 30 feet, with a playing area of 44 by 20 feet divided by a net 34 inches high at the center. The court is surrounded by 12-foot-high taut chicken-wire screens; a ball that strikes the screen remains in play and may be played after one bounce, which is the sport's most distinctive tactical feature. Players use solid paddles up to 18 inches long, perforated with as many as 87 holes, and a spongy rubber ball 2.5 inches in diameter. Scoring is identical to tennis. Serving is overhand and the ball may clip the net and still be valid—there are no lets—with a single serve in doubles (a fault loses the point) and two serves in singles. The APTA sanctions national championships in men's and women's doubles, the sport's dominant form.

## History

Cogswell and Blanchard built the first court in the fall of 1928 on the steep, rocky slope of the Cogswell property on Old Army Road in Scarsdale, where the terrain dictated an elevated platform. After experimenting with deck tennis, badminton and volleyball, they adopted paddle tennis equipment, added fences to keep balls from rolling down the hill, and—crucially—decided that balls bouncing off the fences were in play. The group of friends who shaped the rules, calling themselves the Old Army Athletes, settled the final court dimensions by 1932, and the first national championship was held at the Fox Meadow Tennis Club in 1935. The American Platform Tennis Association, founded by Jack Ten Eyck in the 1930s, grew the sport across commuter suburbs of New York; today the APTA runs national championships and leagues, and the sport is also played in colder regions of Canada and Europe. The Platform Tennis Museum and Hall of Fame in Milton, Massachusetts preserves the sport's history.
