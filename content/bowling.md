## Basic Information

Bowling (ten-pin bowling) is a precision sport in which a player rolls a heavy ball down a polished lane to knock down ten pins arranged in a triangle at the far end. It is governed internationally by World Bowling (IOC-recognized), which organizes the World Bowling Championships; in the United States the United States Bowling Congress (USBC, bowl.com) sets the rules, and the Professional Bowlers Association (PBA) runs the top professional tour.

## Rules

Under standard ten-pin rules (USBC/World Bowling), a game consists of ten frames. In each of the first nine frames the bowler has up to two deliveries to knock down all ten pins; knocking down all ten with the first ball is a strike, with both balls a spare. In the tenth frame a bowler who strikes or spares gets a third delivery. Strikes and spares score 10 plus the pins knocked down with the next one (strike) or two (spare) balls. A perfect game is 300 (twelve strikes). Lane oil patterns and approach rules are standardized for competition.

## History

Bowling descends from ancient Egyptian and Roman pin games; the modern ten-pin game was codified in the United States in 1895 by the American Bowling Congress (now USBC). Professional bowling boomed in the 1950s-60s with television coverage; the PBA Tour was founded in 1958. World Bowling was founded in 1952 (as the International Bowling Federation) and runs world championships. Legends include Walter Ray Williams Jr. (USA), the most successful PBA bowler with 47 titles, and Earl Anthony.
