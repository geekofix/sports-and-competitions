## Basic Information

Scrabble is a crossword-style board game for two to four players in which letter tiles are placed on a 15 × 15 grid — 225 squares including double and triple letter and word premium squares — to build interlocking words that score the letters' values multiplied by the premium squares; a player who plays all seven tiles from the rack in one move earns a 50-point "bingo" bonus (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The game was invented in 1938 by the unemployed American architect Alfred Mosher Butts (1899–1994), who called it Lexiko and renamed it Criss-Cross Words in 1948; international English-language competition is governed by WESPA (World English-Language Scrabble Players' Association), formed at a players' meeting in 2003 and constituted in 2005, which mandates the Collins Scrabble Words (CSW) lexicon of more than 280,000 valid words and publishes the world ratings (WESPA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). The WESPA World Championship 2025 (WESPAC) in Accra, Ghana, 11–16 November 2025, drew 129 players over 32 rounds and was won by Canada's Adam Logan, who beat New Zealand's Nigel Richards 4–2 in the best-of-seven final — Logan's second world title exactly twenty years after his 2005 win — while Team Nigeria took the Best Country title (Wikipedia, n.d.-d) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

The championships are open to all genders and the WESPA ratings rank men and women in one list — after Accra 2025 the top of the ratings is headed by Adam Logan and Nigel Richards, with leading women players among the highest-rated internationals; the WESPA Youth Cup (World Youth Scrabble Championships, since 2006) develops the next generation of players under 18 (WESPA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

## Rules

Each player draws seven tiles from a cloth bag of 100 tiles (English distribution: 2 blanks, 9 A, 12 E, 8 O, 6 R and so on) and keeps them hidden on a rack; players alternate placing at least one tile per turn to form a word connected to the words already on the board, scoring the letter values (A 1, B 3, C 3, D 2, E 1, F 4, G 2, H 4, I 1, J 8, K 5, L 1, M 3, N 1, O 1, P 3, Q 10, R 1, S 1, T 1, U 1, V 4, W 4, X 8, Y 4, Z 10) multiplied by any double or triple letter or word squares the new tiles cover; a player may instead exchange tiles (losing the turn) or pass (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Tournament games are played against the clock, typically with about 15–25 minutes per player; the game ends when the bag is empty and one player uses their last tile — adding the value of the opponents' remaining racks to their own score — or when all players pass twice, and the highest total wins (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). WESPA's international rules (first version August 2009, version 2 in November 2010, version 3 in 2015) standardise play worldwide, including the challenge system and the CSW word list, and WESPA ratings — calculated since 1993 and re-based on the new RJR system from 2025 — seed the international tournaments (WESPA, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

## History

Alfred Butts designed the tile frequencies himself from letter counts in the New York Times and designed the premium-square layout; after manufacturers declined, the game enthusiast James Brunot bought the rights in 1948 and renamed the game Scrabble, and Selchow & Righter acquired it in 1952 and later sold the North American rights to Hasbro (Mattel holds the trademark elsewhere) (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first World Scrabble Championship was held in 1991, and the event has run regularly since, with WESPA organising the WESPAC editions from Perth 2015 through Las Vegas 2023 to Accra 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Early international icons include Allan Simmons of the United Kingdom, the first chair of WESPA (2003–2008), and Wellington Jighere of Nigeria, the first African world champion (2015, Perth) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).
