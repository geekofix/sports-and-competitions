## Basic Information

Scrabble is a word game in which two to four players score points by placing letter tiles on a square board to form words in a crossword-like grid, using the letters' values and premium board squares; for tournament players it is a serious competitive mind sport with international rules, ratings and world championships. Competitive English-language Scrabble is governed by the World English-Language Scrabble Players' Association (WESPA), with the North American Scrabble Players Association (NASPA) running the United States and Canada; Scrabble's trademarks belong to Hasbro in North America and Mattel elsewhere, and parallel world bodies govern French, Spanish and other language editions.

## Rules

Tournament play under WESPA rules uses a standard 15-by-15 board and a bag of 100 tiles; players draw seven tiles, take turns forming words that connect to the existing grid, and score the sum of tile values multiplied by any premium squares (double/triple letter and word scores) crossed by the word played, with the 50-point "bingo" bonus for using all seven tiles at once. Play is strictly regulated: each move must be a valid word in the official lexicon — Collins Scrabble Words (CSW, formerly SOWPODS) internationally, the NASPA Word List (NWL) in North America — challenges to invalid words carry penalties, and games use chess clocks (typically 25 minutes per player with a ten-point-per-minute overtime penalty), with wins by margin recorded for Swiss-paired events. Tournament formats range from best-of-five finals at the World Scrabble Championship to large open Swiss events, and a parallel "duplicate" Scrabble format — all players solving identical racks against the clock — is standard in French competitive play. Strategy rests on a lexicon learned by heart, rack balance, board management (keeping premium squares closed to the opponent) and the endgame skill of tracking unseen tiles.

## History

The game's ancestry is in the 1930s United States: architect Alfred Mosher Butts created a game mixing anagramming with a crossword grid — "Criss-Cross Words" — engineering its letter distribution from English letter-frequency studies, and in 1948 James Brunot refined and renamed it Scrabble, manufacturing it personally until Selchow & Righter took over and the game exploded into the best-selling board game of the early 1950s. Organised club and tournament play followed, with the National Scrabble Association formed in 1978 by Selchow & Righter, succeeded in 2009 by the player-run NASPA; the international community formed the World English-Language Scrabble Players' Association at the 2003 World Scrabble Championship in Kuala Lumpur, formally constituted in 2005 with 24 member organizations, issuing its unified rules from 2009 and maintaining international ratings. The World Scrabble Championship, first staged in London in 1991 by Philip Nelkon of Mattel, continues today as the WESPA Championship, its recent winners including New Zealand's multiple-time world champion Nigel Richards — widely considered the greatest player of all time — alongside champions from Nigeria, Kenya, Australia and India; the Collins Scrabble Words lexicon and its 280,000-plus words, first assembled as an international list in the 1990s and published by Collins since 2003, is now valid everywhere except North America, and youth championships have been staged annually since 2006.
