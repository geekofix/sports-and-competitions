## Basic Information

Soapbox racing is gravity-powered racing: unpowered, homemade carts — soapboxes — race downhill on a course of hills, judged on speed and often on design and showmanship (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the folk/festival novelty family with wok racing and the Red Bull Soapbox Race.

The Red Bull Soapbox Race is the most famous modern series: teams build unpowered, steering-and-brake-equipped carts and race down a city-hill course — the 2017 race at Alexandra Palace, London (photo above) is a classic (Red Bull, 2017). Judging is split between race time and audience appeal/design (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Rules: carts must be unpowered, have working brakes and steering, a maximum size/weight, a roll bar in many events, and the driver wears a helmet; scrutineering before the race (Red Bull, n.d.). Local derbies (e.g. in the UK, Germany’s Seifenkistenrennen — photo) run similar rules; many are charity or youth events. Men and women teams race together.

## History

Soapbox derbies date to the 1930s — the first "soapbox derby" was held in Dayton, Ohio, in 1933, inspired by the MGM film The Soap Box Derby (1932); gravity racing spread worldwide after WWII (Wikipedia, n.d.). Red Bull launched its Soapbox Race in 2000, and it now runs in dozens of countries with celebrity teams (Wikipedia, n.d.).
