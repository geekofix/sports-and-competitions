## Basic Information

Hydrospeed is riding river rapids prone on a small foam board (a monohydrospeed), holding on with the arms and steering with fins on the feet; it is a recognised whitewater discipline alongside rafting and kayaking, with competitions on slalom courses and downhill sprint courses (IRF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Within the paddling, rowing and sailing family on this site, its closest relatives are [Riverboarding](riverboarding.html), [Whitewater Rafting Race](whitewater-rafting-race.html), [Canoe Slalom](canoe-slalom.html), [Freestyle Kayak](freestyle-kayak.html), [Playboating](playboating.html), [Rafting](rafting.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links.

## Rules

Competitors descend a marked course wearing a helmet, life jacket, wetsuit and a foam board (typically 60–90 cm) with handgrips; fins are worn on the feet for steering and propulsion. Events are timed runs or head-to-head heats on rapids up to grade IV; slalom formats set gates on the course. Men's and women's categories are run separately (IRF, n.d.).

## History

Hydrospeed emerged in France in the 1970s–80s, when river runners began riding rapids on foam boards; the French federation organised the first competitions, and the discipline joined international whitewater programmes. Rémi Camus of France famously descended the Mekong by swimming in 2013 (Wikipedia, n.d.).
