## Basic Information

Wheelchair tennis is the adaptation of tennis for players with mobility impairments, played on standard tennis courts with standard rackets and balls and identical scoring — with one defining rule change: the ball may bounce twice before being returned, and only the first bounce need be inside the court. It has been a Paralympic sport since Barcelona 1992 and is fully integrated into the sport's biggest events, with wheelchair draws held at all four Grand Slam tournaments.

The sport is governed by the International Tennis Federation's wheelchair tennis committee, which runs the UNIQLO Wheelchair Tennis Tour with rankings for men, women and quad divisions.

## Rules

Singles and doubles follow the rules of tennis with the two-bounce rule replacing the single bounce; the first bounce must land within the court but the second may fall anywhere. Men and women compete in the open division, while the quad division covers players with impairments in all four limbs who may tape the racket to the hand for grip; one player in mixed quad doubles must be a woman. Matches are best of three sets with tie-breaks as in running tennis, with wheelchairs complying with ITF dimension rules and players prohibited from propelling the chair by any foot contact in divisions where players retain foot function.

## History

The sport was founded by Brad Parks and Jeff Minnenbraker in the United States in 1976, and grew rapidly through the 1980s with national federations and an international tour taking shape under the International Tennis Federation. Wheelchair tennis appeared at the Seoul 1988 Paralympic Games as a demonstration event and became a full medal sport at Barcelona 1992; integration into the Grand Slams came in the 2000s — Wimbledon added its wheelchair events in 2005 with singles from 2016, making it the last of the four majors to include singles and doubles for men, women and quads. Esther Vergeer headlines the Dutch success that has dominated the women's game, with the Netherlands and Japan among its leading nations.
