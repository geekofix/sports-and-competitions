## Basic Information

Wheelchair tennis is tennis adapted for players with mobility impairments: standard courts, rackets and balls, identical scoring, and one defining rule change — the ball may bounce twice before being returned, with only the first bounce required to land inside the court (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). It has been a Paralympic medal sport since Barcelona 1992, is fully integrated into the International Tennis Federation, and all four Grand Slam tournaments stage wheelchair draws — Australian Open since 2002, US Open and Wimbledon since 2005, French Open since 2006, so every major has offered wheelchair tennis since 2007 (Wikipedia, n.d.).

The ITF runs the UNIQLO Wheelchair Tennis Tour with official rankings in three divisions — men's, women's and quad — and the quad division, added at Athens 2004, covers players with impairments in all four limbs, who may strap the racket to the hand and use motorised chairs (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). At the Paris 2024 Paralympics at Roland-Garros, Diede de Groot of the Netherlands won the women's singles gold — she is the first player ever to hold all four Grand Slam singles titles simultaneously, which she achieved in 2019 — while in the quad division Niels Vink and in the men's division Tokito Oda of Japan took gold (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Singles and doubles follow the Laws of Tennis with the two-bounce rule as the only change: the first bounce must land within the court, the second may land anywhere (International Tennis Federation, n.d.). Matches are best of three sets with tie-breaks as in able-bodied tennis, and service alternates every two games as usual (Wikipedia, n.d.). Divisions are men's and women's open plus quad; in the quad division players may use motorised wheelchairs and may tape or strap the racket to the hand for grip, and in quad doubles at least one player of the pair must be a woman (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). Wheelchairs must comply with ITF dimension rules, and players may not use their feet to propel the chair where foot function is retained (International Tennis Federation, n.d.).

## History

The sport was founded in 1976 in the United States by Brad Parks (b. 1957), who became a paraplegic after a freestyle skiing accident, together with Jeff Minnenbraker, and the International Wheelchair Tennis Federation was formed in 1988 before the discipline was integrated into the International Tennis Federation in 1998 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Wheelchair tennis appeared as a demonstration sport at the Seoul 1988 Paralympics and became a full medal sport at Barcelona 1992, where Randy Snow won the first men's singles gold and Monique Kalkman-van den Bosch the first women's gold (International Paralympic Committee, n.d.). The quad division was added at Athens 2004, Grand Slam integration followed in the 2000s, and the women's game has been defined by the Netherlands' Esther Vergeer, who retired in February 2013 after a 470-match winning streak dating from January 2003, collecting seven Paralympic golds and 43 Grand Slam singles titles (Wikipedia, n.d.).
