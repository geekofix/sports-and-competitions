## Basic Information

Xikunahati (jikunahati) is the head-only football of Brazil's indigenous peoples, most famously of the Paresi: like football, but the ball may be touched only with the head — players move on all fours or standing and head the ball to teammates and goal. It was a demonstration/competition event at the 2015 World Indigenous Games in Palmas, Brazil, where a Paresi player headed the ball in front of the crowd. Men's and women's teams play at indigenous games. Within the folk, festival and novelty games on this site, its closest relatives are [World Indigenous Games](world-indigenous-games.html), [Corrida de Tora](corrida-de-tora.html), [Rustic Race](rustic-race.html), [Football](football.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Cultural Survival, n.d.).

## Rules

Xikunahati is played with football-goal rules adapted to head-only play: two teams, a ball, goals at each end; only the head may contact the ball (hands/feet fouls give the other team the ball or a free header); matches are short (e.g. 2×10 minutes at Games demonstrations); any number of players per side in village games. At the World Indigenous Games it was a demonstration event with mixed teams (Cultural Survival, n.d.).

## History

Head-ball games exist in several indigenous cultures of the Americas; xikunahati was showcased at the first World Indigenous Games (Palmas, 23 Oct–1 Nov 2015, ~2,000 athletes, 23 countries, 50 ethnic groups), photographed with Paresi players heading the ball. It remains a staple of Brazilian indigenous games and the 2017 Maskwacis (Canada) edition (Wikipedia, n.d.).
