## Basic Information

Combat sambo is the full-contact form of sambo, the Russian martial art and sport: in addition to throws, takedowns, joint locks and chokes, fighters may strike with fists, elbows, knees and feet. It is governed by the International Sambo Federation (FIAS), which was recognized by the IOC in 2021, and it was on the program of the 2025 World Games in Chengdu. The other FIAS discipline, sport sambo, is grappling only, without strikes or chokes. The FIAS runs World Championships in both disciplines.

## Rules

Under the FIAS International SAMBO Rules, a combat sambo bout consists of three five-minute rounds (with rest between), fought on an 8 or 9 m mat. Fighters wear the sambo jacket (kurtka), shorts, gumshield and groin protection but no headgear. The bout is won by a clean throw, submission (tap out), knockout, or by points when time expires; strikes score points, and a fighter knocked down is counted. Dangerous techniques (e.g., certain neck cranks, small-joint manipulation) are banned. Weight classes and age groups apply at world championships; the first Combat SAMBO World Championship was held in Panama City in 2002.

## History

Sambo ("self-defense without weapons") was developed in the 1920s-1930s in the Soviet Red Army by Viktor Spiridonov and Vasili Oshchepkov, with the competitive sport form standardized by Anatoly Kharlampiev. Combat sambo as a striking-inclusive variant emerged alongside sport sambo, and FIAS organized the first Combat SAMBO World Championship in 2002. FIAS became fully IOC-recognized in 2021, and combat sambo featured at the 2025 World Games in Chengdu, China.
