## Basic Information

Figure skating is an ice sport combining jumps, spins, footwork and choreography, performed individually, in pairs or in ice dance. It is governed by the International Skating Union (ISU), which runs the ISU World Figure Skating Championships, the ISU Grand Prix series (including the Grand Prix Final) and the Olympic figure skating program (since Chamonix 1924).

## Rules

At ISU Championships and the Olympics, individual skaters (men and women) perform a short program (2:30-2:50) and a free skate (4:00 for men and 3:00 for women at the Olympics since Beijing 2022), each judged on technical element score (TES) and program component score (PCS), with deductions for falls, time violations and costume violations. Required elements include jumps (single, double, triple, quadruple), jump combinations, spins (upright, sit, camel, combination, change-foot) and step sequences/choreographic sequences. Pairs and ice dance have their own required elements and lifts/twizzles. The combined short + free scores determine the ranking. The ISU Judging System (IJS) is used, with a technical panel identifying elements and levels and judges awarding grades of execution (GOE) from -5 to +5.

## History

Figure skating developed in the 18th-19th centuries in Britain and the Netherlands (Jackson Haines introduced artistic skating in Vienna in the 1860s), and the ISU was founded in 1892 in Scheveningen. Figure skating was the first winter sport at the Olympics (Chamonix 1924), with singles and pairs; ice dance was added in 1976 (Innsbruck). The ISU, based in Lausanne, governs figure skating, speed skating and short track. Legends include Sonja Henie (three Olympic golds 1928-1936), Dick Button, Peggy Fleming, Dorothy Hamill, Katarina Witt, the pairs team of Rodnina/Zaitsev, and modern stars Michelle Kwan, Yuzuru Hanyu (first man to land a quadruple Axel in competition, and back-to-back Olympic golds 2014/2018) and Nathan Chen.
