## Basic Information

Buzkashi (Persian for "goat pulling") is a traditional Central Asian equestrian team sport in which horse-mounted riders (chapandaz) fight for control of a headless goat or calf carcass and try to carry it around a flag and drop it into a chalked goal circle, the "circle of justice". It is the national sport of Afghanistan and is played across Central Asia under names such as kok-boru (Kyrgyzstan), kokpar (Kazakhstan) and ulak tartysh. It is contested at the World Nomad Games and world kokpar championships.

## Rules

In the traditional game, dozens of riders scrum for the carcass (a headless, gutted goat or calf, traditionally soaked in water for 24 hours to toughen the skin; today often a 30 kg leather-wrapped sack for animal-welfare reasons). A rider scores by grabbing the carcass, galloping around a flag at the far end of the field, and dropping it into the goal circle while rivals whip, block and try to wrest the carcass away. Matches historically could last days; modern regulated tournaments set time limits and team sizes (e.g., four riders per team in Kyrgyz kok-boru, whose rules were first codified in 1949). There are few formal fouls — tradition and the riders' code govern play, though modern federations add safety rules.

## History

Buzkashi began among the nomadic Turkic tribes of the Eurasian steppe and spread westward to Afghanistan between the 10th and 15th centuries. It is deeply embedded in Afghan culture — major matches are held on Fridays after prayers, drawing thousands. The Taliban banned the sport in 1996-2001 as immoral, then allowed it again after 2021. Kyrgyz kok-boru rules were officially defined in 1949 and stadium play began in 1958; the first Asian kokpar championship was in 2013, the first world championship in Astana in 2018, and the second in Turkistan in 2026. The game is part of the World Nomad Games program and was recognized by UNESCO's safeguarding register in 2021 via the Games.
