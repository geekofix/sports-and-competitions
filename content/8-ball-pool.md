## Basic Information

8-Ball Pool is the most widely played pool (pocket billiards) game in the world, played on a 9-foot table with 15 object balls: seven solids, seven stripes and the black 8-ball, plus the cue ball. Professional play follows the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA) rules.

## Rules

After the break the table is "open" until a player legally pockets a ball; that player is then assigned either the solid or the striped group. The assigned player must pocket all balls of their group and then legally call and pocket the 8-ball to win the rack. Pocketing the 8-ball early, on a foul, or off the wrong ball loses the rack immediately. The WPA break requires the cue ball to start behind the head string with ball-in-hand, and a push-out is allowed after a legal break. Standard fouls (scratch, wrong ball first, no rail after contact) give the opponent ball-in-hand anywhere; three consecutive fouls lose the rack. Variants: bar/league rules differ — for example, some casual rule sets treat an 8-ball pocketed on the break as an instant win or an instant loss, and call-shot requirements vary between BCA and WPA events.

## History

8-ball evolved from earlier pool games in the United States in the 19th century and became the standard pub and league game in the mid-20th century. Professional 8-ball boomed from the 1980s onward with events like the WPA World Championship and the US Open. Legendary players include Earl Strickland, Efren Reyes and Shane Van Boening.
