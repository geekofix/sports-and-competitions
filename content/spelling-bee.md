## Basic Information

A spelling bee is a competition in which contestants spell words aloud one at a time and are eliminated after a misspelling; the best-known event is the Scripps National Spelling Bee in the United States, held every year during the week after Memorial Day in Washington, DC — at DAR Constitution Hall since 2026, previously at the Gaylord National Harbor Resort — and sponsored by the E.W. Scripps Company since 1941, with Merriam-Webster's Unabridged dictionary as the official word arbiter since 1958 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The 2026 bee, with a field of 247 spellers from the 50 states, DC, US territories and several other countries, was won on 29 May 2026 by 14-year-old Shrey Parikh of Rancho Cucamonga, California; the 2025 centennial edition was won by Faizan Zaki of Allen, Texas, with Sarvadnya Kadam as runner-up (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The bee is open equally to girls and boys, and young women have repeatedly reached the top of the field: Sarvadnya Kadam was runner-up in 2025, Vanya Shivashankar was a co-champion in 2015, and the 2019 bee ended with eight co-champions after a marathon final round (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Eligibility for the Scripps National Spelling Bee is limited to spellers in grade 8 or below who are not yet 15 years old, and each speller may compete only once at the national level; the field is drawn from sponsor programs across the United States and internationally (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The competition proceeds from a written preliminary test to oral rounds: after the written test the field is cut to roughly the top half, then quarterfinals, semifinals and a final oral round decide the champion, with the last remaining speller winning if all others are eliminated. In the oral rounds the pronouncer says the word, and the speller may ask for the word to be repeated, its definition, its pronunciation, its part of speech, its language of origin or a sentence using it before spelling it letter by letter; a misspelling eliminates the speller, and homophone challenges by other spellers can save them. The winner receives a cash prize of more than $50,000 (about $52,500 in recent editions) plus a trophy and other awards, and the bee is broadcast nationally (Scripps National Spelling Bee, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## History

The Scripps National Spelling Bee began on 17 June 1925 at the Mayflower Hotel in Washington, DC, organized by the Louisville Courier-Journal with nine newspapers as sponsors; the first champion was 15-year-old Frank Neuhauser of Louisville, Kentucky, who spelled "gladiolus" correctly and died in 2011 at age 97 (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The E.W. Scripps Company took over sponsorship in 1941, and Merriam-Webster became the official dictionary in 1958, giving the bee its modern form. Milestones include the 2019 eight-way tie for the title and the 2025 centennial won by Faizan Zaki; girls and boys have always competed together on equal terms, and female champions such as Vanya Shivashankar (2015 co-champion) are part of the title's history (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
