## Basic Information

9-Ball Pool is the premier professional pool (rotation) game: played on a 9-foot table with balls 1 through 9 plus the cue ball, racked in a diamond. It is the main event at major tournaments — including the million-dollar World Pool Championship — and is governed by the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA) (World Pool-Billiard Association [WPA], n.d.-c). See also [8-Ball Pool](8-ball-pool.html), [10-Ball Pool](10-ball-pool.html), [Billiards](billiards.html), [Straight Pool](straight-pool.html) and [Carom Billiards](carom-billiards.html) on this site.

Among the players shaping the game today are the Philippines' Chezka Centeno, one of the leading women in professional pool, and Austria's Jasmin Ouschan, who heads the WPA women's world ranking (WPA, n.d.-a; WPA, n.d.-b). On the men's side, the Philippines' Carlo Biado won the 2025 WPA World Nine-ball Championship in Jeddah 15–13 over Fedor Gorst of the USA, and the November 2026 world tour (WNT) rankings are led by Singapore's Aloysius Yapp, followed by Biado, Spain's Francisco Sánchez Ruiz and Gorst (Matchroom Pool, n.d.; WPA, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under WPA rules the cue ball must contact the lowest-numbered ball on the table first on every shot. Balls are played in ascending order, but any ball pocketed legally stays down — the rack is won by legally pocketing the 9-ball on any shot, including the break; unlike in 10-ball, the 9-ball is not spotted (WPA, 2025). In professional play every shot except the break and a push-out must be called (ball and pocket). After a legal break a push-out is allowed. A foul gives the opponent ball-in-hand anywhere on the table, and three consecutive fouls lose the rack. The table is a standard 9-foot pool table with six pockets and 57.15 mm (2 1/4 in) balls; the balls are racked in a diamond with the 1-ball on the foot spot and the 9-ball in the centre (WPA, 2025). Men, women, junior and wheelchair athletes play the identical rule set, with separate championship divisions (WPA, n.d.-c). Some amateur and bar rule sets spot the 9-ball if it drops on the break or uncalled, so local games can differ from the WPA game (Cue Sports International, n.d.).

## History

9-ball grew out of rotation-style pool games in the early 20th-century United States and became the dominant professional format from the 1980s, with flagship events such as the US Open 9-Ball Championship, the World 9-Ball Pool Championship in Doha and the Mosconi Cup (WPA, n.d.-b). The Philippines' Efren Reyes won the World 9-Ball Pool Championship in Doha in 2012, and the modern WPA era has been shaped by Fedor Gorst's wins in 2019 (13–11 over Chang Yu-Lun) and 2024 (15–14 over Eklent Kaçi in Jeddah, part of the $1 million World Pool Championship) and Carlo Biado's 2017 Doha title and 2025 Jeddah win (WPA, n.d.-b). On the women's side, the WPA runs a Women's World Nine-ball Championship with Jasmin Ouschan of Austria among its leading champions (WPA, n.d.-b). Legends of the game include Efren Reyes, Earl Strickland, Francisco Bustamante and Shane Van Boening (Matchroom Pool, n.d.).
