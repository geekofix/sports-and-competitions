## Basic Information

9-Ball Pool is the premier professional pool (rotation) game: played on a 9-foot table with balls 1 through 9 plus the cue ball, racked in a diamond. It is the main event at major tournaments and is governed by the World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA).

## Rules

Under WPA rules the cue ball must contact the lowest-numbered ball on the table first on every shot. Balls are pocketed in ascending order, but any ball pocketed legally stays down — the rack is won by legally pocketing the 9-ball on any shot, including the break (unlike 10-ball, the 9 is not spotted). In professional play shots must be called (ball and pocket). After a legal break a push-out is allowed. A foul gives the opponent ball-in-hand anywhere; three consecutive fouls lose the rack. Variants: some amateur/bar rule sets spot the 9-ball if it drops on the break or uncalled, so local games can differ from the WPA game.

## History

9-ball grew out of rotation-style pool games in the early 20th century United States and became the dominant professional format from the 1980s, with flagship events such as the US Open 9-Ball Championship and the Mosconi Cup. Legends of the game include Efren Reyes, Earl Strickland, Francisco Bustamante and Shane Van Boening.
