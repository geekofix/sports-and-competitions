## Basic Information

Beach fistball is the sand variant of faustball, played by two teams of five on a 25 m x 12.5 m (or similar) sand court with a lower net, using the same fist/forearm striking rules as indoor faustball. It is governed by the International Fistball Association (IFT), which includes beach fistball in its international program, and is played at the IFT Beach Fistball World Championships and at multi-sport beach events.

## Rules

Beach fistball follows faustball rules adapted to sand: teams of five, the ball may be touched up to three times per side (block at the net counts), and a point is scored when the ball lands in the opponent's court, goes out, or the opponent commits a fault. The court is typically 25 m x 12.5 m (smaller than the 50 x 20 m grass court) with a net height around 2.00 m (2.10 m at some events; women's and youth categories may use lower nets). The serve is struck over the net with the fist or forearm after tossing the ball up. Matches at IFT beach events are best-of-five sets to 11 points (win by two, capped at 15). Sand play slows the ball and changes footing, making the game more athletic and rally-based than grass faustball.

## History

Beach fistball emerged in the 2000s as beach variants of court sports gained popularity, and the IFT formally added beach fistball to its competition calendar, holding world-level beach championships. Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Brazil are the leading nations, and beach fistball has been featured at beach multi-sport games and the World Games beach program discussions. The sport combines the striking technique of faustball with the agility demands of beach volleyball.
