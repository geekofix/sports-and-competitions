## Basic Information

Popdarts is a target game in which players toss double-ended suction-cup darts at a small target marker on any smooth surface — a countertop, window, glass door or table — combining the accuracy of darts with the scoring feel of cornhole. The game went viral on social media in the 2020s and has since developed an organized competitive scene under the American Popdarts League (APL), with sanctioned events, official equipment and televised-style arena competitions.

## Rules

In the standard game, players or teams alternate throws, with each player tossing three Popdarts per round from a designated throwing box, commonly about 10 feet from the target marker in the classic floor format or across a table in league play. A dart must stick upright to the surface; a dart that is not standing upright is dead but remains in play until the end of the round, and it is legal to knock an opponent's dart loose. The dart closest to the target marker scores 3 points, and every other upright dart scores 1 point, with the "Kiss" bonus — a dart touching the target marker — worth 1 additional point. Rounds use cancellation scoring like cornhole: only the net difference is credited, and the first team to reach a set total (commonly 21) wins. Each player also has one called "Instant Win" attempt per game: a dart landing completely inside the Instant Win circle ends the game immediately. APL play adds a 10-second shot clock, one timeout per round and strict equipment rules—only unmodified, league-approved Popdarts darts may be used.

## History

Popdarts was invented in the United States as an indoor-friendly alternative to pointed darts and lawn games, with the suction-cup design making it safe for players, pets and walls alike; the brand's Rookie, Pro and Board editions spread through viral TikTok and YouTube demonstrations in the early 2020s. The concept of the game proved as portable as its equipment — played on refrigerators, windshields and office windows — and in 2024-25 the American Popdarts League formalized competition, publishing official rules, creating the sanctioned Elite dart, staging qualifiers and a national championship in an arena format with ESPN-style production. The sport's growth has been driven partly by bar and "man cave" culture, where the game's short rounds and Instant Win mechanic suit casual leagues.
