## Basic Information

Fives is the English family of hand-ball games played against the walls and buttresses of a court: hard ball struck with a padded or gloved hand in singles or doubles, with the chief variants being Eton fives (played in a three-sided court with a hazard buttress, copied from the chapel at Eton) and Rugby fives (a simpler rectangular court with a lower front wall). The game is a cousin of hand-tennis across Europe.

The sport survives in a thriving schools-and-clubs circuit: the Eton Fives Association runs UK leagues and national championships, Rugby fives runs its Open and schools championships, and university clubs keep both codes alive.

## Rules

Courts have front and back walls plus side walls; Rugby fives uses tennis-style boundaries, Eton fives adds a hazard buttress at the front-left corner of the court, and players strike the hard ball with a gloved hand against the front wall to keep the rally alive. Doubles rotate the serve, and games run to 12 or 15 points with server-only scoring by tradition; the hard ball differs slightly between the two codes, Rugby fives using a wrapped-cork, leather-covered ball.

## History

Fives descends from handball variants against church and school walls played across England since the medieval era; courts were built at Eton in the 18th century, and the chapel buttress gave Eton fives its iconic asymmetric court shape, replicated wherever the game spread. Rugby school's courts codified Rugby fives in the 19th century, playing a major role in public school sport; Old Etonians and Rugbeians spread the game, and associations formalized post-war leagues and championships. Today a mix of schools, universities and dedicated clubs continue the sport with weekly leagues and championships.
