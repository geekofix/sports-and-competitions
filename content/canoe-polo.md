## Basic Information

Canoe polo is a fast team ball game played in kayaks on flatwater or in a pool, governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF). Two teams of eight (five players on the water, three substitutes) paddle small kayaks and try to throw a water-polo-style ball into a goal suspended over the water at each end. It is one of the ten ICF disciplines, alternating between World Cup and World Championship years, and was on the program of the 2025 World Games in Chengdu.

## Rules

Under the ICF canoe polo rules, a match consists of two halves of ten minutes on a pitch about 35 by 23 m (flatwater) or in a swimming pool. Players may hold, pass and throw the ball with hands or paddle, and may be tackled by opponents whose boats are within reach; deliberate contact with the person is a foul. The goal is about 2 m wide and 1 m high, suspended above the water. Fouls are punished with free throws, penalty throws from a fixed spot, or two-minute suspensions (green/yellow cards). Matches are started with a sprint from the center line.

## History

Canoe polo developed from kayak polo games played in the early 20th century in Europe, especially Germany and Britain, and was standardized as kayak polo in the 1970s. The ICF adopted the discipline and has run world championships since the 1990s; it alternates between World Cup and World Championship years. The sport combines sprint paddling, ball skills and physical tackling, and enjoys a strong following in Europe, Australia, Japan and the Americas.
