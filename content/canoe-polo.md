## Basic Information

Canoe polo (kayak polo) is a team ball sport played in kayaks: two teams of five players on the pitch (with up to three substitutes) paddle on a rectangular field of 35 × 23 m in a pool or on flat open water and try to throw a ball into the opponent's goal, which is suspended 2 m above the water (ICF, n.d.). Matches at ICF level are played over two halves of 10 minutes with a 1–3 minute half-time, each half starting with a sprint for the ball thrown in by the referee; a 60-second shot clock may be used (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). The 2026 ICF Canoe Polo World Championships — the 16th edition — were held in Duisburg, Germany, from 14/15 to 20 September 2026, with senior and under-21 tournaments, about 250 games across four pitches, and the event ticketed for the first time; at the July 2026 ICF Congress the federation also changed its name to Paddle Worldwide (Paddle Worldwide, 2026; VFW Kanupolo, 2026). Historically, Australia won the first three men's world titles and two of the first three women's, then Europe dominated: the Netherlands and France alternated titles for six consecutive championships between 2004 and 2014 before Italy broke through at home in 2016 (ICF, n.d.). Women's polo is fully established, with its own world and continental championships and national teams (ICF, n.d.).

## Rules

Under the ICF Canoe Polo rules, the pitch is 35 × 23 m, marked by floating boundary lines; the goal is 2 m wide × 1 m high, suspended 2 m above the water at each end, with a 6 m "zone" in front of each goal where the defending team forms. Each team fields five players and up to three substitutes; helmets with face guards, life jackets and nose/tail boat bumpers are mandatory. The ball (water-polo ball) may be thrown by hand or flicked with the paddle, and players may be tackled by opponents using their kayak within the rules. Matches are two halves of 10 minutes; a shot clock of 60 seconds is often used at elite level. Penalties include free throws, penalty shots (from 5 m, one-on-one with the goalkeeper), yellow (2-minute) and red (permanent) cards for fouls such as holding, pushing, dangerous play or hitting an opponent; the referees stand on the sidelines, assisted by goal-line judges, a timekeeper and a scorekeeper (ICF, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Men's and women's competitions use the same pitch, ball and rules; at the world championships there are separate men's, women's, U21 and (from recent editions) masters events, plus a world ranking based on the previous world and continental championships (ICF, n.d.).

## History

Canoe ball games have been played in Great Britain since the late 19th century — *Punch* magazine pictured "Polo on the Sea" in 1875 — and early forms were played in the US and Europe in the early 20th century. The modern sport was born at a demonstration at the National Canoe Exhibition at the Crystal Palace, London, in 1970; the British Canoe Union's national subcommittee developed the framework, the first English national championship was held in 1971, and the ICF published its first universal rules in 1986. Canoe polo was a demonstration sport at the 1987 Duisburg worlds of canoeing, the first European Championship was held in 1993 in the UK, and the first ICF World Championship in 1994, with worlds every two years since (Wikipedia, n.d.; ICF, n.d.). Canoe polo appeared at the World Games from 2005. In India, university coach Muhammad Yusuf started canoe polo at the University of Kashmir in 2008 on Nigeen Lake (Wikipedia, n.d.).
