## Basic Information

KanJam (now also known as Discflect) is a flying disc game played by two teams of two players with one disc and two plastic cans ("kans") placed 50 feet apart. The thrower tosses the disc toward the opponents' can while his partner stands at that can and tries to deflect the disc into it or against it. It was invented in the late 1980s by two friends in Buffalo, New York, playing "garbage can frisbee", and is today played worldwide, with the annual KanJam World Championship and the KanJam Klassic held in North Tonawanda, New York. The KanJam company remains the official equipment manufacturer.

## Rules

Teams alternate throws. The thrower must release the disc from behind the front edge of his own can, and the partner (deflector) may move anywhere around the opposing can but may touch the disc only once and may not catch or carry it; the disc may be deflected with any body part. Scoring: a disc hitting the can on the fly (dinger) is 1 point, deflected against the can (deuce) is 2 points, deflected into the can (bucket) is 3 points, and a throw that goes straight through the slot or into the top opening of the can without a deflection is an instant win. A game is played to exactly 21 points; if a team is over 21, the score reverts (or overtime is played). Deliberate interference with the can by the defending team forfeits the game.

## History

Charles Sciandra and Paul Swisher, college friends in Buffalo, New York, began throwing discs into garbage cans in the late 1980s and formalized the game as KanJam; the KanJam company was founded and sold its first set in 2005, and the game spread through schools, tailgates and parks across the United States. Competitive play is organized by the Professional Discflect Association (PDFA), which runs the two biggest annual tournaments in North Tonawanda: the KanJam World Championship and the KanJam Klassic. The equipment is still manufactured in Buffalo, and the game has become a staple of American backyard and campus recreation.
