## Basic Information

KanJam — rebranded as "Discflect" in 2020 — is a flying-disc team game invented in the late 1980s in Buffalo, New York, by Charles Sciandra and Paul Swisher, who developed the earlier "Garbage Can Frisbee" game played in the late 1970s in Tonawanda by a group including Mogensen, Raymond, Kopasz and Heron (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Two teams of two players stand 50 feet (about 15 m) apart, each team guarding a plastic can; one player throws the disc toward the opponent's can while the partner may deflect it once with an open hand (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b). Scoring: a deflection that makes the disc hit the can is a "dinger" (1 point), a throw that hits the can untouched is a "deuce" (2 points), and a throw that enters the slot on the lid or the front opening of the can is a "bucket" (3 points); a disc that flies through the slot without touching anything wins the game instantly (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). A game is played to exactly 21 points — scoring plays that would carry a team past 21 are not counted — or ends immediately on an instant win (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b). The Professional Discflect Association (PDFA), founded in 2009, is the governing body of the sport and sanctions the annual World Championship (the Klassic) in North Tonawanda, New York, traditionally held on the second Saturday of August; KanJam LLC, the brand company, was formed in 2006 when Swisher sold a share to Mitchel Rubin (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The World Championship fields open and women's divisions, and women have been among the most successful competitors at the top level — teams such as Dorkus Malorkus (four world titles), the Rebel Survivors and the Sole Survivors have dominated the podium, and the 2014 champion team was the Stallion Survivors (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Under the official PDFA rules, two teams of two play on a field with the two cans set 50 feet (15.24 m) apart; each round consists of four throws, two per team, with partners alternating thrower and deflector roles (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b). The thrower must release the disc from behind the can, and only the designated deflector may touch the disc in flight — a single deflection is allowed, and catching the disc is a foul that awards the opposing team a point (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b). Scoring is: dinger (deflected into the side of the can) 1 point, deuce (direct hit on the can) 2 points, bucket (through the slot or the front opening, with or without deflection) 3 points, and an untouched throw through the slot is an instant win (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b). Games are played to exactly 21 points; if a scoring play would take a team over 21, the points are void and the game continues, and ties at 21 are broken in overtime rounds (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women play under identical rules in open and women's divisions, and youth divisions exist at sanctioned tournaments; equipment is a standard 175 g flying disc and regulation cans supplied or approved by the PDFA (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b). Rule source: the Professional Discflect Association (PDFA), https://pdfa.com/rules/ (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-b).

## History

The game's precursor, "Garbage Can Frisbee," was played in the late 1970s in Tonawanda, New York, by a group including Mogensen, Raymond, Kopasz and Heron (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In the late 1980s Charles Sciandra and Paul Swisher formalized the game in Buffalo, New York, giving it the name KanJam (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). KanJam LLC was founded in 2006, when Swisher sold a share of the company to Mitchel Rubin, and the brand grew into a backyard and tournament staple across North America and Europe (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The Professional Discflect Association was founded in 2009 to govern competitive play and has run the annual World Championship and Klassic in North Tonawanda since (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-a). In 2020 the sport was rebranded as "Discflect" — a blend of "disc" and "deflect" — with the PDFA overseeing all competitive aspects (Professional Discflect Association, n.d.-a). Among the most successful teams in the history of the World Championship are Dorkus Malorkus with four titles, the Rebel Survivors and the Sole Survivors; the 2014 champions were the Stallion Survivors, and Ross Urugbezi was the 2019 individual standout (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
