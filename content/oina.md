## Basic Information

Oină (pronounced roughly "oy-nuh") is the national bat-and-ball game of Romania, played between two teams of eleven in which the batting side tries to score runs while the fielding side tries to get the batters out; the name comes from the Cuman word oyn or oyun, meaning game. It is often described as a cousin of [baseball](baseball.html), and some historians argue that Romanian oină influenced the development of baseball. The game is governed in Romania by the Romanian Oină Federation and is documented internationally by the World Ethnosport Organization.

## Rules

Oină is played on a rectangular field with two teams of eleven players. The batting team, called la bataie, sends a batter to hit a ball thrown by the fielding team, called la prindere, with a wooden bat, and then runs between bases trying to score points called oină; the fielding team tries to catch the ball in the air or tag the runner out. After each half of the game the roles switch, so both teams bat and field once, and the team with the most points wins. The official rules are maintained by the Romanian Oină Federation, which organizes the national championship; a similar game is also played in Moldova, where a second oină federation exists in Chișinău.

## History

Oină is first mentioned in Romanian documents around 1364 and was described in detail by the historian István Mátyus in 1782; the name derives from the Cuman oyn, brought to the region in the Middle Ages. The game was introduced into Romanian school physical education by Spiru Haret in 1898-1899, together with the first annual school competitions, which spread it across the country. The Romanian Oină Federation was founded in 1932 and the sport was reorganized after the 1950s; today oină is practiced in more than 40 counties of Romania and among Romanian communities abroad, and it remains a compulsory subject in many Romanian schools. Because of its age and similarities, oină is frequently cited in debates about the origins of baseball.
