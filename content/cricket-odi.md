## Basic Information

One Day International (ODI) cricket is the 50-over format of the game: each team bats once for a maximum of 50 six-ball overs and bowls 50 overs, with colored clothing, a white ball and day or day/night matches under floodlights (International Cricket Council [ICC], n.d.-a; Wikipedia, n.d.-b). ODIs are governed by the ICC under the Laws of Cricket and the ODI Playing Conditions, and the format's pinnacle events are the quadrennial ICC Cricket World Cup, the ICC Champions Trophy and the World Test Championship for the longer format (ICC, n.d.-a). The 2023 Cricket World Cup in India ended on 19 November 2023 at the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad, where Australia beat India by six wickets to win a record sixth title, Travis Head scoring 137 in the final (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2025 Champions Trophy in Dubai went to India, who beat New Zealand by four wickets in the final on 9 March 2025 — their third Champions Trophy — with Rohit Sharma's 76 player of the match (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). In women's ODIs, the 2025 ICC Women's Cricket World Cup final at the DY Patil Stadium in Navi Mumbai on 2 November 2025 saw India beat South Africa by 52 runs to win their maiden title, Shafali Verma top-scoring 87 and named player of the match and Deepti Sharma player of the tournament (ICC, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The next men's World Cup, in 2027, will be co-hosted by South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia from 2 October to 21 November 2027 with 14 teams in a revamped format — two groups of seven followed by a Super 8 stage and semi-finals (ICC, n.d.-b). In the World Test Championship 2025–27 (fourth edition, started 17 June 2025, final at The Oval by 12 June 2027, points 12 for a win, 6 for a tie, 4 for a draw, ranked by percentage of points), South Africa won the 2023–25 edition by beating Australia by five wickets at Lord's on 11–14 June 2025 with Temba Bavuma's side and Kagiso Rabada's bowling, while Australia led the 2025–27 table as of late 2026; the 2025–26 Ashes in Australia ended with Australia retaining the urn, and the 2027 Ashes series opens with a pink-ball 150th-anniversary Test (ICC, n.d.-b). ODI cricket is played in separate men's and women's internationals under the same playing conditions, with a dedicated women's World Cup and women's World Rankings; at the end of 2026 the leading current players are, in women's ODIs, the 2025 world champion trio Shafali Verma, Smriti Mandhana and Deepti Sharma of India, and in men's ODIs stars such as Virat Kohli, Rohit Sharma and Jasprit Bumrah of India and Kane Williamson and Trent Boult of New Zealand (ICC, n.d.-b) (International Cricket Council, n.d.-a) (International Cricket Council, n.d.-b) (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

## Rules

An ODI is played under the ICC's ODI Playing Conditions supplementing the MCC's Laws of Cricket: each innings lasts a maximum of 50 overs per side, each bowler may bowl a maximum of 10 overs, and the fielding side must observe powerplay restrictions — two fielders outside the 30-yard circle in the first 10 overs, then a maximum of four outside until the 40th over, and five in the final 10 (ICC, n.d.-a). The innings ends when 50 overs are bowled, all ten batters are out, or the chasing side reaches the target; if scores are level the match is a tie, decided by a Super Over in knockout matches (ICC, n.d.-a). The ball (white, 155.9–163 g men's, 142–150.8 g women's), the pitch (22 yards), the wickets and the eleven dismissal modes are as in all cricket; the DRS review system, free hits after no-balls and the Duckworth–Lewis–Stern method for rain-affected matches are standard in ODIs (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). World Cup formats have varied: the 2023 edition used a single round-robin of ten teams followed by semi-finals and a final; the 2027 edition returns 14 teams with group and Super 8 stages (ICC, n.d.-b). There are no gender- or age-based differences in the playing conditions themselves — women's ODIs use the same 50-over structure with a slightly smaller, lighter ball, and U19 World Cups exist for juniors in both genders (ICC, n.d.-a).

## History

The first ODI was played on 5 January 1971 at the Melbourne Cricket Ground between Australia and England — a rain-reduced eight-over-a-side match to make up a washed-out Test, won by Australia by five wickets — and the first Women's ODI followed in 1973 during the inaugural Women's World Cup in England (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). The ICC Cricket World Cup became the format's centerpiece from 1975 in England, with the West Indies winning the first two editions (1975, 1979) under Clive Lloyd and Pakistan triumphing in 1992 under Imran Khan at Melbourne (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Australia's dynasty followed — 1987 and 1999 under Allan Border, 2003, 2007 and 2015 under Ricky Ponting, and 2023 under Pat Cummins — before India won on home soil in 2011 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The 2023 final at Ahmedabad, pictured below as the trophy celebration, saw Travis Head's match-winning 137 for Australia against India's 397; the 2025 Champions Trophy final in Dubai gave India a third title with Rohit Sharma's 76 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Icons of ODI cricket include Viv Richards (b. 1952) of the West Indies, Sachin Tendulkar (b. 1973), the first man to 100 international centuries, and in the women's game Mithali Raj (b. 1982), the highest run-scorer in women's ODIs, Ellyse Perry (b. 1990) of Australia and England's Charlotte Edwards (b. 1979) (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).
