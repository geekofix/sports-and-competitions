## Basic Information

Ski ballet — renamed acroski in the 1990s — was a freestyle skiing discipline in which athletes skied a choreographed two-minute routine down a mogul-style course, performing spins, jumps, flips and balletic movements to music and being judged like figure skaters; it was never a medal event but appeared as a demonstration sport at the Calgary 1988 and Albertville 1992 Winter Olympics, and the International Ski Federation (FIS) ceased all formal acroski competitions after 2000 (FIS, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.). Today it survives in exhibition shows and ballet jam events rather than in formal competition (Wikipedia, n.d.). At the Albertville 1992 demonstration, France's Fabrice Becker won the men's event with a well-choreographed tango routine ahead of Norway's Rune Kristiansen and America's Lane Spina, while Switzerland's Conny Kissling won the women's event — her 102nd career victory — ahead of France's Cathy Féchoz and America's Sharon Petzold (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

Women were central to the discipline's heyday: the Swiss Conny Kissling and the Americans Ellen Breen and Sharon Petzold were world champions, and the American Genia Fuller became the first skier — woman or man — to win all four freestyle disciplines at one event in 1974 (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women competed in separate events judged by identical criteria, and a pair ballet variation with lifts and synchronised movements also existed in the 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.).

 Within the winter ski and snowboard family on this site, its closest relatives are [Skiing](skiing.html), [Snowboarding](snowboarding.html), [Alpine Skiing Downhill](alpine-skiing-downhill.html), [Alpine Skiing Giant Slalom](alpine-skiing-giant-slalom.html), [Alpine Skiing Slalom](alpine-skiing-slalom.html); the full list of 38 related pages is under Important Links.

## Rules

Ski ballet was governed by the FIS (https://www.fis-ski.com/) as one of the freestyle skiing disciplines alongside moguls, aerials and combined (FIS, n.d.). Athletes performed a two-minute routine down a dedicated course containing two lines of moguls plus jumps; a panel of judges scored three components — turns (technical merit), jumps or aerials (acrobatics) and overall impression (artistic performance) — which were combined into a single result, in a system modeled on figure skating (Wikipedia, n.d.). Competitors used short straight skis and poles, wore colorful one-piece suits and performed to music; men's and women's events were judged identically, and from the first FIS Freestyle World Cup in 1980 the discipline was part of the World Cup circuit and, from the first FIS Freestyle World Ski Championships in Tignes 1986, of the world title programme (Wikipedia, n.d.; FIS, n.d.). Because the discipline was never elevated to medal status, it carried no Olympic points; after the Albertville 1992 demonstration it was dropped from the Olympic programme, and the FIS halted all formal acroski competition after 2000 (Olympics, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.).

## History

Ski ballet grew out of the 1960s–70s "hotdogging" counterculture of stunt skiing in North America and Europe; early innovators included the Americans Jan Bucher and Park Smalley, the Swiss Conny Kissling and the German Hermann Reitberger, and the German Richard Schabl — the first skier to perform a one-handed pole flip in a World Cup — became the 1986 world champion (Wikipedia, n.d.). The discipline joined the first FIS Freestyle World Cup in 1980 and the first FIS Freestyle World Ski Championships in Tignes 1986, where ballet and a combined event were on the programme; Ellen Breen of the United States won the women's world title in 1991 in Lake Placid and again in 1993 in Altenmarkt-Zauchensee (Wikipedia, n.d.). It was a demonstration sport at Calgary 1988 and Albertville 1992 — Lane Spina of the United States medalled at both — but when freestyle skiing became an official medal sport at Albertville 1992 with moguls, ballet remained demonstration-only and was dropped from the programme afterwards; the FIS ended all formal acroski competition after 2000 as it concentrated on the Olympic disciplines moguls (1992) and aerials (1994), and the sport's popularity had already declined with the rise of snowboarding (Wikipedia, n.d.; Olympics, n.d.).
