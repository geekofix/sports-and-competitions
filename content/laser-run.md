## Basic Information

Laser-run is the final discipline of modern pentathlon, introduced by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM) in 2009 to replace the separate running and air-pistol shooting phases. Athletes alternate running legs with shooting bouts at targets using a laser pistol, and the first athlete across the finish line wins — a combined event where the leading pentathlete after the first three disciplines starts first and the others follow with time handicaps based on their points.

## Rules

In the laser-run, athletes complete a series of running legs (about 300-800 m each, totaling roughly 1,600-3,200 m depending on the category) interspersed with shooting bouts at a 10 m range: each bout requires five hits on a target, and every missed shot adds a penalty of one second per miss (in modern formats) or forces an extra lap, depending on the event. Athletes carry the laser pistol, which is only activated at the shooting range; shooting before the line is a foul. The combined-start format means the athlete with the most points after fencing, swimming and riding starts first, and each point equals one second of handicap, so the winner of the laser-run is the first to cross the line — the overall Olympic pentathlon champion.

## History

Modern pentathlon was created by Pierre de Coubertin and has been on the Olympic program since Stockholm 1912; for decades it ended with a 4,000 m run and 10 m air-pistol shooting as separate phases. In 2009 the UIPM combined them into the laser-run with a laser pistol, making the final discipline faster and more spectator-friendly; the format has been adjusted since (target size, penalty rules, combined start). The laser-run has decided Olympic and World Championship medals since 2009, and the UIPM, founded in 1948, governs the sport worldwide. Pentathlon is one of the few Olympic sports where a single athlete can win gold in an event combining five very different skills.
