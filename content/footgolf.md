## Basic Information

Footgolf is golf played with a football: instead of clubs, players kick a size 5 ball from the tee into a large cup in as few strokes (kicks) as possible, over a course of 18 holes (FIFG, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed by the Fédération Internationale de FootGolf (FIFG), founded in June 2012 with headquarters in Lausanne, which published the first unified rulebook in 2012 and organizes the FIFG World Cup (FIFG, n.d.-a). The World Cup editions grew from the inaugural 2012 tournament in Budapest — won by Béla Lengyel of Hungary before 126 players from 8 countries — through Pilar, Argentina (2016, more than 230 players from 26 countries), Marrakesh (2018, more than 520 players from 33 countries, won by Matías Perrone of Argentina) and Orlando (2023, 972 players from 39 countries, with Lucka Čermáková of Slovakia winning the women's title) to the 5th FIFG World Cup in Acapulco, Mexico, May 27 – June 7, 2026, which drew more than 1,200 players from over 60 countries and 64 teams, played on the Turtle Dunes Turtle Course and the Tres Vidas Vidas Course, with the individual competition May 29 – June 1 in the Men, Women, Senior and Senior Plus categories (FIFG, n.d.-b). Sweden became the first country to recognize footgolf as an official sport in December 2015, and the first FIFG Youth World Cup was held in Germany in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de FootGolf, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de FootGolf, n.d.-b) (Fédération Internationale de FootGolf, n.d.-c).

At the end of 2026 the leading women are Lucka Čermáková (SVK), the 2023 world champion, and the top players of the 2026 Acapulco world cup in the Women's category, while on the men's side the reigning world champion Matías Perrone (ARG, 2018) and the 2026 Acapulco medallists — including Sergio Toth, winner of the Senior Plus title, and Sergio Vázquez of Argentina, second in the men's under-30 event — head the standings (FIFG, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the FIFG Rules of Footgolf (rulebook in force since 2012), the ball is a standard size 5 football, and each hole ends in a cup of 52 cm diameter and 28 cm deep — large enough for the ball to drop in, with a removable flagstick — set in the green (FIFG, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Courses consist of 18 holes with par values typically 3 to 5; players tee off from a designated tee area and must play the ball as it lies, with subsequent kicks from where the ball came to rest; the hole is completed when the ball rests in the cup (FIFG, n.d.-c). Standard golf-style penalties apply for out-of-bounds balls, lost balls, unplayable lies, water hazards and casual water, and the ball may be cleaned and lifted within the rules (FIFG, n.d.-c). Scoring is medal stroke play: the lowest total number of kicks over 18 holes wins, with playoffs to break ties; team competitions (national teams of 64 at the 2026 World Cup) are played in match-play or stroke-play formats alongside the individual event (FIFG, n.d.-b). Men and women play the same course and rules but compete in separate categories, alongside age categories such as Senior and Senior Plus, and youth events (FIFG, n.d.-b; FIFG, n.d.-c).

## History

Footgolf emerged independently around 2008–2009 in Hungary and the United States, where players began kicking footballs into large cups on golf courses; the first unified rules were codified in 2012 when the FIFG was founded in Lausanne, and the first FIFG World Cup followed the same year in Budapest, won by Hungary's Béla Lengyel (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; FIFG, n.d.-a). The sport spread quickly through Europe — the Netherlands organized early international tournaments from 2012 onward — and to Argentina, where Matías Perrone's 2018 world title in Marrakesh established South America as a powerhouse (FIFG, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Sweden's official recognition in December 2015 made footgolf the first country to grant it full sporting status, and the FIFG has since grown the World Cup from 126 players in 8 countries (2012) to more than 1,200 players from over 60 countries at Acapulco 2026, adding the first FIFG Youth World Cup in Germany in 2026 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; FIFG, n.d.-b). On the women's side, Slovakia's Lucka Čermáková became the first women's world champion in 2023 in Orlando (FIFG, n.d.-b).
