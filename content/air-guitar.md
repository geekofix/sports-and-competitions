## Basic Information

Air guitar is a judged performance competition in which contestants mime playing an invisible electric guitar to a song of their choice — solos, chord changes, headbanging and stage moves — while a panel of judges scores "airness" (technique and believability), stage presence and originality (Air Guitar World Championships [AGWC], n.d.-b). The annual Air Guitar World Championships (AGWC) have been held in Oulu, Finland, since 1996 under the motto "Make Air Not War"; the winner receives a custom-built "Flying Finn" guitar crafted by luthiers Matti and Saara Nevalainen (AGWC, n.d.-a) (Air Guitar World Championships, n.d.-a) (Air Guitar World Championships, n.d.-b).

Among the athletes who shape the competition today, the leading women come first: Japan's Nanami "Seven Seas" Nagura was runner-up at the 2026 world championship, and France's Apolline "Lady Atilla" Andreys finished third in both 2022 and 2025. On the men's side, Finland's Aapo "The Angus" Rautio won his second consecutive world title at the 30th anniversary edition, held August 27–29, 2026 in Oulu — European Capital of Culture 2026 — with more than 50 contestants from 13 countries (AGWC, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Each competitor performs a 60-second routine to a song of their choice, miming every move of a real guitarist; the instrument itself must remain invisible, while costumes, props and pyrotechnics are encouraged. A panel of judges awards points for airness, stage presence and originality, and the highest total wins; national qualifiers in many countries feed the world championship, and some editions add a wild-card freestyle round (AGWC, n.d.-b). Men and women compete in the same open class — there are no separate gender categories — and junior contests exist at national level.

## History

The idea of air guitar as a real competition is credited to Finnish musician Jukka Takalo, and the first Air Guitar World Championship was staged in Oulu in 1996 as part of the Oulu Music Video Festival; since 2011 the event has been produced by Airnest Productions Ltd. Iconic air-guitar moments in music history include Joe Cocker's air guitar at Woodstock in 1969, and the first air guitar contests were held in the United States in the late 1970s. Among the most celebrated champions is Gareth "The Reverend" Icke of the United Kingdom; on the women's side, Lady Atilla's podium finishes in 2022 and 2025 and Seven Seas's runner-up spot in 2026 show that women have been part of the world elite from early on (AGWC, n.d.-a).
