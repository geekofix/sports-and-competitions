## Basic Information

Aerobic gymnastics is a FIG discipline in which athletes perform a high-intensity routine of continuous, complex movement patterns — derived from traditional aerobics — to music on a 10 × 10 m floor, with a senior routine lasting about 1 minute 20 seconds (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique [FIG], n.d.-a). It has been an official FIG discipline since 1995, when the first World Championships were held in Paris (FIG, n.d.-b). The competition categories are individual women, individual men, pair, trio, group of five, aerobic dance and aerobic step (FIG, n.d.-a). See also [Gymnastics](gymnastics.html) and [Acrobatic Gymnastics](acrobatic-gymnastics.html) on this site (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-a) (Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, n.d.-b).

Among the athletes shaping the sport today are Russia's Anastasia Dmitrieva, the 2026 women's individual world champion, and Spain's Miquel Mañé, who won a third consecutive men's individual world title at the 19th World Championships in Pamplona, Spain (September 11–13, 2026), alongside the Russian mixed pair of Elashvili and Shikhaleev; in the team/nation classification, Russia won gold ahead of China and South Korea (FIG, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Routines are performed on a 10 × 10 m floor and last 1 minute 20 seconds (± 5 seconds) in the senior category. The routine is built from the seven basic aerobic steps — march, jog, kick, jack, lunge, knee lift and step touch — combined into choreography, and must include elements from four mandatory groups: dynamic strength (push-up and straddle support families), static strength (support and lever families), jumps (aerial elements) and flexibility (split and pike families), with a maximum number of elements allowed per group (FIG, n.d.-a). Judging covers artistry, execution and difficulty under the FIG Code of Points. Men and women compete in separate individual categories, and pairs, trios and groups may be mixed or single-sex depending on the category, so women and men compete under the same rule set (FIG, n.d.-a).

## History

Aerobic gymnastics emerged from the aerobics fitness boom of the 1980s, when choreographed aerobic routines began to be performed competitively, and the first World Championships were held in Paris in 1995 under the FIG, which made the discipline official that same year (FIG, n.d.-b). Since then the Code of Points has been revised repeatedly as difficulty increased, and the aerobic dance and aerobic step categories were added to broaden participation. Nations such as France, Romania, Russia, China and Spain have traditionally dominated the medal tables, and the women's individual event has produced icons such as Angela McKinnon and, more recently, world champions from Russia and Spain (FIG, n.d.-b).
