## Basic Information

Kickboxing is a group of stand-up combat sports based on punching and kicking, practiced both as a martial art and as a full-contact ring sport. The international governing body is WAKO (World Association of Kickboxing Organizations), founded in 1976 and recognized by the International Olympic Committee; its world championships cover the disciplines point fighting, light contact, kick light (tatami), and full contact, low kick and K1 rules (ring). Professional kickboxing is global, with major promotions such as Glory and the Japanese K-1, founded in 1993.

## Rules

Under WAKO rules, tatami disciplines (point fighting, light contact, kick light) are fought in three two-minute rounds with a one-minute break, stopping after each valid technique; ring disciplines (full contact, low kick, K1) are fought in rounds of two or three minutes with full-power hand and leg techniques, judges scoring, and victory by knockout, stoppage or points. Fighters wear boxing-style gloves, shin guards, a groin protector and a mouthguard (and headgear in some amateur disciplines), and compete in weight classes. Illegal techniques include strikes to the back of the head, throat, groin and below the belt, headbutts, elbow strikes in most disciplines, and attacks on a downed opponent. Warnings, penalties and an eight- or ten-count for knockdowns are enforced by the referee.

## History

Modern kickboxing emerged in the 1950s-1970s when Japanese karateka began allowing full-contact bouts with kicks, and it was popularized in the United States in the 1970s by fighters such as Joe Lewis and Bill "Superfoot" Wallace, who combined karate kicks with boxing. WAKO was founded in 1976 and has since grown to more than 100 member federations; K-1 was created in Japan in 1993 by Kazuyoshi Ishii and became the premier professional kickboxing brand, later joined by Glory. Kickboxing is not yet an Olympic sport, but WAKO is fully recognized by the IOC and the sport is part of the World Games program. Champions such as Rico Verhoeven (Glory heavyweight) have brought the sport mainstream television audiences.
