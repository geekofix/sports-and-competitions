## Basic Information

Five-stand is a clay-target shooting discipline in which the shooter rotates through five stations (stands) and takes five targets at each — typically a mix of singles and simultaneous or report pairs — for 25 targets per round; the layout and target menu at each stand are standardized (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the clay-target family alongside sporting clays, trap and skeet.

*Figure: 23IVAN. (2026-03-17 13:19:41). Five-stand shooting range with stands and traps, Hungary [Photograph]. Wikimedia Commons. CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Compak_Sporting_shooting_stands_Hungary.jpg*

The governing body in North America is the NSSA (which runs five-stand as part of its championship program), and internationally the discipline is shot under FITASC/ISSF-style rules at world-level events (NSSA, n.d.). Stations 1–5 each hold up to three traps (high house, low house and a middle/rabbit trap), and the menu specifies which targets are singles and which are pairs, so every competitor shoots the same 25-target course (Wikipedia, n.d.).

## Rules

Scoring is one point per broken target; a 25-straight is the perfect round and earns special recognition at championships (NSSA, n.d.). Equipment rules mirror other clay disciplines: 12 gauge or smaller, shot limits, gun mounted before the call (Wikipedia, n.d.). Men and women compete in the same event with Lady classifications at major championships; sub-gauge five-stand exists for .410/28 (NSSA, n.d.).

## History

Five-stand was developed in the United States in the 1960s–70s to compress the variety of a full sporting course into five stations, and the NSSA made it a championship event in the 1980s (Wikipedia, n.d.). It became popular because a full round takes under 15 minutes and the fixed menus make results directly comparable between shoots (NSSA, n.d.). Today five-stand is shot at clubs across North America and at world-level events in Europe.
