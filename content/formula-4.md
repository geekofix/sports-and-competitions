## Basic Information

Formula 4 (F4) is the entry level of the FIA Global Pathway from karting to Formula 1: a single-seater category created by the FIA in 2013 (approved by the World Motor Sport Council), with a minimum driver age of 15 (Wikipedia, n.d.). It belongs to the formula-racing family with F1, F2, F3 and F1 Academy.

As of 2025 the FIA recognizes thirteen national and regional F4 championships (British, Italian, Spanish, German, French, US, Japanese, Chinese, UAE, Brazilian, Indian, SEA, NACAM and more), and the inaugural FIA F4 World Cup was held at the 2025 Macau Grand Prix on 16 November 2025, won by Jules Roussel of France ahead of Emanuele Olivieri and Rintaro Sato, with a 20-car invite-only field of reigning F4 champions (Wikipedia, 2025).

## Rules

F4 cars are homologated spec or semi-spec single-seaters — currently Ligier/Mygale M21-F4 chassis with ~1.3–1.6 L turbo engines in most series — with defined aerodynamics and a single tyre supplier per championship; costs are capped by regulation to keep the step affordable (Wikipedia, n.d.). Race weekends run sprint and feature races; points decide the championship. The category feeds F1 Academy, Formula Regional and F3. Both men and women race — F1 Academy graduates regularly join national F4 series.

## History

The FIA’s F4 plan was revealed in 2013 by the WMSC to create a standardized, affordable step between karting and Formula 3 (Wikipedia, n.d.). The first championships launched in 2014 (Italian F4, with the old Abarth F4, was the pioneer); by 2025 thirteen FIA-certified series existed and the category also appeared at the FIA Motorsport Games as the F4 Cup (Wikipedia, n.d.). Macau has hosted F4 since 2020 as the "F4 World Final" before it became the FIA F4 World Cup in 2025 (Wikipedia, 2025).
