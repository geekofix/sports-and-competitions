## Basic Information

Para archery is archery for athletes with a physical impairment, governed by World Archery under the Book of Rules with para-specific regulations and classified by the IPC as W1 (impairments affecting both upper and lower body, shooting from a wheelchair or with prostheses) or W2 (lower-body impairment), with separate recurve and compound "open" events (W2 athletes plus eligible standing archers) (World Archery, n.d.-c; World Archery, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d). It has been on the Paralympic programme since Rome 1960, when 83 athletes from 15 countries contested eight medal events; at Paris 2024, 137 athletes from 47 countries competed in nine events at the Esplanade des Invalides (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; International Paralympic Committee, n.d.).

Among today's leading athletes, the women come first: Öznur Cüre of Türkiye set a world record of 704 in the ranking round and won compound open gold at Paris 2024, then took the 2025 world title in Gwangju (with Büşra Ün in the doubles open), while Fatemeh Hemmati of Iran took silver in Paris and is pictured; Harvinder Singh of India won the recurve open title at Paris 2024 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; World Archery, n.d.-a; Olympics, n.d.). On the men's side, Matt Stutzman of the United States won the compound open gold at Paris 2024 with 149, a Paralympic record, before retiring in January 2025, and the 2026 World Archery Para Series runs five stages from 30 March 2026 (2024, September 8; World Archery, n.d.-a).

## Rules

World Archery's Book of Rules governs, with the para regulations defining the classes: W1 athletes compete with a wheelchair or prostheses and may use a compound or recurve bow, and W2/open events combine wheelchair and standing archers with the same equipment rules — release aids are permitted for W1 (World Archery, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-d).

At the Paralympics, archers shoot a 72-arrow ranking round (recurve at 70 m on a 122 cm face, compound at 50 m on an 80 cm face, 10-ring scoring); the top seeds then contest head-to-head elimination matches shot in three-arrow ends, with ties decided by a single-arrow shoot-off, and mixed team events pair one man and one woman (Wikipedia, n.d.-b; World Archery, n.d.-c). Men and women compete separately in individual events and together in mixed team events; qualification quotas are allocated via world championships, continental championships and world ranking tournaments (World Archery, n.d.-a).

## History

Archery was one of the eight sports at the first Paralympic Games in Rome 1960, staged by the Stoke Mandeville organisers, and the first World Para Archery Championships followed in 1998 in the United Kingdom; the biennial worlds have since visited Dubai 2022, Plzeň 2023 and Gwangju 2025 (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

The sport's best-known icon is Matt Stutzman of the United States, the "Armless Archer" who shoots with his feet and is pictured at London 2012: he won silver on his Paralympic debut at London 2012 and gold at Paris 2024 before retiring in January 2025, and was named World Archery Para athlete of the year (2024, September 8; Wikipedia, n.d.-c). Britain's Danielle Brown won the standing/recurve titles at London 2012 and Rio 2016, and Phoebe Paterson Pine took W1 gold at Tokyo 2020 (Wikipedia, n.d.-d).
