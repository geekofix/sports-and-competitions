## Basic Information

Haggis hurling is a Scottish novelty sport in which competitors throw a haggis — the traditional sheep's-pluck pudding — for distance and accuracy from a platform, usually a whisky barrel; the World Haggis Hurling Championship is held annually at Airth in Scotland, with parallel events such as the Canadian championship in Perth, Ontario, run alongside the Perth Kilt Run (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The "sport" was invented as a joke in 1977 by Robin Dunseath, a publicist for Tom Farmer, then president of the World Haggis Hurling Association, at the Gathering of the Clans in Edinburgh — the stunt was televised on the BBC's That's Life! and only revealed as a hoax in 2004, with proceeds going to charity (BBC, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Competitions are mixed-gender, and women and men throw in the same events; the current leading throwers include reigning champion Garry McClay, who hurled a haggis 220 ft at Ellisland Farm in an ITV-covered event (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

For competition, a haggis must be traditional — sheep's pluck (heart, liver, lungs) minced with suet, oatmeal and seasoning, packed into the sheep's stomach (paunch) and boiled for around three hours — then cooled and inspected; firming agents are not allowed and the haggis must be packed tight (Wikipedia, n.d.-b). A "sporting haggis" weighs 500 g ± 30 g, with a maximum diameter of 18 cm and a length of 22 cm; the heavyweight class allows up to 1 kg, with a standard of 850 g ± 50 g (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Throwers hurl from a raised platform — traditionally a whisky barrel — and are judged on both distance and accuracy; a haggis that splits or bursts on landing is disqualified because it must land intact and edible (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The world record stands at 217 ft (66.14 m), set by Lorne Coltart at the Milngavie Highland Games on 11 June 2011, beating Allan Pettigrew's 180 ft at Inchmurrin in 1984, and the record is recognized by Guinness World Records (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Men and women compete under identical rules in the same championship; age classes (juniors/seniors) are used at some games (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## History

Haggis hurling was invented in 1977 by Robin Dunseath as a publicity stunt for the Gathering of the Clans in Edinburgh, with Tom Farmer's World Haggis Hurling Association lending it a mock-serious veneer; the BBC's That's Life! broadcast the event, and Dunseath revealed in 2004 that the whole thing was a joke, with the proceeds donated to charity (BBC, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). From the joke grew a real, if tongue-in-cheek, competition circuit: the World Haggis Hurling Championship is held annually at Airth, the Canadian championship takes place in Perth, Ontario, alongside the Kilt Run — 571 participants were registered in 2014 and distances are laser-measured — and the first Belgian event was held in Deurne in 2021 (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Record holders include Allan Pettigrew (180 ft, Inchmurrin, 1984) and Lorne Coltart (217 ft, Milngavie, 2011), while Garry McClay's 220 ft throw at Ellisland Farm, covered by ITV, is the best-documented modern mark (Guinness World Records, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
