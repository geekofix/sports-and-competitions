## Basic Information

Flyball is a timed dog relay race in which teams of four dogs race over four hurdles, trigger an automatic ball launcher (spring box) with their paw, catch the tennis ball and return over the hurdles; the fastest team time wins. It is governed in North America by NAFA (North American Flyball Association), which runs the NAFA Flyball World Championship and the CanAm Classic, and by the U-FLI (United Flyball League International) with its own records.

## Rules

A flyball team has four dogs plus up to two alternates. The course is 51 ft (15.5 m) long: the start/finish line, then four hurdles spaced 5 ft apart starting 10 ft from the line, with the spring box at the far end (hurdles are 8-16 in high depending on the team: hurdle height = the shortest dog's shoulder height minus a minimum, with a floor of 7 in and a cap of 14 in — NAFA sets each dog's height as the smallest dog's withers minus 6 in, min 7 in, max 14 in). The first dog triggers the box, catches the ball, and returns over all four hurdles to cross the line, then the next dog is released when the returning dog crosses the start line (a "pass" may be early or clean). The team's time stops when all four dogs have completed their runs with clean catches; a missed ball or knocked hurdle adds a penalty (a "fault" = 5-second penalty or re-run in some formats). The fastest time of the team wins; ties broken by best-of or run-offs. At NAFA Nationals, teams race in divisions by combined time brackets.

## History

Flyball was invented in the late 1970s in California by Herbert Wagner, who built the first spring-loaded ball box for his dog; the first tournament was held in 1983 in Los Angeles, and NAFA was founded in 1985 to standardize the rules, with the first NAFA Nationals held in the late 1980s and a formal rulebook published since. Top teams such as Border Patrol hold the NAFA record (14.433 s), and U-FLI records stand around 14.18 s. The CanAm Classic (nafacanam.com), held annually since 1990 in Windsor, Ontario, is the largest flyball event. The sport has spread to Europe, Australia and Japan, with dogs of all breeds (Border Collies, Whippets and mixed breeds dominate).
