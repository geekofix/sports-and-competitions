## Basic Information

Flyball is a timed relay race for dogs: two teams of four dogs race side by side down a 51 ft (15.5 m) lane, over four hurdles, to a spring-loaded box that releases a tennis ball when a dog presses its pad; each dog catches the ball, returns over all four hurdles and crosses the start/finish line before the next dog may be released, and the team with the fastest clean time wins the heat (NAFA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport is governed in North America by the North American Flyball Association (NAFA), founded in 1984, which today counts more than 1,000 registered clubs, and by the United Flyball League International (U-FLI), which runs its own records and the Tournament of Champions at Purina Farms; the largest single event is the CanAm Classic, held annually since 1990 — the 2026 edition took place September 18–20 at the Indiana State Fairgrounds in Indianapolis (NAFA, n.d.-a; U-FLI, n.d.; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The all-time records stand at 14.433 s for the NAFA team Border Patrol (June 5, 2016, Rockton, Ontario) and 14.182 s for Touch N Go under U-FLI rules (April 12, 2014, Hurricane, Utah), while in Europe the Belgian team Roadrunners set the EFC mark of 15.31 s (NAFA, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

Because flyball is a dog sport, there are no men's or women's divisions — dogs of any sex compete side by side and titles are decided purely by time. The dominant teams of the 2025–26 season include record holders such as Border Patrol in North America and the leading European clubs from Belgium, the Netherlands and the Czech Republic, which meet annually at the European Flyball Championship (EFC) with more than 130 teams (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).

## Rules

Under the NAFA Official Rules of Racing, a race matches two teams of four dogs (plus up to two alternates) in parallel lanes of a 51 ft course: the start/finish line, then the first hurdle 6 ft (1.8 m) out, the four hurdles 10 ft (3.0 m) apart, and the flyball box 15 ft (4.6 m) after the last hurdle, with a runback area of at least 29 ft behind the start line (NAFA, n.d.-b). Hurdle height is set individually for each team: under NAFA rules it is the withers (shoulder) height of the smallest dog minus 6 in (15 cm), with a floor of 7 in (18 cm) and a cap of 14 in (36 cm); the UKFL uses a patented ulna measuring device with 6–12 in, and the EFC allows 17.5–35 cm (NAFA, n.d.-b; Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Each dog runs in relay: it triggers the box with its paw, catches the ball, and returns over all four hurdles; the next dog is released when the returning dog crosses the start line with the ball, and the team's clock stops when the fourth dog crosses. Faults — dropping the ball before the last hurdle on the return, releasing the next dog early, or knocking a hurdle — are penalized (typically a 5-second penalty or a re-run, depending on the rulebook in use), and electronic judging times starts, passes and finishes to the thousandth of a second (NAFA, n.d.-b). A team time below 24.00 s is a "breakout" and beats any non-breaking-out opponent; if both teams break out, the heat is re-run. Title points are awarded for times under 24 s (25 points per dog), under 28 s (5 points) and under 32 s (1 point), and the Flyball Grand Dog Champion (FGDch) title requires 30,000 points (NAFA, n.d.-b). At NAFA Nationals, teams are seeded into divisions by their combined heat times.

## History

Flyball began in Southern California in the late 1960s and early 1970s, when dog trainers combined scent hurdle racing with having the dogs retrieve a tennis ball; Herbert Wagner is credited with building the first spring-loaded flyball box and with demonstrating the sport on *The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson* (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The first flyball tournament was held in 1983 in Los Angeles, and NAFA was founded in 1984 by twelve clubs from Michigan and Ontario to standardize the rules; U-FLI later emerged as a second North American league (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). The sport reached the Netherlands in 1990 through Otto Steijn and spread to Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and Japan, while Europe developed its own annual championship circuit (EFC), held in Belgium, the Czech Republic, Germany, France and the United Kingdom among others (Wikipedia, n.d.-a). Among the most famous competitors are the record-setting teams Border Patrol (NAFA, 14.433 s), Touch N Go (U-FLI, 14.182 s) and the Belgian Roadrunners, whose dogs of Border Collie, Whippet and mixed-breed type dominate the leaderboards (NAFA, n.d.-c; Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
