## Basic Information

Saddle bronc riding is a rodeo roughstock event in which a rider attempts to stay on a specially bred bucking horse for eight seconds, using a specialised saddle, a single rein and one hand. Often called rodeo's classic event because it grew directly from the horse-breaking work of ranch cowboys, it is a headline event of Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA) rodeos, whose season culminates at the National Finals Rodeo (NFR) in Las Vegas, and is contested equally by organisations such as the Canadian Professional Rodeo Association and at events like the Calgary Stampede.

## Rules

The ride must last eight seconds, timed from the moment the horse's front feet hit the ground leaving the chute. A qualifying ride also demands the "mark-out": the rider's heels must be above the point of the horse's shoulders on the first jump out of the chute, and held until the horse's front feet hit the ground — otherwise the ride is disqualified. The rider grips a thick bronc rein (about six feet of braided rope attached to the horse's halter) with one hand, and touching the horse, himself or any equipment with the free hand results in disqualification; losing the rein also disqualifies. Judges score a completed ride out of 100 points, with up to 50 for the rider and 50 for the horse's bucking action, crediting the rider's spurring stroke (heels working in rhythm with the buck), control and style, and the horse's degree of bucking; the rider must keep both stirrups and stay in the PRCA-specification saddle, which is built to a fixed tree with defined fork, gullet and cantle measurements. Spurs with dulled rowels and dry resin on saddle and chaps are permitted; sharp or locked rowels and foreign substances are violations.

## History

The event descends from the ranch work of the 19th-century American West, where breaking broncs was a necessary cowboy skill and informal contests at gatherings such as Prescott, Arizona's 1888 frontier days formalised roughstock competition. The PRCA, founded in 1936 as the Cowboys Turtle Association, standardized saddle bronc riding's mark-out and eight-second rules, and the National Finals Rodeo ran from 1959 before settling in Las Vegas in 1985. Legendary champions shaped the event — Casey Tibbs, whose flamboyant style defined the 1950s, Dan Mortensen with six world titles from the 1990s–2000s, and Billy Etbauer, who as recently as 2004 won his fifth world championship at 41 on a record-tying 93-point ride at the NFR. Modern dynasties include the Wright family of Milford, Utah, and Canada's Zeke Thurston, a four-time PRCA world champion (2016, 2019, 2022, 2023); the highest scored rides sit at the 93–94-point level, with Thurston's 94-point ride at the 2019 Hardgrass Bronc Match in Alberta among the highest ever recorded. Saddle bronc riding remains one of rodeo's most televised events, a fixture from the NFR to the Calgary Stampede, and the breeding of bucking horses — often from documented bloodlines such as the Calgary Stampede and C5 Rodeo strings — has turned the bronc itself into a celebrated equine athlete.
