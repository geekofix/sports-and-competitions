## Basic Information

Dorset wrestling is the West Country catch-hold style of Dorset (the Devon/Cornwall style family): standing jacket grips above the waist, trips and leg hooks allowed, a back fall decides — or three fouls ("go downs") count as one fall — and no groundwork. It was the style of the great West Country wrestling booths at fairs (the "Great Dorset" shows), with champions like Abraham Cann ("the last champion in Devon style"). It survives in heritage matches at Dorset shows and is classed with Cornish wrestling as Celtic style. Within the combat sports and martial arts on this site, its closest relatives are [Cornish Wrestling](cornish-wrestling.html), [Cumberland & Westmorland Wrestling](cumberland-westmorland-wrestling.html), [Lancashire Wrestling](lancashire-wrestling.html), [Wrestling](wrestling.html); the related pages are listed under Important Links (Scientific Wrestling, n.d.).

## Rules

West Country rules: catch-hold of jackets above the waist; tripping and hooking allowed; no ground fighting. A back fall (any part except feet/hands touching the ground) wins the bout; in some venues three fouls or go-downs substitute one back fall; championship matches best of three. Umpire starts from the clinch ("en guard — wrestle"). Costume: shorts, vest, sometimes the championship belt. Women's bouts are increasingly held at modern heritage events (Scientific Wrestling, n.d.).

## History

The West Country (Devon, Somerset, Dorset, Cornwall) was one of England's two great wrestling regions (with the North Country) from the 18th century; matches at fairs and in London (the London Catch-hold) drew massive crowds in the 19th century. The style declined after WWII and is now largely a heritage sport; Abraham Cann (fl. 1846–1859), pictured by Henry Caunter, is remembered as the last Devon-style champion (Wikipedia, n.d.-a).
