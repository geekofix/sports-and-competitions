## Basic Information

Paddleball is an American wall sport played with a solid paddle and a small rubber ball, in two main formats: four-wall paddleball, played indoors on a standard 40 by 20 foot handball court, and one-wall paddleball, played outdoors against a single rebound wall. Four-wall paddleball was invented in 1930 by Earl Riskey, a physical education instructor at the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor. The sport is popular in the Upper Midwest of the United States (Michigan and Wisconsin), on the West Coast (especially southern California) and around Memphis, Tennessee. It is closely related to [handball](handball.html), [racquetball](racquetball.html), [squash](squash.html) and [paddle tennis](paddle-tennis.html).

## Rules

In four-wall paddleball, two players (singles), three (cutthroat) or four (doubles) play on the same 40 by 20 foot court used for American handball, but with a solid paddle and a slower, slightly larger rubber ball. The server stands in the service zone and must hit the ball against the front wall so that it lands in the opponent's court; a serve that hits the floor first (short), flies over the back wall (long), hits the ceiling, or touches two or more walls before the floor is illegal, as is a foot fault. During a rally the ball must be hit so that it strikes the front wall before the floor, and the opponent may play it after one bounce or on the volley. Only the serving side can score, and games are played to 21 points. In one-wall paddleball, played in singles or doubles against a single wall, the ball must strike the wall without the floor being touched more than once in a rally, and points are scored only by the serving side.

## History

Earl Riskey invented four-wall paddleball in 1930 at the University of Michigan's Intramural Sports Building in Ann Arbor, after tennis players there had been using wooden paddle tennis paddles on the handball courts in bad weather; he found that a tennis ball with its fuzz removed had the right bounce, and the paddleball trophy awarded annually for service to the game bears his name. The courts in Ann Arbor are still a center of national championship tournaments. The National Paddleball Association (NPA) served as the official governing body and source of rules, and the United States Paddleball Association was revived in 2005 to promote the original one-wall rules. Noted players include Bud Muehleisen, Marty Hogan and Charlie Brumfield, who held national open titles in both paddleball and racquetball, and eight-time national champion Mike Wisniewski, who builds paddles in Bay City, Michigan. Racquetball itself was invented in 1949 by Joe Sobek when he played four-wall paddleball with a strung racquet.
