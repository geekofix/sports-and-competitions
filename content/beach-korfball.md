## Basic Information

Beach korfball is the sand variant of the Dutch-invented mixed team game korfball, played four versus four — two women and two men — on a 20 m × 10 m sand court with a single korf (basket) per end; it is governed by the International Korfball Federation (International Korfball Federation [IKF], n.d.-b). The IKF World Beach Korfball Championship 2026 was held November 6–8, 2026 on the Shenzhou Peninsula in Wanning, Hainan, China, with 16 teams playing 64 matches; Belgium beat Hungary 2–0 in the final, the Netherlands took bronze, and the continental Beach Korfball Championships 2026 were staged in Europe (Costa de Caparica, Portugal — the Netherlands beat Belgium on a shoot-out in the final), Africa (Egypt) and Asia (Bangkok, Thailand), with Hungary ranked world number one ahead of the event (IKF, n.d.-a). Because teams must field two women and two men at all times, korfball — and its beach variant — has been mixed-gender by law since its invention, and the IKF also publishes an official beach korfball world ranking (IKF, n.d.-b).

## Rules

Indoor korfball is played by two teams of eight (four women and four men) on a 40 m × 20 m court with a 3.5 m high korf (a rimless basket on a pole) at each end; a goal — throwing the ball through the korf from above — scores one point, and matches last two 15-minute halves (IKF, n.d.-b). Beach korfball reduces this to four players per side (two women, two men) on a 20 m × 10 m sand court with the same korf; goals count one point, matches are played in two halves, and a draw is settled by a shoot-out series of one-on-one attempts from in front of the korf. Defending players must stay 2.5 m from the ball-carrier with the ball, may not defend a player of the opposite gender, and physical contact is limited — these rules make the mixed game safe and strategic at the same time. Men's and women's teams play under identical rules, and youth categories shorten the court and game time (IKF, n.d.-a).

## History

Korfball was invented in 1902 by Nico Broekhuysen, a Dutch schoolteacher in Naarden, Netherlands, who adapted the Swedish game ringball (ringtennis) into a mixed-gender ball game suitable for boys and girls playing together in one team; the first demonstration was given at the 1920 Antwerp Olympics, and korfball was a demonstration sport at the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics (International Olympic Committee [IOC], n.d.). The International Korfball Federation (IKF) was founded in 1933 by 11 national associations and has staged World Championships since 1978. Beach korfball emerged as an organised variant in the 2010s and has been played at IKF World Beach Korfball Championships ever since; the 2026 edition in Hainan, China, was won by Belgium over Hungary (IKF, n.d.-a). The women's side has been integral from the start — Broekhuysen's central idea was the four-women, four-men team — and Dutch players such as Gerald van Dijk represent the country where the mixed game was born.
