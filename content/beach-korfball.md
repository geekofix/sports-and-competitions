## Basic Information

Beach korfball is the sand version of korfball, the mixed-gender ball game invented in the Netherlands: usually four players per team (2 women, 2 men) on a smaller court, aiming to throw the ball through the opponent's korf (a pole-mounted basket). It is governed by the International Korfball Federation (IKF), which sanctions beach korfball events and the Beach Korfball World Championship.

## Rules

Under IKF beach korfball rules, teams of four (two female, two male players) play on a sand court of about 20 x 10 m with one korf at each end. A match has two halves of 10-15 minutes. Goals are scored by throwing the ball through the korf from any position except directly in front of it (the "shadow" rule). There is no running with the ball: players may pivot on one foot and pass to teammates; otherwise standard korfball rules apply, adapted for sand, with free substitutions. At international tournaments, a group stage is followed by placement matches.

## History

Korfball was invented in 1902 by Dutch teacher Nico Broekhuysen as a mixed-gender alternative to football. Beach korfball emerged in the 2000s in the Netherlands and Belgium, where korfball is hugely popular, and the IKF began sanctioning international beach events, including the Beach Korfball World Championship. It is also played at multi-sport beach events such as the ANOC World Beach Games.
